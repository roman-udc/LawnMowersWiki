# Why your riding lawn mower smells like gas and how to fix it

Source: https://www.searspartsdirect.com/diy/article/why-your-riding-lawn-mower-smells-like-gas-and-how-to-fix-it

Catching a faint gasoline smell while mowing isn’t unusual, but if the odor is strong enough to turn your head or it lingers long after you’ve parked the mower, something’s not right. Persistent fuel smells can mean something simple, like an overfilled tank, or they could point to a leak or other problem that needs attention. Besides wasting fuel, a gas leak can damage your mower and create a serious fire risk. Before you roll the mower back onto the lawn, take the time to track down the source and fix it so you can mow safely and confidently.

## Why your lawn mower smells like gas

Most strong gas smells come from one of three culprits: a fuel leak, evaporating gasoline, or storing the mower in conditions that encourage fumes to escape. Tracking down the source not only helps you fix the problem, but also keeps your mower safe to run.

### Fuel leaks from the carburetor

If the carburetor’s float valve sticks or its gaskets wear out, gas can drip or seep from spots it shouldn’t. You might spot a small puddle under the mower or notice the carburetor body looks wet. Because the carburetor sits close to the engine, the smell is usually strongest right after you’ve been running the mower.

### Loose or damaged fuel lines

Over time, fuel hoses can dry out, crack, or loosen at their clamps. Even a small split in the line can release fumes and small amounts of fuel. Check along the length of the hose for damp spots, stains, or brittleness, paying special attention to the area near the fuel tank. If the smell is strongest when you’re near the tank, the lines might be to blame.

### Leaky fuel tank or cap

Plastic fuel tanks can crack with age or from impact, and the rubber seal inside the fuel cap can wear out or go missing entirely. Either problem can let gasoline escape as liquid or vapor. Even without a visible leak, a worn cap can allow fumes to escape freely, especially on hot days or when the mower is stored in a closed garage or shed.

### Overfilling the fuel tank

Topping the tank right up to the brim can cause spillage or allow vapors to vent out as the fuel expands in the heat. This is especially common if you fill the tank in the morning and leave the mower sitting in the sun before you start mowing. Aim to stop filling once you reach the fill neck, not above it.

### Storage and temperature changes

Gasoline expands when it gets hot, so storing a mower in direct sunlight or in a hot shed can push fuel vapor past seals and out of the tank. If you store your mower for long periods, drain the tank or run it nearly empty before putting it away to reduce odors and evaporation.

## How to fix a gas smell from your mower

Always work in a well-ventilated area, keep ignition sources far away, and wear gloves when handling fuel or fuel system parts. Even a small spark can ignite gasoline fumes, so take safety seriously before you start troubleshooting.

### Step 1: Check for visible leaks

Inspect the carburetor, fuel lines, fuel filter, tank, and cap for any signs of dampness or staining. Look underneath the mower for fresh spots on the deck or floor. Tighten any loose fittings you find, and wipe up spills right away.

### Step 2: Replace damaged parts

If you find cracks, worn seals, or other faulty components, replacement is the safest fix. For example, a clogged or cracked fuel filter can cause leaks or strong fuel odors, so [replace the fuel filter](https://www.searspartsdirect.com/diy/repair-guide/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-fuel-filter) or fuel line if you see damage. If the carburetor is leaking from worn gaskets or a stuck float, you can [rebuild it](https://www.searspartsdirect.com/diy/repair-guide/how-to-rebuild-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor), but [installing a new carburetor](https://www.searspartsdirect.com/diy/repair-guide/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor) is often quicker.

### Step 3: Avoid overfilling

Stop adding gas once the fuel reaches the bottom of the fill neck. Overfilling can cause spillage and extra vapor release, especially on hot days. Check your service manual for the correct fuel capacity and fill level.

### Step 4: Store your mower correctly

Gasoline expands with heat, so store your mower in a cool, shaded area to limit evaporation. Use a gas can with a tight-sealing spout or cap to prevent extra fumes from escaping into your garage or shed.

## When to seek further troubleshooting

If your mower still smells like gas after you’ve fixed any obvious leaks or overfilling issues, there could be a deeper mechanical problem such as a faulty fuel shut-off valve. At that point, it’s worth taking a closer look with detailed step-by-step guidance from our [riding lawn mower repair help](https://www.searspartsdirect.com/diy/riding-mowers-tractor-repair/1234652) resources.

Addressing a strong gas smell early can prevent wasted fuel, reduce fire hazards, and protect your engine from damage. Make it part of your seasonal mower prep to check for leaks before the first cut of the year. A few minutes spent now can save you time, money, and frustration later.
