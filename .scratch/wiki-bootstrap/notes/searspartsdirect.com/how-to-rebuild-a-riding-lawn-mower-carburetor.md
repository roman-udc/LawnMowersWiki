## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor blends air and fuel into a combustible mixture. If it runs rough because clogged or dirty, clean and rebuild with a manufacturer-approved replacement kit (gaskets, seals, hinge pin, other parts; you probably won't use every part). In this repair the fuel bowl gasket, mounting gasket and hinge pin are used (Craftsman carburetor problems). Applies to common Briggs & Stratton engines on Craftsman, Troybilt, Husqvarna, MTD, Poulan, Toro, Murray, Snapper, Yard-Man, John Deere riding mowers and tractors.
- fact: If too corroded, dirty or damaged to rebuild, replace it (separate guide: replace a carburetor in a riding mower).
- difficulty: 4
- time: 60 minutes or less
- tools: wrench set; nut driver set; punch set; hammer; pliers; hose pinch-off pliers; carburetor cleaner; compressed air; thin wire or paper clip; shop rags; fuel-safe container; petroleum jelly; safety goggles; work gloves
- parts: carburetor rebuild kit (fuel bowl gasket, engine intake/mounting gasket, hinge pin, needle valve, air intake O-ring used in this repair)
- safety: Repairs to power lawn-care equipment can be hazardous; use proper tools and safety gear; don't proceed unless confident; some repairs only by a qualified technician.
- step 1: Disconnect the battery. Park on a level surface in a well-ventilated area, set parking brake, ignition off and key out, let engine cool; gloves and goggles; lift seat; remove bolt connecting the negative cable to the battery and tuck the cable away so it doesn't touch the post.
- step 2: Remove engine blower housing. Lift hood (tip: remove hood for more room). Remove air duct mounting screws and duct. Turn each air filter cover retaining knob counterclockwise, remove cover and air filter. Remove air filter housing screw. Remove front and rear blower housing mounting bolts and lift off housing.
- step 3: Disconnect fuel line. Clamp the fuel line with hose pinch-off pliers; shop rag under the carburetor end; release the clamp at carburetor and pull line off; wipe spills.
- step 4: Remove carburetor. Remove air intake mounting nuts; detach breather tube and remove air intake; disconnect wire from fuel solenoid; photograph linkage connections for reassembly; remove carburetor mounting studs; disconnect throttle rod and throttle spring; disconnect choke rod; pull carburetor off and place on clean work surface in a well-ventilated area.
- step 5: Remove components. Unthread solenoid valve; remove float bowl, draining fuel into fuel-safe container; punch out the float hinge pin; remove float and needle valve; remove float bowl gasket, engine intake gasket and air intake O-ring.
- step 6: Clean. Clean float bowl and gasket seating surfaces with carburetor cleaner and a rag; paper clip to remove gasket and O-ring residue; carburetor cleaner and paper clip to clean all holes, especially the main and idle jets; let dry completely, at least 10 minutes.
- step 7: Reassemble. Lubricate new float bowl gasket with a little petroleum jelly; install new float bowl gasket; install new needle valve; reattach float with new hinge pin; attach float bowl with the solenoid valve; install new air intake O-ring.
- step 8: Install. Position carburetor near engine and reconnect choke rod; connect throttle rod and spring; install new intake gasket on engine; attach carburetor with mounting studs, tighten firmly; connect fuel solenoid wire; attach breather tube to air intake and connect air intake to carburetor; install air intake mounting nuts.
- step 9: Reattach fuel line to carburetor with hose clamp; remove pinch-off pliers.
- step 10: Reinstall blower housing: position and align the air diverter in the housing; install mounting bolts; air filter housing screw; air filter and cover; air duct with screws; lower hood.
- step 11: Reconnect negative cable to negative battery terminal; lower seat.
