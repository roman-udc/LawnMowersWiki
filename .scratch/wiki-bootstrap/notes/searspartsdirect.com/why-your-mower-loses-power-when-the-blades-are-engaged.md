## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "mower loses power when I engage the blades"
- alias: "engine bogs down when blades engage"
- alias: "sputters when I pull the blade engagement lever"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, worn-drive-belt, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, dirty-carburetor, bad-governor, bad-ignition-coil, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, low-compression
- fact: usually a fuel delivery issue, airflow problem, engine weakness under load, or too much resistance in the deck.
- tip: do the basics first (fuel and air).
- pro: if fuel, air, belts and blades check out and it still bogs or stalls with blades engaged, may be low compression or valve trouble; call a professional.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- confirm: pull the filter; dark, damp or debris-covered = replace.
- fact: dirty filter chokes airflow especially under load.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas more than a month or two old may be the problem; old gas doesn't burn as well. Drain and replace if unsure of freshness.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: restricts fuel just when the engine needs it; replaceable in a few minutes.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: blade drive belt transfers engine power to deck pulleys; worn, loose, cracked or stretched belt slips under load, creating drag so the engine bogs or stalls.
- symptom: squealing, burning-rubber smell.
- confirm: glazed, frayed or no longer snug around pulleys = replace the blade drive belt.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed clippings, sticks or debris around the blade area add resistance.
- fix: with mower off and spark plug disconnected, clean the deck underside.
- tip: deck spray such as MO-DECK non-stick spray (id-mo-deck) helps keep grass from sticking.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: bent blade or worn spindle bearing causes imbalance and excess drag; inspect blades, spin each by hand for smooth movement; replace blade or mandrel assembly if damaged.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fact: worn spindle bearing causes drag; replace the mandrel assembly.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: dirty or partly clogged carb may not give enough fuel when the engine needs more power for blades; clean or rebuild it (often solves it, esp. if engine runs rough or bogs under strain).

## page: causes/bad-governor
- fact: governor adjusts throttle automatically to maintain RPM; misadjusted or malfunctioning governor can't react quickly enough when the blades engage.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: runs fine cold but sputters or stalls when hot; loses power only after mowing a while; difficulty restarting after running.
- fact: aged coils break down under heat giving weak or inconsistent spark.
- confirm: test with multimeter or spark tester, or run mower until it stalls and check for spark immediately.
- fix: replace the ignition coil if failed.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: slow ground speed in tall or dense grass; avoid wet grass (clumps and drags the blade system); mow more frequently to prevent overgrowth.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: tall, thick or wet grass puts heavy load on engine and transmission with blades engaged.

## page: causes/low-compression
- fact: low compression or valve trouble are the "more serious engine problem" suspects when basics are fine; professional diagnosis.
