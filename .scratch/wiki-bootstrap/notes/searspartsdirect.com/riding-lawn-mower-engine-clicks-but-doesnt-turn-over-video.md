## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "riding mower clicks when I turn the key but won't turn over"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, bad-wiring-or-ground, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, seized-engine
- fact: Bad starter solenoid is the most frequent cause but not always; less frequent: bad starter motor, wiring failure, weak battery, locked-up engine.
- confirm: Test order in the video: battery, battery cables, starter solenoid, starter cable (voltage drop), seized engine (then starter motor by elimination).
- tools: work gloves and safety goggles, multimeter, wire brush, baking soda and water, petroleum jelly, clip-on meter probes (or a helper)

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: The click means the solenoid coil is getting power. The coil needs little current; the starter motor needs a lot, so a weak battery can click the solenoid but not crank the engine.
- confirm: Ignition off; access the battery (on this type of riding mower, lift the seat). Multimeter on DC voltage: red probe to positive (red) terminal, black probe to negative (black). A fully charged battery reads more than 12 volts DC. To spin the starter the battery typically needs to be at least 75 percent charged.
- fix: Charge if weak or dead; if the starter then spins the engine, done; if not, check the battery cables.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: Corroded or broken cables can keep the starter from getting power.
- fix: Remove corrosion: gloves and goggles; disconnect the negative cable first, then the positive; wire brush cable ends and terminals; if brushing doesn't remove all of it, sprinkle baking soda on the terminals and cable ends and moisten it (bubbles vigorously and dissolves corrosion); when bubbling stops, toothbrush off the remainder; wipe with a shop rag; thinly coat terminals with petroleum jelly to help prevent corrosion.
- confirm: Check the red battery cable delivers power to the solenoid: DC volts, red probe to the red post on the starter solenoid, black probe to the battery negative terminal; should be more than 12 volts. If not, replace the red battery cable.
- safety: Don't let a meter lead touch both solenoid posts at the same time (severe spark).

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- confirm: Disconnect the black starter cable from the solenoid post and attach the red meter probe to that post; clip the black probe to the battery negative; hold the key in START; you should hear the click; read the voltage; key off. More than 12 volts at the click means the black post gets power (solenoid OK). No voltage when it clicks = replace the starter solenoid.
- tip: Clip-on probes, or a helper to turn the key.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- step 1: Disconnect the black starter cable from the solenoid post; attach red meter probe to the post.
- step 2: Clip the black probe to the negative battery terminal.
- step 3: Hold the key in START; solenoid should click.
- step 4: Read voltage, then key off. More than 12 volts = solenoid works; none = replace.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: Starter cable voltage drop test (more accurate than a resistance test for a large multi-strand copper cable; a resistance test only detects any current, not whether the cable can carry enough to spin the starter). Reconnect the black starter cable to the solenoid post; clip red probe to the black post; lift hood and disconnect the spark plug wire so the engine can't start; clip the black lead to the starter motor stud where the black cable connects; hold the key in START and read the voltage after the click; key off.
- fact: Ideal: less than 1 volt drop. More than 1 volt means broken wire strands inside the cable; replace the starter cable.

## page: causes/seized-engine
- confirm: If the starter cable is good, the starter is getting power. Remove the plug from the screen above the flywheel to access the flywheel bolt; turn the flywheel bolt clockwise with a socket wrench to try to spin the engine.
- fix: Engine won't spin (locked up): have a service technician diagnose and repair.
- pro: yes, a seized engine goes to a technician.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: If the engine can be spun by hand and the cable and solenoid are good, the starter motor failed to spin it when activated; replace the starter motor.
