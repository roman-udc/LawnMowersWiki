## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: routine upkeep helps mower start easier, run smoother, cut more evenly and last longer
- fact (start of season): check and change engine oil (old oil can thicken or degrade in storage; drain and refill with oil recommended for the engine); replace spark plug if needed (hard to start last year or corrosion on plug); clean or replace air filter; check fuel system (empty old fuel, inspect lines for cracks, refill fresh gas); sharpen or replace blades (dull blades tear grass; sharpen if good, replace if nicked or worn); inspect and replace belts if cracked or worn (slip or squeal under load; look for fraying, glazing, slack); charge and test battery (trickle charger if unused; confirm it holds charge); check tire pressure and inflate to recommended PSI; grease all fittings per service manual; clean deck and check for rust or buildup (caked grass blocks airflow, uneven cut, corrosion; protective deck spray helps prevent clippings sticking)
- fact (mid season): check oil level before each use (low oil causes overheating or engine damage); clean deck regularly; check blades for chips, cracks, dullness; tighten hardware (vibration loosens bolts, fasteners, blade mounts); inspect belts and pulleys for wear or squealing (cracks, fraying, slipping); clean or replace air filter if mowing in dusty conditions; inspect battery terminals for corrosion, clean with wire brush
- tip: keep a simple service log of replacements and repairs
- fact: source links its own riding mower tune-up guide, DIY repair hub

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact (end of season): if storing more than 30 days, either drain the tank or add fuel stabilizer and run the engine to circulate it (old fuel breaks down and clogs carburetor)
- fact: clean mower thoroughly: deck, housing, air intake; debris holds moisture and causes rust
- fact: remove battery and store indoors if possible; cold garage or shed can shorten battery life; disconnect and store in cool dry place
- fact: sharpen or replace blades now; inspect for nicks and wear
- fact: store in dry place with a breathable/vented cover; avoid trapping moisture under plastic tarps
