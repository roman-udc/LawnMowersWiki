## page: problems/mower-is-noisy
- alias: "riding mower squealing", "grinding", "louder than usual", "rattling", "knocking"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, dull-or-bent-blade, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, clogged-air-filter
- fact: Normal sound: steady engine hum at idle and consistent whirring with blades engaged; smooth, even vibration. Abnormal: squealing belts, grinding metal, rattling parts, high-pitched noises that change with engine speed or blade movement.
- fact: Troubleshooting by sound: Squealing = worn/loose belt, dry bearing, or pulley misalignment; worse when engaging blades or raising throttle. Grinding from the deck = worn spindle bearings or damaged mandrels; only while mowing = blade movement related. Thudding or strong vibration = blades out of balance, loose, or hitting something under the deck; check blade condition and deck debris. Popping or knocking from the engine = low oil, fuel system issue, or spark plug; do a full tune-up.
- safety: Stop using the mower until the issue is found and fixed.
- tip: Prevent with maintenance: sharpen blades, inspect belts, tighten hardware, lubricate moving parts.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: high-pitched squeal when accelerating or engaging blades; whine and power loss.
- confirm: look over drive and deck belts for cracked, glazed, stretched or slipping.
- fix: replace the ground drive or blade drive belt as needed.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: grinding or screeching, especially with blades spinning.
- confirm: check pulleys, spindle housings, deck bearings for play or noise.
- fix: replace the mandrel assembly if shaft or housing damaged/worn.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Grinding/screeching usually means replacing a worn spindle or pulley; check deck bearings and mandrel assemblies for damage or excessive play.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: deep knocking, thudding, excessive vibration; blades unbalanced, bent, loose. Even a small nick can throw off the balance.
- fix: ensure each blade is tight, free of chips/warping and balanced; replace if damaged or balancing doesn't solve it.

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- fact: loose blade makes knocking/thudding; make sure each blade is tightly secured.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Squealing or knocking from the engine area may come from low oil.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: engine noise may be due to a clogged air filter (also a loose muffler or a failing starter).

## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: A riding mower tune-up can often resolve small engine noises.
