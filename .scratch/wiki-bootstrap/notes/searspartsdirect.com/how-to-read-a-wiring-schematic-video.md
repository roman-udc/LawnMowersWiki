Note: Source is written for household appliances (example: refrigerator cold control thermostat), not mowers. Content is generic schematic-reading.

## page: procedures/read-a-wiring-schematic
- fact: Second video in a Sears PartsDirect series on schematics: first explains the most common symbols; third shows how to trace a circuit on a wiring diagram. A multimeter is an essential tool for electrical troubleshooting (a separate video shows how).
- step 1: Find the schematic. Possible locations: the parts diagram page on SearsPartsDirect.com; a sticker on the top or back of the cabinet; a paper copy behind the front toe panel or in the control console; the owner's manual; if not found, may be ordered from the replacement parts diagram page.
- step 2: Lines represent wires connecting electrical components. Follow the lines from the power cord; you'll come to a control with the on/off switch (in the example, a refrigerator cold control thermostat).
- step 3: Wire color abbreviations are labeled on the lines; a color-abbreviation chart is on the schematic itself; use it to check the right wires.
- step 4: Follow wires to isolate the failure. Example: a broken black wire to a thermostat can prevent the refrigerator cooling; if wiring is okay, the thermostat may need replacing. Then buy the right part.
