## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "riding mower smells like hot metal"
- alias: "mower shuts off halfway through the yard"
- alias: "mower running hotter than it should / won't restart until it cools"
- causes-named: dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, bad-engine-cooling-fan, clogged-air-filter, mowing-too-fast, old-fuel, bad-head-gasket, worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: Engine unusually hot to the touch; unexpected shutdown mid-mow; sputtering, stalling, refusing to restart until cool; smell of burning oil or plastic (early warning).
- fact: Overheating happens when airflow is blocked, oil is low or dirty, or cooling components aren't working. High heat can warp metal parts, crack seals and wear gaskets; ignoring it shortens engine life.
- tip: Prevention: clean around the engine and cooling fins regularly; replace oil and air filters on schedule; mow at the right time of day (cooler parts in extreme heat); use the oil and fuel grade recommended for the engine; do seasonal maintenance/tune-ups.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Grass clippings, dust, debris on fins block airflow.
- fix: Engine off and cool; remove the engine cover; use a soft brush or compressed air; be gentle, bent fins don't cool well.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Oil lubricates and carries away heat; low, thick or dirty oil raises friction and temperature.
- confirm: Check oil before mowing; dark or below the fill line means change it.

## page: causes/bad-engine-cooling-fan
- fact: Many riding mower engines use a fan to draw air over the engine.
- confirm: Look for broken blades or debris buildup around the fan shroud.
- fix: Clear obstructions; replace the fan if cracked or not spinning freely.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Clogged filter restricts airflow so the engine runs rich and hot, reduces power.
- fix: Remove and inspect; replace if dusty/coated in grime, or clean if reusable.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Tall, thick grass cut too fast or mowing uphill can push the engine past its limit. If it bogs or gets hot, slow down and raise the deck height; take breaks.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old or stale fuel burns less cleanly and can make the engine run lean, increasing heat. Drain and refill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: Cracked seals or gaskets leak oil slowly (oil keeps running low though topped off) which overheats the engine. Signs: oil spots under the mower, burning smell, smoke near the engine.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: Slipping or misaligned belts create extra friction or block airflow around the engine. Cracked, frayed or glazed: replace (ground drive belt or blade drive belt).

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Hot weather and harsh conditions (thick grass, dirty filter) strain the mower; mow in cooler parts of the day, take breaks.
