## page: guides/reading-wiring-schematics
- fact: a wiring schematic (wiring diagram) is a "map" of the wiring and current flow through the components of an appliance; understanding the symbols and layout helps you test each component and buy the right part.
- fact: the lines are wires; they connect the symbols, which represent components. Following the lines helps determine the cause of a failure.
- fact: example symbols (refrigerator): light and the switch that controls it, defrost heater (connected to a thermostat and a timer), motor (e.g., evaporator fan motor), cold control adjustable thermostat, water valve. Example logic: if a light doesn't work after a bulb change, the schematic helps decide between the socket and the switch.
- fact: this video is the first of three: (1) symbols, (2) where to find the diagram on the appliance and decipher lines and symbols, (3) tracing a circuit.
- tip: a multimeter is an essential tool when troubleshooting electrical problems (Source links a how-to-use-a-multimeter video).
