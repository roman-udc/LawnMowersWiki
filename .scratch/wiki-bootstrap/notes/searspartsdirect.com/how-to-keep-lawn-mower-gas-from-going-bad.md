## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: stale gas can make the mower sputter, hesitate or not start.
- tip: use a plastic gas can, not metal; rust inside a metal can contaminates fuel, making the engine hard to start; if it starts it may sputter and die.
- fact: gas begins to get stale about 30 days after you pump it.
- tip: use a small gas can and buy less at a time; a 2-gallon can should be big enough for a push mower, a 5-gallon for a riding mower.
- tip: if the gas will be around a while, treat the can with fuel stabilizer, which keeps gas from absorbing water and going bad; follow label directions and mix the correct stabilizer-to-fuel ratio in the gas can.
- mistake: never pour fuel stabilizer directly into the mower's gas tank (Source's wording).
- tip: if you didn't use stabilizer, don't leave gas in the tank for end-of-season storage; use it up before putting the mower away.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: without stabilizer, run the tank empty before end-of-season storage.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: sputtering, hesitation or no start (problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start); contaminated fuel from rust in a metal can makes engine hard to start and may sputter and die.
