## page: procedures/read-a-wiring-schematic
- fact: Third video in a series on schematics (wiring diagrams): part one explains common symbols; part two covers where to find the diagram on an appliance and how to decipher lines and symbols; this one shows tracing a circuit.
- fact: Electric current travels in a loop called a circuit. Any break (broken wire or failed component) interrupts it and current will not flow.
- step 1: Find the circuit on the schematic and follow it from the power source through each component and wire back to the return.
- step 2: Unplug the appliance (Source's example) and test each component for continuity with a multimeter. A switch with its plunger extended should measure near 0 ohms (continuity); infinite resistance through the switch means replace it.
- step 3: If the switch is OK, check the wires in the circuit for continuity the same way; repair or replace broken wires.
- step 4: If switch and wiring are good, the remaining cause is the end component (in the example the light socket) because all other causes are eliminated.
- tools: multimeter (essential for electrical troubleshooting; Source links a multimeter video)
- tip: Accurate diagnosis helps you buy the right part. More complicated circuits use the same basic tracing method.
- fact: Worked example in Source is a refrigerator light circuit: positive prong of the power cord to the light switch via the black wire; yellow wire from switch to light socket; white wire carries current to the neutral prong. This is an appliance example, not a mower.
