## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: easiest way to find the right blade is the mower model number; measure when the model tag is missing (hand-me-down, garage sale)
- fact: wrong size can cause poor cutting, hit the deck, rattle loose, or damage the mower; blade size isn't printed on the mower
- fact: key measurements: blade length; center hole type and diameter; blade width (optional)
- tools: tape measure or ruler; flat stable surface; work gloves; the blade removed from the mower; blade removal tool or wrench for the bolt
- tip: blade must be clean and straight; bends or warping skew measurements
- step 1: unplug mower or disconnect the spark plug wire; flip the mower on its side; use blade removal tool or wrench to remove the blade bolt; wear gloves
- step 2: lay the blade flat and measure diagonally from the tip of one cutting edge to the tip of the other = full length; blade length is typically a little smaller than the deck (a 22-inch mower may use a 21-inch blade); riding mowers vary by deck size and number of blades - measure each blade individually
- mistake: don't measure straight across the center - gives a shorter incorrect length
- step 3: measure the center hole: round hole - measure diameter in inches or mm; 5-point or 6-point star - count points and match; H-pattern or bow tie - unique to some manufacturers, shape must match exactly; mismatched hole won't fit the spindle (or could fly loose)
- step 4 (optional): measure width at widest part; thickness usually standard, note if unusually slim or heavy-duty
- fact: model number location - walk-behind: top or side of deck, near the rear wheels, or on engine housing; riding: under the seat, near rear wheels, on the deck, some near engine or battery tray; letters and numbers, near brand or serial number; can also search by OEM part number
- safety: disconnect spark plug wire or unplug before removing blade; wear work gloves; tighten the blade securely on reinstall; replace any blade bent, chipped or cracked; check the rest of the mower too (deck and drive system)

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: signs to replace: visibly chipped or bent; ragged or torn grass after mowing; more vibration than usual; sharpened a few times already
- fact: most blades need replacing every 1-3 seasons depending on mowing frequency and terrain
- fact: blade types: standard (lift) blades - common on side-discharge mowers, straight or slightly curved edges, good for regular mowing and bagging; mulching blades - curved with extra cutting edges, chop clippings fine, for leaving clippings; high-lift blades - steeper angle, more suction for bagging, not great for sandy or dusty lawns
- safety: see measure-a-mower-blade safety
