## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "lawn tractor doesn't start sometimes"
- alias: "intermittent no-start"
- causes-named: bad-safety-switch
- fact: Lawn tractors typically have several interlock switches (seat switch, brake switch) that can intermittently prevent starting. Check wiring connections on the interlock switches (loose wires cause intermittent starting). Side-to-side play in the brake pedal: brake arm may not contact the brake interlock switch; this is a common cause of intermittent starting on some tractors. Tighten the brake arm to remove side-to-side play.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: riding mower shuts off when you engage the blades (problems/engine-starts-then-dies / blades): often a disconnected or defective seat switch.
- confirm: check wiring connections and seat switch condition; if OK, suspect defective brake switch or wire harness failure; then check all safety switches and wire harness connections.
- symptom: intermittent no-start from interlock switches (see above).

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "riding mower runs for a while then stops"
- alias: "shuts off when I engage the blades"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, bad-safety-switch
- fact: check if low on fuel. Blocked vent on gas cap: loosen (don't fully remove) the cap so air flows in; start engine; if it keeps running, clean or replace the gas cap. Dirty fuel filter or clogged carburetor can also kill it; rebuild the carburetor if gummed up.
- brand: some Kohler 7000 Series engines in Craftsman Zero-Turn mowers stall because air in the evaporator line between fuel tank and carburetor interrupts fuel flow through the carburetor. Service technician must connect a vent line between the evaporator line and the air filter housing.
- pro: Kohler 7000 vent line fix needs a service technician.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- see above (loosen cap test).

## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug
- fix: stale fuel: empty tank, refill with fresh gasoline; if no help check dirty air and fuel filters; gummed carburetor (filters dirty or gas old): rebuild carb if necessary; fouled spark plug: replace if damaged.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "riding mower cutting grass unevenly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, unlevel-deck, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: uneven tire air pressure is a frequent cause; check air pressure of all 4 tires and adjust; level the deck if uneven; dull blades: replace if dull or damaged.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- see above.

## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: worn or damaged mower deck drive belt (blade drive belt) prevents blade engagement; replace if damaged or broken. If belt okay, check deck components such as pulleys, cables and springs; replace defective ones.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "riding mower moving sluggishly"
- alias: "mower is slow"
- causes-named: worn-drive-belt, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: slow mower: check the ground drive belt; replace if damaged or broken. Automatic transaxle: air bubbles can form in transaxle oil when driven over rough terrain; may need to purge air from transaxle case per owner's manual.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- see above.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: automatic transaxle air bubbles from rough terrain; purge per owner's manual.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "mower deck vibrates"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: bent or damaged blade: replace. Check for loose blades and pulleys; tighten bolts and fasteners on deck. Worn or damaged deck drive belt also causes vibration: replace.

## page: problems/engine-backfires
- alias: "backfires when it shuts off"
- causes-named: (none listed as part; cause is hot-engine shutoff with excess fuel)
- fact: engine backfires if too much fuel goes into the engine when hot and you turn off the ignition; heat of engine ignites remaining fuel in cylinder.
- fix/tip: move throttle to lowest position and let engine idle 1 minute before shutting off.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: most riding mower engines cannot use E85 (85% ethanol, 15% gasoline). Many can use only unleaded gasoline with up to 10% ethanol (E10). Check owner's manual for fuel specs.
