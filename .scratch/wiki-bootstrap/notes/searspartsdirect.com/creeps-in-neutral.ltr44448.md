## page: problems/mower-creeps-in-neutral
- alias: "riding mower creeps in neutral"
- alias: "zero-turn creeps forward or backward with levers in neutral"
- causes-named: bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fact: A lawn tractor with an automatic transmission that moves with the motion control lever in neutral is dangerous. A zero-turn shouldn't move with both drive levers in neutral.
- fix: Adjust the neutral control (zero-turn) or the motion control lever's neutral position (tractor).

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fact: Mis-adjusted neutral position of the control causes creeping (adjustment, not a failed part, in this Source).

## page: procedures/adjust-the-neutral-control
- step (zero-turn) 1: Pull off the back wheels.
- step 2: Adjust the neutral control to eliminate movement.
- step 3: Check for movement in neutral after each adjustment; continue until the mower stays still in neutral. (A separate repair guide, "How to adjust the neutral control on a zero-turn riding mower", is linked, not in this Source.)
- step (lawn tractor, automatic transmission) 1: Loosen the lever adjustment bolt.
- step 2: Move the motion control lever until the tractor stays still.
- step 3: Tighten the adjustment bolt firmly.
- tip: If this doesn't match your tractor's drive system, refer to the owner's manual for adjusting neutral.
- safety: Creeping in neutral is dangerous.
