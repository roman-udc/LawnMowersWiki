## page: procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key
- fact: Flywheel key is a small rectangular metal piece keeping crankshaft and flywheel aligned when the flywheel nut is tightened. It shears if the blade hits an object hard enough to make the flywheel slip out of alignment, protecting expensive engine parts. Replace a broken/sheared key with a manufacturer-approved part.
- brand: Steps apply to common Briggs & Stratton engines used in Craftsman, Husqvarna, Toro, MTD, Murray, Cub Cadet, Troybilt and Ariens riding mowers and lawn tractors.
- difficulty: 4 (as stated); time: 60 minutes or less
- tools: wrench set, socket wrench set, flywheel puller, slot screwdriver, hammer, work gloves; also torque wrench, nylon rope (or impact wrench), mallet
- safety: Source warning: repairs/maintenance can be hazardous; use proper tools and safety gear; don't proceed unless confident; some repairs should be done by a qualified technician.
- step 1: Disconnect the battery: park on a level surface, set parking brake, ignition off and remove key, let engine cool, wear work gloves, lift seat, remove the bolt connecting the negative cable and tuck the cable away so it doesn't touch the post.
- step 2: Remove the engine blower housing: lift hood; remove air duct mounting screws and air duct; turn each air filter cover retaining knob counterclockwise, remove the cover; remove air filter; remove the air filter housing screw; remove the front and rear blower housing mounting bolts and lift off the housing. Tip: remove the hood for more room.
- step 3: Remove flywheel bolt and fan: remove flywheel screen cap; disconnect spark plug wire and remove the plug; insert nylon rope into the spark plug hole to jam the piston so the crankshaft can't turn (leave some rope out for removal); remove flywheel bolt, washer, flywheel screen; pull off the flywheel fan. Tip: use an impact wrench to avoid inserting the rope.
- step 4: Loosen the flywheel: thread the flywheel bolt into the crankshaft; position the flywheel puller above the bolt and thread the puller bolts into the pre-drilled flywheel holes; tighten one slightly, then the other, alternating to raise the flywheel evenly until it breaks free; if it doesn't, tap the top of the flywheel with a mallet.
- step 5: Remove puller and flywheel bolt; pull the flywheel off the crankshaft; remove remnants of the old key from crankshaft and flywheel.
- step 6: Push the flywheel onto the crankshaft aligning the flywheel notch with the crankshaft slot; insert the new key into the slot and push it flush with the top of the crankshaft.
- step 7: Reinstall fan and screen; washer over the crankshaft hole; thread flywheel bolt, hand tighten; torque the flywheel nut to 100 ft-lbs with a torque wrench; pull out the rope; reinstall spark plug and wire; reinstall flywheel screen cap.
- step 8: Reinstall blower housing (align the air diverter), mounting bolts, air filter housing screw, air filter, air filter cover (knobs clockwise), air duct with screws; lower hood.
- step 9: Reconnect negative cable to negative terminal; lower the seat.
- torque: flywheel nut 100 ft-lbs.

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- fact: Key shears when the blade strikes an object hard enough that the flywheel shifts relative to the crankshaft.
- fix: replace the key (procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key).
