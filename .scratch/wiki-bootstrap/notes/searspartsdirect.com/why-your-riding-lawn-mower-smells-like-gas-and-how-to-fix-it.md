## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "my riding mower smells like gas"
- alias: "gas smell lingers after parking the mower"
- fact: faint smell while mowing isn't unusual; a strong or lingering odor means leak or other problem; gas leaks waste fuel, can damage the mower, and are a serious fire risk.
- fact: most strong smells come from a fuel leak, evaporating gasoline, or storage conditions that let fumes escape.
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-gas-cap-seal, overfilled-fuel-tank
- safety: work in a well-ventilated area, keep ignition sources far away, wear gloves; a small spark can ignite fumes.
- step 1: check for visible leaks: carburetor, fuel lines, filter, tank, cap for dampness or staining; look under the mower for fresh spots; tighten loose fittings; wipe up spills right away.
- step 2: replace damaged parts: cracks, worn seals; replace cracked/clogged fuel filter or fuel line; carb leaking from worn gaskets or stuck float: rebuild, though installing a new carburetor is often quicker.
- step 3: avoid overfilling (below).
- step 4: store correctly (below).
- fact: if still smelling after fixes, deeper problem such as a faulty fuel shut-off valve.
- tip: check for leaks before the first cut of the year as seasonal prep.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: float valve sticks or gaskets wear: gas drips or seeps; small puddle under the mower or wet carburetor body; smell strongest right after running (carb near engine).

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: hoses dry out, crack, or loosen at clamps; even a small split releases fumes and fuel; check along the hose for damp spots, stains, brittleness, especially near the tank; smell strongest near the tank points to lines.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: plastic tanks crack with age or impact.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: rubber seal in the cap wears out or goes missing; lets gas escape as liquid or vapor; even without a visible leak, worn cap lets fumes out, especially on hot days or in a closed garage/shed.

## page: causes/overfilled-fuel-tank
- symptom: filling to the brim causes spillage or vapor venting as fuel expands in heat; common when filled in the morning and left in the sun.
- fix: stop filling at the bottom of the fill neck, not above it; check service manual for fuel capacity and fill level.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gasoline expands when hot; storing in direct sun or a hot shed pushes vapor past seals and out of the tank.
- tip: store in a cool, shaded area; for long storage drain the tank or run it nearly empty; use a gas can with a tight-sealing spout or cap to limit fumes in the garage/shed.
