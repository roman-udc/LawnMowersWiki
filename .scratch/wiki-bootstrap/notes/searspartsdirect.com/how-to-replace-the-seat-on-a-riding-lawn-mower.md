## page: procedures/replace-the-seat
- fact: Riding mower has a seat switch letting it run only when it senses someone on the seat. A cracked seat or missing/broken-down foam cushioning may keep the switch from engaging; then the engine shuts off when you release the brake. Fix: replace the seat with a manufacturer-approved part.
- brand: Steps apply to common Craftsman, Husqvarna, MTD, Troybilt, Toro, Murray, Snapper, Cub Cadet and Ariens riding mowers and lawn tractors.
- difficulty: 1 (scale as given: "Difficulty: 1"); time: 15 minutes or less
- tools: wrench set, slot screwdriver, work gloves
- safety: Repairs can be hazardous; use proper tools and safety gear; don't proceed unless confident; some repairs should be done by a qualified technician.
- step 1: Disconnect the battery: park on a level surface, set parking brake; ignition off, remove key; wear work gloves; lift the seat; remove the bolt connecting the negative cable to the battery and tuck the cable away so it doesn't touch the battery post.
- step 2: Remove the old seat: remove the retaining bolt and washer from the seat bottom; press the seat adjustment lever and slide the seat fully forward; release the locking tab and pull the seat off the bracket while supporting the seat adjustment lever; remove the adjustment lever; disconnect the wire harness from the seat switch; rotate the seat switch counterclockwise to release it and pull it off the seat; remove the old seat.
- step 3: Install the new seat: push the seat switch into the new seat's bottom and rotate clockwise to lock; connect the wire harness to the switch; install the adjustment lever and support it while snapping the new seat into the bracket; reinstall the retaining bolt and washer.
- step 4: Reconnect the negative cable to the negative battery terminal; lower the seat.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: engine shuts off when the brake is released because a worn/cracked seat keeps the seat switch from engaging.

## page: procedures/replace-the-seat-switch
- fact: Seat switch twists off (counterclockwise) and on (clockwise) the seat bottom; wire harness connects to it.
