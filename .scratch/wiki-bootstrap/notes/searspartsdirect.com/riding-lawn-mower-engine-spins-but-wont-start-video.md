## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "riding mower engine spins but won't start"
- fact: engine needs 3 things: fuel, spark, compression
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, disconnected-fuel-solenoid, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, sheared-flywheel-key, overfilled-oil, low-compression
- confirm (starter fluid test, first): in a well-ventilated area pull off the air filter and spray a short burst of starter fluid into the cylinder through the carburetor air intake, then try to start. Engine starts briefly then dies: spark and compression OK, so it's a fuel supply problem. Engine doesn't start at all: skip fuel troubleshooting and go to the ignition system.
- tools/supplies: air filter, fresh gasoline, fuel stabilizer, fuel filter, fuel line, fuel pump, ignition coil, spark plug, spark plug wrench, spark plug tester, flywheel key, flywheel puller, cylinder compression gauge, starter fluid
- step (fuel) 1: dirty carburetor is usually the culprit when no fuel; check basics first so you don't buy an unneeded part
- step (fuel) 2: dirty air filter: replace it
- step (fuel) 3: unused several months: drain tank and refill fresh gas
- step (fuel) 4: replace fuel filter if not changed in the last year
- step (fuel) 5: check wire harness on the fuel solenoid valve; reconnect if disconnected
- step (fuel) 6: check fuel line tank to carb for clogs/damage; clear or replace
- step (fuel) 7: if engine has fuel pump, replace it if it doesn't move gas to carb
- step (fuel) 8: if all fine, rebuild or replace carburetor
- step (ignition) 1: spark plug wire firmly connected; damaged wire means replace ignition coil (wire is part of the coil)
- step (ignition) 2: remove plug; check tip for carbon/oil deposits and insulator cracks; replace if too fouled or cracked
- step (ignition) 3: spark plug tester: install plug, connect tester boot to plug, connect engine's plug wire to other end of tester, crank and look for spark; no spark likely means replace ignition coil
- tip: Source has videos for each replacement (links only)
- pro: for low compression, have a service technician do a leak-down test (air compressor and special tools find where air leaks)

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas absorbs water and loses combustibility over time; if not used for several months drain and refill; add fuel stabilizer before storing several months if not used up

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: need clean air filter so right amount of air mixes with fuel to start

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: replace if not changed in the last year; clogged filter blocks gas flow to carb

## page: causes/disconnected-fuel-solenoid
- fact: fuel solenoid valve shuts off fuel to the carburetor to prevent backfire when you kill the engine
- symptom: if its wire is disconnected, solenoid won't open so fuel doesn't reach carb
- fix: reconnect the valve wire harness

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: clear clogs; replace if damaged

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fix: replace if it doesn't move gas through the line to the carb

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: usually the culprit when engine isn't getting fuel (after basic checks); rebuild or replace

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: carbon or oil on tip, cracked insulator
- fix: replace

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: no spark in tester; damaged spark plug wire (part of coil)
- fix: replace the coil

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- fact: spark timing controlled by flywheel magnet and ignition coil: when magnet passes coil, coil sends current to plug
- fact: flywheel key (small metal rectangle) keeps flywheel aligned on the crankshaft when flywheel bolt tightened; it shears to protect expensive engine parts if the blade hits an object hard enough that the flywheel slips
- symptom: hit a rock or stump and the engine suddenly stopped; plug sparks (coil good) but timing off
- fix: remove flywheel, check key, replace if damaged (flywheel puller listed as tool)

## page: procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key
- fact: remove the flywheel, inspect and replace the key; flywheel puller needed (Source links video)

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- confirm: check oil level for overfill if no ignition problem; drain some oil to drop below max fill

## page: causes/low-compression
- confirm: if oil level OK, pull plug and use compression gauge
- fact: most engines should read over 40 psi; many produce more than 80 psi; under 40 psi the piston isn't compressing
- fact: low reading shows a compression problem but not the cause; may need valve adjustment or new piston rings; damaged cylinder wall can also prevent compression
- pro: leak-down test by service technician

## page: procedures/check-compression
- step 1: check oil level / drain overfill first
- step 2: pull spark plug
- step 3: connect compression gauge to the cylinder through the plug hole; zero the gauge
- step 4: briefly turn key to start so starter spins engine
- step 5: read: over 40 psi expected (many over 80 psi); under 40 psi = compression problem

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- fact: pull wire off plug, remove with spark plug wrench; inspect tip and insulator; replace if fouled or cracked

## page: procedures/replace-the-ignition-coil
- fact: replace if plug wire is damaged or tester shows no spark (Source links video)

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: replace if not changed within a year (Source links video)

## page: procedures/test-the-ignition-coil
- step 1: install spark plug, connect spark plug tester boot to the plug
- step 2: connect engine's plug wire to the other end of tester
- step 3: crank engine; no spark from tester means coil likely bad
