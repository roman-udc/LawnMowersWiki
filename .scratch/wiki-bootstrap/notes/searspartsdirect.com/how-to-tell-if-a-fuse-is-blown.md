## page: causes/blown-fuse
- symptom: riding mower battery is good but nothing happens when you turn the ignition key.
- fact: checking for a blown fuse is one of the first troubleshooting steps.
- confirm: locate the fuse in the mower's wire harness; inspect whether its element is intact; if the element is burned and separated, the fuse is blown.
- fix: remove and replace; this restores power to the ignition system. Use a fuse with the same amperage rating as the original.
- pro: if it keeps blowing, follow the Sears "Troubleshooting a riding lawn mower blown fuse" video to find a short circuit or bad component.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- fact: riding mower fuse looks like either a cylindrical car fuse (silver ends, transparent middle, thin element through it) or a flat automotive fuse (metal prongs into a fuse holder, transparent body, thin element).
- step 1: locate fuse in the mower's wire harness.
- step 2: inspect the element; burned and separated = blown.
- step 3: replace with the same amperage rating.
- step 4: if it blows again, look for a short circuit or bad component.
