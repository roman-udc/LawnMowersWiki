## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- fact: video transcript by Sears PartsDirect ("Wayne"). Uneven deck gives slanted-step cut: blades shave short on the lower side and leave grass tall on the other.
- tools: wrench set, ruler, tire pressure gauge, compressed air supply, work gloves.
- safety: wear work gloves.
- step 1: park on a level surface, set parking brake; raise deck to highest position; disengage cutting blades; shut off engine and remove the key.
- step 2: inflate all tires to recommended pressures, equal left and right (uneven tire pressure can mimic an uneven deck).
- step 3 (side to side): position both cutting blades side to side and measure the height of each outside blade tip; should be within 1/8 inch.
- step 4: if not, turn the lift link adjustment nut clockwise to raise the left side of the deck or counterclockwise to lower it; each full turn changes left-side height about 3/16 inch; recheck and repeat until within 1/8 inch.
- step 5 (front to back): position a cutting blade front to back and measure each blade tip height; the front tip should be 1/8 inch to 1/2 inch lower than the rear tip.
- step 6: loosen the jam nut, turn the front link adjustment nut clockwise to raise the front of the deck or counterclockwise to lower; each full turn is 1/8 inch; recheck and repeat.
- step 7: hold the adjustment nut with one wrench and tighten the jam nut with a second wrench.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "riding mower leaving uneven patches"
- causes-named: unlevel-deck, wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: deck out of level or underinflated tires.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- symptom: uneven cut resembling slanted steps (problems/uneven-cut).
- fact: specs: side to side within 1/8 inch; front 1/8 to 1/2 inch lower than rear.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: uneven left/right pressure mimics an unlevel deck and gives uneven cut; inflate to recommended pressure first.
