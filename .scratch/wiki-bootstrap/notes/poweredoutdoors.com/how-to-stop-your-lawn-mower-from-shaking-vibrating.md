## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "feels like riding a jackhammer when I engage the blades"
- alias: "mower deck vibrates or shakes when blades are engaged"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- safety: step 1: remove the spark plug boot so the engine can't start while working under the deck.
- step 2: inspect engine mounting bolts (motor mounts) for tightness; replace missing, worn or broken. They reduce vibration. On a push mower check them if vibration begins when blades engage.
- step 3: clutch: an electric clutch has a ball bearing that can fail and cause vibration; a manual-engagement clutch with belts and pulleys has bushings in the linkages that wear. Push mowers have no electric clutch but have a belt engagement lever to check.
- step 4: blades: unbalanced blade (worn more on one side, e.g. by sand wearing unevenly or incorrect sharpening). If recently replaced, confirm correct length and center hole size. Check balance before installing with a blade balancer or by hanging on a nail.
- step 5: look at top of the blade for string, wire or other foreign material wrapped around the blade shaft (spindle shaft); remove and check for further damage.
- step 6: spindle housing bearings (called quill assemblies on smaller units): with work gloves, grab both ends and rock up and down; any movement means replace bearing (or the whole cutter housing assembly if available).
- step 7: belts and pulleys: remove deck covers if fitted; clean out; check bearing and bushing on the engagement linkage.
- step 8: debris lodged in mower: author found a wood chip wedged in a pulley; every time the belt passed over it the deck rattled. Little rocks, wood chips, tree debris.
- safety: dry grass packed on top of the deck in the belt housing area can build heat and cause a fire; keep it clean.
- tip: check every option no matter how odd.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- fact: engine mounting bolts are designed to reduce vibration; check tight, replace missing/worn/broken.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- symptom: electric clutch ball bearing failing causes vibration (problems/mower-is-vibrating).

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: unbalanced blade vibrates; worn unevenly (sand, incorrect sharpening) or wrong length/center hole after replacement.
- confirm: blade balancer or hang on a nail.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- confirm/fix: look at the top of the blade for string, wire, etc. on the blade shaft; remove and check for damage.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: gloved, grab both ends of the spindle housing and rock; movement = bad bearing.
- fix: replace bearing or whole housing.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- step 1: remove belt from the pulley: put something narrow between belt and groove of the bad pulley, turn belt by hand, let the object follow belt to the pulley and pry belt off.
- step 2: remove the pulley: socket on the nut on top, normally standard thread (counterclockwise); depending on mower there may be allen or hex set screws holding the pulley to the shaft; a puller may be needed.
- step 3: remove the spindle: 4 to 8 bolts hold it; remove and the assembly falls out the bottom of the deck.
- step 4: set housing on a flat hard surface; sometimes a snap ring holds it together, other times replace the entire housing incl. bearings. Replace bearing if assembly allows; grease the bearing.
- step 5: reassemble in reverse: bolt housing to deck, install pulley, make sure tight with no movement, reinstall belt, turn by hand to check it's on all pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: check belts, pulleys, and the bearing and bushing on the engagement linkage.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: wood chip wedged in a pulley rattled the whole deck.
