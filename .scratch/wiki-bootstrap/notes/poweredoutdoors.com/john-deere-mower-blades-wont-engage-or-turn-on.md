## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing (belt off pulleys), bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug wire; wait for all parts to stop moving.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: worn belt has cracks, wear, fraying, or shiny glazed look; may sit deep in pulley grooves.
- fix: replace; do so as soon as wear shows. Belt grip affects blade speed; fast blade speed is needed for suction to lift grass.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- symptom: belt off the pulleys: tensioner arm and spring hold the idler pulleys in line; hole in the bracket where the spring attaches can wear, or the spring wears; belt loosens and vibrates off.
- fact: other belt-off causes: stretched belt, bad pulley, worn tensioner arm, missing spring.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: bearing wear lets pulley wobble, not parallel to deck, one side higher; belt can roll off.
- confirm: rotate pulley slowly by hand, feel for restriction or listen for bearing noise.
- fix: replace the pulley assembly; some idler pulleys allow replacing the bearing.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch.
- confirm: check continuity; replace with a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to the blades by engaging the drive belt; replace if worn/faulty.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: manual engagement clutch has lever and cable; check lever, cable, spring, bushings, linkages; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: electric clutch uses battery power; weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; charge if less.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for skin (shock) and eyes. Do not touch anything not rubber-coated.
- step 1: access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover; under hood or under seat). Do not remove battery from its casing.
- step 2: connect positive cable first (red/plus) to positive terminal.
- step 3: attach negative cable (black/minus) to negative terminal.
- step 4: set charger voltage and amperage; mower batteries usually 12 volts; more amps charge faster; start with 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps; a slow charge is best.
- fix: if it won't hold a charge, replace; 12 V batteries at hardware/auto/dealer; bring the old battery, core fee averages $20 otherwise.
- fact: article links "5 things draining your mower battery".

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: seat switch engaged when operator in seat; when not engaged, blades won't turn on (article text says "turn off", context clear: they won't run).
- symptom: author's anecdote: on bumps bouncing off the seat disengaged the switch and shut the deck off.
- confirm: multimeter, or temporary bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: never operate without the safety switch installed.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fix: replace with same amperage; if it keeps blowing take to a John Deere dealer or repair shop to find the root cause.
