## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Honda push mower vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, dull-or-bent-blade, worn-blade-sail, bent-crankshaft, sheared-flywheel-key
- fact: some vibration is normal when the engine runs; becomes worse as parts wear. Causes: worn engine mounting bolts, loose engagement lever, broken blade adapter, bent/unbalanced blade, bent crankshaft, sheared flywheel key, missing/loose hardware.
- safety: remove spark plug to prevent starting; protect hands from sharp blade edges; follow owner's manual.
- fact: loose hardware/worn parts can make parts vibrate; check them.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- symptom: vibration when you engage the blade.
- fact: mounting bolts (motor mounts) under the engine absorb engine vibration.
- fix: tighten loose bolts; replace broken or missing ones.

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- fact: Honda push mower uses a lever to engage the blade; check the engagement lever for loose/worn components; tighten and replace worn parts.
- fact: blade adapter breaks from significant impact with a stationary object or weakening from many small impacts; also wears from crankshaft damage, wrong blade, or unbalanced blade.
- fix: replace a broken adapter together with a new Honda blade; inspect crankshaft for damage before installing.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: bent blade from hitting a solid object like a tree root or rock.
- confirm: park on a flat surface, measure ground to blade tip, rotate blade 180 degrees, measure same spot again; more than 1/8 inch variance -> replace blade.
- confirm: or remove blade and match to a new blade; gaps remaining mean it's bent -> replace.
- safety: never try to straighten a blade; metal is compromised and may crack and fly out at very high speed.
- fact: unbalanced blade wobbles at high speed causing significant vibration.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: dirt under the deck wears the blade unevenly; when the ends or the blade sail (high side) are thinner than the center, replace the blade.
- fact: blade can also become unbalanced during sharpening if more metal is removed from one side.
- confirm: use a blade balancer; or put a nail in the wall with the head about 1 inch from the wall, hang the blade by its center hole; a balanced blade sits level. If not, remove a little metal from the lower-hanging side until it sits level.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- step 1: mower on a flat surface; measure ground to blade tip.
- step 2: rotate blade 180 degrees; measure same ground spot to the tip.
- step 3: more than 1/8" difference -> replace blade.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: blade balance check with blade balancer or nail on wall (nail head ~1 inch off the wall); remove metal from the low side to level. Never straighten a bent blade.

## page: causes/bent-crankshaft
- confirm: with plug removed and a clamp holding the safety lever to the handle (disables flywheel brake), carefully spin the blade and watch the center bolt; if it moves all over, crankshaft likely bent.
- confirm: or remove blade and adapter, turn crankshaft and check with a dial indicator or stationary object that the shaft stays centered.
- fix: bent crankshaft -> replace the mower; crankshaft replacement often costs more than a new mower.
- safety: bending the crankshaft back is a significant safety risk; author highly recommends buying a new mower.

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- fix: remove the nut from the top of the crankshaft to inspect the flywheel; a small key sits in the keyway; replace if damaged.

## page: procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key
- step 1: remove the nut from top of crankshaft; inspect flywheel and the small key in the keyway.
- step 2: replace a damaged key.
