## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Hustler mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, incorrect-starting-procedure, blown-fuse, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: won't start when the engine doesn't get air, fuel and spark. Hustler uses engines from Kawasaki, Kohler and Briggs & Stratton.
- safety: before repairs remove the ignition key, set the parking brake, remove the spark plug wire.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: add fresh fuel; check for fuel leaks and repair.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Hustler needs minimum 87-octane and max 10% ethanol (Regular or E10). Gas can start breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol attracts moisture; water/ethanol forms varnish and gummy deposits that clog the fuel system.
- fix: drain old fuel with a fuel siphon pump into an approved container; in another container mix fresh gasoline with stabilizer/cleaner like Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: avoid gas with higher than 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: without a filter dirt wears the engine; dusty mowing conditions load the filter; lack of air prevents starting and running.
- tip: check before each use (a minute or two); start each season with a new filter at full maintenance.
- fix: clean if slightly dirty; replace if heavily dirty or damaged.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove the paper filter from the housing.
- step 2: wipe remaining dirt out of the housing; don't let dirt fall in the intake.
- step 3: tap the filter on a solid surface. Do not use compressed air: it can damage the paper element.
- step 4: hold to a light; reuse if light shines through; replace if not or if damaged.
- step 5: install and attach the cover.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: remove plug(s); look for dirt and carbon buildup, burnt electrode, cracked porcelain, incorrect electrode gap.
- fix: slightly dirty but good - clean with wire brush; gap to engine manufacturer spec; replace if dark or damaged. Reconnect plug wire securely (but not until other repairs are finished).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: screens gas leaving the tank. Good practice: replace annually; more often with dirty fuel or commercial use.
- fix: replace; on an inline filter the arrow must point in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pumps gas from tank to carburetor using crankcase vacuum; replace when it can't draw fuel. Visible damage: small crack in housing or fuel leaking at seams.
- confirm: verify fuel reaches the pump by checking fuel out of the line connected to the inlet port. Then test pump output: use the shut-off valve, fuel clamps or hose pinch-off pliers to stop the flow; with flow stopped remove the fuel line from the carburetor and put it in a container; restore flow and start the mower; watch for steady or pulsating flow. Insufficient flow = replace pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: old fuel deposits stick to lines.
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove the hose end from the section of fuel line furthest from the tank, put it in a container placed lower than the tank (fuel cannot run uphill without a pump), restore flow; poor flow = blockage.
- fix: remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner to loosen the clog, blow through with compressed air; replace if it can't be cleared or the line is cracking.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: varnish from old fuel can plug the fuel jet or make small parts stick; carb regulates fuel mixed with air.
- fix: if mechanical, take apart and clean with carburetor cleaner including float bowl and needle; replace if too dirty to clean.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter, about 12.7 volts; less - charge. Replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: cables loosen from the bouncing and vibration of the mower.
- fix: clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) and a wire brush; make sure terminals are attached securely.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: switches include seat switch, brake switch and clutch switch.
- confirm: test with a multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify.
- safety: never operate without the safety switch or with it bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: multimeter; replace a bad switch. Symptom: key turned, mower won't start or even turn over.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; wire on solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts.
- fix: test per the solenoid article (see procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid); replace if bad.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Hustler needs the parking brake set and steering levers in the open/outward position (on some models the outward position also engages the brake); clutch (blade) must not be engaged. Choke lever must be on to start a cold engine; once warm move it off, otherwise the engine runs sluggish and dies.
- fix: follow the model's operator's manual starting procedure.
- brand: Hustler zero-turn steering levers outward.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke must be on for a cold engine and off once warm.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Hustler fuses typically sit under the seat platform. Replace with the same amperage; repeated blowing means a shop/dealer must find the root cause.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap vent equalises tank pressure; plugged vent forms a vacuum that keeps fuel in the tank.
- confirm: remove the cap and start; if it starts with the cap off, reinstall the cap and let it run; if it eventually runs sluggish and dies, and restarts once the cap is removed again, the cap is bad.
- fix: replace the cap.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- fact: same amperage; see blown-fuse.
