## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carburetor stops working correctly because it gums up, clogging the fuel jet or sticking the float. Old fuel is the main cause.
- symptom: carb can no longer regulate fuel-to-air mixture. Mower won't start (problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start); engine backfires from running lean (problems/engine-backfires); sputters and runs rough (problems/engine-runs-rough); engine surges; consumes too much fuel.
- confirm: with a good fuel supply to the carb confirmed, remove the air filter from housing, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and start the mower. If it starts, runs, then dies, disassemble and clean the carburetor.
- fix: clean carburetor (procedures/clean-the-carburetor); if that fails, rebuild or replace.
- tip: run the right gas and only fresh gas to minimize effects on the carb.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers
- step 1: gather tools and supplies.
- step 2: take multiple photos of the carb before and during teardown (many small parts) to reference at reassembly.
- step 3: shut off the fuel supply: use the fuel shut-off valve on the bottom of the fuel tank; if no valve, crimp the fuel line.
- step 4: detach the throttle and choke cables from the carburetor.
- step 5: remove the hardware attaching the carburetor to the air filter housing so it is detached.
- step 6: remove the carburetor: slowly remove springs, do not stretch them (stretched springs must be replaced); may need to twist the carb to get springs off. Do not rip the gasket between engine block and carb or it must be replaced.
- step 7: remove the carburetor bowl (on the bottom; holds small amount of fuel): have a rag for remaining fuel; remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and lower the bowl off. A gasket around the bowl looks like a rubber band.
- step 8: check the stem hanging down in the center of the carb for clogged holes (gummed by old fuel); with a flashlight, clear holes with a thick wire.
- step 9: check other components and the carb body for gumming and white crusty buildup; remove gummy deposits and as much crusty material as possible with carburetor cleaner. Almost impossible to remove all the crusty material.
- step 10: ensure all components are in good working order including float needle and float; reassemble in reverse order using the photos.
- step 11: add fresh fuel (right type) and allow the fuel bowl to fill.
- mistake: getting carburetor cleaner or any other substance on the bowl gasket ruins it; if it happens, replace the gasket before reattaching the bowl.
- brand: Craftsman uses other manufacturers' small engines in its mowers; have engine model and spec on hand to order the correct parts.
- tip: after the job, run fresh gas, don't let it sit long, and add a stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (author uses it in his own mower).

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: when cleaning fails, small components may need replacing or old fuel may have damaged the carb so much it needs replacement.
- fact: order a replacement carb or rebuild kit online, at a local Craftsman dealership, or at an authorized engine dealership; need engine model and spec.

## page: procedures/replace-the-carburetor
- fact: same pointer as rebuild above: replace when cleaning unsuccessful; need engine model/spec; Craftsman uses other makers' engines.

## page: problems/engine-runs-rough

- symptom: sputters and runs rough, surges, consumes too much fuel with a failing carburetor.

## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor
- symptom: won't start with failing carburetor.

## page: problems/engine-backfires
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor
- symptom: backfires from running lean with a bad carburetor.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: use a stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment in the fuel; don't let fuel sit in the mower for long periods; fresh gas minimizes carburetor issues.
