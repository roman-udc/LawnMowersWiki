## page: guides/mower-got-wet
- fact: Most likely no significant damage; riding mowers are built to withstand light rain; after a heavy rain, moisture must be removed from the mower and electrical components.
- fact: A mower left in rain may not start because of water in the engine oil or fuel tank, or water in electrical components (ignition switch, spark plug wires, safety switches).
- step 1: Dry out the mower, especially around electrical components, wiring, cables, switches. Author uses a leaf blower, then leaves it in the sun to evaporate remaining moisture.
- step 2: Check oil for water (milky appearance; use a flashlight); if milky, do a routine oil change.
- step 3: Check the air filter; if water was in the oil it probably entered the crankcase via the air filter. A wet filter is compromised; author recommends replacing it (paper can be air-dried).
- step 4: Check the fuel tank for water (hard rain can enter via the cap vent); water is not combustible and causes rough running or no start. Drain with a siphon hand pump into a container for recycling; replace the fuel filter; refill with fresh fuel plus an additive like STA-BIL or Sea Foam to reduce moisture (gas shelf life about 30 days).
- step 5: Check the carburetor: if water was in the tank and you ran the mower, probably water in the carburetor. Remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl, remove the bowl, catch the gas/water mix in a rag, replace the empty bowl.
- step 6: Spray ignition switch with WD-40 to displace water on connections; then spark plug wires, plug boots and switches.
- step 7: Spray cables with WD-40 to displace moisture so the conduit doesn't rust; also all wires that got wet.
- tip: Best storage is indoors out of the weather (garage or shed); never store in your home, basement or next to ignitable items. If outdoors, use a marine-grade cover.

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- fact: Ignition switch, spark plug wires, boots and safety switches collect water and give bad connections; displace with WD-40.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- fact: Water gets in via the cap vent; not combustible; rough running or no start. Drain, replace filter, refill with fresh fuel and additive; check carburetor bowl.

## page: causes/oil-in-air-filter-or-tipped-mower
- fact: (water in oil route) milky oil; water likely entered through the air filter; replace the filter.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: If oil is milky do a routine oil change (steps in the author's riding mower maintenance guide, not in this article).

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Wet paper filter is compromised; replace.
