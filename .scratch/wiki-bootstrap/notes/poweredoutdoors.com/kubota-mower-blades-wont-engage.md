## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Kubota mower blades won't engage or turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- safety: never reach under the deck until following the operator's manual precautions: remove ignition key, remove spark plug boots, wait for all parts to stop moving.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: deck belt (blade belt) turns blades via pulleys; worn belt slips.
- confirm: worn belt has cracks, wear, fraying or shiny glazed look, or sits deep in the pulley grooves.
- fix: replace a worn belt even if not the main cause; belt grip affects blade speed, and fast blade speed creates the suction that lifts grass for an even cut.
- fact: belt off pulleys: look for stretched belt, bad pulley, worn tensioner arm or missing spring.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: a tensioner arm and spring hold idler pulleys in line (pulley one side of bracket, spring the other); the bracket hole where the spring attaches or the spring can wear, loosening the belt so it vibrates off the pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: bearing wears so pulley no longer sits parallel to the deck and wobbles; one side may sit higher; extra movement can roll the belt off.
- fix: replace pulley with bad bearing.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch is a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch; failed switch = blades won't turn.
- confirm: check continuity; replace if broken.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace if worn/faulty.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: Kubota with manual engagement clutch has a lever and cable; check lever, cable, spring, bushings and linkages for wear; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: electric clutch draws on the battery; weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; charge if lower.
- fix: replace if it won't hold a charge; take old battery to avoid core fee (average about $20); batteries at hardware, automotive store or mower dealer.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for skin (shock) and eyes.
- step 1: access the battery under the hood or under the seat (may need a screwdriver to uncover); do not remove it from its casing.
- step 2: connect charger positive (red, plus) first to positive terminal.
- step 3: connect negative (black) to negative terminal.
- step 4: don't touch anything without rubber coating.
- step 5: set charger to 12 V (usual for mower batteries); amperage: start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge is best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Kubota seat switch under the seat senses the operator; blades won't engage without the operator in seat; failed seat switch blocks blades.
- confirm: test with a multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: fuse protects against surges/shorts; blown fuse = no battery power to clutch.
- fix: replace with same size fuse; if it keeps blowing, take to a Kubota service dealer or repair shop for root cause.
