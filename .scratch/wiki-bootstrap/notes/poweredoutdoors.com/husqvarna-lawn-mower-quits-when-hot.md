## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- alias: "Husqvarna quits when hot"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys (plugged deck + dull blades)
- fact: Engine gets extremely hot and stops, possibly with smoke. Don't keep running it. Some items cause overheating and shutdown; others cause stopping after running a while, easily confused with hot shutdown.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Plugged filter greatly reduces air; engine gets hot and quits.
- fact: Change the filter at least once a year; clean several times a season; more often in dusty conditions.
- fix: See procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove the filter from the housing without knocking dirt into the intake; wipe remaining dirt out of the housing with a dry clean cloth.
- step: Paper - tap against a solid surface to knock dirt out; do not use compressed air (damages it). Hold to a light; reuse if light shows through, replace if not. Reinstall filter and cover.
- step: Foam - replace if dry, brittle, torn or brown-spotted. Otherwise wash with mild dish soap and water to remove dirt and oil; rinse until clear and soap-free; lay flat to air dry (sun speeds it); coat with filter oil and squeeze out excess (must not drip); reinstall with cover.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old gas separates into ethanol/moisture and gummy deposits that clog the fuel system and cause quitting after running a while; the separated water/ethanol mix runs through the engine, causing it to run hot and quit.
- fix: Siphon the tank into an approved container and recycle the old fuel. Refill with fresh gasoline with ethanol no more than 10%. Add a cleaning/stabilizing additive; author recommends Sea Foam Motor Treatment (petroleum-based, contains cleaning agents). If still clogged, see the related article on Husqvarna not getting fuel.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Gummed jet can't supply fuel; engine quits after running a while.
- confirm: Remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake, start; if it runs a bit and shuts down, disassemble and clean.
- fix: Clean (if a little mechanical), replace, or have a repair shop clean it.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Best oil is air-cooled engine oil with a high concentration of zinc (zinc described as a cooling agent). Small engines are air cooled, unlike cars.
- fact: Most small-engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; hotter ambient temperatures may need a different viscosity such as 20W-50. Source refers to a Kawasaki Motors oil viscosity chart plus the operator's manual.
- symptom: Wrong oil can overheat the engine and shut it down. Too little oil: friction builds extreme heat and the engine shuts down; low oil may mean an oil leak or internal problem burning oil.
- fix: Wrong oil: drain and refill with appropriate air-cooled oil. Low oil: add oil and try to start, but most likely extensive engine damage is already done.
- pro: Have an experienced small engine mechanic test the engine; running low on oil can mean engine replacement.
- tip: Check oil level before every use.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Overfull crankcase: crankshaft and rod can't move freely through excess oil; crankcase pressure rises; internal parts loaded, engine gets hot and quits. Excess oil also smokes, which clogs the air filter, and the mower shuts off from lack of air.
- fix: Disconnect the spark plug wire(s) first. Drain a little oil via the drain plug, oil filter or oil fill hole, or pull oil out of the fill with an oil evacuator or turkey baster. Recheck level on the dipstick and adjust. Check the air filter for smoke clogging. Reattach plug wire(s).
- pro: If problems continue, have an engine mechanic check it.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: Disconnect spark plug wire(s).
- step 2: Drain a little oil through the drain plug, oil filter or fill hole, or use an oil evacuator or turkey baster at the fill.
- step 3: Check the dipstick level; drain or add as needed to the recommended level.
- step 4: Check the air filter for smoke clogging; reattach plug wire(s).

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins help push air to cool engine block and cylinder; clean annually.
- fix: Remove debris around fins, replace broken fins, remove dirt around the engine shroud, make sure the heat shield is firmly in place.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Carbon on the tip may spark enough to start but not keep running.
- fix: Check and clean the tip; replace if extremely dark or damaged; check wires are securely attached.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Coil stops working when hot; windings can separate and short; can't provide enough voltage to the plug.
- confirm: Ohmmeter continuity test; break in continuity = replace.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Vented cap plugged creates vacuum in the tank; mower dies after running.
- confirm: Run with and without the cap; if it shuts off only with the cap on, suspect the cap.
- fix: Try cleaning; author replaces with a new cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: A deck packed with grass and dirt strains the engine so it overheats and shuts down; dull blades make it worse.
- fix: Scrape the deck periodically and sharpen blades; avoid mowing in wet conditions.
