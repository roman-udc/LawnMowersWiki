## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Exmark belt keeps coming off"
- alias: "Exmark belt keeps breaking"
- alias: "belt shredded on the edges"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: belt comes off or breaks when worn/damaged; bad bearing in pulley or spindle housing; deck bracket failed; debris interfering.
- fact: belts don't always break because they are old; new belts often snap because foreign material is caught in the pulleys.
- fact: belts can cost $60 to $250 (drive belts and deck belts most common). Author recommends OEM belt; cheaper online/auto-parts-matched belts rarely last as long or perform as well.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt doesn't sit tight around pulleys and comes off.
- confirm: shiny glazed appearance, belt sitting deep in pulley grooves, or stretched belt = worn. Fix: replace.
- symptom: shredded belt edges, normally from rubbing a bracket. Confirm: find the bracket with a smooth shiny spot from rubbing. Fix: adjust bracket into place so it no longer rubs; check bushings in Exmark brackets and replace if bad; some brackets allow bushing replacement, others need whole bracket assembly replaced.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: pulleys must sit flat and parallel to the deck; belt more likely to come off when they don't. Turn each pulley by hand; resistance or bearing noise means replace pulley.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: excessive vibration causing the belt to come off.
- confirm: grab each side of blade and rock up and down; movement or knocking indicates bearing problem.
- fix: remove cutter housings; Exmark uses a spindle housing with a sealed bearing, so replace the whole assembly (procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly).

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: small stones, wood chips, acorns, mud caked in pulley grooves; belt breaks from stress stretching around the debris.
- fact: debris on top of deck can inhibit the tension pulley from moving, so belt loses proper tension and comes off.
- tip: after each mowing remove belt covers and clean debris from that area; a clean deck also helps spot problem parts.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Exmark idler bracket holds idler pulleys in line, spring on one side and pulley on other. Spring can stretch/wear or the bracket's spring hole can wear, causing constant vibration.
- fact: bushing in idler bracket can wear if not greased; pulley then runs at an angle; vibration plus angled pulley makes belt come off.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: rust from rusting pulleys dries out the belt and cracks it prematurely; clean rust off pulleys or replace if excessively rusty.
- fact: oil leak dripping on deck/belt long-term makes belt swell, and it can't grab pulleys or slips off.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- fact: Exmark sealed-bearing spindle housing is replaced as a whole assembly.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: belt cost range $60-$250; OEM recommended.
