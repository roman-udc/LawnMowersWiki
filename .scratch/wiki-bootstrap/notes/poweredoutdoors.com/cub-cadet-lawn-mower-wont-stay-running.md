## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: engine won't stay running (problems/engine-starts-then-dies).
- fact: Gas sitting in mower or container begins to break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase.
- fact: Ethanol gasoline attracts moisture from air, causing corrosion in fuel system and gummy deposits that restrict fuel flow; water/ethanol mixture separates and sinks to tank bottom; it runs hotter through the engine, which can cause damage.
- fact: Old fuel, dirt, debris cause plugged fuel filter, clogged fuel lines, bad fuel pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor regulates fuel mixed with air for combustion; old fuel makes it dirty; fuel jet may clog or small components cannot move freely, so insufficient fuel reaches engine.
- confirm step 1: confirm fuel reaches carburetor (fuel pump moving fuel; else check filter, lines, pump).
- confirm step 2: remove air filter from housing without letting dirt fall into the intake.
- confirm step 3: spray carburetor cleaner into air intake and let the mower run; if it runs strong then turns sluggish and may shut off, carburetor is probably dirty.
- fix: disassemble and clean carburetor; if problems continue, rebuild or replace.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: engine starved of air, runs sluggishly, may stop; dirty filter can also cause significant engine damage (problems/engine-starts-then-dies).
- fix: replace air filter annually; clean several times through the season.
- step (paper): remove filter; wipe dirt from housing (none into intake); tap filter on a solid surface to knock out dirt; hold up to a light - if light shines through the paper reuse, otherwise buy new; reinstall with cover.
- step (foam): remove; wipe housing; inspect - dark spots, tears, or dry/brittle = replace; else wash in water and mild detergent, rinse; lay flat to dry (sun speeds it); when completely dry coat with foam air filter oil (fully covered, not dripping; wring out or blot excess with paper towel); reinstall.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- (see steps under causes/clogged-air-filter above; Cub Cadet paper and foam filters). Replace annually, clean several times per season.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke helps start a cold engine: closed position restricts airflow and lets more gas into the cylinder. Once warm, move to open so more air flows for correct gas/air mixture.
- symptom: choke not adjusted correctly = engine runs sluggish and won't stay running because airflow too low.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: plug coated with carbon, dirt and oil fails to spark.
- confirm: remove and check; very dark, broken porcelain or burnt electrode = replace. Lightly dirty with good condition: clean with wire brush to remove carbon and dirt.
- fact: Check gap against manufacturer's spec in owner's manual; plug not gapped correctly or not securely attached also causes running problems.
- fix: if still issues after good/new plug, check ignition coil.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: coil windings can separate and short out when the mower gets hot; spark plug can't get voltage so no spark (problems/engine-starts-then-dies).
- confirm: check for a break in continuity with an ohm meter.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Gas cap has a vent to equalize air pressure so tank doesn't form a vacuum; vacuum prevents gas flowing through fuel lines and shuts engine down.
- confirm: once mower stops, remove fuel cap and start; if it starts and runs fine suspect cap vent. For further confirmation, put cap back on while mower runs and see whether it shuts off after a while.
- fix: replace the fuel cap.
