## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Walker mower won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "zero turn very weak while running"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: don't assume the hydrostatic transmission first.
- fact: intro text says "drive release levers are in the operating position" but body says they must be in the operating position to move (the intro appears to be a wording slip; the body is authoritative: the bypass position is what stops movement).
- safety: wait for the transmission to cool; careful with hot hydraulic fluid; remove ignition key and spark plug wires before repairs.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: to push the mower you release the hydros by placing the transmission bypass levers/arms in "bypass"; a lever or arm on each hydro; adjust lever or pull out the arm to free-roll (careful on non-level ground, mower may roll).
- symptom: Walker zero-turn won't move when hydros are not engaged to power the wheels.
- fix: put levers in the operating position so drive levers move the mower.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: pump drive belt worn, loose, or broken (fallen off) affects movement. fix: check it is positioned correctly and securely around pulleys; replace if loose or worn.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: tensioner pulley bearing or the pulley itself can break; most are hard plastic and wear or break. fix: replace worn pulley or bad bearing; keep the tensioner arm greased so it moves freely and doesn't seize.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: idler spring tensions the pump drive belt; replace if broken or fallen out.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: old or low fluid can make it not move or seem very weak.
- tip: check level before use while oil is cool; wipe around caps before removing; dirt is often a factor in failed hydraulic systems, avoid contamination.
- fix: add fluid to the full level on the dipsticks or overflow tanks (model dependent); change fluid at regular intervals.
- brand/part: Walker ZTO: Hydro Gear ZT3400, 5.2 qt, SAE 20W-50.
- brand/part: Walker ZXL: Hydro Gear ZT4400, 8 qt, synthetic 15W-50.
- brand/part: Walker ZXT: Hydro Gear 16 cc pumps and 18 cu in wheel motors, 8 qt, synthetic 15W-50.
- tip: when oil is low, also check for hydraulic leaks; contact a Walker dealership for leaks.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: capacities and oils as in the table above; change at regular intervals.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: bad or low fluid can't lubricate efficiently, causing increased friction and overheating.
- pro: Walker dealership if the hydraulic pump leaks or the mower runs fine cold but stops when hot (could be more extensive damage).

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- symptom: moves slowly or not at all; noisy when it moves; oil gets hot and expands more than normal.
- fact: bleed air after changing the fluid or any time the transmission is opened.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- fact: for most Walker zero-turns; consult operator's manual for more.
- safety: make sure the transmission has sufficient oil before starting.
- step 1: securely support the rear to raise drive tires off the ground (if unable, do it in an open flat area free of obstructions).
- step 2: open the bypass valves, release the parking brake, start the engine.
- step 3: move the drive levers slowly forward then reverse about 6 times.
- step 4: close the bypass valves; throttle at high; move drive levers forward and reverse about 6 times.
- step 5: repeat until no excessive noise and tires move at normal speeds.
- step 6: shut off the engine, engage parking brake, remove the jacks/support and lower the tires.
- step 7: check the expansion tanks; oil must be at the FULL COLD line; add hydraulic oil to that line if not.
