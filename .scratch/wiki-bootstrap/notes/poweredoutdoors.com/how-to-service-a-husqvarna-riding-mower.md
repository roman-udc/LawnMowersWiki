## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Full service once a year; use the list with the operator's manual.
- tools: socket or spanner wrenches, filter wrench, deck scraper and/or wire brush, plastic funnel, oil drain pan
- parts: engine oil, oil filter, spark plugs, air filter, fuel filter, grease, spray lubricant, rubber gloves and heavy work gloves, rags/paper towel, mower blades (if not sharpening and reusing), fuel stabilizer, cardboard/newspaper for the work surface
- step 1: Park on a flat level surface; run a few minutes to warm the oil (warm oil flows better); put cardboard/newspaper under the mower.
- safety: Set parking brake; shut off and remove ignition key; disconnect spark plug wires; beware hot engine, hot transmission and sharp blades; read the owner's manual. Do not reattach spark plug wires until service is complete.
- step 2: Change engine oil (see procedure).
- step 3: Replace the spark plug(s).
- step 4: Clean debris from the cooling fins and engine; remove the engine cover.
- step 5: Clean debris and dirt from the hydro fans and transmission area (fans keep the transmission cool; transmission may be hot).
- step 6: Clean battery terminals.
- step 7: Remove debris around electrical components and wiring (moisture on debris corrodes components and fails them early).
- step 8: Replace the air filter.
- step 9: Replace the fuel filter.
- step 10: Check the fuel system for leaks along the line from tank to carburetor; hose connections tight.
- step 11: Check tire pressures with a gauge; secure lug nuts; pressure per the psi on the tire sidewall (low tire causes steering problems and an unlevel deck).
- step 12: Clean top of deck; check pulleys and belts; scrape deck underside; check blades and spindles; check deck level.
- step 13: Check the safety system (operator presence control: deck will not run without sensing the operator in the seat); never operate with safety elements not functioning.
- step 14: Check hardware; replace worn or missing parts.
- step 15: Grease zerks on front wheels, front axle and deck wheels (high-performance grease) plus any others; spray lubricant into lift linkages, pivot points and brake.
- step 16: Wash with garden hose and dry completely.
- tip: Oil change interval for Husqvarna: every 50 hours of use; a new engine gets an initial change after the first 5 hours.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- step 1: Place the drain pan under the drain plug or drain hose (depends on model; found under the hood at the side of the engine near the bottom). Wipe around the fill area and plug so debris does not enter the crankcase.
- step 2: Remove the drain plug and drain; remove the dipstick to speed flow.
- step 3: Reinstall the drain plug once drained.
- step 4: Remove the oil filter with a filter wrench; catch the oil that comes out.
- step 5: Put a light film of fresh oil on the seal of the new filter and install it.
- step 6: Add oil until the manufacturer's level; verify with the dipstick at the full level.
- step 7: Wipe off spilled oil; put used oil in a container for recycling (author reuses oil bottles marked "For Recycling"; some auto supply stores accept used oil).
- mistake: Running with a lower or higher oil level can cause engine damage and running problems.
- fact: Interval 50 hours; first change on a new engine at 5 hours.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- step 1: Remove plug(s) with a 3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on engine model.
- step 2: Replace annually during service; gap to engine manufacturer's recommendation.
- tip: If not replaced, inspect and clean; replace for broken porcelain or burnt electrode.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- step 1: Remove the engine cover; clean debris around the engine and cover.
- step 2: Remove debris collecting in the cooling fins.
- fact: Debris blocks airflow around engine block and cylinder.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove the filter from housing; wipe remaining dirt with a dry cloth; keep dirt out of the intake.
- step 2: Install a new filter and cover.
- fact: Replace at least once a year, more often in dry dusty conditions; check and clean regularly during the season.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: Shut off fuel with the shut-off valve or crimp the fuel line.
- step 2: Remove the filter and install the new one with the housing arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow. Replace annually.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- tip: Clean corrosion from battery terminals with a solution of 2 cups water and 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda and a wire brush; make sure terminals make good connections.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- step 1: Side to side: park on flat level surface with deck in its highest position; measure from the blade edge to the ground on the left and right; adjust until equal.
- step 2: Adjust with a wrench on the lift link adjustment nut on the side of the mower: turn left to lower, right to raise.
- step 3: Pitch front to rear: rotate the blade so one end faces front and the other rear; measure blade tip to ground at front and rear.
- step 4: Front of the deck should sit 1/8" to 1/2" lower than the rear.
- step 5: Loosen (do not remove) the jam nut on the front link adjustment; turn the adjustment nut left to lower the front, right to raise; hold the adjustment nut while tightening the jam nut.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Check spindle housings by rocking each blade end up and down with heavy work gloves; extra play or knocking means disassemble the housing; most likely the bearing failed and needs replacing.
- fact: Replace blades that are bent, have large gouges, or are extremely worn (ends rounding, or sail thinner than the center). Sharpen otherwise to put an edge on and smooth small nicks.
- tip: Scrape the deck underside with a deck scraper or wire brush; a plugged deck gives uneven cut and puts extra load on the engine.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: Signs of wear: cracking, shiny glazed appearance, shredding, stretched and no longer tight to pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: Pulley must sit flat and parallel to the deck; vertical movement means replace. Spin each pulley by hand; restriction or bearing noise means replace.
- tip: Clean rust from pulleys (rust dries out the belt so it breaks); replace extremely rusty pulleys.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Debris on the deck restricts tensioner bracket movement and belt movement; dry debris on the deck increases fire risk from belt heat and friction.
- step: Lower the deck to its lowest position, remove belt covers, clear under pulleys and brackets and the deck top; if no access, remove deck per owner's manual.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- tip: Wash with a garden hose; if using a pressure washer, do not use high pressure (damages sealed bearings). Dry completely: leaf blower or sun/breeze; storing wet components corrodes and fails electrical components.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- tip: Stabilize fuel (ethanol attracts moisture, the mixture separates and gums the fuel system); author uses Sea Foam. Without additive, drain all fuel, then run the engine until it shuts off.
- tip: Fully charge the battery (a fully charged battery will not freeze) or remove and store it in a cool dry place away from freezing and combustibles. Source links "A Guide to Winter Lawn Mower Battery Care".
- tip: Use a rodent deterrent; author has seen extensive wire harness damage from rodents; uses Grandpa Gus's Rodent Repellent (pouches in the mower, spray on the wiring harness).
