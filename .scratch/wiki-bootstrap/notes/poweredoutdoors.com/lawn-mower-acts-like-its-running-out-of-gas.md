## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "mower sputters and hesitates like it's running out of gas though tank has fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, water-in-fuel
- safety: Work in a well-ventilated area on the fuel system; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Ethanol (from corn or other high-starch plants) attracts moisture; ethanol-water mix corrodes components and leaves varnish that gums up the fuel system. Gas can begin to break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase; use within 30 days or add a stabilizer (Sea Foam). Author adds Sea Foam to every tank.
- fix: Siphon tank into an approved container; refill with fresh fuel plus additive that cleans and reduces moisture.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Strains fuel as it exits the tank. Replace annually, more often if gas was dirty.
- fix: Install with housing arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow, toward carburetor, away from tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Old fuel leaves gummy deposits narrowing the line.
- fix: Shut-off valve at bottom of tank (not every mower has one); otherwise hose pinch pliers.
- step 1: Work one section at a time; start with the line furthest from the tank (good flow there means skip others).
- step 2: Stop fuel flow (valve or pinch pliers).
- step 3: Remove the end of the line furthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank.
- step 4: Restart fuel flow. Poor flow = restriction.
- step 5: Stop flow, remove the line; spray carburetor cleaner in and blow out with compressed air; repeat until open.
- step 6: Reattach. If not clearable, replace line; also replace a dry cracked line before it leaks.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Plastic or metal pump present only if tank sits lower than carburetor; vacuum pump builds pressure off the crankcase to push fuel up to carburetor.
- confirm: Check flow to pump first; then remove line from carburetor into container, start flow, start mower; steady or pulsating stream = OK.
- fix: Replace if cracked, damaged, leaking or no stream.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Mounted top or side of engine block, usually below or behind the air filter.
- confirm: Remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake, start. If it starts and runs fine and then sputters again, clean the carburetor.
- tip: Author prefers carb cleaner over starter fluid (starter fluid is a dry chemical).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Tank must vent so inside pressure equals atmosphere; vent is in the cap on most mowers; plugged vent makes a vacuum and starves the mower.
- confirm: Run with and without the cap; runs well without it, then sputters some time after reinstalling = bad cap.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- symptom: After rain exposure or long storage; water is not combustible and sinks to the bottom (heavier than gas); may be visible with flashlight.
- fix: Drain the tank, fuel lines, and carburetor bowl; use air to dry remaining water; fill with fresh gas plus additive (Sea Foam) to reduce moisture; run mower to circulate.
