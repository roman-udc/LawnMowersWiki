## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Gravely mower won't start"
- fact: engine needs fuel, air and spark to start
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, wrong-fuel, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-recoil-starter, bad-ignition-coil, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- safety: follow Gravely owner's manual; remove spark plug wires before repairs
- pro: charging system faults: take to Gravely dealer; without experience you'll throw parts at it and most centers don't accept electrical part returns
- brand: guide covers gas-powered Gravely mowers

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: add fresh fuel; also check for fuel leaks (tank may have emptied faster than expected)

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Gravely uses unleaded gasoline, 10 percent ethanol or less, minimum 87 octane; higher ethanol can significantly damage the engine; lower ethanol is better

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gasoline breaks down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol attracts moisture, leaves gummy deposits on evaporation that clog fuel components
- fix: drain and flush the tank using a fuel siphon; add fresh fuel with additive/stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: blocked vent makes tank act like a vacuum, starving the engine
- confirm: remove cap and start the mower; if it starts and runs, put cap back and run longer; if it shuts off, cap is the problem
- fix: clean cap and clear vent with thin wire; else replace cap

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: carbon buildup or oil on plug causes misfire/no start; wrong gap or loose connection too
- fix: remove and inspect for carbon or cracked porcelain; replace; gap per engine manufacturer

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: first line of defense; dirt can damage seals and score the cylinder; plugged filter makes engine run rough or not start
- tip: start each season with a new filter; check/clean several times a season; more often in dry dusty conditions
- step (paper) 1: remove filter from housing
- step (paper) 2: wipe dirt out of housing without letting it fall into intake
- step (paper) 3: tap filter on solid surface
- mistake: do not use compressed air on the paper element, it can damage it
- step (paper) 4: hold up to light; if light shines through reuse, else replace

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump uses crankcase vacuum to build pressure; visible damage may be a crack in housing or leaking seams
- confirm: first confirm fuel comes out of the line at the pump inlet; then check pump output; use shut-off valve, fuel clamps or hose pinch pliers to stop/start flow; stop flow, remove the line from the carburetor and put it in a container; start flow and start the mower; watch for steady or pulsating flow; insufficient flow means replace pump

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- confirm: cracked, dark in colour, or won't pass fuel means replace
- fix: install new filter with the arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove hose end from the section of line furthest from the tank, put in container, start flow; insufficient flow means remove line and clear
- fix: spray carburetor cleaner into tube and blow compressed air through until clear; replace line if can't clear

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old fuel deposits build up; carb regulates fuel mixed with air
- fix: if a bit mechanical, take apart and clean with carb cleaner including float bowl and needle; replace if too dirty

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter should read about 12.7 volts; below that charge it
- fix: replace if it won't hold a charge

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: confirm terminals aren't corroded and are tight; clean in baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) with wire brush

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: several switches stop deck running without operator present; another prevents starting when parking brake isn't engaged
- confirm: multimeter, or temporarily bypass to identify
- safety: do not operate without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned and mower doesn't start or even turn over
- confirm: test with a multimeter; replace if bad

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: some Gravely push mowers use recoil; fails from bad pulley, loose/missing spring, broken clips
- fix: try replacing spring and restringing; if clips or pulley damaged replace the recoil assembly

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: coil supplies voltage so the plug fires
- confirm: after verifying plug is good, check coil continuity with a multimeter; replace if break in continuity

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: not main cause but can leave a weak battery; bad stator or alternator among others
- confirm: author links ohmmeter test article
- pro: dealer for troubleshooting

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Gravely safety features require certain steps to start/operate; follow operating manual so safety features don't shut the mower off
