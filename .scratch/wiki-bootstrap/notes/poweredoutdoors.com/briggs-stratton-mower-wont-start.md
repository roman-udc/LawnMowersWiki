## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Briggs and Stratton mower won't start"
- fact: B&S engines are used on many brands incl. select Toro, Craftsman, Yard Machines, Cub Cadet mowers. Won't start when no longer getting sufficient air, fuel and spark.
- fact: likely bad spark plug, plugged air filter, wrong choke setting, dirty carburetor, plugged fuel filter, clogged fuel line, bad safety switch, old gas. Electric-start riding/push mowers may add weak battery, corroded/loose cables, bad ignition switch.
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-safety-switch, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- safety: remove the spark plug boot(s) before any repair.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- symptom: nothing happens when key turned (bad ignition switch); click or hum when turning key points to the solenoid; weak battery; loose/corroded cables.
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-safety-switch

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: may be a faulty fuel gauge or a fuel leak burning more fuel than normal.
- fix: fill with fresh unleaded, minimum 87 octane, ethanol no greater than 10%.
- brand: current B&S mowers use 4-cycle engines requiring straight gasoline; older mowers may have a 2-cycle engine needing a gas and oil mix, with only one fill port for the mix.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas deteriorates after about 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture which, once evaporated, leaves gummy residue clogging lines, filter, carburetor.
- tip: buy fuel from a busy station and use within 30 days; otherwise add stabilizer. Author prefers Sea Foam Motor Treatment; STA-BIL also good.
- fix: siphon out old fuel (fuel siphon pump), add fresh fuel plus additive; once started, let it run a while so treated fuel works through the system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: plugged cap vent creates a vacuum, no gas to carburetor.
- confirm: remove cap and start; if it starts, reinstall cap with mower running; wait for sputter/die from lack of fuel; remove cap and see if it restarts. Start/die with cap off/on indicates cap problem.
- fix: replace the fuel cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: carbon buildup or oil on the tip fouls the plug, misfire, no start; loose wire or wrong gap also cause start/run problems.
- fix: remove, inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain, burnt electrodes; replace; gap correctly to engine manufacturer spec using a feeler gauge; check wire connection.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: coil provides voltage so the plug can fire.
- confirm: after verifying plug is good, check coil continuity; replace if a break in continuity.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: extremely dirty filter starves the engine; engine may overheat and draw air from the crankcase causing extensive damage.
- fix: clean; replace if extremely dirty or damaged. Check operator manual for filter type.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: PAPER:
- step 1: remove air filter cover, then filter.
- step 2: remove loose dirt from housing and cover with a clean dry cloth without letting dirt fall into intake.
- step 3: tap element on a solid surface to loosen dirt.
- step 4: hold up to light; good light through paper means reuse; otherwise or if damaged, replace.
- step 5: install filter, reattach cover.
- fact: FOAM:
- step 1: remove cover and filter; clean housing/cover with dry cloth.
- step 2: inspect; very dark, brittle or torn means replace.
- step 3: wash in water and mild detergent; rinse until clear; squeeze, do not wring (can tear).
- step 4: let dry.
- step 5: apply clean engine oil to fully cover, squeeze out excess.
- safety: NEVER apply oil to a foam pre-filter (the filter wrapping a paper element); it can damage the paper element.
- step 6: install, reattach cover.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: B&S engines have a fuel pump only when the carburetor sits higher than the tank; likely not on a small push mower. Old fuel deteriorates pump components.
- confirm: inspect vacuum pump for cracks/fuel outside; cracked pump can't use crankcase pressure. Use shut-off valve or clamps to stop/start flow and confirm gas reaches the pump (if not, check line/filter). Then remove line from carburetor into container, start flow and mower; steady or pulsating flow means OK.
- fix: replace the pump if no steady/pulsating flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: filter types: inline between fuel lines; small narrow filter at the bottom of the fuel tank.
- confirm: fuel running into the filter must run out; if it stops at the filter it's plugged.
- fix: replace; arrow on the filter points in direction of flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow compressed air through until free of dirt and gummy residue; or replace the line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: crusty buildup and gummy deposits from old ethanol fuel; engine runs rough or won't start.
- fix: clean it yourself if somewhat mechanical or take to a repair shop; replace if very bad.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence system kills the engine when the operator leaves the seat on a riding mower; on a push mower it won't start unless the safety bail control bar is brought into the handle. Brake switch on a riding mower is another to check.
- confirm: temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed; replace a bad switch.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: check terminals/cables for corrosion and for loose wiring. Follow the cable from the positive battery terminal to the solenoid and from the solenoid to the starter.
- fix: tighten; remove corrosion with water and baking soda mix.
- safety: remove the negative battery cable first, then the positive.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter reading about 12.7 volts for a 12-volt mower battery; charge if lower. Replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned, nothing happens. Confirm: test with multimeter. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor.
- symptom: click or hum on turning key; a wire at the solenoid getting hot, smoking or melting.
- fix: test the solenoid (article links steps); replace if bad.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: not main no-start reason, but can leave a weak battery on a riding mower. Bad stator or alternator among possible parts; test with ohmmeter.
- pro: let a small engine mechanic identify the part; guessing is expensive.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- brand: on a B&S riding mower the brake must be engaged and the PTO switch off for the engine to start.
- fact: check the operating manual so safety features don't block starting.
