## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "Ferris zero turn runs rough"
- alias: "Ferris bogs down"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, mowing-too-fast, wrong-engine-speed, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: engine not getting enough air, fuel and spark; or overworked
- safety: park on a flat surface, remove spark plug wires, remove ignition key, wait for all parts to stop moving

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: not enough air, rich mixture, rough running; can also overheat engine
- tip: replace once a year and clean several times per season (author recommendation); more often in dusty conditions
- fix: clean if a little dirty; replace if extremely dirty, damaged or no longer seals the intake

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove filter from housing
- step 2: wipe dirt remaining in housing; don't let dirt fall into the intake
- step 3: tap filter on a solid surface to knock dirt out
- step 4: hold to light; if light shines through the paper element reuse, otherwise replace
- step 5: reinstall and attach housing cover
- step 6: foam pre-filter - wash with mild detergent and water, rinse, air dry; DO NOT apply oil to the pre-filter
- step 7: if there is an inner filter, replace it annually

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke lever closes the choke plate to reduce air for a cold start (rich mixture); once warm it must be off/open
- symptom: choke in wrong position or stuck open/closed - engine runs rough and bogs down
- fix: choke closed for cold start, open for warm start/run; check the choke plate opens and closes with the lever; use carburetor cleaner to free a stuck choke; replace a worn choke cable

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: varnish and gummy deposits causing corrosion and restrictions
- fact: gas breaks down as soon as 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture, water/ethanol leaves varnish when it evaporates
- fix: replace old fuel; add fresh gas and fuel cleaner/stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment
- fact: use minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; ethanol-free best

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: use the fuel shut-off valve or hose crimp pliers to stop/start flow and check flow from the line; collect fuel in a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump)
- fix: shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air; repeat until clear; replace with same length and diameter if unclearable or cracking

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Ferris zero-turns use an inline filter between fuel lines; replace annually
- fix: install new inline filter with the arrow pointing in the fuel flow direction

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: remove old inline filter
- step 2: install new, arrow in the direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: first make sure fuel reaches the carburetor (check fuel line, filter, fuel pump if equipped); then remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and let it run; if it runs well then sluggish again, clean the carburetor
- fact: article links its own Ferris carburetor cleaning steps (not in source)

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Ferris tank vent is built into the gas cap; blocked vent creates vacuum
- confirm: when running rough, loosen the cap; if it runs better suspect cap; retighten, wait for sluggishness, loosen again to confirm
- fix: new Ferris gas cap

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: intermittent spark; also wrong gap or loose wire
- fix: remove and inspect; replace if very dark, burnt electrode or damaged; gap to engine manufacturer spec; attach wire securely

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: thick, tall or wet grass must be mowed slower; too fast strains engine, bogs, and gives a poor cut
- fix: slow down when the engine bogs; for tall grass make multiple cuts - highest cutting height first, then lower

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: run the highest engine speed when engaging blades; throttle lever to the rabbit icon on many mowers

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: deck plugged with clippings makes the engine work harder
- fix: scrape with a deck scraper or wire brush; avoid wet/damp grass; deck spray helps but is no miracle

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: dull blades tear grass, brown tips a couple of days after mowing; magnify plugged deck problems and bog the engine
- fix: remove, sharpen and balance (article links its own guide)
