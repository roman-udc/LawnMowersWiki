## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "strong gas smell in the garage"
- fact: fuel evaporates and doesn't leave big stains, so a leak is hard to find unless there's a puddle. Follow fuel lines from tank checking for wet spots.
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: vulnerable parts: carburetor, fuel pump, fuel filter, fuel lines, fuel tank, shut-off valve, primer bulb, gas cap.
- pro: if unsure how to diagnose safely, dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: leak from the bottom of the carburetor: rubber-band-like gasket between carburetor base and bowl dries and becomes brittle from heat/cool cycles.
- fix: step 1: shut off fuel (valve or clamps). step 2: remove the screw and the bowl, rag ready to catch fuel. step 3: replace gasket, reinstall bowl and screw. step 4: turn fuel on, wipe down, inspect for more leaks; if leaks, check float and needle.
- symptom: leak from the air intake port = most likely a stuck float (float regulates gas into the bowl; stuck float won't stop fuel flow). Fix: take carb apart to see what's sticking; cleaning procedure.
- symptom: stuck float needle: can try tapping carburetor side gently with the handle end of a hammer; works a time or two but recurs; rebuild kit (DIY or shop) or replace carburetor.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: inline filter plastic softens from fuel constantly sitting in it and leaks; replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most Craftsman pumps are plastic; used when carburetor sits higher than tank; plastic can crack, seep at the seams, deteriorate from old fuel.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: most tanks are high-density polyethylene; seams eventually fail; older Craftsman may have metal tanks that can rust through. Replace tank (poly seam leak or metal rust hole).

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: valve on the bottom of the tank (may or may not be fitted); metal and plastic types both prone to leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: bad fuel and weather dry and crack lines; check near line clamps (can puncture or weaken the line). Replace cracked hoses.
- tip: author prefers worm gear style hose clamps over pinch style.

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- fact: pressing the bulb moves fuel to the carburetor; can leak when overfilled, when plastic wears/cracks, or where bulb meets fuel line.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: gas smell with no leak found; fuel evaporates before you find wet spots.
- confirm: rock mower back and forth to splash fuel up to the cap and look for leaks around the cap base.
- fix: replace cap (online or Craftsman servicing center).

- fact: replacement is the stated fix for a leaking filter or cracked line; no steps given.
