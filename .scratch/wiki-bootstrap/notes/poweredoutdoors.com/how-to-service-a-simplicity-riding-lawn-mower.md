## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: do a full service once a year to keep the mower at its best and catch small problems; skipping can lead to non-running, overheating or bad cut from plugged filters, corroded electrical/fuel parts, dull blades, worn belt, seized components.
- tools: socket or spanner wrenches, filter wrench, deck scraper, wire brush, plastic funnel, oil drain pan, rags/paper towels, grease gun/grease, spray lubricant, rubber gloves, work gloves, safety glasses, cardboard or newspaper for work surface.
- parts: engine oil filter, engine oil, spark plugs, air filter, fuel filter, mower blades if not reusing, fuel stabilizer if storing for the off-season.
- step 1: gather materials (above).
- step 2: park on flat level surface, lay cardboard/newspaper; run engine a few minutes to warm the oil (warm oil drains better); shut off. Set parking brake, remove ignition key, disconnect spark plug wires.
- step 3: change engine oil and filter (see procedures/change-the-engine-oil).
- step 4: inspect spark plug and gap; author replaces plugs once a year at full service; if not replacing, clean dirty plugs. Don't reattach plug wires until done servicing.
- step 5: remove dirt and debris from cooling fins and engine area (dirty fins prevent air circulating around block and cylinder).
- step 6: clean debris from hydro fans (which cool the hydraulic transmission) and the transmission area.
- step 7: clean battery terminals; corrosion: solution of 2 cups water and 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda, scrub with wire brush.
- step 8: remove debris around electrical components and wiring (debris holds moisture and causes corrosion); confirm good connections.
- step 9: replace air filter at full service; check and clean it several times during season; plugged filter can shut the mower down.
- step 10: replace fuel filter; arrow on filter side points in direction of fuel flow, toward the air filter (carburetor) and away from tank.
- step 11: check fuel system for leaks: hoses secure, no punctures or cracks, components sealing, plastics not degrading.
- step 12: check tire pressures with gauge; uneven pressure makes deck unlevel, a low tire causes steering issues; set to psi in owner's manual (can differ from max psi on sidewall), equal left and right; confirm lug nuts are secure.
- step 13: remove the deck (see below).
- step 14: clean dirt/debris from top of deck (restricts tensioner bracket movement and belt travel; remove belt covers to clean around pulleys). Dry debris plus belt friction heat is a fire hazard.
- step 15: check belt and pulleys (see below).
- step 16: scrape deck, check spindle bearings, sharpen/replace blades.
- step 17: reinstall deck in reverse order; check deck level.
- step 18: check the safety system per owner's manual; skipping compromises safety.
- step 19: replace damaged, worn or missing parts; all hardware securely in place.
- step 20: grease front wheels, front axle and deck wheels (a grease zerk on each); lubricate lift linkages, pivot points and brake so they move freely.
- step 21: wash with garden hose; if using a pressure washer do not use high pressure (damages sealed bearings); dry completely (sunny/breezy day, or leaf blower); moisture causes premature corrosion. If moving mower for washing, reattach spark plug wires so it starts.
- step 22: storage for off-season: remove fuel from tank or add fuel stabilizer; fully charge the battery so it doesn't freeze, or remove and store in a cool dry place.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- brand: Simplicity riding mower.
- step 1: warm oil, drain pan under drain plug or drain hose (whichever model has); clean around fill and drain areas with a rag so debris doesn't enter crankcase.
- step 2: remove drain plug (under the hood on the side of the engine, near the bottom); remove dipstick so oil flows faster.
- step 3: reinstall drain plug once fully drained.
- step 4: replace oil filter using a filter wrench; put a light film of fresh oil on the new filter's seal then install.
- step 5: add new oil to proper level, verify with dipstick; do not overfill; if overfilled remove a little.
- step 6: wipe spilled oil off the mower so it doesn't smoke as it burns off the engine; dispose used oil at a recycling center or auto parts store that accepts it.
- fact: interval: change after every 50 hours and before storage; initial oil change on a new engine after the first 5 hours.

- fact: Simplicity: change every 50 hours and before storage; first change at 5 hours on a new engine.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- brand: Simplicity riding mower.
- step 1: flat level surface, deck lift at middle position.
- step 2: side-to-side: measure ground to blade tip on right and on left; if different, adjust the left adjustment rod under the rear fenders: loosen the upper nut (do not remove), turn lower nut clockwise to raise left side of deck, counter-clockwise to lower; when level, tighten upper nut.
- step 3: front-to-rear pitch: front of deck must be 1/4 inch to 3/8 inch lower than the rear. Adjust at the stabilizer bracket (outer and inner nut at its front): loosen outer nut (do not remove); tighten inner nut to raise the front, loosen lock nut to lower it; when front measures 1/4-3/8 inch less than rear, retighten outer nut.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: removing the Simplicity deck (needed for cleaning; may vary by model, check manual): parking brake on, PTO off, deck in lowest position.
- step 1 (manual PTO only): loosen hardware on right and left idler pulleys without removing; remove the hex screw holding the rod over the engine pulley and remove the rod; remove PTO cable and unhook spring by removing the pin attaching the cable to the deck bracket.
- step 2: move deck drive pulley: put a ratchet in the square hole of the idler bracket and turn until the pulley is forward.
- step 3: remove the belt from the PTO pulley.
- step 4: remove deck lift arms: cotter pin at the end of each lift arm near the rear (left and right); remove pins, slide deck pin out of lift arms.
- step 5: remove the front lift rod: cotter pin at deck; slide rod out of bracket; deck lift lever to highest position helps clear it.
- step 6: confirm nothing else attaches the deck and slide it out from under the mower.
- tools: ratchet, pliers for cotter pins.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- confirm: replace the deck belt if worn with a glazed shiny look, cracked, stretched or shredded.
- confirm: rotate pulleys by hand; replace if resistance or bearing noise; clean rust or replace very rusty pulleys.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: grab each blade and rock up/down; movement or knock means remove spindle housing and check; replace bad bearings or the whole cutter housing assembly.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- step: remove and sharpen blades, replace if necessary; scrape deck of clippings and dirt with a deck scraper or metal brush (clogged deck hurts cut quality, strains engine, and moisture corrodes the deck).

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: for the off-season remove fuel or add stabilizer; fully charge battery to prevent freezing or store it cool and dry.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: wash with garden hose; avoid high-pressure washer (sealed bearings); dry fully; leaf blower can help.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: full service once a year; check/clean air filter several times per season; spark plug replaced yearly; oil every 50 hours.
