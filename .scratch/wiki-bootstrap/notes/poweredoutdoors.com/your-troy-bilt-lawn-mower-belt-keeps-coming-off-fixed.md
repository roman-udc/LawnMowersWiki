## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Troy-Bilt belt keeps falling off / snapping"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Deck belt is usually one of the most expensive wear parts.
- tip (extend belt life): clean the deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off the belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix or replace damaged brackets or belt keepers.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: loose, worn, cracked, shiny glazed; replace.
- fact (shredded sides): find what it rubs against (belt keeper or bracket on the deck) or check routing; smooth shiny metal area marks the rubbing; adjust the deck bracket. A bracket with a bushing: sometimes the bushing can be replaced, other times only the bracket-and-bushing assembly is sold.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Troy-Bilt may use a belt keeper rod, a small rod-shaped post alongside the pulleys; it sits between 1/8" and 1/4" from the pulley so the belt doesn't rub it. A slack belt can hit and bend it; then the belt can come off. Bend back with a hammer or replace.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: Check each pulley sits flat and parallel to the deck (not one side higher). Slowly turn it; feel resistance, listen for noise; replace. Idler pulleys have sealed bearings so the whole pulley assembly is replaced.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- safety: Remove ignition key and unplug spark plug boot(s) before working under the deck.
- confirm: Wear work gloves; grab each end of the blade and rock up and down; check for movement; listen for knocking; if so, remove spindle housings and check for a bad bearing or bent spindle shaft. Some housings can be rebuilt; others sold as assembly.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Tensioner bracket and spring hold the idler pulley in line (pulley one side, spring other). Spring wears or breaks; the spring-mount hole wears and vibration lets the belt off. Replace the worn or missing part.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Mowing kicks up stones, acorns, sticks; buildup stops the tension pulley moving properly, belt loosens. Debris in pulley grooves or packed mud stresses the belt as it stretches around, so it comes off or snaps.
- safety: Belt friction and heat at speed can ignite dry debris.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil leak drips on the belt; it slips; slipping builds heat and wears the belt. Rust dries and cracks the belt. Fix the leak or rust, clean rust from pulleys (replace if bad), replace the belt.
