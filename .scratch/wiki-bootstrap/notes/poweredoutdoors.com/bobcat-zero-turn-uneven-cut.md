## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Bobcat zero turn has an uneven cut"
- alias: "scalping"
- alias: "step cut / ridges in the lawn"
- alias: "streaking / strips of uncut grass"
- alias: "stringers / patches of uncut grass"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, worn-blade-sail, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, unlevel-deck, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: Cut-quality types and typical causes: Scalping (close cut in areas): low tire, uneven lawn, unlevel deck, deck height too low. Step cut (ridges): unlevel deck, bent blade, low tire, spindle damage, wrong blade installation. Streaking (strips of uncut grass): worn blades, dull blades, clogged deck, incorrect mowing paths, mowing too fast, engine speed too low. Stringers (patches of uncut grass): worn blades, dull blades, clogged deck, mowing too fast, engine speed too low.
- fact: Quick table: worn blade -> sharpen/replace; low tire -> add air; bent blade -> replace; blade incorrectly installed -> reinstall correctly; clogged deck -> scrape; deck not level -> level; spindle damage -> replace spindle/housing; bad belt -> replace; bad pulley -> replace; low engine speed -> full throttle; ground speed too fast -> adjust; incorrect paths -> overlap; deck shell damaged -> repair/replace; unlevel yard/high spots -> smaller deck mower / roll the lawn.
- tip: Check tire pressure BEFORE adjusting the deck, since a low tire throws off deck level.
- safety: Remove ignition key and spark plug wires, wait for all parts to stop moving, wear appropriate safety gear.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: A low tire can throw off deck level.
- step 1: Check pressure with tires cold.
- step 2: Remove the valve stem cap.
- step 3: Place a tire gauge on the stem.
- step 4: Check Bobcat's recommended PSI.
- step 5: Add air with a compressor to match recommended PSI.
- step 6: Replace the cap.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- symptom: uneven-cut; strips of uncut grass.
- fact: Blade sail is the high side of the blade that creates air movement; the airflow/suction under the deck stands grass up for an even cut.
- confirm: Replace the blade when the sail is thinner than the center of the blade.
- fact: Wear comes from dirt and debris sucked into the deck; unavoidable, normal.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: Worn blade ends may leave strips between blades; replace a blade with rounded ends.
- fact: Dull edge does not cut precisely, beats up/tears grass; grass tips may turn brown a day or two after mowing. Sharpen a dull blade still in good overall condition; replace if in bad condition or edge gouges can't be fixed in sharpening.
- fact: A blade bends when hitting a hard object; sometimes a jolt and grinding of metal, sometimes too minor to notice, so always check for bend when cut is bad.
- confirm: Removed-blade check: park on flat level surface, remove key and plug wires, wait for parts to stop, access deck underside, remove the blade and lay it on a new blade; replace if gaps show.
- confirm: In-place check: measure ground-to-blade-tip on one side, rotate blade 180 degrees, measure other side; if difference is more than 1/8 inch, replace blade.
- mistake: Never try to straighten a bent blade; metal is compromised, weak area likely to crack and break off at high speed, endangering people, pets and structures.
- fact: Unbalanced blade also causes uneven cut: balance means equal weight at both ends; unbalanced by uneven wear or by sharpening that shaves more from one side.
- step: Balance with a blade balancer: place blade on balancer, shave a little metal off the heavier side.
- step: Or use a nail in a wall with head sticking out 3/4 to 1 inch: hang blade center on nail head, remove metal from the side hanging lower until level.
- tip: Follow the operating manual to install blade with correct hardware.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- symptom: step cut / uneven cut from wrong blade installation.
- fact: Article only says blade must be installed correctly with correct hardware per manual; does not explicitly say upside-down.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: uneven-cut; blade wobbles when turning; vibration at high speed.
- confirm: Wear heavy gloves; grab both ends of blade and rock up and down checking for extra play; listen for knocking. If either, remove the spindle housing and check spindle and bearing for wear/damage.
- fix: Replace damaged spindle or housing.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: Clippings/dirt reduce the under-deck volume needed for airflow and suction; also strain the engine and can shut it down.
- fix: Scrape deck regularly; wet grass clumps faster. Avoid mowing wet grass; if you must, scrape afterwards.
- tip: Silicone or Teflon spray can reduce sticking, though nothing prevents all buildup.
- fact: Tire tracks show prominently in wet grass.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: Deck higher on one side gives uneven cut. Follow Bobcat operator's manual to measure and level side-to-side; also set rake (pitch of deck front to rear). Measurements vary by deck model, so consult the manual.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: uneven-cut; slowed blade revolutions.
- confirm: Look for cracking, shredding, shiny glazed appearance, or belt sitting deep in pulley grooves.
- fix: Replace with a new Bobcat belt.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: uneven-cut; pulley shifts side to side when deck engaged.
- fact: Good pulley is secure, flat and parallel to the deck.
- confirm: Rotate pulley slowly by hand feeling for resistance and listening for bearing noise; grab both sides and rock up/down.
- fix: If resistance, noise or excess play, replace bearing or pulley assembly (sometimes only bearings, sometimes whole assembly, varies by model).

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: Blade tip speed affects cut quality. Always run full throttle with deck engaged.
- pro: If at full throttle but power is down, take to dealer/small engine shop.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: Thick, tall or wet grass loads the engine; slow down. Too fast pushes grass over and does not give time to stand grass up.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- symptom: strips of uncut grass.
- tip: New owners may need a few mowings to judge how much overlap is needed.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- symptom: uneven-cut.
- fact: Deck shell damaged by hitting post, tree, building. Repair or replace. Get a price on a full deck replacement too; the difference may be small, especially with labor to swap components. Consider repair cost against mower age.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: See debris-on-deck; avoid wet mowing.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades wear normally from debris; belts crack/glaze; pulley and spindle bearings wear.

## unmapped
- suggested slug causes/uneven-lawn-or-wide-deck-scalping: Lawn with high spots (molehills) or steep dips/ditches causes scalping; mowers work best on level lawns; a lawn roller (weighted roller) may help; wide-format Bobcat decks can't flex with dips, so use a small push mower there.
