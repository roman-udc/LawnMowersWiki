## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Gas-powered lawn tractor needs unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol (sold as regular or E10). Best is ethanol-free, which costs more.
- safety: Never put diesel in a gas tractor or gas in a diesel tractor; some tractors are diesel; check operator's manual.
- fact: Ethanol is corn-based and attracts moisture from the air. When the gas breaks down, the ethanol-water mix separates and sinks to the tank bottom, causing gumming and degradation of fuel components; it can potentially overheat the engine (runs very hot, can cause significant damage).
- fact: Higher-ethanol fuels at stations: E15, E30, E85 (15%, 30%, up to 85% ethanol).
- fact: Ethanol-free fuel does not damage the fuel system like ethanol gas. Sold in canisters at hardware stores (convenient for small tanks, pricier than at the pump) or at the pump as recreational fuel (REC-90), up to $3.00 more per gallon. Not all stations carry it; puregas.org lists stations in the US and Canada.
- fact: Ethanol-free fuel needs no stabilizer; if you accidentally add it, no need to drain; it won't hurt or help.
- tip: Buy fresh gas from a busy station; use within 30 days; stabilize fuel not used within 30 days; store away from the weather and away from combustibles.
- opinion: Author recommends higher-quality fuel if you won't use gas promptly or stabilize it.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Gas can start breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase; buy only what you can use in 30 days. Excess: add an additive to stabilize and minimize moisture; author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment.
- fact: Old fuel clogs and degrades the fuel system, contributing to leaks and blockages.
- tip: Store fuel away from outdoor elements and combustibles.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Diesel in a gas tractor or gas in a diesel tractor must never happen; high-ethanol (E15/E30/E85) fuels are a hazard.
