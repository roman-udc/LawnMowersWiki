## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Cub Cadet turns over but won't start"
- alias: "turn the key, push the button or pull the rope and it won't start"
- causes-named: wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Engine turns over but can't form combustion due to lack of gas, air or spark.
- safety: Remove the spark plug wire before any repair; follow the Cub Cadet operator's manual safety precautions.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: hard starting or no start; engine only runs with choke on (problems/engine-only-runs-with-choke-on).
- fact: Choke restricts air so a cold engine gets more gas. After start and warm-up, move the lever to off/open so more air flows. A warm engine must be started with choke off.
- fix: Choke on/closed for a cold engine, off/open for a warm one.
- confirm: If setting is right but still airflow issues, check the choke plate isn't stuck and the choke cable moves freely. Use carburetor cleaner to free a stuck plate and linkages.
- fix: Replace a stretched or worn cable with a new Cub Cadet choke cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- tip: Start each season with a new air filter and clean it several times per season. Check it before operating.
- step 1: Remove filter from housing without letting dirt fall into the intake.
- step 2: Wipe housing with a clean dry rag.
- step 3: Tap paper filter on a solid surface.
- step 4: Hold to a light source; reuse if light shows through.
- step 5: Replace if no light, extremely dirty, or damaged.
- step 6: Install and reattach the cover (with foam pre-filter installed first if used).
- fact: Foam pre-filter is an extra filter used with a paper filter; not the same as a primary foam filter. NEVER add oil to a pre-filter or it damages the paper filter.
- step: Pre-filter: inspect, replace if torn or brittle; wash with water and mild dish soap; rinse until soap gone and water clear; lay flat to dry (sun outdoors speeds it); reinstall with paper primary filter and cover.
- pro: Unsure of filter type? Refer to the operator's manual.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Paper filter and foam pre-filter steps as listed under causes/clogged-air-filter (Cub Cadet). Never oil a pre-filter used over paper.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Remove with a 3/4 in or 5/8 in socket wrench depending on engine model.
- symptom: dark-colored tip, burnt electrode, or broken porcelain: install a new plug.
- fix: A slightly dirty plug in good condition can be cleaned with a small wire brush; check electrode gap is correct; securely attach wire.
- tip: Start each season with a new spark plug.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; leaves varnish and sticky deposits causing restrictions and component failures. Buy fresh and use within 30 days.
- brand: Cub Cadet 4-cycle engines use gasoline, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; 2-cycle engines use gas/oil mix.
- fix: Drain the tank with a fuel siphon pump; refill with fresh fuel plus a fuel stabilizer such as Sea Foam. If it starts, let it run so the treated fuel works through the system.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter between fuel lines strains fuel from the tank; plugs if not replaced regularly.
- fix: Replace old/dirty filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Follow line from tank to carburetor looking for kinks. Then shut off fuel (valve or pinch-off pliers), remove the end of each section furthest from the tank into a container, turn the fuel on, check flow.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove line, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow compressed air through; repeat until clear. Reinstall. Replace if the restriction stays or line is aged and cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Used when the carburetor is higher than the tank; most mowers use a vacuum pump that uses crankcase vacuum.
- confirm: First ensure fuel reaches the pump inlet (else check line/filter). Then remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start flow and start the mower; expect steady or pulsating fuel. If not, replace pump.
- confirm: Electronic fuel injection pump: measure with a fuel pressure gauge; compare to the Cub Cadet operator's manual spec. Replace if below engine manufacturer spec.
- fix: Replace if cracked or failed.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old gas leaves varnish that may plug the fuel jet or make internals stick.
- confirm: Confirm fuel flow to the carburetor; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner into intake and start. If it starts then runs sluggish/dies when the cleaner burns off, carburetor is likely dirty. Don't use starter fluid.
- fix: Clean, replace faulty parts, or replace carburetor (links to Cub Cadet carburetor cleaning article).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Tank must vent to equalize pressure; on Cub Cadet the vent is in the gas cap. Plugged vent makes a vacuum, gas can't reach the carburetor, engine turns over but won't start.
- confirm: Loosen the cap and try to start; if it starts, cap may be the cause. Further confirm: run it and tighten the cap; if it sputters, shuts down, and won't restart until the cap is loosened, it's the cap.
- fix: Replace the plugged cap.
