## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "my Exmark zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil
- fact: not-starting is usually a fuel or air restriction; check carburetor, fuel filter, fuel lines, and air filter first, then electrical (battery, safety switches, ignition coil).
- fact: table of causes with fixes below (Cause / reason / solution).

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: fuel breaks down over time, becoming less efficient and prone to clogging the fuel system (also engine damage when it causes dying).
- fix: drain the fuel tank and fill with fresh fuel; use an additive like Sea Foam to stabilize fuel and help clean the fuel system.
- symptom: won't start (problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start); dies while running (problems/engine-starts-then-dies)

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vent in the cap can clog, tank forms a vacuum and restricts fuel flow. Shows up after mowing a little while (engine dies).
- fix: clear the vent with a thin piece of wire; replace cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: excessively dirty or broken plug, bad connection, or not gapped correctly.
- fix: replace with a new plug, secure connections, gap to manufacturer spec.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dirt and grass plug the filter and prevent airflow. Also causes dying and smoking.
- fix: remove and clean the filter; replace if in bad condition.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: a dirty filter restricts fuel flow; replace the fuel filter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: a failed or leaking pump no longer creates the pressure needed to pump fuel; can leak at the seams as it wears.
- fix: replace the fuel pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: bad fuel deposits and dirt build up in the lines and starve the engine.
- fix: carb cleaner and compressed air to clear the clog; replace the line if necessary.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: becomes dirty and clogged from bad fuel and ethanol deposits, preventing fuel flow; also buildup from old gas can cause blockages or part failures and leaks.
- fix: clean the carburetor components; replace if needed (leak case: clean and replace damaged parts).

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: dead battery causes starting problems.
- fix: charge the battery, replace it if it will not hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: make sure cables are tight and clean corrosion from terminals.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: safety switches prevent the mower from starting in certain situations; a failed switch may mean no start.
- fix: test and replace the faulty switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: mower will not start when the ignition switch is bad. fix: test and replace.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: mower will not start when the coil is bad. fix: test and replace.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Exmark dies while mowing"
- alias: "mower loses power and dies"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor
- fact: same fuel/air starvation items as no-start cause dying while mowing; a clog can move through the fuel system. Overheating can shut the mower down; inspect cooling fins for breakage or dirt. Fins keep air moving around the engine block and cylinder head.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins packed with dirt, oil and grass cannot cool the engine block.
- fix: replace broken fins and clean the fins.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket
- fact: smoke arises when the engine lacks the correct air/fuel ratio or oil gets hot and burns off.
- fix (overfilled): excess oil pressurizes the engine and oil gets into the cylinder through the valve train; drain a little oil to the manufacturer's recommended level.
- fix (low oil): lack of oil increases friction and extreme heat; take to a small engine repair shop to inspect for damage; adding oil is often too late.
- pro: internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) go to a small engine repair shop.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Exmark zero turn won't move or moves slowly"
- alias: "hydro seems weak"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system, bypass-valve-open
- fact: drive release (bypass) lever allows hydrostatic mowers to roll freely; it must be in the operating position or the mower will not move (relevant after manually moving the mower).
- fact: if the drive belt has fallen off or is worn, the mower will not move; make sure the belt is securely around the pulleys. Replace a worn or cracked belt.
- fact: bad tensioner pulley - bearing can fail; replace the tensioner pulley.
- fact: missing/broken idler arm spring means no tension on the drive belt; replace the spring.
- fact: low or old hydraulic oil fails to lubricate; change at the interval Exmark recommends and check the level periodically.
- fact: hot hydraulic fluid can signal hydrostatic transmission damage. Let it cool; if the mower runs fine cold but weakens when the hydraulic system heats up, have it checked at a local Exmark dealership.
- fact: air must be bled from the hydraulic system after changing hydraulic fluid, or the mower cannot move at normal speed; follow Exmark's procedure in the owner's manual.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- symptom: won't move (problems/mower-wont-move)
- fix: put drive release lever in operating position.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: frayed, cracked or worn drive belt makes the drive pump work inefficiently (weak hydro) or the mower not move. Replace.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: bad tensioner pulley fails to keep tension on the drive belt (weak hydro). fix: replace the pulley and grease the tensioner arm.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fix: replace the broken or missing spring.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: low or old hydro oil gives weak transmission or no/slow movement.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- see above (problems/mower-wont-move).

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- see above (problems/mower-wont-move).

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "Exmark won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper (tracking adjustment: see unmapped)
- fact: unequal tire pressure pulls the mower to one side; keep tires at manufacturer spec with equal pressure on each side.
- fact: bad zero-turn dampers make the mower jerk to one side when moving forward; replace the damper.
- fact: on a zero-turn or stand-on mower, if one tire moves faster than the other the mower pulls; adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the tire rotation speed (see owner's manual).

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fix: check tire pressures and fill to manufacturer spec; both tires per side/pair equal.

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fix: replace bad damper.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- fact: adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the rotation speed of the tire; refer to owner's manual.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Exmark vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: normal engine vibration is always present; a severe, atypical vibration must be addressed to minimise damage.
- fact: loose or missing engine mounting bolts (bolts reduce vibration) - secure and replace missing.
- fact: bad clutch - bearing failure where bearing falls out of the clutch causes vibration; a manual-engagement clutch can have bushing and linkage failures. Replace clutch.
- fact: unbalanced or damaged blades - replace; blades must be balanced before installing.
- fact: string or long weeds wrapped around blade shaft - remove all, check spindle for damage.
- fact: bad spindle housing bearing - replace the bearing or the whole spindle housing assembly.
- fact: worn pulleys or deck belt - check belt wear and pulley bearing failure; replace.
- fact: debris lodged in the deck or mower - check everything, don't overlook small items.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "I smell gas / burnt grass spots"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- confirm: with a strong gas odor or burnt-grass spots, follow the fuel lines out of the tank to find the leak; leaks are hard to find because fuel evaporates.
- fact: common leak area is the carburetor: dried-out gasket around the carb bowl or a stuck float. Old gas can degrade plastics so fuel filter, pump or tank seams leak.
- fact: old/plugged fuel filter - replace. Fuel pump wear leaks at seams - replace. Metal tank can rust through; poly tank leaks at seams - replace; sometimes a rust hole can be repaired if no replacement tank is available. Metal and plastic shut-off valves prone to leaking - replace. Rubber fuel lines dry and crack - replace. Gas cap seal failure lets gas splash out around the cap - replace the cap.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fix: rubber lines dry out and crack; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fix: replace tank; rust hole sometimes repairable.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fix: replace the shut-off valve.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fix: replace the gas cap.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: gasket around carburetor bowl dries and loses sealing, or float sticks.
