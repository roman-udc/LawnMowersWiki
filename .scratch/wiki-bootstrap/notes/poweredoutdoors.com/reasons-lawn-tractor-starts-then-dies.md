## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "lawn tractor starts then dies"; "dies after running"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Starts then dies when it can't get the air, fuel and spark needed for combustion. Begin each season with full routine service.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: A fuel leak can empty the tank sooner; the tractor may also die running on a hill with a low tank. Fill the tank.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas tractor needs unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Lasts about 30 days before breaking down. Ethanol/moisture solution gums and degrades the fuel system, separates and sinks to the tank bottom, and burning it makes the engine extremely hot and shut down.
- fix: Siphon the tank into an approved recycling container. Fill with fresh fuel plus Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans). Run the tractor a few minutes so treated fuel moves through the lines.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Gummed components; runs sluggish or shuts down.
- fix: Clean it if somewhat mechanical (many little parts) or take it to a shop to be cleaned or rebuilt.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace; the arrow on the side points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: A pump is required because the tank sits lower than the carburetor. Seams or the pump can fail from fuel; internal damage is hard to see.
- confirm: Shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank or hose pinch pliers to stop flow. (1) Fuel off, remove the line from the pump inlet into a container, start flow; fuel should run out. If not, look for blocked lines or clogged filter. Reattach. (2) Remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start mower and flow; expect a steady or pulsating flow. If none, shut off, ensure no blockage in the line to the carburetor; if clear, replace the pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove the line; spray carburetor cleaner then blow compressed air; repeat until clear; reinstall or buy a new line (hardware store or online). Replace lines that are dry and cracked.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: Remove the cap, start and run the tractor. If it no longer dies, replace the cap and keep running; if it dies with the cap in place, the cap is the problem.
- fix: Try cleaning the vent; otherwise replace.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Plugged filter starves the engine (the Source says "starved of fuel" but means air) and it shuts down; can lead to overheating and internal damage.
- tip: Start each season with a new filter; clean several times during the season.
- step: Paper filter cleaning: remove from housing; wipe housing with a clean dry cloth without letting dirt into the intake; tap against a solid surface; never use compressed air; hold to light, reuse if light shows through, else replace; reinstall with cover.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: Remove the engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins. (The Source mentions a Husqvarna in this section.)

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Too little oil: extra friction, overheating, dies.
- fix: Check the dipstick (wipe it off with a dry clean cloth, reinsert, remove and read). Low: change the oil and fill with fresh oil; may not undo damage.
- pro: If it overheated and shut down from low oil, likely engine damage; have a small engine mechanic troubleshoot.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Too much oil smokes and dies while mowing; the smoke clogs the air filter so the engine looks elsewhere for air; possible internal damage and engine replacement.
- fix: Remove a little oil via the drain plug or oil filter, or with an oil evacuator or turkey baster.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Plug covered in carbon or oil causes intermittent running and dying; loose wires or wrong gap also.
- fix: Clean; replace if very dark with a burnt electrode or cracked porcelain; check gap and wires.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Windings separate and short out when hot; no voltage for spark; dies after getting hot.
- confirm: Ohm meter, break in continuity = replace.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: Choke restricts air so more fuel enters; needed for cold start. Leaving it on after the engine warms makes the tractor die.
- fix: Adjust the choke to the off position once warm.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Deck packed with grass and debris, plus dull blades, loads the engine until it shuts down.
- fix: Inspect for damage, scrape the deck, sharpen blades, run full throttle, avoid wet grass.
