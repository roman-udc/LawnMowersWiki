## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "John Deere zero turn starts then dies"
- alias: "zero-turn quits in the middle of the lawn"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: causes group into fuel restriction, air restriction, lack of spark, or overheated/overworked engine
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug wires; allow engine to cool; follow John Deere manual

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: consume fuel within 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture; ethanol and water separate and sink to the tank bottom; corrosive and leaves varnish and sticky deposits causing failures and restrictions, sluggish running and quitting
- fix: if fuel sat over 30 days without stabilizer, drain tank; only fresh gas; add Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilize, reduce moisture, clean) before filling; run about 15 minutes to work mixture through the system
- brand: John Deere zero-turns require unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: inline filter strains fuel; annual maintenance part; plugged filter starves engine
- fix: replace; arrow on housing side must point in the direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: old fuel leaves gummy substance narrowing the passageway
- confirm: use fuel shut-off valve to start/stop flow, check each section of line for flow
- fix: turn off fuel, remove line, spray carb cleaner to loosen, blow compressed air through; if not clear install new line same length and diameter; turn fuel on

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most John Deere zero-turns use a vacuum pump (engine vacuum moves fuel to carburetor); can fail with age or old fuel
- confirm: verify flow to pump through the line; then shut off fuel, remove line from carburetor into container, turn on fuel, start; steady or pulsating flow means pump works; poor flow: replace pump

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: varnish from old gas plugs passages and makes small internal parts stick
- confirm: verify fuel flow to carburetor; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner into air intake and start; if it starts, runs fine, then bogs down and shuts off, carburetor is the problem
- fix: remove, disassemble, clean; or small engine shop; or replace carburetor assembly

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: zero-turns create dusty conditions; replace air filter once a year and clean several times per mowing season; more often in very dusty/commercial use

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove paper element from housing without letting dirt fall into the intake
- step 2: wipe dirt out of housing with clean dry cloth
- step 3: tap filter on a hard surface; do not use compressed air (damages paper)
- step 4: hold up to light; replace if light doesn't shine through, damaged or oily; otherwise reuse
- step 5: reinstall and reattach cover

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: remove dirt on fins, replace broken fins, clean around engine block and shroud; make sure the heat shield is correctly installed for air circulation

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: check oil with dipstick before each use; low oil: friction creates crankcase heat, burns oil and parts
- fix: can try adding oil but usually significant damage; have experienced small engine mechanic look; John Deere service center or the engine maker's dealer service center

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: excess oil builds crankcase pressure
- fix: remove a little oil via drain plug, oil filter, or fill opening with oil evacuator or turkey baster; add/remove until dipstick shows full

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: remove oil from drain plug, oil filter or fill area using an oil evacuator or turkey baster
- step 2: continue removing/adding until level is at full mark on dipstick

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: dirty or wrong gap or loose wires make it sluggish or die
- fix: remove, clean with wire brush; very dark or damaged: replace; gap per engine manufacturer's spec; wires secure

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: winding separates and shorts when hot; no voltage to plug; dies after running a while
- confirm: check for a break in continuity; replace if break

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke on/closed to start cold engine, then off/open when warm; left on after warm-up the engine needs more air and dies

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vented cap; failed vent forms vacuum so gas can't flow; starts fine then shuts down after a while
- confirm: loosen cap and see if engine runs better; tighten and run a while to see if problem returns; replace bad cap

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck strains engine; dull blades magnify; regularly scrape deck and sharpen blades; deck uses blades and under-deck area for air movement to lift and cut grass

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- fact: run zero-turn at high engine speed; don't idle long since battery charges from engine power; if you keep having to charge battery and battery and wiring are good and secure, suspect charging system
- pro: if charging system isn't working right, have an experienced mechanic diagnose; many components can fail; parts-throwing is expensive and electrical parts usually can't be returned
