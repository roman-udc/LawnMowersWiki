## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "strong fuel smell in garage from my Husqvarna"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Gas evaporates, so the leak is hard to find. Follow lines from the tank checking each section for wet spots.
- pro: If not comfortable, contact Husqvarna dealer or repair shop; only perform repairs you are mechanically able to do.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Bowl at bottom of carb stores fuel; gasket (looks like a rubber band) where bowl attaches wears out and leaks. Common leak spot: near engine, heat when running and cool when off dry it and it loses its seal.
- fix steps: shut off fuel (shut-off valve under tank, or clamp the fuel line); remove the screw and the carb bowl (rag to catch fuel); replace the gasket; reinstall bowl and screw; wipe carb down; turn fuel on and check; if still leaking, check float and needle.
- symptom (stuck float): leak around the air intake port; float regulates bowl fuel; if it keeps letting fuel in, excess runs out. Pull carb apart to find cause; clean or rebuild.
- fact (stuck float needle): needle works with float to keep gas flowing; may need replacement; repair yourself or have Husqvarna dealer rebuild. Temporary: gently tap carb side with hammer handle end; may work only a time or two.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Gas makeup deteriorates plastic inline filters; replace a leaking filter. Be careful removing filter ends from hose; plastic gets soft over time. Replacement at hardware store or online.
- fact: Husqvarna needs fresh unleaded fuel with octane 87 or higher and ethanol no greater than 10 percent; gas has short shelf life.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most Husqvarna pumps are plastic; used when the carburetor sits higher than the tank (some models have none). Plastic deteriorates, damaged internally or leaks at seams; can't build pressure. Replace a leaking pump.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Older mowers have metal tanks (rust holes); newer use high-density polyethylene (seams can fail). Metal: seal hole with petroleum-resistant epoxy such as TankWeld by J-B Weld, or replace if available. Poly seam failure: best solution is replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Plastic or metal valve under the tank is prone to leaking; replace if it's the source.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Weathering dries and cracks lines; check hose sections held by clamps (common leak spot). Replace cracked lines.
- tip: Consider replacing pinch-style clamps; author prefers worm gear clamps (less likely to pinch lines and cause leaks).

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- fact: Bulb may leak when overfilled with fuel from pressing, or where it meets the fuel line. Relatively easy to replace.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Seal dries and no longer seals; check last if nothing else found.
- confirm: Rock the mower back and forth to splash fuel up around the cap; a wet spot means replace the cap.
