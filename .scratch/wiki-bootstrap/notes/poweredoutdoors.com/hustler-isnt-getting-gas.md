## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "engine turns over but won't start; not getting gas"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- safety: work on the fuel system in a well-ventilated area; gas fumes are harmful in enclosed areas.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- brand: Hustler requires unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol.
- fact: fuel can degrade as soon as 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture, causing premature corrosion of fuel system parts and varnish that restricts fuel. Best choose ethanol-free or low-ethanol gas; consume within 30 days; use additive when you have more than you can use.
- fix: drain tank (fuel siphon pump, fuel container to collect), refill with fresh fuel with additive. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: inline filter between the fuel lines; replace at least once a year.
- fix: replace; arrow on the plastic housing points toward the carburetor, away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: gummy deposits from old fuel.
- confirm: use the fuel shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank to start/stop flow; if no valve, hose pinch pliers; isolate the section that doesn't flow.
- fix: shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow with compressed air; replace if it can't be cleared; replace a dry cracking line before it leaks.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: plastic or metal pump is present when the fuel tank is lower than the carburetor; vacuum pump builds pressure from crankcase vacuum.
- confirm: confirm flow to the pump inlet; remove line from carburetor into a container; start flow and mower; steady or pulsating stream = OK.
- fix: replace a cracked, damaged, leaking pump or one not supplying enough fuel.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carburetor mounted on top or side of engine block, usually below or behind the air filter. Fuel jet and components clog.
- confirm: confirm fuel gets to the carb; remove air filter, spray a little carb cleaner into the intake, start; if it starts, runs then shuts off, remove and clean carb. If it won't start on cleaner, problem may not be fuel.
- fix: clean (homeowner if somewhat mechanical) or repair shop.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: remove cap, start and run; install cap while running; if it eventually shuts down with cap on and restarts when cap off, clogged vent.
- fix: try cleaning; often must buy a new cap.
