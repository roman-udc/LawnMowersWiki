## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Troy-Bilt push mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-safety-switch, sheared-flywheel-key, bad-recoil-starter, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-battery, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, blown-fuse, incorrect-starting-procedure, fuel-valve-off, wrong-choke-setting, bad-ignition-coil
- safety: Remove the spark plug (wire) during repairs.
- fact: Engine compresses fuel/air mix, ignites with spark. Verify sufficient fuel, air, spark. Before testing verify 4 things: (1) fuel shut-off valve open (if equipped; often closed in storage/transport); (2) enough fresh fuel; (3) bail (safety) lever engaged when starting; (4) choke on/closed for a cold engine (most current models have automatic choke, no lever).
- confirm (fuel system test): remove air filter cover and filter; spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake (do NOT use starter fluid; author has seen too much damage); try to start (recoil or key). If it attempts to start or starts -> fuel system problem (most common: dirty carb, clogged fuel filter, clogged fuel line). If not -> most likely spark plug problem.
- confirm (airflow test): remove filter cover and filter; inspect filter (clogged restricts air; clean or replace). With filter removed inspect choke plate. Manual choke: lever on = plate closed; lever off (on some models means throttle in fast position) = plate open. Auto-choke: closed when cold, opens when warm; controlled by a thermostat near the muffler. If not opening/closing correctly, check linkage and choke cable; lubricate linkages and choke shaft with carburetor cleaner; replace bad choke cable (if used).
- confirm (spark test): remove spark plug boot; attach a spark plug tester to the ignition wire, other end to the plug (still installed); crank (pull cord or electric start); glow in the transparent section = spark. No glow: bad plug or bad ignition coil; on electric start also battery, wiring, starter solenoid.
- pro: If the list doesn't solve it, consult a small engine mechanic; don't throw parts at it.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: Push mowers have very small tanks; could have a fuel leak making you run out sooner. Fill 4-cycle with 87+ octane, ethanol no greater than 10%.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Manufacturers recommend consuming fuel within 30 days of purchase; ethanol attracts moisture and leaves gummy substance and crusty material in the carb.
- tip: Buy fresh 87+ octane, max 10% ethanol; 4-cycle takes straight gas, 2-cycle needs gas and 2-cycle oil mix; consume in 30 days; use additive (Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL); store fuel in a cool dry location.
- fix: Remove old fuel (best with a siphon pump; or remove fuel line from carb into a container). Add fresh fuel mixed with additive; start and let it run through the system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Vent lets air in as fuel is consumed; failed vent forms vacuum.
- confirm: Loosen the cap and try to start; if it starts and runs, tighten while running; if it runs sluggishly or shuts down, loosen the cap to see if it runs better.
- fix: May clean the vent; author prefers to replace.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Carbon buildup: clean or replace if tip very dark; broken porcelain or burnt electrode: replace. Start each season with a new plug. Wrong gap or loose connection also cause starting problems.
- fix: Inspect, replace, gap to manufacturer spec, wires secure.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- safety/tip: Never run without an air filter, even briefly.
- step (paper): tap on solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light passes else replace; reinstall cover.
- step (foam): replace if dark spots or dry and brittle; wash with mild dish soap and water; rinse; lay flat to dry (sun speeds); lightly saturate with foam filter oil (not dripping); reinstall.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Often a narrow inline filter in the line off the tank and hard to see; check the bottom of the fuel tank (flashlight into tank). Replace: drain tank, remove fuel line from the bottom of the tank, remove old filter, insert new filter in the line, reattach.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove line, carb cleaner, compressed air, repeat; replace if clog can't be removed.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Ethanol/additives form gummy substances that clog small parts. Clean yourself if mechanical, or repair shop; may replace if very bad.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Kills engine when operator releases the bail lever (safety lever); faulty switch may not recognize the engaged lever. Check manual for other safety switches on your model.
- confirm: Temporarily bypass to identify. safety: never operate without safety switch installed. Replace bad switch.

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- fact: Timing off: spark must release once piston compresses the fuel/air mix correctly. Key shears when the blade hits something solid (crankshaft stops suddenly but flywheel keeps going) or from running a loose blade.
- fix: Inspect the flywheel key and replace a broken one.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fix: Restring the rope if no longer wrapped; may have broken pulley, spring, or clips. Price parts vs whole recoil assembly; replacing the assembly may be more cost effective.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Electric start: secure cables and terminals; clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda).

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fix: Test with a multimeter; replace a bad Troy-Bilt battery.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: Press switch and nothing happens -> check with multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on turning key; wire on the solenoid gets hot and smokes or melts. Replace solenoid/starter if bad.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse near the battery; replace a blown fuse.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Troy-Bilt won't start unless starting procedure is followed (engage bail lever, adjust choke); varies by model; refer to operating manual.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- fact: Fuel shut-off valve is often closed during storage/transport.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: see airflow test; choke on for cold start; stuck choke lubricate.
