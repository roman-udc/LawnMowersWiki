## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "John Deere lawn mower is smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket, wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Smoke when oil burns due to low oil, failed engine gasket, piston ring or valve train problem; an air restriction making the engine run rich also contributes.
- fact: Black smoke = running rich (more fuel than air); check the air filter, then other airflow restrictions including a stuck choke. Blue or white = excess oil burning; check oil level first, then piston rings, valve train, gasket.
- confirm: Remove spark plug(s) (3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on engine) and check the tip for oil; indicates internal problem (ring or valve train), needs mechanic.
- tip: Check oil level and air filter before each mowing.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Never run without a filter. Plugged filter makes the engine run rich (black smoke from the muffler) and may pull air and oil from the crankcase, which burns. Replace annually; check and clean through the season, more often in dry dusty conditions.
- step 1 (paper): Remove the filter cover from the housing and gently pull the filter out without knocking dirt into the intake.
- step 2: Wipe housing with a clean dry rag.
- step 3: Tap the filter on a solid surface.
- step 4: Hold to light; reuse if light shows through; else replace.
- mistake: Never blow out a paper filter with an air compressor.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Most John Deere engines have a dipstick; level should be at full between the marks. Check each time you mow, with the mower off and engine cool.
- fact: Low oil: friction heat melts parts and burns oil. An oil change usually won't fix it once it smokes and quits; internal damage; send to a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Overfill raises crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and up into the intake through the valve train; burns and smokes.
- fix: Drain until between the dipstick marks. Options: drain plug/drain valve cap (remove and replace quickly to let only a little out); oil filter (author text says "remove the air filter" here, apparently meaning the oil filter; drain a little, reattach); turkey baster (don't reuse for cooking); oil evacuator. Recheck with dipstick; replace the air filter if oil got on it.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Engine teardown needed; scoring in the cylinder wall lets oil into the combustion chamber.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: Overheated valve can burn; red hot muffler can indicate a burnt valve or timing problem; confirm only with cylinder head removal and leak-down test by a mechanic, who cuts valve and seat at certain angles.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: Oil leaks from a bad engine gasket onto the hot muffler and burns; may be easy to find and replace but harder depending on location.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: A stuck choke is named as another airflow restriction that causes running rich and black smoke.
