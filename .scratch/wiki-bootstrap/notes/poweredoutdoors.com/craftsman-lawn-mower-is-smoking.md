## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "big cloud of smoke when I start my Craftsman"
- alias: "mower started smoking once the engine got hot"
- alias: "blowing white / blue / black smoke"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket
- fact: Source order, simplest to hardest: plugged air filter, too much or too little oil, piston ring problem, valve train problem, bad engine gasket.
- fact: Black or gray smoke is most likely the engine running too rich (more fuel burned than air); check the air filter first, replace if clogged and not cleanable, then check for another airflow restriction.
- fact: Blue or white smoke is usually the engine burning excess oil; check oil level first, then piston rings, valve train, engine gasket.
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety rules; take care around a hot engine when removing the air filter.
- pro: Piston ring and valve train diagnosis is harder; take it to a certified small engine mechanic if not mechanically inclined.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: A clogged filter makes the engine draw air from the crankcase or another internal part, which often pulls in crankcase oil; burning that oil makes smoke. Plugged air filter is one of the least expensive repairs.
- fix: step 1: remove the air filter cover (above the carburetor; knobs, a screw, or clips depending on engine).
- fix: step 2: pull the filter from the housing (sometimes at the side of the engine next to the carburetor); do not let dirt fall into the intake pipe.
- fix: step 3: inspect for clogging from dirt and grass; replace, or knock loose dirt out if not in bad condition.
- fix: step 4: hold the filter to the light and confirm light shows through every part; if blocked, replace.
- fix: step 5: replace the cover.
- tip: Have engine information to buy the correct filter.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Too much oil raises crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and possibly into the air intake via the valve train; oil in the cylinder burns off when hot and smokes.
- fix: Remove excess oil to Craftsman's recommended level: drain a little, or extract oil. An inexpensive turkey baster works; or use the drain plug, or the oil filter if the model has one.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- tip: Turkey baster works; or drain plug; or oil filter on models that have one. Bring level to Craftsman's recommended level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Low oil reduces lubrication; engine runs very hot, can melt internal components and burn oil, causing smoke. Adding oil usually comes too late by this point.
- tip: Check engine oil level before each mowing to verify level and catch leaks.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- confirm: Remove the spark plug (3/4" or 5/8" socket) and look for excessive oil buildup; this signals a larger internal problem, possibly ring problem and scored cylinder wall that lets oil into the combustion chamber.
- fix: May require replacing the engine in the Craftsman mower.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- confirm: Only by removing the cylinder head and doing a leak-down test, by a trained small engine mechanic.
- fact: Overheating can burn a valve (edges fall apart). A muffler glowing red hot is another sign of a burnt valve or timing problem.
- fix: Replace the valve and grind the seat; valve and seat must be cut at certain angles.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Source says a bad engine gasket can let oil leak into the muffler where it burns; can be hard to identify depending on gasket location. (Source says "engine gasket", not specifically head gasket.)

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- tip: Check oil level before each mow; regular engine oil changes are essential; skipping them causes significant engine damage (Source links elsewhere).
