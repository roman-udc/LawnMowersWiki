## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Husqvarna mower loses power and bogs down"
- fact: loses power when engine runs hot or lacks airflow/fuel flow; or when under load (plugged deck, ground speed too fast).
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- tip: also "blocked air passageways": remove grass/dirt/debris under engine shroud; heat shield securely in place.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: replace annually, clean several times per season; more often in very dusty conditions.
- step: paper filter: remove cover (clips, knobs or wing nuts); remove filter without dropping dirt in intake; wipe housing with dry rag; tap filter on hard surface; hold to light, reuse if light shines through; replace if not, damaged or oily; reinstall.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down as soon as 30 days; ethanol/moisture leave gummy deposits that restrict flow.
- fix: drain tank, fill with fresh gas.
- brand: Husqvarna gas mowers need unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; today's Husqvarna use 4-cycle with straight gasoline, but older Husqvarna push mowers may be 2-cycle needing gas/oil mix.
- tip: Sea Foam when more gas than can be used in 30 days; author adds to every tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace annually; shut off fuel via shutoff valve or fuel pinch-off pliers; remove clamps, pull filter, install new; arrow toward carburetor, away from tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove end of a hose section into container, restore flow and check.
- fix: remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air; replace with same length/diameter line if clog remains or line dry/cracked.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake, start; if it won't stay running, disassemble and clean.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: intermittent spark; carbon, dirt, oil on tip.
- fix: replace; or if only dirty and not very dark, wire brush and reuse; gap per operator's manual.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low oil from underfilling at change, leak, or burning oil from wrong viscosity. Lack of lubrication causes friction, heat, overheating, smoke from burning oil, melting parts; can mean engine replacement.
- confirm: check oil before each use: pull dipstick, wipe, reinsert, pull, should be at full; add fresh oil until full, don't overfill.
- pro: if problems continue after refilling, have a small engine mechanic diagnose (internal damage).

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: bluish-white smoke as oil burns in cylinder; thick smoke can plug air filter (check air filter and spark plug).
- fact: pressure builds, oil pushed into cylinder through valve train. Continued running can cause seal damage, hydrolock and bent piston rod.
- fix: remove a little oil by loosening and quickly tightening drain plug, or draw from oil filter/fill area with turkey baster or oil evacuator.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: air circulates around block and head; fins plugged with grass and mud inhibit airflow; replace damaged fins; also clean around engine shroud and air intake.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: slow down in thick, wet or tall grass and on inclines.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: wet grass makes engine work harder, clumps and sticks under deck; mow dry. For tall grass double or triple cut: highest deck setting first, then lower.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck makes engine work harder turning blades; scrape regularly.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify plugged-deck problem, need more engine power.
