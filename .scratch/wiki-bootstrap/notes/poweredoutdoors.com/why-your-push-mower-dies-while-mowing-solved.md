## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "push mower starts then dies"
- alias: "push mower dies while mowing"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, dirty-carburetor, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dies from lack of fuel, air or spark (plugged filters, bad fuel, dirty carb, clogged fuel system, bad plug, faulty coil); low/high oil and plugged deck can also kill it.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: add fresh fuel (author notes it as the obvious one).

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down after about 30 days; ethanol plus moisture leaves a gummy substance when it evaporates.
- tip: use fuel within 30 days, buy from busy stations, don't leave gas outdoors (attracts moisture), store in a dry place, buy only 30 days' worth or add stabilizer (Sea Foam Motor Treatment).
- fix: drain the tank into a container for proper disposal; add fresh fuel with a stabilizer/cleaner additive.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: push mowers use unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol (Regular or E10); read the label. Most current push mowers are 4-stroke (4-cycle) using unleaded; older ones may be 2-stroke needing a gasoline/oil mix per owner's manual ratio. Wrong fuel can mean replacing the mower.
- fix: drain and refill with fresh fuel treated with stabilizer and cleaner.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: gum and crusty buildup from old/ethanol fuel; runs sluggish and shuts down when air/fuel are insufficient.
- fix: clean; rebuild or replace if excessively dirty or broken parts.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most push mowers have no fuel pump; needed when the carburetor sits higher than the tank. Leaking or cracked = bad.
- confirm: check fuel reaches the inlet port; remove the hose from the carburetor, start, look for consistent or pulsating flow; none means pump is most likely the problem. Replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: some push mowers have an inline filter; if not found, probably a filter at the bottom of the fuel tank.
- step (inline) 1: turn off fuel with the shut-off valve (if equipped) or pinch the hose.
- step (inline) 2: remove the hose clamps holding the filter.
- step (inline) 3: remove the filter.
- step (inline) 4: install a new filter between the fuel lines and secure with clamps.
- step (inline) 5: turn the fuel on.
- step (tank-bottom) 1: drain the tank (fuel siphon pump or remove the carburetor fuel line into a container).
- step (tank-bottom) 2: remove the clamp and fuel hose from the tank bottom.
- step (tank-bottom) 3: remove the filter.
- step (tank-bottom) 4: insert new filter, reinstall hose and clamp.
- step (tank-bottom) 5: refill with fresh fuel.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: inline and tank-bottom steps as above.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: use fuel clamps to start and stop fuel to find the restriction.
- fix: remove line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air; replace if it can't be cleared or is dry and cracked (buy online or at a hardware store).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: remove the cap and see if it runs well; reinstall while running; if it dies with the cap on and restarts with the cap removed, the cap is bad.
- fix: try cleaning the vent; else buy a new cap.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: no air and the mower dies; neglect can cause significant engine damage.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step (paper) 1-3: remove cover and filter; wipe loose dirt from housing and cover with a dry cloth without letting dirt fall in the intake.
- step (paper) 4: tap on a solid surface.
- step (paper) 5: hold to light; reuse if good light passes; else replace.
- step (paper) 6: install and reattach cover.
- step (foam) 1-3: remove cover and filter; clean housing/cover.
- step (foam) 4: inspect; very dark, brittle or torn = replace.
- step (foam) 5: wash in water and mild detergent; rinse until clear; squeeze out water, don't wring (can tear).
- step (foam) 6: dry.
- step (foam) 7: apply clean engine oil to cover the filter completely and squeeze out excess (the Source elsewhere says foam filter oil, lightly coat).
- step (foam) 8: install and reattach cover.
- mistake: NEVER apply oil to a foam pre-filter (one used together with a paper element); it can damage the paper filter.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: plug may start the mower but not keep it running; dirty plug with carbon buildup fouls out causing intermittent running.
- fix: try cleaning; an excessively dark or damaged plug must be replaced. Change plug annually at service. Make sure plug wires are secure and plug is gapped to the engine manufacturer's spec.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: mower dies once it gets hot (problems/engine-dies-when-hot).
- fact: coil windings separate and short; plug can't get needed voltage.
- confirm: ohm meter, check for a break in continuity; replace the coil if found.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: too much or too little oil can cause the engine to die, overheat and be damaged, even needing engine replacement.
- fix: change oil per manufacturer; fill to the correct levels. If it won't run after correcting the level, a small engine technician must test the engine. Depending on age and cost it may be best to replace the mower; labor rates can cost between $45 and $100 per hour depending on area.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: see above.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: plugged deck makes the engine work harder; dull blades add draw; engine can overheat and die under load. Scrape the deck and sharpen the blade.

## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- fact: bad ignition coil can cause the mower to die once hot; overheating from a clogged deck/dull blade also kills it.
