## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "John Deere engine smells hot"
- alias: "John Deere mower overheats"
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: stop the mower and troubleshoot when the engine smells hot to avoid damage.
- safety: stop mower, remove key, wait for engine to cool, remove spark plug boots before repairs; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: gas small engines (Kohler, Briggs & Stratton, Kawasaki) on JD riding mowers, lawn tractors and zero-turns use air-cooled engine oil with a high concentration of zinc (zinc as a cooling agent); car (liquid-cooled) motor oil has little zinc.
- fact: most small-engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; may need 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures and 5W-30 in cold. Source points to the Kawasaki viscosity/temperature chart.
- fix: drain wrong oil and add correct air-cooled engine oil.
- symptom (low oil): friction builds heat; oil thickens when run low, causing significant damage. Check oil before each use.
- confirm (if consistently low): oil leak (find source, replace gaskets, check filter seal); burning oil (plugged air filter makes oil burn: clean/replace filter); using oil (overheating, or valve/ring problem: repair center diagnosis).
- fix: correct a slightly low level; if the engine smells hot do a full oil change (heated oil may have thickened). If trouble continues there may be extensive damage; take it to a John Deere servicing dealer or small engine dealer for tests.
- pro: JD dealer / small engine dealer if damage suspected.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: the fins and a fan on top of the engine pull cool air in, blow it down through the hot parts and out the bottom; plugged or damaged fins fail to dissipate heat.
- fix: remove the engine cover, clean the fins, replace damaged fins, remove dirt from the cover and around the engine; make it annual maintenance.
- mistake: don't wash the engine/spray water into the engine area; it pushes dirt and water in.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: engine guard (shield) must be in place so cool air circulates around the engine; without it the cool air exits the area.
- fix: check position, tighten or replace mounting screws, replace a missing or damaged guard.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter starves the engine of air; it runs hot.
- confirm: flashlight through the paper element; good light and good condition = knock dirt out by tapping on a solid surface and reinstall; dull/no light, damaged or dark = replace.
- fact: some JD engines have an inner air filter (narrow filter inside a canister paper filter) or a foam pre-cleaner wrapped around the paper filter.
- tip: replace annually; clean several times per season; more often in dust or heavy use. Author: don't try to stretch air filter life; cheaper than engine repair.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck makes the engine work harder to turn blades. Scrape with deck scraper, wire brush or putty knife; check often. Deck coating sprays (e.g. DuPont non-stick dry film lubricant) help reduce but don't eliminate buildup. Avoid cutting wet grass and run full throttle.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify plugged-deck strain. Homeowner blades: sharpen twice a season or about every 25 hours.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: long, thick or wet grass loads the engine.
- fix: slower ground speed; avoid wet grass; double- or triple-cut long grass (highest deck setting first, then lower a little for a second cut); full throttle; reduce speed.

## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- fact: let the engine cool after mowing before putting the mower in a garage/storage unit; best placed outdoors; with an under-hood engine lift the hood to expose it; let it run under no load so normal cooling begins. Source links to "Lawn Mower Engine Cooling Times Explained".

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: zinc-rich air-cooled engine oil; SAE30 / 10W-30 typical; 20W-50 hot; 5W-30 cold.
