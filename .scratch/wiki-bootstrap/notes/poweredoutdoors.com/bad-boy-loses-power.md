## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Bad Boy zero turn loses power and bogs down"
- alias: "mower loses power when engaging the blades"
- alias: "engine feels sluggish"
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Loss of power comes from old fuel causing restrictions, an overworked/non-performing engine, or insufficient air reaching the engine.
- safety: Follow the operator's manual safety precautions. Remove the ignition key and spark plug boots before any repair.
- safety: Consult a professional if you lack the skills, knowledge or condition to do the repair safely.
- pro: If the engine still has problems after oil is topped up, running low on oil may have caused internal damage needing an experienced small engine mechanic.
- tip: Check engine oil before each mowing to catch leaks/engine problems early.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: engine-loses-power (Bad Boy zero turn).
- fact: Most gas sold today includes ethanol, which attracts moisture from the air into the fuel system. The water/ethanol mix is corrosive to mower parts and leaves varnish that restricts fuel and causes loss of power.
- tip: Only buy the amount of fuel you can use within 30 days before it breaks down. Add a fuel additive to keep gas stable longer and reduce moisture in the fuel system.
- fix: Drain bad fuel into a container for recycling; refill with fresh fuel.
- brand: Bad Boy gas mowers use unleaded gasoline with minimum 87 octane and maximum 10% ethanol. Do not use higher-ethanol fuel.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: engine-loses-power.
- fact: Carburetor regulates how much fuel mixes with air; residue from running gas clogs fuel passageways so the mix is wrong.
- confirm: First confirm fuel is reaching the carburetor. Then remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake. Start the engine; if it runs strong and then begins to lose power or die, clean the carburetor.
- fix: Clean a carburetor that is not letting fuel reach the cylinder.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: engine-loses-power; sluggish running.
- fact: Dirt and residue from old fuel clog the fuel filter, restricting flow.
- fix: Replace a clogged inline fuel filter with a new one.
- tip: Replace the filter annually.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: engine-loses-power.
- confirm: Start/stop fuel flow with the fuel shut-off valve on the bottom of the fuel tank or by crimping the fuel line. Stop flow, remove the end of one line section furthest from the tank, place in a container, restart flow to check for blockage. Continue with other sections as needed.
- fix: Remove the clogged section, spray carburetor cleaner into it to loosen clogs, then blow compressed air through. If the blockage will not clear or the line is dry and cracked, replace the line. Also check the fuel filter and replace it if plugged.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: engine-loses-power (not enough air).
- fact: Filter keeps dirt out of the intake; must be cleaned/replaced regularly.
- tip: Replace annually and clean several times through the mowing season. Commercial or heavier-than-homeowner use needs cleaning/replacement more often.
- fix: Inspect and clean; replace if extremely dirty or damaged.
- step 1: Remove the air filter from the housing.
- step 2: Wipe remaining dirt out of the housing with a dry cloth, without letting dirt fall into the air intake.
- step 3: Tap the filter on a solid surface to knock dirt loose.
- step 4: Hold the filter up to a light source.
- step 5: Reuse if light shows through the paper element and it is undamaged and not extremely dirty; otherwise replace.
- fact: Thick smoke from overfilled oil can plug the air filter.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Bad Boy paper element procedure: remove from housing; wipe housing with dry cloth without letting dirt into intake; tap on solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light passes through and undamaged, else replace.
- tip: Replace annually, clean several times per season; more often for commercial/heavy use.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: intermittent spark, loss of power.
- confirm: Inspect for carbon buildup, wear or damage.
- fix: Replace with a new plug (author's recommendation). Optionally clean with a wire brush and reuse if in good condition and not very dark.
- fact: Make sure electrode gap meets engine manufacturer spec and plug wires are securely attached.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: engine-loses-power.
- fact: Low crankcase oil means increased friction, which builds heat, makes the engine extremely hot and lose power. If not caught quickly, heat can melt internal engine parts, leading to a big repair bill or engine replacement.
- fix: Add oil to the manufacturer's recommended level.
- tip: Check oil before each mowing. Always run the correct amount; wrong amounts cause running and overheating problems.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: engine-loses-power; engine smokes with bluish-white smoke (problems/mower-is-smoking).
- fact: Too much oil raises pressure and oil can be pushed into the cylinder through the valve train, burning as bluish-white smoke.
- mistake: Continuing to run with too much oil can cause seal damage, hydro lock, and a bent piston rod.
- fix: Remove a little oil. Then check the air filter (smoke can plug it) and spark plug; clean or replace as needed.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: engine-loses-power.
- fact: Fins circulate air around the engine; they can plug with mud and grass clippings, reducing airflow. Also debris collecting around the engine/under the shroud hurts cooling.
- fix: Remove all debris collected under the engine shroud; clean debris around the cooling fins and replace any broken fins.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- symptom: engine-loses-power, especially on blade engagement.
- fix: Slow ground speed to suit conditions: thick lawns and inclines make the engine work harder.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- symptom: engine-loses-power.
- fix: Mow when dry. Mow regularly. For tall grass, set deck to highest setting for the first cut, then lower for the next cut. Slow ground speed if you must mow such grass.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: engine loses power while cutting; also a bad cut.
- fact: Clippings/dirt plugging the deck force the engine to work hard turning blades through buildup.
- fix: Scrape the deck regularly. Avoid mowing wet conditions.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: dull blades magnify the power loss from a clogged deck.
- fix: Check blades; sharpen or replace as needed.
