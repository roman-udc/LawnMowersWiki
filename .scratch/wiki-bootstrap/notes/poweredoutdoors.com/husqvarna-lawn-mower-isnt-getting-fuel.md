## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- causes-named: plugged-fuel-cap-vent, dirty-carburetor, old-fuel
- fact: If mower runs fine with cap off but shuts off after a while with cap on, cap vent is probably plugged.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas breaks down in long storage; ethanol attracts moisture; ethanol plus moisture makes gummy deposits that clog fuel system. Fuel can begin breaking down as quickly as 30 days after purchase.
- fact: Use unleaded gasoline minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Avoid E15 and E85 (15% and 85% ethanol). Lower ethanol better.
- tip: Fuel additive in every tank; author prefers Sea Foam Motor Treatment (petroleum-based, stabilizes and cleans fuel system); source says Sea Foam can stabilize fuel up to two years.
- fix: Remove old fuel with a fuel siphon into approved container for later recycling; refill with fresh fuel plus additive.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Change annually during full maintenance. Install with side arrow pointing in direction of flow: away from the tank and toward the carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Use fuel shut-off valve (or crimp the line) to stop flow. Shut off flow, remove the end of a line section furthest from tank, put end in a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump), open flow. No fuel into container = plugged.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove clogged section, spray carburetor cleaner into line, follow with compressed air. If clog won't clear or line is dry and cracked, replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Fuel sitting in pump degrades it, or normal wear; can't create pressure to push fuel to carburetor.
- confirm: First confirm fuel reaches the pump's inlet port. Then remove fuel line from carburetor, place in a container, start engine; watch for steady or pulsating flow from pump. No constant flow = bad pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Gummy substances and crusty deposits from fuel.
- confirm: Remove air filter from housing, spray carburetor cleaner into air intake, start engine; if it starts and runs a bit then shuts down, clean the carburetor.
- fix: Clean yourself (if mechanical), replace, or have repair shop clean it.
- tip: For replacement carburetor, need engine model and spec number (not mower model/serial). Engines from Briggs and Stratton, Kawasaki, Honda, Kohler etc.; model and spec usually on engine cover; some use only a QR code scanned with a phone.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plugged vent makes tank act like a vacuum. Test: run mower with and without cap. Try cleaning; else buy new Husqvarna cap.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: arrow toward carburetor; replace annually.
