## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "John Deere riding mower won't move forward or reverse"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, missing-axle-key, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: Guide covers John Deere lawn tractors (riding mowers) with hydrostatic transmissions; the zero-turn version is a separate guide.
- safety: Work on a flat level surface, wait for the transmission to cool, remove spark plug boot(s) before repairs; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- symptom: Drive release/bypass lever still in bypass position after pushing the mower by hand.
- fact: Never tow a hydrostatic riding mower; only push by hand, after disengaging the transmission with the bypass control lever.
- fix: Return the lever to the operating position to close the valve so the pedals work. On some models the bypass valve closes automatically when started and the pedals are used; make sure it isn't stuck open.

## page: causes/missing-axle-key
- fact: Axle key is a small narrow bar about 1" to 2" long. Can fall out after a tire change. Required for the wheel to move.
- fix: Check it's installed if the model uses one.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fix: Check the belt hasn't fallen off and sits tight around the pulleys; replace if worn, cracked or broken.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: Tensioner pulley (most likely hard plastic with a center bearing) can break or its bearing fail.
- fix: Replace; keep the tensioner arm greased so it moves and doesn't seize.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: Idler spring tensions the drive belt. Replace if stretched, broken or missing.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- symptom: Old or low hydraulic oil: mower won't move or seems very weak.
- brand: Some riding mowers, including John Deere 100, 200 and x300 series, have sealed, non-serviceable transmissions; contact a John Deere dealer.
- brand: John Deere recommends replacing hydraulic/transaxle oil and filters after the initial 50-hour break-in and every 200 hours after, for most current lawn tractors including x500 and x700 series; check the manual for your model.
- brand: Use John Deere low-viscosity HY-GARD hydraulic/transmission oil. Other oils may damage the transmission or make it move slowly; never mix with HY-GARD (may be incompatible).
- fix: Add oil to the full level with cool oil; check for leaks when low.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: John Deere interval: after 50-hour break-in, then every 200 hours (oil and filters), x500/x700; HY-GARD only. Sealed units (100, 200, x300) not serviceable.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- symptom: Wrong, old or low fluid can't lubricate, causing friction and overheating of the oil.
- pro: If extremely hot after an oil change, see a John Deere dealer; may be an oil cooler or transaxle fan problem or transmission damage.
