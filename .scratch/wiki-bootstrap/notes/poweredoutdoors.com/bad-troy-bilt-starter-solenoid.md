## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: Click or hum when the ignition key is turned and mower doesn't start (problems/engine-wont-crank). Another sign: a wire gets hot and smokes or melts.
- fact: A solenoid is an electromagnet switch that engages the starter motor. Often mounted on the starter motor but not required to be; some are mounted on the frame closer to the battery. Found on every Troy-Bilt electric-start engine.
- fact: Inside is a spring and a copper plate. It fails when the spring gets weak or the copper plate corrodes; also from a weak starter, bad battery, or bad ground.
- fact: Identify it: round or square, 3 or 4 posts, some attach at the top of the starter. The positive (+) battery wire attaches to one side; follow the positive cable from the battery to find it.
- fact: Wiring schematics for 3- and 4-post solenoids differ between mowers; some include lights and 12-volt ports.
- confirm: Solenoid bypassed and starter turns the engine = solenoid likely bad.
- mistake: Replacing the solenoid before checking for loose or corroded wires or a bad ground, which can keep the solenoid from getting the power it needs.
- fix: See procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver (long), continuity light, wrenches (check loose wires), needle nose pliers (if screwdriver doesn't work), battery charger (optional)
- step 1: Confirm battery has at least 12.3 volts on a voltmeter.
- step 2: Set the parking brake, make sure mower is in neutral, turn the key to ON.
- step 3: Bypass: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the two cables (cable from the battery and cable to the starter). A spark is common. If the engine turns over with the solenoid bypassed, the solenoid is likely bad. Needle nose pliers can be used instead of the screwdriver.
- step 4: Before replacing, check for loose or corroded wires or a bad ground.
- step 5: Bench test for starter-mounted solenoids: remove starter from engine; attach battery charger negative (-) clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid. Quick bench test with starter out.
- safety: Spark when bypassing is normal; always wear safety gear. Follow the Troy-Bilt operator's manual.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Battery should read at least 12.3 volts before solenoid diagnosis.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: Loose or corroded wires keep the solenoid from getting required power; check before replacing it.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: A bad ground can cause the solenoid to fail / keep it from receiving power; check before replacing the solenoid.
