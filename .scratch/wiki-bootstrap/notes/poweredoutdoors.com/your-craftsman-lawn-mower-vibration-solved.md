## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Craftsman mower vibrating so badly it seems it will fall apart"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: From loose hardware, bad bearings in pulleys and spindle housings, debris lodged in the mower, worn parts, and incorrect or unbalanced blades. Routine service helps.
- safety: Park on level surface, set the parking brake, remove key, disconnect spark plug wire(s), wear gloves for blades.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- fact: Mounting bolts under the engine (motor mounts) absorb engine vibration. Check secure, not broken or worn; replace as needed. On a push mower, check them if excessive vibration after engaging the blade.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: Riding/zero-turn Craftsman has an electric or manual engagement clutch (not push mowers). Vibration when a ball bearing fails in an electric clutch, or when linkage, bushings fail in a manual engagement clutch (the manual type works with belts and pulleys). Push mower: lever engages the blade - check for worn or loose components.

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- fact: Wrong blades or wrong installation vibrate. Need correct size with correct center hole.
- tip: Aftermarket blades: some listing the right center hole and length still don't fit; verify by physically matching against an OEM blade.
- confirm: Check hardware against the parts diagram/owner's manual; must be installed in the order shown. A blade not secured correctly moves and shakes the mower.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Unbalanced blade (one side heavier) spins uneven; causes vibration and spindle bearing damage. Dirt sucked into the deck wears one side more; sharpening can remove more metal from one side.
- fix: Balance with a blade balancer after sharpening and before installing.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- fact: Wire, string, long weeds wrap around the blade shaft (spindle) and cause vibration.
- fix: Inspect blade shafts, remove; check shafts and spindle housings for damage.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Bad bearings let the blade move, more pronounced at speed. Gloves on, grab each side of the blade and rock up and down; check for movement and knocking sound.
- fix: Remove the spindle housing and check the bearing; some models allow bearing replacement, some only the complete spindle housing assembly.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fix: Replace a glazed, stretched, cracked or shredded belt. Belt not sitting securely on pulleys vibrates. Source references its article on replacing a Craftsman deck belt.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Pulley not level and parallel to the deck vibrates as the belt runs. Bad pulley bearing keeps the pulley from sitting securely.
- confirm: Rotate each pulley by hand; restriction or bearing noise means replace.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Loose or worn idler bracket and spring make the mower shake; replace. Also check bearing and bushing in the engagement linkage and replace if necessary.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: If belt covers (if used) aren't removed and the deck top cleaned often, debris collects under pulleys so they don't sit flat/parallel, vibrating the deck. Even tiny items can cause shaking; look over every part. Clean after each mowing.
