## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: 4-cycle (the Source says "4-stage") push mower: unleaded gasoline, octane 87 or higher, ethanol 10% or less. Separate fill ports for gas and oil; never use an oil/gas mix in a 4-cycle engine.
- fact: 2-cycle push mowers have a single fill port for an oil and gas mix; common ratios 50:1, 40:1, 32:1; follow the operator's manual.
- fact: Most push mowers use unleaded but some need other gas; check the manufacturer's guide. For any major-brand current 4-cycle push mower, traditional unleaded is the best choice. Best gas overall: ethanol-free.
- fact: Ethanol (corn-based) attracts moisture; the water/ethanol solution causes gumming and corrosion in the fuel system, restricts fuel and fails components; over time it separates and sits at the tank bottom; running it can make the engine run very hot and cause significant damage.
- fact: Ethanol-free sources: pump at fuel stations (recreation fuel REC-90 at select stations, up to $3.00 per gallon more than regular; station list at puregas.org for US and Canada); canisters of 4-cycle fuel at hardware stores or online (most expensive but convenient, good for small push mowers and handhelds).
- tip: Buy from larger well-known stations for better quality. More expensive unleaded is not always better.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Gas can start to break down in as little as 30 days; buy only what you use in 30 days.
- tip: Best way to store gas is with a stabilizer, added while fuel is fresh (cannot reverse degradation). Author likes Sea Foam (reduces moisture buildup, stabilizes, cleans fuel system).
- tip: Use a can designed for unleaded gas (other plastic containers can break down); store in a cool dry place away from the elements and moisture; rotate stored gas, oldest first.
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety guidance when draining or fueling.
