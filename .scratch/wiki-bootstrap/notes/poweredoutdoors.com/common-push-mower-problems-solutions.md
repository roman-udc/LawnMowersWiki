## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "push mower won't start"
- alias: "won't start after winter storage"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- fact: A push mower that won't start after coming out of storage is often caused by old fuel left over winter, which clogs/damages fuel components. Other reasons: bad fuel cap, failed mechanical components, plugged filters, loose wires, failed electrical components, broken recoil.
- fact: Source is a cause/reason/solution table (no step-by-step); the rows are recorded under each cause below.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: Fuel breaks down over time, becomes less efficient and prone to clogging the fuel system (and engine damage).
- fix: Drain the tank and refill with fresh fuel; use a fuel additive like Sea Foam to stabilize the fuel and help clean the fuel system; use the right fuel.
- fact: Causes starting failure after storage, and engine dying while mowing.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Cap vent clogs so the tank forms a vacuum, restricting fuel flow.
- fix: Clean the cap / clear the vent; replace cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Excessively dirty or broken plug; bad connection; not gapped correctly.
- fix: Replace with new plug, secure connections, gap to manufacturer spec.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Dirt and grass plug the filter and block airflow; can also make engine hot and smoke (burns more fuel than the air needed for combustion).
- fix: Remove and clean the filter; replace if in bad condition.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: A dirty fuel filter restricts fuel flow; replace it.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Bad fuel deposits and dirt build up and clog the lines so engine doesn't get fuel.
- fix: Use carb cleaner and compressed air to clear the clog; replace the line if necessary.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Dirty/clogged from bad fuel and ethanol deposits, preventing fuel flow.
- fix: Clean all components of the carburetor; replace if needed.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fix: Charge the battery; replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Make sure cables are tight; clean corrosion from terminals.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: Safety switches prevent starting in certain situations; a failed switch may stop the mower starting.
- fix: Test and replace a faulty safety switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fix: Mower won't start when ignition switch is bad; test and replace.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Won't start when coil is bad. Also: coil windings can separate and short out when the mower is hot, causing the engine to die while mowing.
- confirm: Test the coil for a break in continuity.
- fix: Replace the ignition coil.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: Pull-start mower with a damaged or broken recoil can't start engine.
- fix: Check whether the recoil can be restrung or broken parts (e.g. pulley) replaced; replace the recoil assembly if needed.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "push mower dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Mower starts then dies from an air or fuel restriction (bad fuel, plugged air or fuel filter, clogged fuel lines, dirty carburetor). Ignition coil can also be the cause when it gets hot and the windings separate.
- fix: Fixes per cause as in the causes sections above (drain/replace fuel with Sea Foam stabilizer; clean/replace filter; carb cleaner and compressed air on lines; clean all carburetor components, replace if needed; test coil for continuity break, replace).

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "push mower is smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, oil-in-air-filter-or-tipped-mower, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train
- fact: Smoking when the engine gets hot from lack of airflow or low oil in crankcase. Valve train and piston ring problems can push oil into the cylinder to burn off. Also check for oil leaks where oil drips onto the muffler.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Excess oil builds pressure in the engine and lets oil into the cylinder through the valve train; smoking.
- fix: Drain a little engine oil until at the manufacturer's recommended level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Low oil increases friction and extreme heat; smoking.
- fix: Take to a small engine repair shop to inspect engine damage; adding oil may be too late.

## page: causes/oil-in-air-filter-or-tipped-mower
- symptom: Tipping the mower or using it on a hillside lets oil get onto the air filter or carburetor.
- fix: Always tip with the carburetor and filter on the high side; clean the carburetor and replace an oil-covered filter.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) must be caught soon.
- pro: Bring to a small engine repair shop for troubleshooting.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "push mower feels like a jackhammer"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, dull-or-bent-blade, bent-crankshaft, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Vibration can be so strong it feels like holding a jackhammer; from debris stuck in mower, worn engagement linkages, bad clutch, loose engine mounting bolts, unbalanced blades or damaged crankshaft.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- symptom: Engine bolts reduce vibration; loose or missing bolts vibrate.
- fix: Secure bolts and replace missing ones.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: Damaged and unbalanced blades send vibration through the mower; blades must be balanced before installing.
- fix: Replace damaged blades and ensure balanced.

## page: causes/bent-crankshaft
- symptom: If blades aren't the cause, check crankshaft; bends when blades hit a solid object.
- fix: Small engine mechanic replaces it; engine must be removed. Note: may be cheaper to buy a new mower.

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- symptom: Linkages and bushings wear and loosen, causing vibration.
- fix: Tighten linkages; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Small debris stuck in deck or mower can cause excessive vibration; don't overlook small stuff.
- fix: Check deck and mower for places items lodge; remove.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "push mower bad or uneven cut"
- causes-named: bad-tire, worn-blade-sail, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, bent-crankshaft, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-cutting-height, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast
- fact: First thing to look at is the wheel: a damaged wheel means the deck won't sit level. Then deck items: bent or worn blade, incorrectly installed blade, bent crankshaft, clogged deck.
- fact: Source table lists causes only: worn mower blade; damaged wheel; engine speed too low; bent blade; incorrect cutting height adjustment; ground speed too fast; blade installed wrong; bent crankshaft; clogged mower deck.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "smell gas but can't find leak (push mower)"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Fuel can evaporate leaving no trace after the mower sits; unless a big puddle, check each fuel component. A leak must be fixed: continual leaking can damage the lawn plus cost of fuel.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: Metal tank can develop a rust hole; poly tank can leak at the seams.
- fix: Replace the tank; sometimes a rust hole can be repaired if no replacement is available.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: Metal and plastic shut-off valves prone to leaking.
- fix: Replace the valve.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: Rubber fuel lines dry and crack, gas leaks.
- fix: Replace the lines.

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: Rubber cracks or isn't attached well to fuel hose and leaks.
- fix: Replace the primer bulb.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: Seal fails, gas splashes out around the cap.
- fix: Replace the gas cap.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom (leak): Buildup from old gas causes blockages or parts failures.
- fix: Clean carburetor, replace damaged parts.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix (leak table row "Old Fuel Filter"): filter can become plugged; replace.
