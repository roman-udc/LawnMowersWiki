## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "I smell gas coming from my Simplicity mower"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Evaporated fuel leaves no wet spot, so leaks are hard to find.
- safety: Gas fumes are harmful; work in a well-ventilated area away from combustibles; wait for the engine to cool; remove spark plug boot(s).

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Carb leaks common. Old gas leaves varnish making internal parts stick. Check bowl gasket, stuck float, stuck needle.
- fact: Bowl gasket looks like a rubber band; dries and goes brittle from engine heat cycling, loses seal.
- tip: Get the correct gasket with the engine model and spec number (different from mower model/serial number).
- step (replace gasket) 1: Wipe around the outside of the carb. 2: Remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl, rag ready for gas. 3: Lower and remove the bowl. 4: Remove old gasket, install new. 5: Keep lubricants off the new gasket. 6: Reinstall bowl, tighten screw.
- symptom (stuck float): leak near the top of the carb by the air intake port; float fails to stop gas entering bowl, overflows. Fix by disassembling; clean carb, rebuild with kit or replace.
- fact (stuck needle): needle works with float; if stuck, rebuild. Temporary: tap carb with rubber mallet or rubber tool handle; works once or twice, then replace the float needle.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Gas degrades plastic housing; leaks at seams. Replace leaking filter; careful, plastic ends go soft and may break off in the line. Replace annually.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Plastic pump leaks at seams for same reason; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Simplicity tanks are high-density polyethylene; seams may fail; replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Valve near bottom of tank or inline in the fuel line; prone to leaking; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Lines dry and crack with age. Follow the line from the tank; check lines, hose connectors, clamps for loose connections or tears from the clamps. Match the diameter of replacement hose. Replace dry/cracked lines before they leak.
- tip: Replace pinch-style clamps with worm gear clamps (less likely to puncture the line).

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- confirm: Check for a wet spot around the cap; absence doesn't rule it out. Carefully rock the mower back and forth (not too aggressively, don't tip) to splash fuel around the cap; wet area outside the tank = replace cap.
