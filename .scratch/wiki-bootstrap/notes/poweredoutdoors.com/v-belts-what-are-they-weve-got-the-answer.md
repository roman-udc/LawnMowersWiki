## page: guides/belt-types
- fact: a v-belt transmits power from the engine to the application (mower blades); shaped like a trapezoid
- fact: three main categories in America: fractional horsepower, classic, narrow
- fact: all v-belts are made of synthetic tensile cords twisted from strong fibrous material, encased in rubber, formed as a closed loop
- fact: category chosen by engine horsepower: fractional: motors under 1 hp; classic: a fraction of 1 hp up to 500 hp; narrow: 1 hp to 1,000 hp
- fact (fractional hp): small, often electric motors (household fans, washing machines, refrigerators). Cross-sections listed in Source: 0.25 in top width x 0.16 high; 0.375 in? (Source prints "3.75 inches top width by .22 inches high", probably a typo); 0.5 x 0.31; 0.66 x 0.375. Designated 2L, 3L, 4L, 5L (number = eighths of an inch of top width, then L)
- mistake: never use a fractional hp belt to replace a classic or narrow belt; it handles only a fraction of 1 hp and breaks under heavier loads
- fact (classic): oldest style (leather originally); used in lawn mowers, classic cars, industrial and agricultural machines; sturdy, tolerates dirty conditions; can't take the heavy loads narrow belts can
- fact (classic designations): letters A through E plus inside length. A .5 x .31 in; B .66 x .41; C .88 x .53; D 1.25 x .75; E 1.5 x .91 (top width x height). W, Y, Z exist but aren't common in America
- fact (narrow): narrower top and bottom width, handles higher horsepower, about three times the power of classic; designation: number for top width, "V", number for outside length, e.g. 5V500; 3V .38 x .32 in; 5V .62 x .54; 8V 1 x .88
- fact: riding mowers typically have two belts, drive belt and deck belt; do different jobs so buy as two different belts even if both classic or narrow
- fact (drive belt): transmits engine power to the wheels (forward/reverse); typically polyester tensile cords
- fact (deck belt): turns the blades at cutting speed; should be sturdier than a drive belt because blades slow in thick/tall grass while the belt wants to keep going, creating high friction; author suggests kevlar cords rather than polyester for a deck belt
- tip: author links an article on finding deck problems if belt won't stay on

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: deck belt sturdier (kevlar cords suggested) than drive belt
