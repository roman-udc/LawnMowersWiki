## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "I smell gas coming from my Exmark but can't find the leak"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: gas smell without a puddle; gas evaporates so leak is hard to find
- fact: fuel degrades plastics over time, causing leaks from the fuel pump, tank and filter
- brand: Exmark; engines are Kohler, Kawasaki or Briggs (Exmark-branded engine on Radius models)

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: carburetor bowl gasket hardens and turns brittle (sits next to the engine, constant heating and cooling); it's between the bowl and carburetor and looks like a rubber band
- symptom: stuck float - leak near air intake port; float no longer regulates fuel into the bowl; fuel overflows
- symptom: stuck float needle - needle works with float to control flow into the bowl; stuck needle needs repair
- fix (gasket): find engine model and spec number; unless an Exmark-branded engine on Radius models, use numbers off the side of the engine cover for a Kohler, Kawasaki or Briggs engine; step 1 shut off fuel at the shut-off valve under the tank (or clamp fuel line); step 2 remove screw and carburetor bowl, rag to catch fuel; step 3 remove old gasket, put new on bowl; step 4 reinstall bowl, tighten screw; step 5 wipe carburetor, turn on fuel, check leaks
- fix (float/needle): if still leaking check float and needle; disassemble carburetor to find cause; may be cleaned or need rebuilding; or have small engine mechanic rebuild
- tip: tapping the carburetor gently with a rubber hammer handle or mallet frees the needle only temporarily

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: gummy and crusty buildup keeps parts from working correctly, fuel leaks when parts stick or gasket fails
- fact: carburetor is where fuel is stored after leaving the tank

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: stuck float or needle - take the carburetor apart, clean, rebuild as needed (article links own carb cleaning steps)

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: plastic inline filter deteriorates, leaks at seams or cracks
- fix: replace; be careful removing filter ends from fuel lines because softened plastic can break off
- fact: fuel type and storage affect the fuel system (ethanol; stabilize)

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: vacuum fuel pump wears; plastic degraded by today's fuel leaks at the seams; can't build pressure to pump fuel to the carburetor
- fix: replace; Exmark dealer or online for Briggs, Kawasaki or Kohler engine

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Exmark tanks are high-density polyethylene; seams can fail and leak
- fix: replace the tank

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: valve on the bottom of the tank starts/stops flow; leaks whether plastic or metal
- fix: replace

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: old lines dry, brittle and crack; leaks at clamps (loose clamps or ones that puncture the hose)
- fix: replace cracked lines; replace pinch-style clamps; author prefers worm gear clamps (less likely to pinch)

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: leak around the cap leaves a wet spot briefly before evaporating; check if everything else is ruled out
- confirm: rock the mower back and forth to splash gas up around the cap and watch for wet areas
- fix: replace the cap when gas shows on the outside of the tank

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: remove filter ends carefully; replacement inline filter online or at hardware store

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- fact: replace cracked lines and consider replacing pinch clamps with worm gear clamps
