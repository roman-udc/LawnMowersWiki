## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "lawn tractor leaking gas / strong gas odor, no wet spot"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Gas evaporates leaving no wet spot; odor is the clue.
- safety: Work in a well-ventilated area with fresh air; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Carburetor is where fuel first sits after the tank; old gas leaves gummy deposits that stick components.
- symptom (bowl gasket): rubber-band-like gasket between bowl and carburetor dries and goes brittle from heat cycling next to the engine; fuel leaks around the bowl.
- fix (gasket): clean outside of carburetor; remove screw at bottom of bowl (rag to catch gas); remove bowl; replace gasket; reinstall bowl and tighten screw.
- symptom (stuck float): leak near the air intake port; float regulates fuel into bowl; stuck float lets fuel overflow. Disassemble carb; clean to unstick, or rebuild/replace.
- symptom (stuck float needle): float OK but needle stuck; rebuild carburetor with a kit from tractor or engine dealership.
- tip: Tapping the carburetor with a rubber mallet or rubber end of a hammer may temporarily free a stuck needle once or twice; replace the needle to fix properly.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Old gas degrades plastic filter, soft or cracked at seams. Replace annually in tractor maintenance. Caution: soft plastic filter ends can break off in the hose.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Check pump seams for leaks; replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Older tractors have metal tanks that rust and develop holes: best fix is replacement; if no replacement available, repair with a product like JB Weld. Newer tractors use high-density polyethylene tanks that can fail and leak at seams: replace when seam leaks or damaged.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Valve at bottom of tank commonly leaks with time; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Lines dry and crack with age; leaks at cracks or punctures from clamps. Replace, ideally before leaking.
- tip: Replace pinch-style clamps (more likely to puncture lines); author prefers worm gear clamps.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Tractor bounces; fuel splashes at cap and leaks if seal bad; may only smell fumes if evaporated.
- confirm: Rock the tractor back and forth to splash fuel up to the cap; watch for wet spot around cap outside.
- fix: Replace cap.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: Do annually.
