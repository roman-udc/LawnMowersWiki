## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Craftsman riding mower won't stay running"
- alias: "mower stops running while mowing; sometimes runs again after sitting"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, bad-battery, faulty-charging-system, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, engine-overheats
- fact: dies when old/bad fuel plugs the fuel filter, clogs lines or makes the carburetor fail; also bad plug, bad battery, faulty charging system, bad fuel cap, plugged air filter, wrong choke, faulty ignition coil.
- safety: follow operator's manual safety guidelines before working.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: fuel sitting long becomes less stable and attracts moisture, corroding the fuel system; ethanol plus moisture leave gummy deposits that clog it.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: old fuel, dirt and debris cause clogged fuel lines, plugged filter, bad pump. Fuel pump is only present if the carburetor sits higher than the fuel tank.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: a fuel pump is found on mowers where the carburetor is higher than the tank.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: sluggish running or won't stay running (problems/engine-starts-then-dies).
- confirm: first confirm fuel flow reaches the carburetor (no restriction elsewhere). Then remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and start. If it runs fine then dies, the carburetor likely needs cleaning and inspection for failed parts.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dirt/clippings plug the filter; engine can't stay running and a dirty filter can cause significant engine damage. Replace annually, clean several times per season.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: paper filter and foam filter procedures.
- step 1 (paper): remove filter from housing.
- step 2 (paper): wipe out dirt in housing without letting it fall into the intake.
- step 3 (paper): tap against a solid surface to knock dirt loose.
- step 4 (paper): hold to light; if light shines through paper element, reuse; otherwise buy a new one.
- step 5 (paper): reinstall filter and housing cover.
- step 1 (foam): remove filter from housing; wipe housing, don't let dirt fall into intake.
- step 2 (foam): inspect; dark spots, tears, or dry and brittle -> replace; otherwise clean.
- step 3 (foam): wash with water and mild dish soap, rinse soap out.
- step 4 (foam): lay flat to dry; sun speeds drying.
- step 5 (foam): when completely dry, coat with foam air filter oil: fully covered but not dripping; wring out or blot excess with paper towel.
- step 6 (foam): reinstall filter and cover.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts airflow for cold start (richer); once warm it must be adjusted to let air mix or mower stops for lack of airflow.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: dirty plug = carbon or oil buildup on tip; damaged = cracked porcelain or burnt electrodes; runs rough or quits.
- fix: replace dirty/damaged plug; gap to engine manufacturer spec; wires securely attached. Best practice: new plugs annually.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: dies when the mower gets hot; coil windings separate and short out when hot, so plug gets no voltage/spark.
- confirm: check for a break in continuity with an ohmmeter.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fix: check cables/terminals are secure and corrosion-free; test battery holds charge; if so, check charging system (article links to a battery draining guide).

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: check after confirming battery holds a charge.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Craftsman cap vents air; without it the tank makes a vacuum blocking fuel to engine; mower runs a bit then shuts down.
- confirm: after it stops, remove fuel cap and start; if it runs fine, suspect the cap vent. Put cap back on and keep running to see if it shuts off again after a while.
- fix: can try cleaning and unclogging the vent but that doesn't always work or only temporarily; author recommends a new Craftsman fuel cap.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, clogged-deck-chute, clogged-air-filter
- fact: overheated Craftsman engine shuts down: low oil level, wrong type of oil, clogged mower deck, plugged air filter.
