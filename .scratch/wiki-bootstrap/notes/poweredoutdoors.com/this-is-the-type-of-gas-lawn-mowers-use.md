## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Lawn mowers use unleaded gasoline, min 87 octane, 10% ethanol or less (sold as regular or E10). 4-stroke (4-cycle) engines use straight gas; 2-stroke (2-cycle) engines need a different fuel (linked push mower gas article).
- fact: Most mowers have a four-stroke engine using unleaded; some need other types; check the manufacturer's guide. For any major brand with a 4-cycle engine, traditional unleaded is best.
- tip: If local gas has over 10% ethanol, buy elsewhere. Buy fuel before mowing and use it quickly; buy from a larger well-known station (higher quality, often fresher). Never let gas sit for weeks or mix with water if stored outdoors; gas left outside in plastic cans can lose potency.
- fact: Ethanol is corn-based, corrosive to the small-engine fuel system, attracts moisture; when gas breaks down the water-ethanol mix sits at the tank bottom; running it can make the mower run extremely hot, overheating and significantly damaging the engine.
- fact: Ethanol-free fuel (author recommends) does not damage the fuel system. Recreation fuel REC-90 at select stations up to $3.00 per gallon more; list at puregas.org (US and Canada). Canisters of 4-cycle fuel at hardware stores or online are the most expensive but convenient for small mowers/handhelds.
- opinion: More expensive unleaded is not always better.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Best way to store gas: add a stabilizer while the fuel is fresh; once degraded, the additive can't reverse it. Author likes Sea Foam (reduces moisture, stabilizes, cleans).
- tip: Use a plastic gas can designed for unleaded gas (other plastic containers break down). Avoid buying more than a 30-day supply. Rotate stored gas, oldest first.
- fact: Basics: get fresh fuel, use a stabilizer if needed, avoid more than 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Over 10% ethanol, old, or water-contaminated gas harms the small engine.
