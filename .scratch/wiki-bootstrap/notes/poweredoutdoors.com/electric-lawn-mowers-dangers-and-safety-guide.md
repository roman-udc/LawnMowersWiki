## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- fact: two electric types: battery powered (built-in pack, charged when not in use, cordless) and corded (plugged into an outlet, typically GFCI; a GFCI adapter can be used if the home has no built-in GFCI).
- fact: electric mowers are dangerous because they are powered units with fast-moving blades, can become unbalanced on inclines, and risk electrocution in wet conditions. Corded mowers add trip hazard and risk of running over the cord exposing wires.
- fact: both types have low overall maintenance, are lightweight; battery type limited by charge level, corded limited by cord (maneuverability); both are NOT recommended for wet lawns.
- safety: read the manual (manufacturers' safety standards differ).
- safety: power cords: don't yank cords from sockets; inspect for damaged, uninsulated portions; use GFCI outlets or an adapter.
- safety: wear goggles, proper shoes and pants (debris injures eyes and lower extremities).
- safety: clear the area of debris before mowing; debris can launch as projectiles, or accumulate and clog the mower causing it to overheat, shut down, or catch fire.
- safety: maintain: inspect for accumulated debris (injury and motor overheating), keep blades sharp, protective guards in working order.
- safety: kids and young adults need a proper walk-through and training before using any mower.
- safety: treat like any electrical appliance: use in dry areas only; inspect cord insulation; know where the cord is at all times.
- safety: appliance must be off and unplugged from any outlet before opening and cleaning it.
- fact: danger table, battery mowers: battery leaking corrosive chemicals if physically damaged; generally heavier than corded so easily tilt and tumble, especially on inclines (mow crosswise on hills, not up and down); electrocution exposure in wet conditions.
- fact: danger table, corded mowers: damaged or run-over cord exposing wires can cause electrocution; trip and fall hazard from tangling in the cord; electrocution exposure in wet conditions.
- fact: Source links to its article "Electric Lawn Mowers And Water".

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- symptom: electric mower overheats, shuts down, and can potentially catch fire.
- fact: cut grass and debris get caught in the air vents, blocking air circulation that cools the motor.
- fix: if it overheats: turn it off immediately; unplug the cord if corded; let it cool; when cool, check air vents for debris; clean manually or with an air compressor; check for damage to the engine/motor before attempting restart; if unsure it is safe, consult a specialist.
- causes-named: clogged-motor-vents

## page: causes/clogged-motor-vents
- fact: debris in vents blocks cooling air; clean vents by hand or with an air compressor; applies to battery and corded mowers.

## page: problems/battery-mower-wont-start
- fact: Source frames as "electric mowers give out": suddenly stutters or shuts down mid-mowing.
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, clogged-motor-vents, bad-motor, bad-charger

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: cordless mowers need the battery fully charged at the start of mowing; if it does not charge properly or can't hold a charge, replace it.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: corded: constant pulling, flexing or yanking can damage the wires inside the power cord; check internal damage with a multimeter; cords are easily replaced.
- fact: both types: loose connections inside the mower; check wires and reconnect loose ones (if battery/cord is not the problem).

## page: causes/bad-motor
- fact: frequent malfunctions despite proper maintenance and care may mean the motor is failing, from wear and tear or reaching its factory limitations.
