## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "lawn mower sputtering but runs"
- alias: "mower sounds like it's running out of gas"
- alias: "mower sputtering white smoke"
- alias: "mower sputtering and blowing black smoke"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, water-in-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, bad-gas-cap-seal, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-safety-switch
- fact: sputtering that still runs is usually not engine damage if addressed right away; ignored it can cause significant damage. Often an insufficient air/fuel mix: clogged air filter, dirty carburetor, bad gas cap, moisture in tank, old fuel; also bad plug, overworking engine, dirty engine.
- fact: FAQ "sounds like running out of gas": fuel restriction most likely (old gas, dirty carburetor, plugged fuel filter); also clogged air filter, fouled plug, overworking engine.
- fact: FAQ white smoke while sputtering: burning oil, from tipping the mower or too much oil in the crankcase.
- fact: FAQ black smoke: engine running rich (higher gas-to-air ratio).
- fact: a bad safety switch can mimic sputtering: if it doesn't sense the operator (e.g. deck running, not sensed in seat) the mower shuts off; each bump may cause cut-outs because the switch isn't making good contact. Check and replace faulty switch.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: filter plugs quickly with kicked-up grass and dirt; a dirty filter can cause extensive engine damage, sputtering and overheating.
- step 1: remove cover from housing (typically one or two thumb screws).
- step 2: remove filter without knocking dirt into intake; wipe housing with dry cloth.
- paper: tap on a solid surface; hold to light; if good light shows through, keep; else replace (also if damaged, very dirty, oil-covered). Do not use an air compressor: forces dirt into the small holes in the paper and clogs them further.
- foam: replace if excessively dirty, dark spots or torn; else wash with mild dish soap, rinse until soap is gone, lay flat to dry (sun speeds it), when dry lightly saturate with clean engine oil, not dripping.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- see causes/clogged-air-filter note above: paper (tap, light test, no compressor) and foam (soap wash, rinse, dry, light oil) steps.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: most 4-cycle mowers use regular gas, octane 87 or higher, no more than 10% ethanol. Gas shelf life 30 days before it breaks down.
- fix: drain old fuel into an approved container. Push mower: tip mower to drain or remove fuel line from carburetor into a container. Riding/zero-turn: easiest with a fuel siphon pump. Mix fresh gas with Sea Foam or STA-BIL to reduce moisture and help clean the fuel system, add to tank, start and let it work through the system.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: octane 87+, max 10% ethanol for 4-cycle mowers; wrong gas or old gas causes sputtering.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- fact: ethanol attracts moisture and eventually separates; ethanol/water mix sinks to the bottom of the tank.
- confirm: shine a flashlight into the tank and look for gas/water separation.
- fix: drain tank into approved container; refill with fresh gasoline with additive (Sea Foam or STA-BIL); run until it no longer sputters or smokes from water.
- tip: prevent: add stabilizer; store mower inside or covered; store all fuel in a dry location (gas indoors); keep fuel cap on securely and seal in good condition.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gas shelf life 30 days; consume within 30 days; stabilizer Sea Foam or STA-BIL reduces moisture; keep cap seal good; store fuel dry.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: bad fuel and ethanol break down into a gummy deposit that clogs filter and lines.
- confirm: use the shutoff valve at the bottom of tank to start/stop flow; if none, clamp the hose; check each section: stop flow, put the end of the section into a container, restore flow and see if fuel runs.
- fix: remove the blocked section; spray carburetor cleaner in; blow compressed air through; reattach. Replace dry or cracked hose before it leaks.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace clogged filter.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carburetor regulates air/fuel mix; dirty -> wrong mix, sputter.
- fix: take apart and clean deposits; small parts (float needle, springs, gasket) that are damaged mean rebuild or replace.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: dirty/bad plug gives intermittent spark; may sputter or quit.
- fix: remove; carbon on tip -> clean with wire brush; excessively dirty, cracked porcelain or damaged electrode -> replace. Must be gapped to manufacturer spec or it may still sputter.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: tank vents through the cap to let air in as gas is used; bad cap forms a vacuum that restricts fuel to the carburetor.
- confirm: loosen the cap; if it runs better/not sputtering, retighten and run to see whether sputtering returns; loosen again to confirm.
- tip: author prefers to replace the cap rather than clean it.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: clogged deck loads the engine (seems like sputtering); dull blades magnify it.
- fix: scrape underside with a deck scraper, putty knife or wire brush.
- tip: avoid mowing wet grass (clumps and sticks to the deck); a deck spray helps reduce sticking (not a miracle product), best with dry grass.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: blades need lots of power; sputters when not at full throttle. Run at full throttle. Most push mowers lack throttle control.
- pro: if the engine seems to lack its old power, take it to a small engine repair shop for testing.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: tall, thick or wet grass load the engine and cause bogging/sputtering; slow down to match conditions.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: small engine uses air to stay cool; keep air intake and cooling fins clean; fins dissipate heat.
- fix: clean around the engine with a small bristle brush; careful with water, no high pressure.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- see problems/engine-runs-rough note (operator presence switch mimics sputtering).

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: regular maintenance (engine oil, filters, safety switches, scraping the deck) prevents many sputter causes.
