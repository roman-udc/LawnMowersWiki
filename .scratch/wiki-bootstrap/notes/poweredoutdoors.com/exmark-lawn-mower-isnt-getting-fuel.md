## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Exmark isn't getting fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Engine needs air, fuel and spark; with spark and air but not enough fuel the mower runs sluggish or not at all.
- safety: Follow Exmark operator's manual including removing the spark plug wire.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Fuel-starved mower may run sluggish.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can start breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Most pump gas contains ethanol, which attracts moisture, leaving varnish and gummy deposits that restrict flow; moisture can also prematurely corrode fuel system components.
- fix: Siphon the tank into a container; refill with fresh fuel plus a stabilizer (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment, which also cleans the fuel system).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Replace annually, or sooner for commercial use. Clogs from dirt and debris from the tank.
- fix: Replace; the plastic inline filter has an arrow that must point in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Varnish left by old fuel makes deposits that restrict flow.
- confirm: Check each section of the line; use the fuel shut-off valve or fuel pinch-off pliers to stop/start flow while checking flow through each line.
- fix: Remove the clogged section; spray carburetor cleaner into the hose; blow out with compressed air; repeat until clear. If you can't clear it or the line is dry and cracked, replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: A pump is used when the carburetor sits higher than the tank (pumping uphill against gravity). Most mowers use a vacuum fuel pump that builds pressure off the crankcase.
- confirm (vacuum): No visible cracks or leaking? Verify fuel reaches the pump inlet (else check line/filter blockage). Reattach line to the inlet; remove the fuel line from the carburetor into a container; start fuel flow and start the mower. Should be a steady or pulsating flow; if not, replace the pump.
- brand: If the Exmark has an electronic fuel injection pump, measure with a fuel pressure gauge; see operator's manual for spec; replace the pump if pressure is below the engine manufacturer's spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Small parts inside (fuel jet, float, float needle) can stick or clog.
- confirm: First confirm fuel reaches the carburetor. Remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake, start the engine; if it starts and then won't keep running, remove the carburetor and clean it.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Tank pressure must equal atmospheric pressure via a vent in the cap; a clogged vent makes a vacuum that stops fuel moving to the carburetor.
- confirm: Loosen the cap; engine should start and run smoothly; reinstall and see if it starves after running a while; if sluggish and runs better when the cap is loosened again, replace the cap.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Always use gasoline with a minimum octane of 87 and maximum 10% ethanol.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Consume gasoline within 30 days; if you buy more, add a stabilizer to extend life. Author adds Sea Foam Motor Treatment to every tank.
