## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Craftsman blades won't engage", "blades won't turn on", "blades won't spin"
- causes-named: worn-drive-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: Summary list: worn/stretched deck belt; belt off pulleys; bad PTO switch; faulty clutch; weak battery; bad safety switch; bad fuse. Article numbers 8 reasons (including manual-clutch cable).
- safety: Never reach under the deck until following the operator's manual precautions: remove ignition key, remove spark plug wires, wait for all parts to stop moving.
- pro: If you can't safely diagnose, use a dealer/repair shop. Repeated blown fuses: take to a Craftsman service dealer/shop to find the root electrical failure.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- symptom: belt slips on pulleys, no longer grips to turn them.
- confirm: inspect for cracks, fraying, shiny glazed appearance, belt sitting deep in the pulleys.
- fix: replace; check regularly and replace well before it fails. Slipping reduces blade tip speed, which is needed for the suction that lifts grass.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Tensioner arm and spring hold the idler pulleys in line. The bracket hole where the spring attaches can wear, or the spring itself wears; belt gets loose and vibrates off the pulleys. Belt-off-pulleys causes: stretched belt, bad pulley, worn tensioner arm, missing spring.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: pulley wobbles; one side sits higher off the deck; extra movement can roll the belt off the pulley.
- fix: replace the pulley when its bearing is bad.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch is usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch; when it fails blades won't turn on.
- confirm: check for continuity; replace if there's a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO (Power Take Off) clutch transfers power from engine to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace a worn or faulty clutch that fails to power the blades.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: A Craftsman with manual engagement clutch has a clutch lever and cable. Check condition of lever, cable, spring, bushings and linkages; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Electric clutch uses battery power; a weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid to engage blades.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; charge if lower.
- fix: if it won't hold a charge, replace with a new 12 V battery (hardware/automotive store or dealer). Bring the old battery; core fee averages $20 otherwise.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for skin (shock) and eyes; don't touch anything without rubber coating.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (under hood or seat; screwdriver may be needed to uncover). Do not remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect charger cables positive first (red/plus) to the positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach negative (black/minus) to the negative terminal.
- step 4: Set voltage/amperage: usually 12 volts; more amps charge faster; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; a slow charge is best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Seat switch under the seat is part of the operator presence system; blades won't engage without the operator seated; a bad switch can't sense the operator so blades are blocked.
- confirm: test with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed; keep safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: No power from battery to clutch may be a blown fuse; replace with the same size fuse; repeated blowing needs a shop.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: worn belt must be replaced; check regularly.
