## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- symptom: dirty carb symptoms: push mower won't start; backfires from running lean; sputters and runs rough; surges; consumes too much fuel.
- fact: Old fuel sits in the carburetor, gumming and crusty buildup; parts stick and the fuel jet clogs. Old fuel and ethanol fuel are hard on a carburetor.
- confirm: Before disassembly (1): confirm fuel reaches the carburetor; check fuel line for clogs/kinks; if the mower has a fuel pump or fuel filter, check them for restriction.
- confirm: Before disassembly (2): remove the fuel filter from its housing, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and start the mower. If it starts, runs well and then shuts off, the problem is likely in the carburetor: take it apart and clean it. If it doesn't start/run well, may not be fuel-related; check for air restriction or no spark.
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers
- step 1: Gather tools.
- step 2: Take photos with a phone before and during teardown to remember where parts go.
- step 3: Shut off fuel using the shut-off valve at the bottom of the fuel tank; if none, crimp the fuel line with pinch pliers.
- step 4: Detach throttle and choke cables from the carburetor.
- step 5: Remove the hardware attaching the carburetor to the air filter housing.
- step 6: Slowly remove springs from the carburetor; don't stretch them or you'll have to replace them; may need to twist the carburetor. Be careful not to rip the gasket between the engine block and carburetor (replace with new if damaged).
- step 7: Remove the bowl at the bottom (have a rag for remaining fuel): remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and lower the bowl. Gasket between bowl and carb looks like a rubber band; do not get carb cleaner or any substance on it or it must be replaced before reattaching.
- step 8: Check the stem hanging down in the center of the carb; holes can clog with gummed old fuel; use a flashlight and thick wire to unclog.
- step 9: Remove gummy deposits and white crusty buildup from other components and the carb body with carburetor cleaner and a clean cloth. Almost impossible to remove all the crusty material.
- step 10: Confirm float needle and float are in good working order; reassemble in reverse order using the photos.
- fix: If cleaning doesn't work, small components may need replacement or the carburetor must be replaced/rebuilt (old fuel damage).
- tip: After, fill with fresh gas plus a fuel additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system). For an older two-cycle push mower, use good fuel and the correct fuel-to-oil ratio.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: won't start, lean backfire, rough running, surging, high fuel consumption.
- confirm: Spray carb cleaner into the air intake with the filter removed; starts/runs then dies = carburetor.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: When cleaning fails, small components may need replacing (rebuild).

## page: procedures/replace-the-carburetor
- fact: Replace when old fuel has done so much damage that cleaning fails.
