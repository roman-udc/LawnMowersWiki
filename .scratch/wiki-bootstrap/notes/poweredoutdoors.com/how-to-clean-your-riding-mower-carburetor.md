## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor controls air/fuel mixture for combustion. Bad carburetor causes sputter, stall, no start. Most problems solved by cleaning.
- symptom: engine won't start; starts then dies; backfires from running lean; sputtering and running rough; surging; consuming too much fuel.
- confirm: remove air filter from housing, spray carburetor cleaner into air intake, start mower. If it starts and then dies, take the carburetor apart and clean it. Don't disassemble if carb isn't the problem.
- fact: Also named as cause of not starting, quitting after starting, and leaking fuel.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers; rag; flashlight; phone/camera.
- difficulty: for those a little mechanical and fine with small parts; otherwise local small engine repair shop can clean or rebuild.
- step 0: test with carb cleaner in intake (see dirty-carburetor confirm).
- step 1: take photos before removing and during every step of disassembly (many small parts).
- step 2: shut off fuel supply with fuel shut-off valve (at bottom of fuel tank); if no valve, crimp the line. Remove fuel line from carburetor.
- step 3: remove throttle and choke cables.
- step 4: remove hardware attaching carburetor to air filter housing; detach housing.
- step 5: slowly remove springs without stretching them (may need to twist carburetor). Be careful not to tear the gasket between engine block and carburetor; if torn, replace.
- step 6: remove carburetor bowl (bottom, held by a screw); rag to catch remaining gas; don't damage gasket between bowl and carburetor (looks like a rubber band); don't let carb cleaner touch that gasket or it must be replaced.
- step 7: check the stem (piece hanging down from center): holes can clog from old fuel so it won't draw fuel up to the jet; use flashlight; clean holes with thick wire.
- step 8: check carb and parts for white crusty buildup (from fuel additives including ethanol); remove as much as possible with carb cleaner (almost impossible to remove all); clean any sticking or clogged parts.
- step 9: confirm float and float needle move freely; reassemble using photos; reattach springs, filter housing, throttle and choke cables (if used), fuel line.
- step 10: open fuel flow; fill tank with fresh gas mixed with fuel additive like Sea Foam Motor Treatment; let fuel fill the bowl; start engine.
- safety: gas in bowl; rag.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: if cleaning doesn't work (too much buildup, stuck or damaged parts) rebuild or replace. Compare price of rebuild kit vs new carburetor; sometimes close, so replace instead.
- tip: have engine make and model number when buying parts; many lawn tractors use another manufacturer's engine.
