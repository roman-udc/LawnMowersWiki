## page: problems/engine-only-runs-with-choke-on
- alias: "John Deere only runs with the choke on"
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Occurs when the engine gets too much air or not enough fuel. Lack of fuel: old gas, dirty carb, clogged line, bad pump, plugged filter, bad cap. Too much air: bad carb gasket, puncture in fuel line. Choke is used to correct the air-to-fuel ratio.
- safety: Remove ignition key and spark plug boot before repairs; follow John Deere operator's manual.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas starts to break down about a month after purchase; gums the system and corrodes components. Ethanol attracts moisture, ethanol and water separate and sink to the bottom; evaporation leaves sticky deposits; water is corrosive.
- fact: John Deere requires unleaded gasoline minimum 87 octane and maximum 10% ethanol; never more than 10%.
- fix: Drain old gas; add fresh gas with Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL to clean, reduce moisture, keep gas stable longer.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Passageways and internal parts clog, usually from old gas. Clean or replace; if cleaning fails, rebuild or replace; a mechanic can do it.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: A clog restricts fuel; a puncture draws air in (too much air). Both make the mower run only with choke on.
- confirm: Inspect lines from tank to carburetor for punctures or cracks. Clog check: shut off fuel at the shut-off valve, remove end of a section into a container, turn fuel on, watch for good flow.
- fix: If poor flow, shut off, remove line, spray carb cleaner then compressed air, repeat. Replace with new line of the same diameter if can't clear or punctured.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Punctured or cracked line sucks air into the line; engine gets too much air.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter sits between lines; replace annually. Install with the arrow on the housing pointing in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Moves fuel uphill to carb; most mowers use a vacuum pump driven by engine vacuum.
- confirm: Confirm fuel reaches the inlet port; remove line from carb into a container and test flow.
- fix: Replace if insufficient flow, cracks or leaks.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Gasket behind the carb wears and no longer seals; extra air drawn in makes engine run lean (higher concentration of air, less fuel); choke compensates.
- confirm: Inspect carb; bolts secure, gasket good.
- fix: Remove linkages and bolts, remove carb and gasket, install new gasket, reattach carb, bolts, linkages; good time to clean carb.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plugged vent forms a vacuum in the tank; fuel won't reach the carb. A pressure gauge can check for vacuum.
- confirm: Loosen the gas cap to let air in; put choke off; if the engine continues running with choke off, cap may be the problem. Then tighten the cap and keep running choke off; if it sputters after a while and won't run well until the cap is loosened, bad cap.
- fix: Replace.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke needed to compensate for lean or fuel-starved condition (not itself the fault here).
