## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: A full service = engine oil and filter change; check fuel system for leaks; inspect components for wear/damage; scrape the deck; sharpen blades; replace filters; lubricate. Skipping maintenance can damage engine, fuel and electrical systems.
- tools: socket or spanner wrenches, filter wrench, deck scraper, wire brush, plastic funnel, oil drain pan, rags/paper towel, grease, spray lubricant, rubber gloves, work gloves, safety glasses, cardboard/newspaper for the work surface
- parts: engine oil filter, engine oil, spark plug (confirm correct plug from engine), air filter, fuel filter, replacement blades if not reusing, fuel stabilizer for off-season storage
- step 1: Park on a flat level surface; lay cardboard/newspaper to catch drips; run the engine a few minutes to warm the oil (warm oil drains better); shut off.
- safety: Set the parking brake; remove ignition key (author pockets/hides it); disconnect spark plug wires; engine may still be hot.
- step 2: Change engine oil and filter (see procedures/change-the-engine-oil).
- step 3: Inspect spark plug and gap; author replaces plugs once a year at full service; otherwise clean if dirty. Dirty or wrongly gapped plugs cause intermittent running/starting problems. Don't reattach spark plug wires until service is done.
- step 4: Remove dirt/debris from engine cooling fins and engine area (clogged fins block airflow around block and cylinder).
- step 5: Clean dirt/debris from hydro fans (keep the hydraulic transmission cool) and the transmission area.
- step 6: Clean battery terminals; if corroded use baking soda solution of 2 cups water + 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda and a wire brush.
- step 7: Remove debris around electrical components and wiring (debris holds moisture, causes premature corrosion/failure).
- step 8: Air filter: author changes inner and outer filters at each full service; excessively dirty filters can damage the engine.
- step 9: Replace the fuel filter; arrow on the inline filter shows fuel flow direction; install accordingly.
- step 10: Check the fuel system for leaks: hoses secure, no leaks (punctures/cracks in lines, poor sealing, degrading plastics).
- step 11: Check tire pressures with a gauge; uneven pressures make the deck unlevel (bad cut); low tire can cause steering issues. Set to psi on tire sidewall, equal left and right; confirm lug nuts secure.
- step 12: Remove the deck for cleaning: parking brake on, PTO off, deck in lowest position. If manual PTO: loosen (do not remove) hardware on right and left idler pulleys; remove hex screw holding the rod over the engine pulley and remove rod; remove PTO cable and unhook spring by removing the pin attaching cable to deck bracket. Then standard: put a ratchet in the square hole of the idler bracket and turn to move the pulley forward; remove belt from PTO pulley; remove cotter pins (left and right, near rear) and slide deck pin out of the lift arms; remove front lift rod's cotter pin and slide the rod out (raise deck lift lever to highest to move rod aside); slide the deck out from under the mower.
- step 13: Clean the top of the deck (debris restricts tensioner bracket and belt movement; remove belt covers). Dry debris plus belt friction heat is a fire hazard.
- step 14: Check belt: replace if glazed/shiny, cracked, stretched, shredded. Spin pulleys by hand: should be smooth; replace on resistance or bearing noise; clean rust or replace if very rusty.
- step 15: Underside: check spindle bearings by rocking each blade up and down; movement or knocking = remove spindle housing, replace bad bearings or whole cutter housing assembly. Remove and sharpen/replace blades. Scrape deck with scraper or metal brush (clogged deck hurts cut quality, strains engine; moisture corrodes deck).
- step 16: Reinstall deck in reverse order. Side-to-side: flat level surface, deck lift in middle position, measure ground to blade tip on right and left; if different, loosen (don't remove) upper nut on the left adjustment rod under the rear fenders, turn lower nut clockwise to raise left side, counter-clockwise to lower; tighten top nut.
- step 17: Pitch front-to-rear: flat surface, lift in middle position. Front of deck must be 1/4" to 3/8" lower than rear. Adjust at the stabilizer bracket (outer and inner nut at front): loosen outer nut (don't remove); tightening inner nut raises the front, loosening the lock nut lowers it; retighten outer nut once front measures 1/4"-3/8" lower than rear.
- step 18: Check the safety system per the owner's manual.
- step 19: Replace damaged/worn/missing parts; hardware secure.
- step 20: Grease zerks on front wheels, front axle and deck wheels; lubricate lift linkages, pivot points and brake. Author uses high-performance grease tube and spray lubricant.
- step 21: Wash with garden hose; if using a pressure washer do not use high pressure (damages sealed bearings). Dry completely (sunny/breezy day; leaf blower helps) so moisture around electrics evaporates; reattach spark plug wires if moving mower to wash.
- step 22 (off-season storage): remove fuel from the tank OR add stabilizer (author recommends Sea Foam Motor Treatment); fully charge the battery so it doesn't freeze, OR remove and store it cool and dry.
- fact: Change oil every 50 hours of use and before storage; the initial oil change on a new engine after the first 5 hours.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- step 1: Warm the engine a few minutes, shut off, take safety precautions.
- step 2: Place oil drain pan under the drain plug or drain hose (whichever your Cub Cadet uses); wipe around oil fill and drain areas first so debris doesn't enter the crankcase.
- step 3: Remove drain plug (under the hood on the side of the engine near the bottom); remove dipstick so oil flows faster; reinstall plug when fully drained.
- step 4: Replace oil filter (use filter wrench; put a light film of fresh oil on the new filter's seal).
- step 5: Add new oil to proper level checking dipstick; don't overfill; if overfilled remove a little.
- step 6: Wipe spills; dispose of used oil at a recycling center or auto parts store that accepts it.
- fact: Interval 50 hours and before storage; first change after 5 hours on a new engine.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins dissipate engine heat; clogged fins stop air circulating around block/cylinder; remove debris around fins and engine area.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter has an arrow showing fuel flow direction; install using the arrow.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- fact: Side-to-side: deck lift in middle position; measure ground to blade tip each side; adjust left rod under rear fenders (loosen upper nut, lower nut clockwise raises left side); tighten top nut.
- fact: Pitch: front 1/4" to 3/8" lower than rear; adjust at the stabilizer bracket (inner nut tighten raises front; loosen outer nut first, retighten after).

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- fact: Author replaces annually at full service; clean if keeping; check gap.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Oil every 50 hours/before storage, first change at 5 hours; annual plug; filters at each full service; grease zerks on front wheels, axle, deck wheels; pressure washing at high pressure damages sealed bearings.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Drain fuel or add stabilizer; fully charge the battery or store it cool/dry.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: Wash with hose; no high-pressure washing (sealed bearings); dry completely, sunlight/breeze/leaf blower; moisture left causes corrosion.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: belt (glazed/cracked/stretched/shredded), pulleys/bearings, spindle bearings, blades, spark plug, filters.
