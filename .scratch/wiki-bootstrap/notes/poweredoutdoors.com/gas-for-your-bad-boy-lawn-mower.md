## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Gas-powered Bad Boy mowers use unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, ethanol content no greater than 10 percent. Bad Boy also offers diesel mowers; check owner's manual if unsure.
- brand: Bad Boy lineup (push, zero-turn, stand-on, walk-behind) all use four-stroke engines on unleaded gasoline.
- fact: Use fresh gas; store in a dry area so water doesn't mix in; lower ethanol is better.
- fact: Gas can start breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase; buy only what you'll use within a month, otherwise use a stabilizer additive.
- fact: E15 and E85 (15% and 85% ethanol) must never be used in small engines.
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture; the solution separates and sinks to the bottom of the tank, runs hot and can cause significant engine damage.
- fact: Old fuel leaves a gummy residue that degrades/clogs fuel system parts: sluggish running or no run.
- fact: Ethanol-free fuel is better still (doesn't break down like ethanol blends), more expensive in canisters; stations sell it as "recreation fuel" or REC-90; pure-gas.org locates stations (author's recommendation).
- tip: Author adds Sea Foam Motor Treatment to each tank; claims it stabilizes fuel up to two years, reduces moisture, cleans the fuel system. Use when storing excess fuel and when storing the mower with fuel in off-season. (Brand claim, opinion.)

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Wrong fuel (ethanol above 10%, E15/E85, low octane, stale) harms fuel system and engine.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas degrades from ~30 days; leaves gummy deposits.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Store fuel dry; buy a month's supply; add stabilizer (Sea Foam, up to 2 years claimed) for stored fuel and off-season storage with fuel in mower.
