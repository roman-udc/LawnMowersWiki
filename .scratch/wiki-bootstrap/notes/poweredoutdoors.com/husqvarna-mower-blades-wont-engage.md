## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Husqvarna blades won't engage / turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- safety: Before reaching under the deck: remove ignition key, remove spark plug wire(s), wait for all parts to stop moving; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: Cracks, wear, fraying, shiny glazed look; sits deep in pulley grooves.
- fix: Replace even if not the main cause. Belt grip on pulleys affects blade speed; fast blade speed creates suction under the deck to lift grass for an even cut.
- fact: Belt off the pulleys: look for stretched belt, bad pulley, worn tensioner arm, missing spring.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- symptom: Tensioner arm and spring hold idler pulleys inline (pulley one side of bracket, spring on other). Spring hole in bracket or the spring itself wears, belt loosens and vibrates off the pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: Worn bearing lets pulley sit not parallel to the deck, wobble, one side higher; deck belt rolls off.
- fix: Replace the pulley.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch; failed switch means blades won't turn.
- confirm: Check continuity; replace switch with a break.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace worn or faulty clutch.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: Manual-engagement clutch has a lever and cable; check lever, cable, spring, bushings, linkages for wear; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Electric clutch uses battery power; weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid.
- confirm: Multimeter; fully charged 12 V is about 12.7 V; charge if less.
- fix: Replace if it won't hold charge. 12 V batteries at hardware, auto, or dealer; bring old battery to avoid core fee (average $20).

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Wear protective gear (skin, eyes). Access battery (under hood or under seat; screwdriver may be needed); don't remove from casing.
- step 2: Connect positive (red/plus) first, then negative (black/minus).
- step 3: Touch nothing without rubber coating.
- step 4: Set charger to 12 V; start with 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Seat switch under the seat senses operator; blades won't engage without operator in seat. A failed seat switch fails to sense the operator and blocks blades.
- confirm: Multimeter, or temporarily bypass for troubleshooting.
- safety: Never operate without the safety switch installed.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse protects electrical system; no power from battery to clutch may mean blown fuse. Replace with same size fuse; repeat blowing: dealer or repair shop for root cause.
