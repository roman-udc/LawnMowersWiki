## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Bad Boy lawn mower is smoking"
- alias: "blowing blue, white or black smoke"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, bad-head-gasket, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, spilled-oil-on-engine, bad-valve-train
- fact: Smoke starts when the air filter is plugged, oil level is insufficient, an engine gasket failed, or there is internal damage (piston ring or valve train). Also when oil is spilled or leaking onto the engine or muffler.
- fact: Smoke can be an easy fix if caught early.
- fact: Blue or white smoke = excess oil burning (damaged piston rings, valve train, gasket leaks, or oil pushed into cylinder by a plugged air filter). Also check for oil burning off the muffler/engine from a leak or oil spilled during an oil change.
- fact: Black smoke = excess fuel burning; plugged air filter gives a higher fuel concentration (runs rich). If the air filter is not the cause, look for another air restriction.
- confirm: Remove the spark plug(s) and look for oil on the plug; oil indicates a piston ring or valve train problem (does not say which; needs a mechanic).
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety instructions; consult a professional if not skilled or not in condition to repair safely.
- pro: Internal engine problems (piston ring, valve train, low-oil damage) need a small engine mechanic with special tools (leak-down and compression checks, engine teardown).
- tip: Before paying for engine repair, get a repair quote and compare it with engine replacement; depending on damage and machine age you may choose a new engine or mower.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: mower-is-smoking. Black smoke from running rich.
- fact: Never run the mower without an air filter; it keeps dirt and debris out of the engine (damage to piston, seals, internal parts, cylinder).
- fact: A plugged filter makes the engine run rich (black smoke). If it restricts all air, the engine draws air from wherever it can, including the crankcase, pulling oil from the crankcase that burns in the cylinder (blue/white smoke). Smoke can also plug the filter, making the problem worse.
- tip: Author recommends a new air filter at the start of each mowing season, cleaning it several times a season; clean/replace more often in dry dusty conditions.
- fix: Clean or replace filter (see procedure notes below).

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1 (paper element): Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe out dirt remaining in housing; don't let any fall into the air intake.
- step 3: Tap filter on a solid surface to loosen dirt.
- step 4: Hold up to light; reuse if light shines through the paper; if not, install a new filter.
- step 5: Reinstall filter and cover.
- step 1 (foam element): Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe housing with a clean dry cloth; keep dirt out of intake.
- step 3: Inspect foam; if brown areas, tears, or dry and brittle, do not reuse, replace.
- step 4: If good, wash with mild dish soap and water to remove dirt and oil.
- step 5: Rinse until water runs clear and soap is gone.
- step 6: Lay flat to air dry (outside on a sunny or breezy day speeds drying).
- step 7: Once dry, lightly saturate with foam filter oil, not dripping; squeeze out excess or blot with paper towel. A new foam filter also gets filter oil.
- step 8: Reinstall filter and housing cover.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: mower-is-smoking. Low oil means friction and heat; oil can burn and engine parts can begin to melt, causing smoke.
- fact: Adding fresh oil may not fix it; usually the engine is permanently damaged and needs repair by an experienced small engine mechanic.
- tip: Check oil level before each mowing to catch the problem early.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: mower-is-smoking. Too much oil increases crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and into the air intake through the valve train; oil burns in the cylinder and smokes.
- fix: Remove a little oil via the drain plug or oil filter, or with an oil evacuator or turkey baster through the oil fill area. Recheck with the dipstick; keep adding/removing until correct. Check the air filter for oil coating or smoke plugging; install a new one if so.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: "Bad engine gasket" lets engine oil leak; oil on a hot surface such as the muffler burns off and smokes. (Article says "engine gasket", not specifically head gasket.)

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Engine must be torn down to find a piston ring problem. A bad ring or scoring on the cylinder wall lets oil enter the combustion chamber and burn, causing smoke.
- confirm: Oil on spark plug suggests ring or valve train problem; exact cause needs teardown, leak-down and compression checks by a mechanic.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: A mower that has overheated can develop a burnt valve. Only way to confirm is removing the cylinder head and doing a leak-down test; mechanic must cut the seat and valve correctly to complete the combustion chamber.

## page: causes/spilled-oil-on-engine
- fact: Oil spilled during an oil change or leaking onto engine/muffler burns off and smokes.
