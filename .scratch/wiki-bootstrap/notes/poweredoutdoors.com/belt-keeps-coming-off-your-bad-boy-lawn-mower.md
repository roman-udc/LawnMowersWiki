## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "belt keeps coming off my mower deck"
- alias: "have to keep reinstalling the deck belt"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, wrong-belt, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- safety: remove the ignition key, disconnect spark plug boots, protect hands from sharp objects; never work until all moving parts have stopped; see owner's manual
- pro: if unsure how to diagnose safely, have a dealer or repair shop do it

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: glazed shiny look, stretching, cracking; belt sits deep in pulley grooves or sits loose on pulleys
- fix: replace with a new deck belt
- tip: taking the old belt to an auto parts store to match doesn't always get the right type, size or quality; author prefers OEM belts from a Bad Boy dealer (high quality, right material, last longer)
- symptom: shredded belt (edges shredding) is weak and more likely to break; most common reasons are incorrect installation or rubbing on a bracket that moved out of place
- confirm: check it's the right belt and routed correctly around deck pulleys (decal diagram on mower, owner's manual or parts diagram); with deck engaged look for a shiny wear spot on a bracket the belt rubs
- fix: adjust the rubbing bracket; if it has a bushing the bushing may need replacing; replace a bracket that can't be adjusted; then fit a new belt

## page: causes/wrong-belt
- fact: when belt shreds along edges, first confirm it is the right belt and correctly routed

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: blade wobbles (extra movement where blade attaches); at high speed the wobble causes excessive vibration that knocks the belt off (zero-turn)
- confirm: from underside of deck, hands protected, grab each end of the blade and rock it up and down feeling for movement or hearing knocking; if blade is secure to the cutter housing but there is still movement/knock, remove blade and disassemble the cutter housing assembly
- fix: replace the bad bearing; some models allow bearing-only replacement, others need the whole cutter housing assembly because the bearing is sealed

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: pulley not sitting flat and parallel to deck (one side higher) causes vibration and the belt comes off
- confirm: spin each pulley slowly by hand; resistance or noise from the bearing means it's bad
- fix: replace the pulley

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- symptom: too little tension lets belt slip or come off; too much damages deck components
- confirm: check with a belt tension gauge; most Bad Boy mowers need 70-75 lbs belt tension (check owner's manual for the model)
- fix: some models: slide the bolt on the deck to adjust spring tension; others: loosen jam nuts and adjust the eye bolt the tensioner spring attaches to
- tip: recheck tension after running about 30 minutes
- brand: Bad Boy 70-75 lbs

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: dirt/debris in pulley grooves makes belt stretch around extra material; belt can roll off or snap
- fix: clean dirt, stones and grass packed in or under pulleys

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- symptom: oil leaking onto deck and pulleys gets on belt and makes it slip off; oil sitting on belt for long periods makes the belt swell
- fix: repair the oil leak, clean oil off deck, replace belt
- symptom: rust dries out belt so it cracks and breaks
- fix: clean rust off deck and components; replace very rusty pulleys that can't be fully cleaned; install new belt

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: replace belt after fixing the cause; confirm routing via decal / manual / parts diagram; author prefers OEM belt
