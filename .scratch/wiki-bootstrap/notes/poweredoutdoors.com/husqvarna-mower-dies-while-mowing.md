## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Husqvarna starts then dies"
- alias: "mower dies while mowing"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Starts then dies when mower can't regulate the air-fuel mixture needed for combustion.
- pro: Small engine mechanic or Husqvarna dealer after wrong-fuel use, after oil level corrected but problems continue (low-oil shutdowns often cause permanent damage), and for charging-system diagnosis.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: Easy to forget when troubleshooting; check gasoline first.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Regular gas lasts about 30 days before breaking down. Ethanol attracts moisture; moisture/ethanol separates, harming engine; ethanol leaves gummy substance that clogs fuel system causing sputter and die.
- fix: Drain tank; fill fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam Motor Treatment; run a few minutes so treated fuel works through system.
- tip: Sea Foam reduces moisture and cleans fuel system (author preference).

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Small engines need unleaded gasoline 87 octane or greater, no more than 10% ethanol. Read the pump label.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Starved of air, mower dies while mowing; plugged filter can also overheat engine causing internal damage.
- step (paper): remove filter from housing without knocking dirt into intake; wipe housing with dry cloth; tap filter on solid surface; hold up to light; if no light visible through paper, replace.
- mistake: Never blow out a paper air filter with an air compressor (damages it).
- step (foam): remove; wipe housing; wash with mild dish soap, rinse until soap gone; air dry in sun until completely dry; apply foam filter oil to a light coating; reinstall. Brown spots, brittle or torn = replace.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- see causes/clogged-air-filter above (paper: tap, light test, no compressor; foam: dish soap, dry, light coat of foam filter oil; replace if brown spots/brittle/torn).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Plugged filter restricts fuel to carburetor: sluggish running then dies.
- fix: Remove and install new inline filter between fuel lines with the arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step: Remove old filter; install new inline filter between lines with arrow in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Clogged by dirt and gummy buildup from old fuel; replace line if dry and cracked.
- fix: Remove line, add carburetor cleaner to loosen clog, blow compressed air through, reinstall; replace if clog won't clear.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Mowers with gas tank lower than carburetor have a fuel pump. Fuel can make pump seams or pump fail. Check for damage or leaking at seams; internal damage must be tested.
- confirm step 1: Stop fuel flow with fuel shut-off valve, or pinch fuel line with clamp.
- confirm step 2: Remove line from pump inlet port, place in container (lower than fuel supply so fuel runs downhill), start flow; fuel should run out. No fuel = look for blocked lines or plugged filters. Reattach.
- confirm step 3: Remove fuel line from carburetor, end in container, start mower; expect steady or pulsating flow.
- confirm step 4: No flow: shut off mower and fuel valve; check no blockage in line to carburetor; if clear, replace the pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Float, needle and other components gum up, preventing proper air-gas mixture.
- fix: Clean if somewhat mechanical (many little parts); otherwise take carburetor to repair shop to clean or rebuild.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Caps are designed to vent; blocked vent creates vacuum in tank restricting fuel flow.
- confirm: Remove fuel cap and run engine; if it no longer dies, cap is the problem. Keep dirt out of fuel system during the test.
- fix: Clean cap to vent, else replace.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Fouled plug fires intermittently: runs sluggish or dies. Also bad if not properly gapped, damaged, or wire loose.
- fix: Clean tip; replace if very dark, burnt electrode or damaged; gap to engine manufacturer spec; attach wires securely.
- tip: Author recommends replacing plugs annually.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: dies after running a while (hot).
- fact: Coil winding can separate and short out when hot; plug doesn't get voltage for spark.
- confirm: Ohmmeter check for break in continuity; replace coil if break.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins damaged or packed with grass and mud can't circulate air; overheats and dies mid-mow.
- fix: Remove engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: High oil can make engine smoke and die after starting; smoke can clog air filter; can cause internal damage and need for engine replacement.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil: extra friction, overheating and dying. Change oil per Husqvarna recommendations; fill to correct level.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Deck packed with grass/debris makes engine work harder turning blades through buildup; dull blades add load, eventually shutdown.
- fix: Inspect deck, scrape debris, sharpen blades, run at full throttle, avoid cutting wet grass.

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "Husqvarna battery not charging"
- fact: Run engine at high speed; don't idle long (engine power needed to charge battery). If battery and wiring good and secure but battery keeps needing charging, suspect charging system.
- pro: Have an experienced mechanic diagnose the charging system; parts-throwing is costly and electrical parts usually can't be returned.
- causes-named: faulty-charging-system, weak-or-dead-battery

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- see above.
