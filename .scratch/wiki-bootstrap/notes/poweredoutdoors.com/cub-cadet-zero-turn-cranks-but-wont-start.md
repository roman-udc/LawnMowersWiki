## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "you can hear the engine wanting to start but it just won't"
- fact: cranks but won't start when not getting air, spark or fuel.
- causes-named: wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- safety: always remove spark plug wires before repairs.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts airflow so engine runs rich (more fuel, less air) to start cold. Once warm, choke lever must be off so the engine gets enough air.
- symptom: a warm engine won't start with choke engaged; cold won't start without it.
- fix: if setting is right and airflow trouble persists, check the choke plate isn't stuck (carburetor cleaner frees it) and the choke cable moves freely (lubricant like Sea Foam Deep Creep); replace damaged/worn cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dirt past the filter can cause permanent engine damage; plugged filter can also cause overheating.
- tip: start each season with a new air filter, clean several times per season; more often with heavier-than-homeowner use.
- safety: engine can be hot when working near it.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: PAPER:
- step 1: remove filter from housing.
- step 2: wipe out dirt in housing; none into intake.
- step 3: tap against a solid surface.
- step 4: hold to light; light visible means reuse; if not, damaged or very dirty, buy new.
- step 5: reinstall, attach cover.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- tools: 3/4" or 5/8" socket wrench depending on engine model.
- symptom: damaged/dirty plug gives intermittent or no spark; check for burnt electrode, broken porcelain, dark-colored tip.
- fix: install a new plug in those cases; if good but a little dirty clean with a wire brush; verify gap, secure the wire.
- tip: start each season with a new plug.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase, leaving varnish and sticky deposits. Buy fresh and use within 30 days; use minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; avoid high ethanol.
- fix: drain with a fuel siphon pump; refill with fresh fuel with stabilizer. Author likes Sea Foam Motor Treatment. Run the mower so treated gas works through.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: zero-turn uses an inline fuel filter between the fuel lines; plugs if not replaced regularly.
- fix: replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: follow line from tank to carburetor looking for kinks; then shut off flow (shut-off valve or fuel pinch-off pliers), remove the end furthest from the tank into a container, turn fuel on and check flow section by section.
- fix: carburetor cleaner then compressed air, repeat; replace if not cleared or line is aged and cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump needed when carburetor is higher than tank. Most zero-turns use a vacuum fuel pump (crankcase vacuum).
- confirm: first confirm fuel reaches the pump inlet (if not check line/filter); then remove line from carburetor into a container, start flow and mower; need steady or pulsating flow; otherwise replace.
- brand: EFI pump: check operator manual for fuel pressure spec; replace if below the engine maker's spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old gas leaves varnish that plugs the fuel jet or sticks internal components.
- confirm: confirm good fuel flow to carb; remove air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into air intake and start; if it starts on cleaner then runs sluggish/dies when cleaner burns off, carb likely dirty. Don't use starter fluid.
- fix: disassemble and clean, replace faulty parts, or replace carburetor.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vent is in the gas cap; plugged vent causes vacuum keeping gas from the carburetor; mower turns over but won't start.
- confirm: loosen cap and try to start; if it starts, keep running while tightening; if it sputters, shuts down and won't restart until cap loosened, replace cap.
