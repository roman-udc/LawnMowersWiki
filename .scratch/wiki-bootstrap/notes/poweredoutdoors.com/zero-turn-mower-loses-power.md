## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "zero turn has no power"; "engine doesn't give the power it once did"
- causes-named: mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Could be maintenance or operator error, or something that causes engine damage if ignored. Most causes are a lack of air or fuel; a few are electrical or internal engine issues.
- pro: If problems persist after these checks, take it to a knowledgeable mechanic (dealer or small engine repair shop).

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- symptom: Too fast for conditions loads the engine; slow down for thick lawns and inclines (inclines make the engine work harder).

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fix: Mow dry; mow regularly; tall grass: highest deck setting for the first cut then lower for the next; if unavoidable, slow down. Wet grass clumps under the deck.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Buildup makes the engine work hard turning blades; also bad cut.
- fix: Scrape the deck regularly; avoid wet conditions.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull blades plus a clogged deck magnify power loss; check and sharpen or replace.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Engine sluggish and possibly quits.
- step: Paper filter: remove; wipe housing with a dry cloth without letting dirt into the intake; tap against a solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light shows through, replace if not.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel can begin to become less effective and unstable as quickly as 30 days after purchase; gummy deposits block the fuel system. Use a stabilizer if you can't use it in time.
- fix: Drain old fuel into a container for recycling; refill fresh. Gas zero-turns usually take unleaded with minimum 87 octane and maximum 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- tip: Replace annually at routine maintenance; inexpensive part.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Shut-off valve on the bottom of the tank or crimp the line to stop flow; remove the end furthest from the tank of one section into a container; start flow; check each section.
- fix: Remove the clogged section, spray carburetor cleaner, blow with compressed air; replace if it won't clear or is dry and cracked.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake, start; if it runs but won't stay running, clean the carburetor.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Carbon, dirt, oil on the tip gives intermittent spark and power loss.
- fix: Author recommends replacing; may clean with a wire brush and reuse if in good condition and not very dark.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Low oil: friction, heat, power loss; if not caught, internal parts can melt, leading to major repair or engine replacement.
- tip: Check oil before each mowing; catches leaks or engine problems early.
- pro: Continued problems after topping up to the recommended level may mean internal damage; needs an experienced small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Pressure builds and oil is pushed into the cylinder through the valve train; bluish-white smoke from burning oil. The smoke can plug the air filter.
- mistake: Continuing to run with too much oil can cause seal damage, hydro lock and a bent piston rod (Source's wording).
- fix: Remove a little oil with an oil evacuator, drain plug or turkey baster; check/clean or replace the air filter and spark plug.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: Remove grass clippings, dirt and debris around the engine and under the shroud; clean fins (plugged with mud and clippings); replace broken fins.
