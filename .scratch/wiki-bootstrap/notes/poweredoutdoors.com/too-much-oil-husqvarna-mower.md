## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil can cause engine damage from overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets, or a hydrolocked engine. Pressure building in the crankcase can potentially cause engine damage; correcting the level may not be a simple fix.
- safety: before troubleshooting, remove the spark plug boot(s) and wait for the engine to cool.
- symptom: seal leaks (oil leaking from engine); overheating; hard turn-over / won't turn (hydrolock); runs poorly and smokes whitish-blue stinky smoke; fouled spark plug; blown gaskets.
- fact (seals): crankcase pressure can blow out seals and leak oil; if not found quickly the engine can run out of oil and blow: heat can spin a rod or weld it to the crankshaft, break the rod and send it out of the block, or weld the piston to the cylinder (seize).
- fact (overheat): crankshaft and rod push through more oil, raising crankcase pressure and loading internal parts; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil may not flow properly; aluminum gets very hot; can pop a valve guide or valve seat from the block, which needs an experienced small engine mechanic ("scraped out").
- fact (hydrolock): oil getting into the cylinder past worn rings; the piston cannot compress oil; a good chance the piston bends when starting.
- fact (smoke/foul): oil reaches the spark plug, fouling it and inhibiting spark; oil enters the valve train and burns in the cylinder giving thick whitish-blue smoke; a clogged air filter may pull air and oil from the crankcase.
- fact (gaskets): excess heat can blow gaskets; same on push, riding and zero-turn; gasket replacement on a twin-cylinder engine costs more because the engine must be removed (extra labor).
- fact (will it start?): too much oil can prevent the engine running; the small engine oil pan has little room for error; oil can also get into the carburetor through the valve train and pass into the air filter, stopping the engine.
- mistake: assuming going over the required amount is okay; a little extra is a huge deal.
- pro: don't keep running once problems appear; consult an experienced small engine technician, who can test whether the engine is repairable or should be replaced.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- safety: first remove the spark plug wire.
- step 1 (drain plug or valve port): find it on the side of the engine by the dipstick or at the bottom of the oil pan under the mower; pan ready; remove the plug for a quick second, replace it, check the oil level.
- step 2 (oil filter): if the engine has one, loosen or remove it to drain a little oil; have a rag ready.
- step 3 (fill hole): many push mower engines have no drain plug; tip the mower to drain a little out of the fill hole.
- step 4 (oil extractor pump / evacuator): removes oil through a tube inserted in the fill hole.
- step 5 (turkey baster): good for small amounts; don't reuse for cooking.
- tools: drain pan, rag, oil evacuator, turkey baster

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: look up crankcase capacity in the owner's manual (or search online). Most push mower small engines take about 3/4 of a quart; a v-twin about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" engines (about 34 horsepower or larger) can take 3 quarts.
- tip: know your crankcase capacity and add only 1/2 quart at a time, checking the dipstick/gauge; near full, add a little at a time.
- tip: check twice, add only what the engine manufacturer requires.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: overfilled oil gives thick whitish-blue stinky smoke (see causes/overfilled-oil).

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-oil
- fact: crankcase pressure from overfilling can blow out seals and leak oil.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- fact: overfill can overheat the engine (see above).

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- fact: oil past the rings fills the combustion chamber; the piston can't compress oil or water; pulling the rope or starting won't let the piston move; piston may bend.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: oil from overfill fouls the plug.
