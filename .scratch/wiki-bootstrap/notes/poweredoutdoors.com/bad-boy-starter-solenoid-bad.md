- alias: "turn the key and all I hear is a click or hum"
- alias: "Bad Boy zero turn won't start, just clicks"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-starter-motor, bad-wiring-or-ground, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch
- fact: if the solenoid tests good but the mower still will not turn over or crank, check battery, cables, starter switch and safety switches.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on turning the key and the mower does not start (problems/engine-wont-crank); a wire gets hot and begins to smoke or melt.
- confirm: bypass test - the solenoid is likely bad when the starter motor turns the engine over with the solenoid bypassed.
- fact: a solenoid is an electromagnet on/off switch that engages the starter motor. Usually mounted on the starter motor but not required to be; some are mounted closer to the battery. Every Bad Boy electric-start engine has one.
- fact: inside are a spring and a copper plate; it can go bad if the spring gets weak or the copper plate corrodes. It can also fail as a result of a weak starter, bad battery or bad ground.
- fact: identify it: round or square, 3 or 4 posts, some attach on top of the starter. The positive (+) battery cable attaches to one side; following the positive cable from the battery finds the solenoid.
- fact: wiring schematics for 3- and 4-post solenoids differ by mower; some include wiring for lights and 12-volt ports.
- mistake: replacing the solenoid before checking for loose or corroded wires or bad ground, which can keep the solenoid from getting the power it needs.
- brand: Bad Boy zero-turn; Bad Boy operator's manual safety precautions apply.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver (long), continuity light, wrenches to check loose wires, needle-nose pliers (if screwdriver does not work), battery charger (optional)
- step 1: confirm the battery has a full charge; voltmeter should read at least 12.3 volts.
- step 2: ready the mower: set the parking brake, put in neutral, turn the key to the on position.
- step 3: bypass the solenoid: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the two cables - the cable from the battery and the cable to the starter. A spark is common. If the engine turns over while bypassed, the solenoid is probably bad. Needle-nose pliers can be used if the screwdriver does not work well.
- step 4: bench test for starter-mounted solenoids: remove the starter from the engine, attach the negative (-) charger clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid, to watch whether the starter works with the solenoid.
- safety: the bypass may spark (normal); always wear safety gear; follow the operator's manual.
- tip: check for loose or corroded wires or bad ground before replacing the solenoid.
