## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "lawn mower won't stay running"
- alias: "mower only runs for a few seconds then dies"
- alias: "mower runs a few minutes then dies"
- alias: "lawn mower keeps stalling"
- alias: "mower quits in the middle of the lawn"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, wrong-choke-setting, mowing-too-fast, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-engine-speed
- fact: A mower that starts and won't stay running is not getting sufficient air, fuel, and spark.
- fact (FAQ): runs only a few seconds then dies -> dirty carburetor, wrong choke setting, or bad gas causing fuel restrictions.
- fact (FAQ): runs a few minutes then dies -> most likely faulty ignition coil, bad gas cap, dirty carburetor, old gas.
- fact (FAQ): keeps stalling -> deposits from old gas restrict fuel lines, carburetor, fuel filter; also clogged air filter, bad ignition coil, bad gas cap, overworked engine.
- tip: Routine maintenance prevents this issue.
- pro: Engine or electrical problems may be better taken to a local mechanic; author has seen owners throw parts at a mower, which gets expensive. If unsure of cause, consult an experienced mechanic.
- safety: Follow operator's-manual safety instructions; consult a professional if you lack skills/knowledge or condition to do the repair safely.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies.
- fact: Fuel sitting a long time becomes less stable and attracts moisture from the air, causing corrosion in the fuel system. Ethanol plus moisture leave gummy deposits that clog the fuel system and keep fuel from reaching the engine.
- fix: Drain the old fuel from the tank. Mix a fuel stabilizer (Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL) with fresh gas and add to the tank.
- tip: Stabilizer also helps clean the fuel system and remove moisture, and keeps fuel stable so it lasts a little longer.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies.
- fact: Filter protects fuel system/engine from dirt and contaminants from the tank. May be an inline plastic filter between two fuel lines; if none is visible, the filter is probably at the bottom of the fuel tank. Clogs when not replaced regularly or when running dirty fuel.
- fix: Shut off fuel supply, remove clamps holding the inline filter, remove filter, insert new filter between the lines, install clamps, turn fuel on.
- fix: If no accessible inline filter, check the filter at the bottom of the tank when line flow is poor and the line isn't clogged: empty the tank first, then replace the filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies.
- fact: A restriction keeps a good fuel supply from reaching the carburetor.
- confirm: step 1: shut off fuel (shut-off valve to off; if no valve, clamp the line). step 2: remove the line from the carburetor (from the pump if the mower has a fuel pump). step 3: put the line in a container placed lower than the fuel tank (fuel follows gravity, can't flow uphill without a pump). step 4: turn fuel on / remove clamp. step 5: watch flow; if good, move on to other fuel issues.
- fix: If flow is poor, shut off fuel, remove the line section, spray carburetor cleaner into it to loosen the clog, push compressed air through, repeat as needed.
- fix: Replace the line if the restriction can't be removed, or if it is dry and cracked.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies; a sluggish mower that won't stay running often signifies a dirty carburetor.
- fact: Carburetor regulates fuel mixed with air for combustion. Old fuel makes small parts plug or stick.
- confirm: Confirm fuel reaches the carburetor (no restriction elsewhere). Then remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, start the mower. If it runs fine and then dies, the carburetor likely needs cleaning and inspection for failed parts.
- fix: Clean it; if cleaning doesn't work, rebuild or replace the carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies.
- fact: Dirt and clippings thrown into the air plug the filter; an engine lacking clean air fails to stay running. A dirty filter can also cause significant engine damage.
- fix: Replace the air filter annually and clean it several times in the mowing season.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- tip: Replace annually; clean several times through the mowing season.
- step 1 (paper filter): Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe out dirt in the housing; don't let dirt fall into the air intake.
- step 3: Tap the filter against a solid surface to knock loose dirt out.
- step 4: Hold it to a light; if light shines through the paper element, reuse; if not, buy a new one.
- step 5: Reinstall filter and housing cover.
- step 1 (foam filter): Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe dirt from the housing; don't let it fall into the intake.
- step 3: Inspect; dark spots, tears, or dry/brittle foam means replace. If good, proceed.
- step 4: Wash with water and mild dish soap, rinse soap out.
- step 5: Lay flat to dry; sun speeds drying.
- step 6: When completely dry, coat with foam air filter oil: fully covered but not dripping; wring out extra or absorb with a paper towel.
- step 7: Reinstall filter and cover.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies.
- fact: Choke restricts airflow so a higher fuel concentration enters the combustion chamber when starting a cold engine. If not adjusted after warm-up, the mower stops because of insufficient airflow.
- fix: Choke lever on (choke plate closed) to start a cold engine; then off for a warm engine.
- confirm: If lever position is right, verify the choke plate opens/closes properly; view it by removing the air filter.
- fix: If the plate isn't moving, use WD-40 to loosen the choke shaft and linkages.
- brand: Automatic choke mowers use a temperature gauge to open/close the choke; if not working, the choke may be stuck or the temperature gauge placed near the muffler may be faulty.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies; runs rough or quits.
- fact: Dirty = carbon or oil buildup on the tip. Damaged = cracked porcelain or burnt electrodes.
- fix: Replace if either. Gap to engine manufacturer's specification; ensure spark plug wires are securely attached.
- tip: Best practice is new spark plugs annually.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies (stops when hot).
- fact: Coil (armature) windings can separate and short out when the mower gets hot; plug then can't get the voltage to spark.
- confirm: Check for a break in continuity with an ohmmeter.
- fix: Replace a bad coil.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: engine-starts-then-dies.
- fact: Cap vent lets air pass; without it the tank acts like a vacuum and won't release fuel.
- confirm: After the mower stops, remove the fuel cap and start it. If it runs fine, put the cap back on while running; if it shuts off after running a while, the cap is bad.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap/unclogging the vent (doesn't always work); may need a new cap.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Overworking checklist item: running the mower too fast for mowing conditions can make the engine stop.
