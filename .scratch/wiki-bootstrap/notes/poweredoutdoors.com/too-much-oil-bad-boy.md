## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: engine damage from overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets, hydrolock; fouled plugs; smoke as excess oil burns off a hot engine; whitish-blue stinky smoke; engine runs poorly or won't run.
- safety: before troubleshooting remove spark plug wires and let engine cool; consult an experienced small engine mechanic for significant engine issues.
- fact: seals: crankcase pressure may blow out seals; an oil leak must be fixed right away or you may run out of oil and blow the engine (heat can spin a rod, weld rod to crankshaft, break the rod out the side of the block, weld piston to cylinder and seize).
- fact: overheating: crankshaft and rod must push through the extra oil; pressure loads internal parts; valve spring and rocker arm get hot, oil may not flow; aluminum engines get very hot; can pop out a valve guide or valve seat (needs engine scraped out by a mechanic).
- fact: hydrolock: oil gets into the cylinder; if oil gets past worn rings, the piston can't compress oil, engine turns over very hard, piston may bend when starting.
- fact: oil on plug inhibits spark; oil can reach the valve train and burn in the cylinder; clogged air filter may make the engine pull air and oil out of the crankcase; oil can pass through the carburetor into the air filter and stop the engine.
- fact: blown gaskets are costlier on a Bad Boy twin-cylinder engine than a push mower because the engine must be removed from the mower (more labor).
- tip: a little extra oil is "a huge deal"; check twice and add only what the engine manufacturer requires; stop running if engine problems appear; a technician can test whether it's repairable.
- tip: small engines have little oil pan room for error.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- causes-named: overfilled-oil
- fact: whitish-blue smoke from oil burning in the cylinder after oil reaches the valve train.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: overfilled oil can hydrolock engine so it won't turn when pulling rope or using electric starter; also fouled plug or oil in carburetor/air filter can stop it running (see overfilled-oil).

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- safety: remove spark plug wire first.
- step 1 (drain plug/valve port): find on side of engine by the dipstick or at bottom of oil pan; drain pan ready; remove plug for a quick second and replace; check level.
- step 2 (oil filter): loosen or remove filter to drain a little; rag ready.
- step 3 (fill hole): many push mower engines have no drain plug; tip the mower to drain a little from the fill hole.
- step 4 (oil extractor pump): oil evacuator removes oil through a tube inserted in the fill hole.
- step 5 (turkey baster): good for small amounts; don't reuse for cooking.
- tools: drain pan, rag, oil evacuator, turkey baster.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- tip: look up crankcase capacity in the owner's manual (or search online); most v-twin engines take approximately 2 quarts.
- tip: best way not to overfill: know capacity, initially add about 1/2 quart less than required, then add a little at a time checking dipstick/gauge until at the mark.
