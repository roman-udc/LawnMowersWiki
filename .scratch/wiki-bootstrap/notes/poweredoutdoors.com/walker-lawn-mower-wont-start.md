## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Walker zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure, blown-fuse
- fact: not getting fuel (old gas, plugged filter, clogged lines, bad pump, dirty carburetor), air (plugged air filter), spark (plug), or electrical fault

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: add fresh fuel; check for fuel leaks (may have lost fuel faster than normal)

## page: causes/old-fuel
- brand: Walker: unleaded, ethanol 10% or less, octane 87 or higher (Regular or E10); never E15, E30, E85
- fact: gas breaks down as soon as 30 days; ethanol moisture leaves gummy deposits as it evaporates, clogging fuel components
- fix: siphon pump drain, fresh gas with Sea Foam Motor Treatment

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: remove cap and start; if starts, reinstall cap and keep running; if it runs sluggish and dies, and restarts when cap removed again, bad cap
- fix: replace

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: dirt, carbon, oil cause intermittent starting; damaged, wrong gap, or loose wires prevent start
- confirm: remove plug(s); inspect for buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, burnt electrode: replace; slightly dirty: wire brush; check gap with feeler gauge to engine maker's spec; secure wire

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- tip: replace annually, clean several times per season; never run the mower without an air filter, even to finish the job

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove paper filter from housing
- step 2: wipe remaining dirt from housing without letting dirt fall into intake
- step 3: tap on solid surface; no compressed air (damages paper)
- step 4: hold to light; see light = reuse; none = replace
- step 5: install and attach housing cover

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace if plugged, damaged or leaking; replace annually, more often with dirty fuel; arrow on inline filter points in direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most lawn mowers use vacuum pumps (crankcase vacuum); electric/electronic pumps exist: confirm power to pump and consult engine dealer for test procedure and pressure specs
- symptom: housing crack or leaking seams
- confirm (vacuum): verify fuel at inlet port line; shut-off valve, fuel clamps or hose pinch pliers; with flow stopped remove line from carburetor into container; start flow and mower; watch for steady/pulsating flow; insufficient = replace pump

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove end of hose from section furthest from tank into container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without pump); start flow
- fix: remove line, carb cleaner, compressed air; replace if can't clear or line cracking

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: varnish plugs fuel jet or sticks small parts
- fix: if mechanical, take apart and clean with carb cleaner incl. float bowl and needle; replace if too dirty

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fix: clean corroded terminals with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 rounded tablespoons) and wire brush; test: about 12.7 V; charge if lower; replace if no hold

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: switches prevent deck running without operator; prevent start when parking brake not engaged
- confirm: multimeter or temporary bypass; never operate bypassed

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: multimeter; replace if bad

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- confirm: after plug verified good, check coil continuity with multimeter; replace if break

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: contributes to weak battery; bad stator or alternator among items
- pro: take to local Walker dealer; electrical parts usually can't be returned

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: must set parking brake, steering levers in open/outward position, PTO not engaged; choke lever on for cold engine, off after warm-up or engine runs sluggish and dies
- fix: follow Walker operator's manual

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: fuses located under the seat panel
- fix: replace with same amperage; repeat blowing: shop or dealer
