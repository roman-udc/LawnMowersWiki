## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "zero turn deck belt keeps slipping off"
- alias: "have to keep reinstalling the mower deck belt"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: summary: belt may come off when it is loose or worn; bearings bad in spindle housings or pulleys; brackets worn or out of place; or debris interfering with belt tension.
- safety: before working on the deck follow the operator's manual: remove ignition key, disconnect spark plug boots, set parking brake. Use jack stands to securely raise the deck (some zero turns require removing the deck). Wear heavy work gloves around blades.
- tip: deck belts are pricey; regularly clean and inspect belt, spindle housings, pulleys and brackets to prolong belt life.
- pro: if unable to find the solution or uncomfortable, go to a dealer or an experienced small-engine mechanic.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt rolls off pulleys when not in good condition; signs: stretching, cracking, shiny glazed appearance.
- fix: install a new zero-turn deck belt.
- symptom: shredded belt edges (belt weak, prone to breaking); two most common reasons: belt incorrectly routed around pulleys at install, and belt rubbing against a bracket that is out of place.
- confirm: for shredding, first verify correct routing per operator's manual or parts diagram (some zero turns have a routing decal on the mower); then look for brackets the belt catches on with the deck engaged; a shiny wear spot on a bracket shows where it rubs.
- fix: adjust brackets causing rub; some brackets have a bushing that may need replacing.
- fact: easier to install belt on some zero-turns than others.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: some zero turns use belt keepers (belt guides): brackets and rods that sit very close to the belt but do not touch it; they prevent the belt jumping off pulleys.
- symptom: bent keeper or guide.
- fix: bend it back with a hammer or vice grip; if it can't be returned to original position, replace the keeper.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fact: spindle housing assembly is the piece on the deck the blade attaches to.
- symptom: bad bearing lets blade wobble (extra play where blade attaches); at high speed wobble causes excessive vibration that can knock belt off.
- confirm: from under the deck, protect hands, grab each end of the blade and rock it up and down feeling for movement or hearing noise. If the blade is secure to the cutter housing but there is extra movement or knocking, remove the blade and disassemble the cutter housing assembly; highly likely bad bearing.
- fix: replace bearing; some models allow bearing-only replacement, others need the whole cutter housing assembly because the bearing is sealed.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: each pulley should sit flat and parallel to the deck, attached securely, with no movement where one side is higher than the other.
- symptom: a pulley not sitting flat causes vibration and the belt can come off.
- confirm: slowly spin each pulley by hand; resistance or noise from the bearing means bad bearing.
- fix: replace the pulley.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: zero-turn has an idler tensioner bracket; the spring attached can wear a larger hole in the bracket, causing vibration; the spring can also become weak or fall off, making the belt keep coming off.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: debris collecting on deck top builds up under the tensioner and stops it moving as designed, leaving the belt loose so it comes off.
- fact: debris stuck in pulley grooves (stones, wood chips, mud) can make belt roll off or break from stretching around the material.
- tip: clean mower after each mowing; author likes a leaf blower for dirt, leaves, clippings.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- symptom: oil leaking onto deck and pulleys gets on belt and makes it slip off; oil sitting a long time swells the belt.
- fix: fix the oil leak, clean oil off deck, replace belt with a new one.
- symptom: rust dries the belt out making it likely to crack and break.
- fix: clean rust off deck and components; replace very rusty pulleys (those where rust can't all be removed); install a new belt.
