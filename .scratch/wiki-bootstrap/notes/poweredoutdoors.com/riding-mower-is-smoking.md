## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "riding mower is smoking"
- alias: "white / black / blue smoke from the mower"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, spilled-oil-on-engine, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train
- fact: Smoke isn't always an expensive repair, but late catching can cost up to an engine replacement.
- fact: Smoke causes: clogged air filter, oil burning off a hot surface, wrong oil level, internal issues (valve train, piston rings). Also bad engine gasket.
- fact: Black smoke: engine running very rich (more fuel burned than air). Check air filter, then other airflow restrictions including a stuck choke.
- fact: Blue or white smoke: excess oil burning. Check oil level first, then piston rings, valve train, engine gasket.
- tip: Color confirms findings but doesn't replace the steps.
- tip: Check oil level and air filter before each mowing.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: black smoke (running rich); also smoke from the engine pulling air and oil out of the crankcase when it can't get air through the intake.
- tip: Replace annually; check and clean several times a season; dustier conditions need more.
- step: Paper: remove; wipe housing with clean dry cloth without dropping dirt in; tap against solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light shows, replace if not.
- safety: Never run the mower without an air filter, even temporarily; dirt in the engine can cause a significant repair bill.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Dipstick has markings; oil must be at the full level, not higher or lower.
- fact: Low oil: lack of lubrication, friction, heat; parts can melt and oil burn off creating smoke. Adding oil likely won't fix it; by the time it smokes there's already significant internal damage.
- pro: A qualified small engine mechanic should diagnose the extent of damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil builds crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and up the air intake through the valve train; burns off when hot and smokes. This smoke can plug the filter or oil can get on it; replace the air filter if so.
- fix: Remove a little oil via drain plug and oil filter, or use an oil evacuator or turkey baster at the oil fill; recheck dipstick; add/remove as needed to the engine manufacturer's recommended level.

## page: causes/spilled-oil-on-engine
- fact: Oil leaking onto a hot engine or muffler burns off as smoke; a bad engine gasket may be the leak source; find and replace the failing gasket. Oil spilled during an oil change can also smoke; rule out a spill first.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- confirm: Remove the spark plug (3/4 in or 5/8 in socket depending on engine model); oil on the plug may indicate a piston ring or valve train problem. It can't pinpoint which.
- pro: A mechanic must tear down the engine; may find scoring on the cylinder wall letting oil into the combustion chamber.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: A burnt valve (overheated) can cause smoke. An extremely hot muffler can indicate a burnt valve or timing problem.
- confirm: Only by removing the cylinder head and doing a leak-down test (by a small engine mechanic).
- fix: Valve and seat must be cut at certain angles to seat correctly.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: "Bad engine gasket" in the Source means an oil-leaking engine gasket onto hot surfaces; not specifically the head gasket.
