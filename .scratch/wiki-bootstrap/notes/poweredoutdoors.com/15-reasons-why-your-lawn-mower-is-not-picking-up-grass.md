## page: problems/not-bagging-grass
- alias: "mower isn't picking up grass"
- alias: "grass catcher / bagger keeps clogging"
- alias: "bags won't fill with grass"
- causes-named: clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet, clogged-deck-chute, worn-blade-sail, blade-installed-upside-down, worn-drive-belt, worn-blower-fan-or-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, bad-bagger-or-full-bag, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Zero turn or riding mower isn't picking up grass when air is restricted by clogs in the blower system, often from dull blades, debris buildup and a blocked inlet screen. A belt-driven collection system can also have a bad belt, damaged impeller or bad bearings.
- fact: Identify the collection system type first. Three types: flow, belt powered, pull-behind.
- fact: Flow system (simplest, riding/zero-turn): airflow created by the blades moves cut grass through a tube into the bag. On a push mower grass is pushed through the discharge chute directly into the bag.
- fact: Belt-driven system: an additional belt drives an impeller fan for greater air velocity; fan is in a blower housing attached to the deck side discharge; a tube runs from the housing to the bags.
- fact: Pull-behind system: draws grass through a tube off the deck into a trailer-type collector; a separate engine runs a vacuum system.
- safety: Follow the operator's manual safety instructions before diagnosing/repairing; consult a professional if lacking skills or not in condition to do the repair safely.
- safety: Raise the mower to look under the deck using jacks or jack stands to keep it in the air.
- tip: Order of checks in the Source: 1 clogged tubes, 2 clogged deck, 3 worn blades, 4 wrong/incorrectly installed blades, 5 clogged chute, 6 clogged inlet tube, 7 plugged inlet screen, 8 bad deck belt, 9 bad blower belt, 10 worn fan/housing, 11 bad blower bearings, 12 engine speed, 13 wet grass, 14 wrong/damaged bags, 15 bagger not secured.

## page: causes/clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet
- symptom: Bagger not picking up grass (problems/not-bagging-grass); old grass, sticks or debris plugging the tube.
- confirm: Remove the blower tubes and look down them for old grass, sticks, debris.
- fix: Scrape and clean out grass and debris. Before refitting, spray the inside of the tube with silicone spray to stop grass sticking for a while; doing this every other mow means grass rarely builds up.
- confirm: Inlet tube - remove bags/catchers from the grass hopper and look at the inlet tube for plugging; clean if necessary.
- fact: Inlet screen is the thing most people forget to check. It lets air out of the hopper so it can carry grass up the tubes. Usually at the top of the hopper on the side of the hopper cover not exposed to you, so it is out of sight and fills with clippings. If plugged, the air draft is lost and the system will not load the bags.
- fix: Clean the screen by hand; a wire brush for tough buildup; then spray with silicone spray and let dry.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- symptom: Chute plugged with old or wet grass; no air moving grass up the tubes.
- fix: Clean the chute; silicone spray inside the chute helps grass flow.
- confirm: Raise mower safely (jacks/jack stands), check the deck underside: packed with grass means it must be removed and the deck scraped clean. A grass-packed deck lacks airflow needed to pick up grass and for a nice cut, and may not push grass through the tubes.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Mower deck full of grass gets no airflow for pickup/cut; scrape deck clean (see causes/clogged-deck-chute for check). Wet grass sticks under the deck and in blower chute.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- symptom: not-bagging-grass.
- confirm: Inspect blades to see if the sails (the high side of the blade) are worn off the top. Worn sails cannot move grass around the underside of the deck or into the tubes.
- fix: Make sure blades are sharp and in good condition.
- fact: For bagging use a high-lift blade, which has a taller sail (fin) to create the lift and air movement needed.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- confirm: When inspecting blades on the deck make sure the sail is up when installed, pointed toward the deck; this creates a vacuum under the deck.
- fact: Source refers to its article "This is the Way the Blade Goes On A Lawn Mower" for blade types and installation.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- symptom: Belt-driven collection system not picking up grass.
- confirm: Deck belt (drives the blower belt for the fan): remove belt covers and check the drive belt from blade to blade. Cracked, glazed or shiny means replace. If the belt sits deep inside the pulleys, consider replacing.
- confirm: Blower belt (on the fan): replace if shiny with multiple cracks.

## page: causes/worn-blower-fan-or-bearing
- fact: The fan in the blower housing is called the impeller on belt-driven systems. Check fan blades and housing; worn fan/housing loses airflow. A large gap between fan and housing loses airflow.
- symptom: Bad bearings on the fan shaft. String or grass wrapped around the fan shaft can ruin the bearings.
- confirm: On most units the blower housing must be disassembled to check the fan.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: Bagger needs the engine at max horsepower. If the engine sounds like it is not giving all it has, look at the engine. Most engines should run about 3200 to 3650 rpm.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Never cut wet grass and expect good bagging. Wet grass is heavier, builds up and sticks under the deck and in the blower chute, restricting airflow. Wait until grass is dry; also less cleanout maintenance later.

## page: causes/bad-bagger-or-full-bag
- fact: Bags used to be cloth and sometimes repairable; today most are mesh or loosely knitted polyester/nylon and not easily repaired.
- fact: Bag life is two to three years depending on dirt/debris picked up and whether anything gets caught in the bag.
- fact: Newer bags have mesh vents to let air through; without bags that pass air the bags continually expand with grass and air buildup and get damaged.
- mistake: Bagger not assembled/secured properly lets air escape, reducing air movement and causing grass buildup.
