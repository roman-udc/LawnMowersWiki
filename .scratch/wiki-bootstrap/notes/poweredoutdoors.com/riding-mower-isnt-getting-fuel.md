## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "riding mower running sluggish and acts like it's going to shut off"
- alias: "riding mower isn't getting fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor
- fact: old fuel clogs and degrades fuel components causing a plugged filter, clogged lines, dirty carburetor, failed pump.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas begins to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol and the moisture it attracts separate from gasoline and leave a sticky substance when it evaporates, which gums fuel components.
- fact: ethanol okay in most vehicles but not small engines; use 10% or less.
- fix: drain into an approved gasoline container (plastic containers not designed for gas degrade and leak); siphon recommended; take old fuel to an authorized recycling center; refill with gas plus additive (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment: stabilizes and cleans).

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: use a stabilizer to reduce moisture and minimize clogging; ethanol 10% or less; 30 day breakdown.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace plugged filter; replace annually at riding mower service. Arrow on plastic housing points in direction of fuel flow: away from the tank and toward the carburetor.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: annual replacement; arrow points away from tank toward carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop/start flow while checking each section: shutoff valve at bottom of tank or hose pinch-off pliers; disconnect hose at the end furthest from the tank into a container; flow means that section isn't blocked.
- fix: remove the clogged line; spray carburetor cleaner in; blow compressed air through; replace if can't clear or if it's dry and cracked.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most mowers use a vacuum-style pump that builds pressure off the crankcase to pump fuel from tank to carburetor.
- confirm: look for damage, cracks, leaks. Then confirm flow to pump: stop flow, remove line from pump inlet into a container lower than the tank, restart flow; if no fuel, blockage is in the lines or filter. Reattach. Then remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start fuel flow and engine; should see steady or pulsating flow; if not, replace pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: regulates air and fuel; old fuel sitting in it clogs small components.
- confirm: before disassembling, remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner in the intake, start the mower; if it starts, runs, then shuts off, proceed to clean the carburetor.
- fix: clean (DIY for someone a little mechanical), or have a repair shop do it, or replace the carburetor.
