## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: service every 50 hours or annually, whichever first. Also clean air filter, grease, check tire pressures, sharpen blades, scrape deck several times per season. Skipping maintenance leads to starting, overheating and poor cutting.
- fact: checklist: prepare mower; change oil and filter; replace spark plug; clear debris from exhaust/engine/transmission; clean battery terminals; clear debris around electrical/wiring; replace air filter; replace fuel filter; check fuel system; check tire pressures and lug nuts; clean top of deck; inspect belt/pulleys/spindle housings; sharpen or change blades; scrape deck; level deck; lubricate; wash.
- tools: engine oil, oil filter, air filter, fuel filter, spark plugs, blades, socket or spanner wrenches, drain pan, funnel, filter wrench, rags, work gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover, deck scraper / wire brush / metal putty knife
- step 1: park on flat level surface; cardboard/newspaper for drips; run a few minutes to warm oil; shut off.
- step 2: set parking brake and chock wheels; remove ignition key; disconnect spark plug wires; read owner's manual.
- step 3: change engine oil and filter (see procedures/change-the-engine-oil).
- step 4: replace spark plug(s) annually, gap per engine manufacturer.
- step 5: remove engine shroud, clear dirt/debris around engine, exhaust and cooling fins; replace damaged fins; make sure heat shield is securely in place.
- step 6: clean battery terminals with 2 cups water and 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda and wire brush; thin coat of white lithium grease.
- step 7: remove debris around electrical components and wiring (debris holds moisture, causes premature corrosion).
- step 8: air filter: author starts each season with new filter, cleans it several times per season; replace both inner and outer filter if both present; don't knock dirt into intake; wipe housing.
- step 9: fuel filter annually (ethanol gum, old fuel softens plastic); arrow on filter side points toward carburetor, away from tank.
- step 10: check fuel lines for dry/brittle; replace old; author recommends worm gear clamps instead of pinch-style clamps; check for leaks.
- step 11: check lug nuts; tire pressure as shown on tire sidewalls and equal on both sides (uneven pressure makes deck unlevel and uneven cut).
- step 12: remove deck: parking brake on, PTO off, engine off, deck lowest, key out, spark plug wires disconnected. If manual PTO: loosen idler pulley hardware (don't remove), remove hex screw holding rod over engine pulley and the rod, remove PTO cable and unhook spring by removing pin to deck bracket. Then: ratchet in square hole of idler bracket to move pulley forward; remove belt around PTO pulley; remove deck lift arms (cotter pin at end of each arm near rear, left and right; slide deck pin out); remove front lift rod (cotter pin; can raise deck lift lever to highest); slide deck out.
- step 13: clean top of deck (debris restricts tensioner bracket movement, affects cut and belt coming off); remove belt covers and clean around pulleys; dry debris plus belt friction heat is a fire hazard.
- step 14: belt worn when cracked, stretched, shredded, glazed shiny, or sitting deep in pulley grooves; pulleys parallel to deck and rotate smoothly by hand; resistance or bearing noise = replace; clean rust, replace if very rusty (rusty belt dries, cracks, breaks); rock blades up and down to check spindle bearings; movement or knock means remove housing, replace bearings or whole cutter housing assembly.
- step 15: replace blades annually (author's habit) and sharpen through season; sharpen after about 25 hours, more in sandy conditions.
- step 16: scrape deck with deck scraper, metal putty knife or stiff wire brush; use deck wash port with garden hose if equipped; may still need scraping.
- step 17: reinstall deck in reverse; level deck (see below).
- step 18: check safety system (won't start without parking brake; engine shuts off when leaving seat with PTO engaged); see manual.
- step 19: replace damaged/worn/missing parts; secure hardware.
- step 20: grease zerks on front wheels, front axle, deck wheels; lubricate lift linkages, pivot points, brake (author uses high-performance grease tube and spray lubricant).
- step 21: wash with garden hose; if pressure washer, not high pressure (damages sealed bearings); dry completely, sunny day/breeze, leaf blower helps; moisture causes corrosion.
- time: annually or every 50 hours.
- safety: park brake, chocks, key out, spark plug wires disconnected.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: change oil and filter every 50 hours or annually; new engine: change after the initial 5-hour break-in.
- step 1: run few minutes to warm oil; open hood, find drain plug low on engine; clean around plug.
- step 2: drain pan under plug, remove plug, remove fill cap for better flow. Some Craftsman mowers have an oil drain port with attachable hose: open port, attach hose, other end into drain pan.
- step 3: filter wrench to remove oil filter, install new one; reinstall drain plug after drained.
- step 4: fill crankcase with manufacturer-recommended type/quantity. Most gas engines in Craftsman mowers use 10W-30 air-cooled engine oil. Level cannot be too low or too high.
- step 5: reinstall spark plug boots, run engine a few minutes, shut off, let cool, recheck dipstick, add if needed.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- tools: measuring tape, wrenches
- step 1: flat level surface, deck lift in middle position.
- step 2: side-to-side: measure ground to blade tip on right then left. If different, adjust left adjustment rod under the rear fenders: loosen upper nut (don't remove), turn lower nut clockwise to raise left side of deck, counter-clockwise to lower; when even, tighten the top nut.
- step 3: pitch front-to-rear: flat surface, deck lift in middle; front of deck must be 1/4" to 3/8" lower than the rear. Adjust at stabilizer bracket (outer and inner nut at front): loosen outer nut (don't remove); tighten inner nut to raise front, loosen lock nut to lower; retighten outer nut once front measures 1/4"-3/8" lower than rear.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: service interval 50 hours or annually; remove debris before each mowing (debris near engine/transmission causes overheating; on deck interferes with tensioner; under deck prevents good cut).

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: wash with hose, not high pressure; dry fully.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- fact: remove engine shroud, remove dirt around fins, replace damaged fins, secure heat shield.
