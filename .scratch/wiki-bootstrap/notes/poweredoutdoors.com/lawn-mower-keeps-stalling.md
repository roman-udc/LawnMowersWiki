## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "lawn mower keeps shutting off"
- alias: "mower stalls and dies while mowing"
- alias: "mower running sluggish then stalling"
- causes-named: old-fuel, empty-fuel-tank, wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-deck-chute, mowing-too-fast, bad-safety-switch
- fact: Stalls when it lacks spark, fuel or air, or when the engine is under load.
- fact: Source lists 12 reasons: old gasoline, insufficient fuel level, incorrect choke setting, plugged air filter, bad spark plug, plugged fuel filter, clogged fuel lines, dirty carburetor, bad fuel cap, plugged mower deck, incorrect operation for conditions, bad safety switch.
- fact: Overheating also shuts a mower down; Source links "7 Things That Can Cause a Lawn Mower to Overheat".

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Gasoline begins breaking down as quickly as 30 days after purchase. Ethanol (usually corn-based in the US) is fine in most vehicles but not good for small engines; use unleaded with no more than 10% ethanol.
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture; water is corrosive; the water-ethanol mix leaves gummy deposits restricting fuel flow.
- fix: Drain the tank with a fuel siphon pump into a container for proper disposal; refill with fresh gas plus an additive like Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans).
- fact: A 2-cycle engine needs oil mixed with gas and has slightly different fuel requirements (Source links a separate article).

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Tank not empty but mowing on a hillside or uneven ground can interrupt fuel supply.
- fix: Add more fuel if it stalls on slopes. Avoid steep inclines (safety hazard).

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies (stalls after warming up)
- fact: Choke restricts airflow for cold starts (richer mixture); once warm it must be moved so the engine gets enough air.
- fix: Check the choke lever is in the correct position.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- tip: Replace annually; clean several times in the season. Never run without an air filter, not even briefly.
- fix: Paper step 1: remove filter from housing (cover held by clips, knobs or wing nuts).
- fix: Paper step 2: wipe out dirt in housing, keep it out of the intake.
- fix: Paper step 3: tap on a solid surface.
- fix: Paper step 4: hold to light; reuse if light shines through; replace if no light, oil-covered or extremely dark.
- fix: Paper step 5: reinstall and reattach cover.
- fix: Foam step 1-2: remove foam filter, wipe housing.
- fix: Foam step 3: replace if brittle, torn or damaged.
- fix: Foam step 4: wash with mild dish detergent and water; rinse until water runs clear.
- fix: Foam step 5: lay flat to air dry; when dry coat with foam filter oil, lightly saturated, not dripping; reinstall.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove filter (cover by clips, knobs or wing nuts); wipe housing, keep dirt from intake.
- step 2: Paper: tap, hold to light, replace if oil-covered/very dark. Foam: inspect, wash with mild dish detergent, rinse clear, air dry flat, coat with foam filter oil (light, not dripping).
- step 3: Reinstall and reattach cover.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies (intermittent running issues)
- confirm: Remove boot and plug; inspect for dirty tip, broken porcelain, burnt electrode.
- fix: Clean a dirty tip with a wire brush; replace if very dark or damaged. Gap to manufacturer spec; securely attach the boot (a loose plug wire causes running problems).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fix: Shut off fuel with the shut-off valve (or crimp the line with pinch pliers), remove the filter, install a new one with the arrow toward the carburetor and away from the tank. Replace annually, more often with dirty fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- confirm: Check each section of the line by stopping/starting fuel flow (shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank or pinch pliers) and checking flow from each section.
- fix: Remove the clogged section, spray carburetor cleaner, blow out with compressed air; repeat. If it will not clear or the line is dry and cracked, replace the fuel line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Old gas clogs the fuel jet and makes internal components stick.
- confirm: Confirm fuel reaches the carburetor, remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner in the intake, start. If it runs well then shuts off or runs sluggish, suspect the carburetor.
- fix: Clean the carburetor; if cleaning does not help or parts are damaged, rebuild or replace.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- confirm: Remove the cap, start and run; if it runs fine, reinstall the cap while running; if stalling returns with the cap on, the vent is not passing air.
- fix: Replace the cap; cleaning may not fix it.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies (plugged mower deck loads the engine and bogs it)
- fix: Scrape the deck regularly with a deck scraper, metal putty knife or wire brush.
- tip: Avoid cutting wet or damp grass; dull blades magnify the problem.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Tall, thick or wet grass needs a slower ground speed. Always operate at full throttle; slow down when the engine bogs.
- tip: For tall grass make multiple cuts: highest cutting height first, then lower for subsequent cuts.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Safety switch (push mower safety bar, riding mower seat switch) shuts the mower down when it doesn't sense the operator; a bad connection makes it keep stalling. Very bumpy conditions can bounce the operator off the seat switch.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter; may temporarily bypass for troubleshooting only.
- safety: Never operate without the safety switch or with it bypassed.
