## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Greenworks mower bad or uneven cut"
- alias: "grass tips turn brown after mowing"
- causes-named: bad-tire (damaged wheel), wrong-tire-pressure, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, wrong-cutting-height, clogged-deck-chute, unlevel-deck, wrong-engine-speed (blade speed too slow), mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell, uneven lawn (scalping)
- safety: shut off mower, remove battery (and safety key), wait for all parts to stop before troubleshooting

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: damaged wheel or low tire makes mower sit unlevel so deck is closer to ground on one side
- confirm: zero-turn air-filled tires: gauge front and rear; fill to Greenworks spec in operator's manual; left and right rear tires must be equal
- fact: push mower wheels can loosen or be damaged: tighten hardware, replace damaged wheels

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: lawn turns brown a day or two after mowing; grab a handful and look for tears in tips
- fix: sharpen if still in good condition; replace if extremely worn, gouged, rounded blade ends, or sail thinner than center; sail = high side that creates airflow under the deck
- fact: bent from hitting rock, stump, toy; causes uneven cut and vibration; walk the lawn first
- confirm: park on flat level surface, remove safety key and battery pack(s), wait for parts to stop; remove blade and lay it on a new Greenworks blade; if no new blade, take to Greenworks service center or parts store
- safety: never straighten a bent blade to reuse; weakened metal can break off and fly out at high speed

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: replace blade if sail is thinner than center (see above)

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: balance: equal weight on both ends; blades go out of balance from uneven wear by sand or by removing more metal from one end when sharpening
- confirm: blade balancer (relatively inexpensive, worth it; unbalanced blade may cause significant damage), or hang blade on a nail in wall with head sticking out 3/4" to 1"; remove metal from the end hanging lower until level

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: install with sails up (high sides that create airflow to toss grass); sail facing ground can damage the blade motor; beats up and tears grass; can hit high spots and damage motor

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- fact: blade not tight won't rotate and mower won't cut; torque blade bolt to spec in operator's manual

## page: causes/wrong-cutting-height
- fact: deck too low can't handle the amount of grass; raise to higher setting for thick tall grass

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: plugged deck leaves insufficient airflow area under deck; also works the motor harder
- fix: clean deck regularly with deck scraper or wire brush; avoid wet grass (clumps and sticks)

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- fact: zero-turn deck must be level side to side or cut is higher on one side; adjust per operator's manual

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: some Greenworks mowers have a blade speed setting to conserve battery; reduced speed gives bad cut in thick grass; tall/thick grass needs faster blade tip speed; if speed increased and height right but still bad cut, contact Greenworks service center (possible blade motor or power supply problem)

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: too fast pushes grass over instead of cutting; slow down in thick, tall or wet grass; some models have a speed setting; non-self-propelled push mowers: adjust walking speed

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: deck shell designed with airflow; blade air creates suction that stands grass up; damaged shell hurts cut; attempt to restore shape, replace if can't

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: wet grass clumps on deck; heavy load with thick, tall, wet grass
