## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Ariens mower belt keeps coming off"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Also a shredded belt (installed wrong or rubbing on a bracket) and debris in pulley grooves.
- safety: Make sure the mower can't start while working on the deck.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt doesn't sit securely against pulleys though installed correctly (stretched); cracking, fraying, shiny glaze; sits deep in pulley grooves.
- fix: Replace. Author prefers OEM belts from an Ariens dealer; an automotive-store match probably won't be exact, performs and lasts worse, and an incompatible belt can pose safety risks.
- fact (shredded edges): find the cause first or you'll keep buying belts. (1) Incorrect routing: follow owner's manual or the routing decal. (2) A metal bracket rubbing the belt: look for a shiny smooth spot; bend the bracket back, replace if you can't; if it's a bracket with a bushing you may need to replace the bushing or the bracket assembly. A shredded belt is weak and may break.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Idler tensioner bracket has a pulley on one side and a spring on the other; the spring weakens or falls off; the bracket hole wears larger and causes excessive vibration. Replace worn bracket or spring.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Debris under the tensioner stops it moving freely, so the belt loses tension and falls off. Mud packs in pulley grooves; stones, acorns lodge there; the belt must stretch around it and slips or breaks. Clean grooves.
- safety: Debris plus a hot belt's friction can start a small fire.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: A bad bearing lets the pulley sit higher on one side, not flat and parallel to the deck; belt slips off.
- confirm: Spin each pulley slowly by hand; feel restriction, up-and-down movement or bearing noise; replace the pulley. Some pulleys allow bearing replacement; others have sealed bearings and must be replaced as assembly.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fact: Bad spindle bearing wobbles the blade; with the deck engaged this is a serious vibration that can knock the belt off.
- confirm: Wear work gloves; access underside of deck; grab each side of a blade and rock it up and down; no movement or knocking when good. If movement or noise, remove spindle housings and check the bearing.
- fix: Bearing replaceable in most Ariens spindle housings; some models need the whole housing assembly.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil leak drips onto deck and belt, belt slips off; belt may swell. Find and fix the leak, clean oil off deck and pulleys, replace the belt. Rust dries the belt prematurely so it cracks and breaks; clean rust off deck components, replace very rusty ones, then fit a new belt.
