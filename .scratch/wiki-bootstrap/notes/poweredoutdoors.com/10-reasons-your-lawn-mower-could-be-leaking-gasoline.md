## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "lawn mower is leaking gas"
- alias: "gas puddle under my mower"
- alias: "I smell strong gas odor but can't find the leak"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, bad-gas-cap-seal, cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: strong gas odor; small dead-grass spots in the yard the day after mowing (gasoline kills grass).
- confirm: park the mower on cardboard overnight and look for gas spots to locate the leak.
- confirm: smelling gas but never finding the source suggests the fuel cap; gas evaporates around the leak before it is seen.
- tip: Source says finding and repairing a fuel leak "isn't too difficult" on a mower.
- safety: follow operator's manual safety instructions; consult a professional if lacking skills or knowledge or not in condition to do the repair safely.
- fact: Source lists 10 leak points in order: carb bowl o-ring, stuck float, stuck float needle, fuel lines, fuel tank, fuel valve, fuel filter, fuel pump, gas cap, primer bulb.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas) leak at the bottom of the carb bowl, the most likely first leak spot.
- fact: a very thin o-ring (looks like a rubber band) sits between the carb base and the bowl. It is near engine heat and cycles hot to cold, which hardens it and it loses sealing strength.
- symptom: leak out of the carburetor air intake port means the float may be stuck. The float regulates how much gas is stored in the bowl and effectively turns gas on/off from the tank; if stuck it will not shut the gas off and fuel runs out of the carb.
- fix: stuck float: take the carb apart to find why; carb can be cleaned and rebuilt or replaced (or have a local engine dealer check it).
- fact: the needle (with the float) keeps gas flowing to the bowl; it can stick.
- tip: gently tap the side of the carburetor with the handle end of a hammer to free a stuck needle; works once or twice only, then the carb must be taken apart, the reason found, and the carb rebuilt.
- fix: replace the bowl o-ring gasket: get make, model and spec number off the engine and take to local dealer for the correct o-ring.
- step 1: shut fuel off to the mower.
- step 2: unscrew the bowl screw and drop the bowl.
- step 3: replace the gasket; reinstall bowl and screw.
- step 4: turn fuel back on, wipe down the bowl and check for leaks.
- tools: screwdriver; replacement o-ring gasket.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: dry-rotted or cracked fuel lines leak (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).
- confirm: follow fuel lines from the tank; check areas held by clamps for damage from the clamps.
- fix: replace lines that are dry-rotted or cracked.
- tip: if factory pinch-style clamps are used, consider replacing them with worm-gear style clamps.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: leak from the tank (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).
- confirm: on an aged steel tank, inspect for a small rust hole; on a plastic tank, check the molded seams, which can separate over time.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: leak at the fuel valve at the bottom of the tank (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).
- fact: valves may be plastic or metal; both styles are prone to leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: plastic inline fuel filter softened/melted from gas sitting in it (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).
- confirm: ends may crush when you go to remove an old filter because they became soft or look melted. Bad gasoline can cause this.
- tip: author recommends using recreational fuel in mowers (see choosing-the-right-gas).

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: leak from pump seams or diaphragm (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).
- fact: most mower fuel pumps are plastic; gas sits in them for years and they rot from the inside out; diaphragm leaks and seams can leak like tank seams.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: gas leaks out of the top of the tank at the cap when fuel sloshes while mowing (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas); constant gas smell with no visible source.
- confirm: inspect cap for proper venting and sealing; shake the mower so fuel splashes around the cap and see if a wet spot develops.
- fix: replace with a new gas cap.

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: some small mowers have a primer bulb on the side of the air filter; it can leak fuel when it fills, leak where it connects to the fuel line, or become weak and brittle with age (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Source includes a carburetor cleaning section (see procedures/clean-the-carburetor). Hard crusty white buildup in the carb is fuel additives including ethanol.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step 1: remove the air filter, spray carb cleaner into the air intake, start the engine. If it fires up but will not stay running, the carb must be opened.
- step 2: gather pliers, screwdriver, sockets and ratchets so parts are not destroyed.
- step 3: take a photo of the carburetor (linkage and springs especially) for reassembly.
- step 4: remove throttle cable and choke cable (not every mower has them).
- step 5: undo the filter housing and the nuts and screws holding the carburetor.
- step 6: remove springs slowly so they are not stretched; may need to twist the carb. Watch the gasket between engine block and carburetor so it does not tear.
- step 7: remove the screw at the bottom of the float bowl; bowl has gas in it so have a rag ready.
- step 8: remove the bowl without damaging the o-ring around it.
- step 9: inspect the stem that hangs from the center of the carb for clogged holes; plugged holes (old fuel) will not draw fuel up to the jet. Clean with thick wire (use a flashlight), then rinse with carb cleaner.
- step 10: inspect carb for hard crusty white buildup (fuel additives including ethanol); remove as much as possible, "nearly impossible to get it all out".
- step 11: reassemble in reverse order using the photo.
- step 12: add new fuel containing fuel stabilizer, let it fill the bowl, start; with a pull cord it may take several pulls then should keep running.
- safety: do not get carb cleaner or any chemical on the bowl o-ring; it stretches out and cannot be reused.
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, sockets, ratchet, carb cleaner, thick wire, flashlight, rag, phone camera.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: author recommends recreational fuel in mowers because bad gasoline can soften/melt plastic filters.
