## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Scag offers gas and diesel zero-turns; this article covers gas engines only.
- fact: Scag mowers require unleaded gas, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Never use higher ethanol content; it can damage the fuel system and engine.
- fact: Manufacturer says 10% ethanol or less is OK, but the author says the best gas for Scag is ethanol-free (opinion).
- fact: Stations now offer REC-90, E15, E85 in addition to regular, premium, diesel; Source says they contain up to 0%, 15% and 85% ethanol respectively (REC-90 is ethanol-free recreation fuel). E15 and E85 exceed the 10% limit.
- fact: Gas begins to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; buy only what you can use within 30 days.
- fact: Ethanol is plant-based and attracts moisture; the water-ethanol mixture corrodes components and leaves gummy deposits that block the fuel system. The mixture separates from gas over time and sinks to the bottom of the tank, and runs very hot through the engine, potentially damaging it.
- fact: Ethanol-free (recreation) fuel is best because it does not break down like fuel with ethanol, but costs more, especially in canisters at hardware stores; buying at the pump is cheaper. Source points to www.pure-gas.org to find stations selling ethanol-free fuel.
- tip: Author adds fuel stabilizer to every tank; likes Sea Foam Motor Treatment (claims stabilizes fuel up to two years, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system). STA-BIL is named as a good alternative.
- tip: Use stabilizer when storing extra fuel and when storing the mower with fuel in the off-season.
- tip: Store fuel in a dry area so water does not mix in.
- tip: Buy the best quality low-ethanol gas you can afford (opinion).

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Same stabilizer and 30-day freshness facts as above; store fuel dry.
