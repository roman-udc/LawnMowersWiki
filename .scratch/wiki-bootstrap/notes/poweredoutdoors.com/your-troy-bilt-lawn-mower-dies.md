## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Troy-Bilt comes to a stop and shuts down while mowing"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, dirty-carburetor, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Fuel or air restriction; low oil, blocked cooling fins and a clogged deck can overheat and shut the engine down.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- tip: Author has had customers troubleshoot filters and switches before checking fuel. Fill with fresh gas.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel begins to lose combustion properties after about a month; ethanol attracts moisture and gums the fuel system; ethanol can damage a small engine's fuel system and engine.
- tip: Use fuel within 30 days; buy from busy gas stations; do not leave fuel outdoors where it attracts moisture; store in a dry place; add stabilizer if unable to use within 30 days. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment (petroleum-based; stabilizes, removes moisture, cleans).
- fix: Drain the tank; add fresh fuel with additive. Dispose of old gas by recycling; do not dump on the ground.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Stations offer E10 unleaded, E85 (85% ethanol), diesel, non-ethanol; pump handle colours and setups vary; read the label.
- brand: Troy-Bilt requires min octane 87 and no more than 10% ethanol (sold as regular or E10). Less ethanol is better.
- fix: Drain, flush the tank, refill with fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: dies while mowing
- fact: White hard crusty buildup is fuel additives including ethanol; nearly impossible to remove all.
- fix: See procedure below, or have a shop clean it, or replace.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, sockets, ratchets
- confirm: Remove the air filter, spray carb cleaner into the intake, start; if it fires but still won't stay running, go inside the carburetor.
- step 1: Take several photos for reassembly.
- step 2: Remove throttle cable and choke cable if present.
- step 3: Remove the filter housing, then the screws and nuts holding the carburetor.
- step 4: Remove springs slowly without stretching; may need to twist the carburetor; do not tear the gasket between the engine block and carburetor.
- step 5: Remove the bottom screw of the float bowl (where gasoline is stored); have a rag ready; do not damage the rubber-band-like o-ring around it.
- mistake: Do not get carb cleaner or any substance on that o-ring; it stretches and cannot be reused.
- step 6: Check the stem hanging from the center of the carburetor; clogged holes will not draw fuel up to the jet; use a flashlight and thick wire to clear them, rinse with carb cleaner.
- step 7: Remove the hard crusty white buildup as much as possible.
- step 8: Reassemble in reverse using the photos.
- step 9: Add fresh fuel plus stabilizer; let it fill the bowl; start.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- brand: Troy-Bilt needs a pump when the carburetor sits higher than the supply; most use a vacuum fuel pump that builds pressure.
- confirm: Damage or leaks visible; otherwise use the shut-off valve or hose clamps to stop and start flow; verify fuel at the inlet port, then check the outlet port for pumped fuel.
- fix: Replace if no steady or pulsating flow from the outlet.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- confirm: Use clamps or the valve to test flow in the lines on each side of the inline filter.
- fix: Replace; relatively inexpensive; author changes it once a year.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air, reinstall; replace if unclearable or dry and cracked (buy online or hardware store).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: Remove the cap and let the mower run (do not let debris into the fuel system); if fine, put the cap back on; if it dies, the cap is not venting.
- fix: Clean the vent or buy a new cap.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fix: Remove paper filter, tap on a hard surface, hold to light; reinstall if light shows, replace if not.
- fact: Life depends on how much dust and debris the mower kicks up; check often.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins create air movement around the block and cylinder head; damaged or packed fins cause overheating and shutdown.
- fix: Remove the engine cover, clean the fins, replace damaged fins.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Too little oil: more friction from lack of lubrication; overheating and dying.
- fix: Change oil per Troy-Bilt's recommendations; fill to the correct level.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil can cause smoking and dying while mowing, and significant internal engine damage, possibly engine replacement.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: Deck packed with grass and dirt makes the engine work harder; dull blades add draw, engine can overheat and shut down.
- fix: Inspect the deck for damage, scrape it, sharpen blades; always run at full throttle; avoid cutting wet or very tall grass; adjust speed to conditions.
