## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Toro push mower won't start"
- alias: "Toro Recycler 22 won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, sheared-flywheel-key, clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-safety-switch, bad-recoil-starter, low-compression
- fact: Most likely: dirty carburetor, clogged air filter, dirty spark plug, bad fuel cap, clogged fuel filter, sheared flywheel key, old fuel.
- safety: Remove the spark plug wire/plug when repairing.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, blown-fuse (electric start models)

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: Check for a fuel leak if running out sooner than expected. 4-cycle: 87+ octane, max 10% ethanol. Older 2-cycle Toro engines need a gas and oil mixture.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- confirm: Old gas looks darker yellow than fresh and has a strong pungent odor.
- tip: Fresh fuel min 87 octane; max 10% ethanol; store in a sealed container in a cool dry location (gas attracts moisture); use within 30 days; mix with stabilizer.
- fix: Remove old fuel; add fresh gas with additive (Sea Foam Motor Treatment: cleans, reduces moisture, stabilizes).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Without a vent the tank acts like a vacuum; old fuel can clog the vent.
- confirm: Run with the cap off then on. Fix: clean the vent or replace the cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: Check for spark with a spark plug tester; if poor, inspect the wire and plug.
- fix: Remove plug from the cylinder head; replace if damaged, extremely dirty or burnt electrodes; if slightly dirty wire-brush clean (don't damage the metal); check gap to spec; install; attach wire securely.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Also called ignition module. Check the plug first. Spark plug wire cracked, worn or with exposed wires means replace the coil (exposed wires can ground out and fail the coil). Check continuity (spark tester or multimeter); replace if break.

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- fact: Sheared key throws engine timing off so spark isn't at the right time.
- confirm: Inspect the blade; damage means an impact may have sheared the key (remove plug wire first). Pull the starter rope; it springs back and doesn't pull smoothly.
- fix: Replace the key.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Never run without a filter even briefly. Clogged filter stops starting.
- step (paper): tap out dirt; hold to light; reuse if light passes else replace; install and cover.
- step (foam): replace if dark spots or dry and brittle; else wash with mild dish soap and water; rinse and lay flat to dry (sun speeds it); lightly oil with foam filter oil, not dripping; install and cover.
- tip: Remove filter carefully to keep dirt out of the intake; wipe housing with a clean cloth.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke plate closed to start a cold engine, open for a warm engine. Most current Toro push mowers have an auto-choke; older have manual.
- confirm: Remove the air filter to see the plate and confirm it opens and closes; free a stuck choke by lubricating linkages and choke shaft with carburetor cleaner. Manual: lever on = plate closed; lever off (on some models, fast throttle position) = plate open. Auto: closed when cold, opens as the engine warms.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: May be an inline filter between the fuel lines, or a filter in the bottom of the tank (check with the cap off and a flashlight).
- fix: Bottom-of-tank filter requires draining the tank (siphon pump best); remove the fuel line from the bottom of the tank and then the narrow filter; replace with a clean one.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air through, repeat; replace if the clog can't be removed.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fix: Clean yourself if mechanical, else a repair shop; replace if very bad.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Bail lever (let go and the engine stops). Follow the cable down to the switch; it must be grounded to stop the mower when the bail lever is released and not grounded when the bail is pulled against the handle to run.
- safety: Don't operate without the safety switch.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Restring if the rope is off the recoil; broken pulley, spring or clips may need replacing; compare part prices with the full assembly.

## page: causes/low-compression
- fact: Poor compression indicates head gasket, valve, cylinder wall or piston ring damage.
- confirm: Quick check: remove the spark plug wire and slowly turn the blade in reverse to the compression stage; you should feel some resistance. This doesn't prove sufficiency; do a compression test or leak-down test. With automatic compression release (ACR) engines, prefer a leak-down test unless the manufacturer gives ACR compression test specs.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact (electric start): secure cables; clean with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); test battery with a multimeter; replace a bad battery.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: Nothing happens when switch pressed; test with a multimeter; replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; wire at the solenoid hot, smoking or melting; replace solenoid/starter if bad.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse near the battery; replace with the same amperage.
