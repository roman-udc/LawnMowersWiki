## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "click, click, click when I turn the key"
- alias: "riding mower clicks but won't start or turn over"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, faulty-charging-system
- fact: Most likely an electrical or charging system problem. If battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid are good, suspect the starter motor.
- safety: Always disconnect the negative (black) battery cable before electrical repairs; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: problems/engine-wont-crank
- confirm: Check cables to the battery, wiring from battery to solenoid, and solenoid-to-starter wiring for good, secure connections.
- fix: Disconnect the battery (negative first), clean corrosion with a baking soda bath: 2 cups water + 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda, with a wire brush. Replace bad terminals or wiring; add dielectric grease to slow further corrosion. Replace cables that keep coming loose or are broken (bad cables can contribute to a bad battery).

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- symptom: problems/engine-wont-crank
- confirm: Check the black cable from the battery to the mower frame; check the solenoid ground. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: Replace a damaged battery-to-frame cable; remove corrosion at the battery and solenoid grounds for good contact.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: problems/engine-wont-crank (just clicks)
- confirm: Multimeter red/black prongs to matching terminals. Common mower batteries are 12 V. Reading between 11.5 and 12.7: 11.5 V almost dead, 12.7 V fully charged.
- fix: Charge it; if it will not hold a charge, replace. A battery that dies but charges may point to a charging system problem.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear gear protecting eyes and skin from acid and electrical shock.
- step 1: Access the battery and terminals (may need a screwdriver to uncover).
- step 2: Leave the battery in its casing with terminal cables attached.
- step 3: Connect charging cables positive (red, plus sign) first, red clamp to positive terminal, black to negative.
- step 4: Touch only the rubber coating of the cables and clamps.
- step 5: Set the charger voltage (average 12 V) and amps: start at 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps; more amperage charges faster.
- step 6: If the charger has a gauge, keep it connected until fully charged.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step: Measure voltage with a multimeter; 11.5 V almost dead, 12.7 V fully charged.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: problems/engine-wont-crank (keeps clicking)
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnetic on-off switch that engages the starter motor. Most mount on the starter but need not; follow the positive wire from the battery to find it.
- fact: Failure causes: internal spring weakens, copper plate corrodes, bad ground, weak starter or bad battery.
- confirm: Test with a volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light and wrenches (Source links "How to Tell Your Lawn Mower Solenoid is Bad"). If bypassing the solenoid starts the mower, replace it.
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches
- fact: Bypassing the solenoid and starting the mower confirms a bad solenoid.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- symptom: problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: Only after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid.
- confirm: The starter can be removed and tested.
- pro: Have a shop that specializes in starter/alternator repair test and rebuild it before buying a pricey new starter.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: problems/battery-keeps-dying, problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: Bigger riding mowers may have an external car-style alternator; most have an internal one under the flywheel. Possible faults: stator/alternator, regulator, other electrical.
- pro: Troubleshooting is difficult; have a mechanic familiar with it perform tests; otherwise you throw parts at it and electrical parts cannot be returned.
