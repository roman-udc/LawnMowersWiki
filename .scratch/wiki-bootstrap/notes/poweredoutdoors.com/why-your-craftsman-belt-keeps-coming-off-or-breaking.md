## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Craftsman mower belt keeps coming off or breaking"
- causes-named: belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, oil-or-rust-on-belt, wrong-belt
- fact: Depending on which belt falls off, the mower won't move or the deck blades stop spinning.
- fact: Summary of causes: stretched or shredded belt; bent belt keepers/brackets; damaged spindles, pulleys or tensioner spring; debris lodged in the deck.
- pro: If unsure how to repair safely, have a dealer/repair shop do it.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Belt keepers are small rod-shaped posts beside the pulley, which guide the belt. Slack or a snapped belt bends them out of place so the belt jumps off. Should sit close to the belt but not touch it.
- fix: Adjust with a small hammer or vice grips to 1/8 in to 1/4 in away from the pulley.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: loose belt (worn or stretched from normal use); replace. Shredded belt marks: running against a bracket or keeper; shiny spot on bracket metal is a tell. Adjust keeper/bracket so it doesn't rub, check idler bracket bushing, then replace the shredded belt with a new Craftsman belt.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Pulleys not flat and rotating straight suggest a bad bearing, most often in an idler pulley; failed bearings cause excessive vibration that can throw the belt.
- confirm: Spindle housing: wear heavy gloves, grab both ends of the blade, rock up/down; movement or noise means replace the bearing.
- fix: Some bearings are replaceable in pulleys/spindle housings; you may have to buy a pulley with bearing already installed (common for idler pulleys).

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Rock the blade ends up/down for movement or noise; replace bearing.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Stones, wood chips, dry-caked mud in pulley grooves or under pulleys; belt stretches around the extra material and can break.
- fact: Grass and debris on the deck can interfere with belt tension so the tensioner can't work and the belt won't stay tight.
- tip: Periodically remove belt covers and clean debris from the area.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Bracket has a spring on one side and pulley on the other; a worn spring or worn spring hole creates a vibration that can knock the belt off. Bracket bushing wears if not greased, pulley runs at an angle and can cut into the mower, causing vibration.
- fix: Replace damaged or worn tensioner parts; grease the idler bushing.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil leak onto pulleys and belts: belt slides off or slips; oil left a long time makes belt swell; rust dries out and cracks the belt prematurely. Keep pulleys clean of oil and rust; replace a belt with wear and cracking.

## page: causes/wrong-belt
- fact: Aftermarket or hardware-store matched belts are tempting; author buys OEM belts, which tend to last longer and handle the mower's stress; wrong belts pose problems and safety concerns.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- fact: A loose or worn belt can fall off and also keep blades from spinning fast enough for a good cut.
