## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Gravely blades: change once a year, inspect periodically for damage during season. Sharpen every 25 hours of use (about twice a year for the average homeowner); dry dusty conditions need more frequent sharpening.
- fact: New blades don't need sharpening; factory sharpened, paint coating wears off the edges with use.
- tools: ratchet/socket, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional); for sharpening: 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener or disc/angle/bench grinder, vise, blade balancer or nail on the wall, rags, wire brush
- safety: Follow owner's manual; prevent mower starting or shifting while under the deck. Remove spark plug boots and ignition key, set parking brake. Wear safety glasses and work gloves.
- step 1: Remove spark plug boot(s) and ignition key; set parking brake.
- step 2: Remove blade bolt with socket and ratchet (impact gun if stubborn). Stubborn bolts: put a block of wood on the bolt head and hit it with a hammer.
- step 3: Remove bolt, blade, and any washers. Reinstall in reverse order. Reused blades must be sharpened and balanced first.
- step 4 (sharpen): Clean blade with rag, wire brush for buildup. Secure in a vise; flip and secure to do the other side.
- step 5 (file): push file at an angle in one direction along the blade; no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges, flip, repeat.
- step 5 (drill sharpener): place flat guide along flat portion of blade with cutting edge in the bevel slot; move up and down the edge; flip and repeat.
- step 5 (angle/bench grinder): hold the grinder perpendicular to the blade edge, run along the length to level rough spots/nicks; keep it moving, removing a little metal at a time for an even result; flip and repeat.
- step 6 (balance): blade balancer; shave metal off the heavy side until equal. Nail method: nail head about 1 inch from the wall, hang blade by center hole, remove metal from the side that hangs lower until level.
- tip: Keep a second set of sharpened blades on hand.
- fact: Unbalanced blades make the mower shake.
- pro: Professional sharpening: author has the dealer do it (drop off, pick up a couple days later). Reasons: safer; sharpening often cheaper than new blade; pro can grind out large nicks and gouges; proper balance reduces vibration and deck damage.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- confirm: Off the deck: lay used blade on a new blade; if it doesn't lie flat, it's bent, replace.
- confirm: On the deck: park on hard level surface, measure ground to blade tip, rotate blade 180 degrees, measure again; more than 1/8" difference means replace.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: Worn/damaged/dull blade gives uneven or bad cut, can damage deck components, makes engine work harder (problems/uneven-cut).
- fix: Small chips and dings: sharpen out. Large chips and gouges: replace. Bent blade: replace, never straighten (even slight) - straightening weakens the metal and a piece can break off and fly. Cracked blade: replace (can break off and injure people or damage house siding).
- tip: Walk the yard and remove sticks, rocks, toys before mowing. Sand/debris dulls blades faster (sparse lawn has more sand). Lawn chemicals can corrode deck parts and fuse blades to the deck; remove blades periodically so they don't seize.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- symptom: Sand and dirt sucked into the deck wear the sail; top of the sail appears thinner than toward the blade center.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: After a significant impact, before removing the blade, grab both blade ends (gloves) and rock up and down; movement or knocking means remove spindle housings and check for damage.
- fix: Usually can replace spindle bearing or the whole spindle assembly (Gravely).

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- fact: Unbalanced blades or spindle damage from impact cause vibration.
