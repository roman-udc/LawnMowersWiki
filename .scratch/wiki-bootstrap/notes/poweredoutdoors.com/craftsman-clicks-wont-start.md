## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Craftsman mower clicks but won't start or turn over"
- alias: "turn the key, hear clicking, nothing happens"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, faulty-charging-system
- fact: Clicks but won't turn over when battery is weak, cables/wiring loose or corroded, ground wire bad, solenoid faulty, or starter motor failed.
- safety: Always disconnect the negative (black) cable from the battery before repairs to the electrical system; follow operator's manual.
- pro: Starter is pricey; have a local dealer confirm a starter motor problem before replacing, or take the starter to a shop specializing in starter/alternator repair (they can test and often rebuild).

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: Check cables and wiring from battery to solenoid and solenoid to starter are secure, in good condition, not corroded; check terminals for corrosion and good contact (excellent conductivity needed).
- fix: Remove corrosion on wires/terminals; replace cables or wiring that keep coming loose or are broken; disconnect the battery and clean terminals with a wire brush and baking soda mix of 2 cups water + 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda; repair/replace corroded wiring; replace damaged terminals; add dielectric grease to protect from corrosion.
- fact: Bad cables can contribute to a bad battery and starting issues.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Weak battery won't provide enough power to turn over. Check voltage; trickle charge if needed; replace if it won't hold a charge. If it charges but keeps dying, check the charging system.
- confirm: Multimeter red/black prongs to matching terminals. Common riding/zero-turn mower batteries are 12 V. Reading may be 11.5-12.7 V; 11.5 V = almost dead, 12.7 V = fully charged.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Put on safety gear to protect eyes and skin from acid and electrical shock.
- step 2: Get access to battery and terminals (may need a screwdriver to remove body cover/casing).
- step 3: Leave battery in its casing with terminal cables attached.
- step 4: Connect charging cables, red (positive) first; red clamp to positive terminal, black clamp to negative.
- step 5: Keep skin on rubber coating of cables and clamps only.
- step 6: Set charger voltage (usually 12 V for a mower) and amperage; more amps charges faster (start with 2 A, work up to no more than 10 A).
- step 7: If charger has a gauge, stay connected until fully charged.
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: Check the black ground cable from battery to mower frame for good contact and no corrosion; check ground from the solenoid. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: Replace a damaged ground cable; remove corrosion from battery grounds and starter solenoid grounds.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: mower keeps clicking (engine-wont-crank).
- fact: Solenoid is an on-off electromagnet switch that engages the starter motor. Most are mounted on the starter but they don't have to be. Follow the positive wire from the battery to find it. Causes of failure: weak internal spring, corroded copper plate; a bad ground, weak starter or bad battery can also cause failure.
- confirm: Test with a volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches. If the mower starts when bypassing the solenoid, replace it.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: If battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid are all fine and it still won't turn over, suspect the starter; it can be removed and tested.
- pro: see above (dealer confirm / starter-alternator repair shop).

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: A bad charging system won't keep the battery charged. Depending on size, a mower may have an external alternator (like a car) or, more commonly, an internal one under the flywheel. Possible bad stator/alternator, regulator, or other electrical problem. Test with a volt-ohm meter (linked article).
- pro: Have a mechanic familiar with the charging system test it; guessing means throwing parts, and electrical parts can't be returned.

## page: causes/bad-alternator
- fact: see faulty-charging-system: alternator may be external or internal (under flywheel).

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches
- fact: Bypassing the solenoid and getting a start means the solenoid is bad (details in a separate article).

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: Voltage 11.5 V almost dead, 12.7 V full. Charge at 2-10 A, 12 V. Dielectric grease and baking soda cleaning for terminals.
