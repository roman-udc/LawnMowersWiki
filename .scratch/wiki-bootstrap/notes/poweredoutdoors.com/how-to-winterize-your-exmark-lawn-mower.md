## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: author winterizes Exmarks in Michigan; keep mower out of the cold and free of moisture. 12 steps.
- tools: wire brush, deck scraper or putty knife, rubber gloves, rags/paper towel, fuel stabilizer, gas container (if draining), cardboard/newspaper for work surface; for oil change: plastic funnel, socket or spanner wrench for drain plug, filter and filter wrench, 2-3 quarts oil (type/quantity per owner's manual).
- step 1: wash with a garden hose, careful around the deck so as not to damage bearings; wash early on a sunny day so it dries completely and moisture doesn't collect in the electrical components; a handheld leaf blower helps dry. Never store wet (premature rust).
- step 2: check engine oil; if dirty or 50 hours since last change, change it: warm oil by running a couple of minutes, shut off, disconnect spark plug lead(s), drain, replace filter, refill per engine manufacturer.
- step 3: hydraulic oil/filter if needed (most Exmark models have serviceable hydro drive; see hydraulic oil change article).
- step 4: disconnect the spark plug boot(s) so the engine can't start while working under the deck. Do not skip.
- step 5: access underside of deck; inspect blades and remove them (good work gloves) if needed for access or sharpening/replacement; scrape the deck with scraper or putty knife (wire brush also OK); reconnect spark plug boots when done underneath.
- step 6: fuel: either drain (siphon out as much as possible, then run engine until it shuts off and won't restart) or stabilize. Author does not drain; adds stabilizer to a full tank per bottle directions and runs a few minutes to work through. Without stabilizer, spring starting problems from gumming/buildup in the fuel system. Author prefers Sea Foam (stabilizes up to 2 years and reduces moisture).
- step 7: battery: either remove to storage in a dry cool place, or ensure it is 100% fully charged (a fully charged battery won't freeze). To remove: wear gloves and eye protection; mower off; remove hold-down brackets; adjustable wrench, black negative terminal counterclockwise to loosen and remove, then red positive; inspect terminals/cables and wire-brush corrosion; store in cool dry place away from combustibles.
- step 8: check air filter, fuel filter and spark plugs; author replaces them once a year.
- step 9: lubricate cables and grease all grease zerks.
- step 10: store in a cool dry place; tarp or large drop cloth indoors (dust), waterproof material outdoors.
- step 11: with pneumatic front caster tires, put cardboard under them to keep them off cold concrete.
- step 12: rodent prevention: wires are often coated with soy-based material rodents chew; use a deterrent safe for pets; mothballs around the mower, or author's choice Grandpa Gus's Mouse Repellent (packets and spray on wires); can save thousands in electrical repairs.
- fact: indoor storage: garage/pole barn just cover with a tarp; basement or in the house, do not store with fuel and keep away from combustibles.
- fact: outdoor storage (under deck/overhang): use a heavy-duty mower cover of marine-grade fabric; rain falls between deck boards and snow blows in.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: stabilizer in a full tank, run a few minutes; Sea Foam stabilizes up to 2 years; or drain with a siphon and run dry.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: store removed battery cool and dry away from combustibles; fully charged (100%) battery will not freeze; remove negative first then positive.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: winterizing step: remove blades with work gloves if they need sharpening or replacing while the deck is accessed.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: change if dirty or 50 hours since last change; warm the oil a couple minutes, disconnect spark plug lead first.
