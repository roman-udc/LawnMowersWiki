## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Husqvarna mower clicks but won't start"
- alias: "mower just clicks or hums when I turn the key"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, faulty-charging-system
- safety: always disconnect the negative (black) battery cable before electrical repairs; consult professional if uncomfortable with electrical system

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: constant vibration loosens cables; check cables, wiring, terminals before the battery
- confirm: cables battery-to-solenoid and solenoid-to-starter motor securely attached; terminals/connections free of corrosion
- fix: replace cables/wiring that keep coming loose or are broken; disconnect battery, remove corroded components; wire brush and baking soda mix (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); repair/replace corroded wiring; replace damaged terminals
- tip: dielectric grease protects terminals and wiring from corrosion; store mower dry in a dry location to reduce corrosion
- fact: bad cables can contribute to a bad battery

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: weak battery may not turn over the engine
- confirm: multimeter red/black probes to same-colored terminals; most Husqvarna batteries are 12 V; reading range 11.5 to 12.7 V; near-dead about 11.5 V, fully charged 12.7 V
- fix: charge; if it won't hold a charge replace; if it charges but keeps dying, check charging system

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver (to uncover battery)
- safety: safety gear for eyes and skin (acid, shock); skin only touches rubber coating of cables and clamps
- step 1: access battery and terminals
- step 2: leave battery in casing with terminal cables attached
- step 3: connect red (positive) cable first to positive terminal; black clamp to negative terminal
- step 4: set charger to 12 V typical; more amps charges faster; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps
- step 5: if charger has a gauge, keep connected until fully charged

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step 1: multimeter probes to like-colored terminals; 12 V battery reads 11.5 (almost dead) to 12.7 (full)

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: black ground cable from battery to frame must make good contact and be corrosion-free; also check the solenoid ground; a 3-post solenoid is self-grounded
- fix: replace damaged ground cable; remove corrosion at battery and solenoid grounds

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: clicking or humming sound and mower doesn't start (often this)
- fact: solenoid is an electromagnetic on-off switch that engages the starter motor; most are mounted on the starter but needn't be; follow positive wire from battery to find it
- fact: failure causes: internal spring weak or copper plate corroding; bad ground, weak starter or bad battery can also kill it
- confirm: test with volt-ohms meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches; if mower starts when solenoid is bypassed it must be replaced
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches
- tip: if bypassing solenoid starts the mower, replace the solenoid

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: after battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid are good, starter can be removed and tested
- tip: starters are pricey; have dealer confirm, or a starter/alternator repair shop test it

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: bad charging system won't keep battery charged so weak battery won't start mower; check with volt-ohm meter
- pro: if not charging, get a mechanic familiar with the charging system; hard to troubleshoot; parts-swapping is expensive and electrical parts can't be returned; could be bad stator/alternator, regulator, or other electrical fault
