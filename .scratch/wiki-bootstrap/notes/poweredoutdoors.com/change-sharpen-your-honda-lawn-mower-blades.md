## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- safety: do more than remove the key: disconnect the spark plug boot; wear work gloves and safety glasses
- fact: replace Honda blades once a year (more often with dirt/gravel), at the annual service; if old blades aren't damaged or excessively worn, sharpen and keep as spares
- fact: sharpen after every 25 hours of use; average homeowner sharpens twice a year; more often in sand/gravel
- fact: new blades do not need sharpening; maker sharpens and paints them, paint wears off the edge in use
- tools (remove/install): socket wrench, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, block of wood
- step 1: disconnect spark plug boot, remove key if any, wear gloves
- step 2: turn fuel valve to off to minimize leaks, tip mower on its side with air filter on the high side
- step 3: wedge a block of wood between blade and deck housing to stop the blade turning; remove bolts and washers with socket wrench
- step 4: remove the blades
- step 5 (install): clean the area under the deck where blade mounts; if reusing sharpened blades, balance them first
- step 6: install upper and lower blades with bolts and washers; concave side of washers faces the blade
- step 7: torque bolts to the manufacturer's spec in the owner's manual with a torque wrench (block of wood to hold blade); correct torque avoids blade coming off
- tools (sharpen): 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; gloves
- step S1: clean blade with rag, wire brush for stubborn buildup
- step S2: secure blade in vise; flip and re-secure to do other side
- step S3 file: push file at an angle in one direction along blade; no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edge, flip, repeat
- step S3 drill sharpener: flat guide of sharpener on flat part of blade, cutting edge slot into the bevel; move up and down edge length; flip and repeat
- step S3 grinder: hold angle grinder perpendicular to blade edge, run along length to level rough spots/nicks; keep moving so it removes a little metal at a time and evenly; flip and repeat
- tip: pro sharpening: safer than DIY, often cheaper than new blade, can grind out large nicks and gouges, properly balances; author has tools but has his dealer sharpen for a cleaner edge
- pro: if not comfortable sharpening/balancing; unbalanced or still-worn blades can damage crankshaft or engine

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: bent blade check: match old blade against a new one; if no new blade, take old blade to dealer
- fact: sail wear: replace blade where the sail is thinner than the center of the blade
- fact: small chips can be sharpened out; larger nicks and gouges mean replace
- fact: balance check: blade balancer, or nail in wall with head 3/4" to 1" from the wall, blade center on nail; if it doesn't sit level, shave metal off the side hanging lower

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: brown tips on grass a day or two after mowing; handful of grass shows torn tips (problems/uneven-cut)
- fact: bent blade from hitting a solid object like tree root or rock; never straighten and reuse a bent blade (compromises structure, can crack)
- fact: nicks in edge compromise cut quality
- safety: a cracked blade must be replaced immediately; it can break off and fly out of the mower

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: sail is the high side of the blade; dirt sucked under deck wears it thin; thin sail can't move the air to form suction that stands the grass up for a precise cut
- fix: replace blade where sail is thinner than the center

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- fact: if bad/uneven cut, check blades for dull, worn, chipped, bent
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, worn-blade-sail

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- fact: unbalanced blade causes vibration and can damage crankshaft
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Honda blades: change yearly; sharpen every 25 hours
- fact: sand and debris wear blades prematurely; lawn chemicals may corrode blades and deck and can fuse blades to deck components; regularly remove and clean blades to prevent that
- tip: walk the lawn first to remove toys, sticks, rocks
