## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel breaks down as quickly as 30 days after purchase; applies to gas and diesel. Ethanol (corn-based) attracts moisture leaving sticky residue that clogs the system; water-ethanol mixture corrodes parts and as gas ages it separates and sinks to the tank bottom; runs hot, potential engine damage.
- fact: Right gas: unleaded, min 87 octane, no more than 10% ethanol; less or ethanol-free is best.
- fact (diesel): sludge builds in diesel tanks (even station tanks) if it sits; bad diesel looks dark; the fuel filter looks dark from straining sludge.
- fix: Drain the tank with a siphon pump into a container; dispose of old fuel at a recycling facility; refill with fresh fuel plus an additive (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Change at least once a year; more often with old or dirty fuel.
- fix: Replace; arrow on the plastic housing must point in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor, away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Sticky deposits from evaporated ethanol and water clog the line.
- confirm / step 1: Shut off fuel with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank, or pinch pliers if no valve.
- step 2: Pick a section; remove the end furthest from the tank and put it in a container.
- step 3: Restore flow (open valve/remove pliers).
- step 4: Watch for flow. If none, make sure the container is lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump). Still none means a clog.
- step 5: Shut off fuel; remove the clogged section.
- step 6: Spray carburetor cleaner into it; blow compressed air through; repeat until clear.
- step 7: If it can't be cleared, replace the line.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Plastic or metal pump used when the tank sits lower than the carburetor; most mowers use a vacuum pump (crankcase vacuum). Cracked, damaged or leaking means replace.
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches the pump; remove the carburetor-end fuel line into a container; open flow and start the mower; expect a steady or pulsating stream; replace if not.
- brand: Injection pump: get spec from the operator's manual; measure pressure with a fuel pressure gauge; replace if below spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor sits on the top or side of the engine block, usually below or behind the air filter. Fuel jet and other parts clog.
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches the carburetor; remove air filter; spray a little carburetor cleaner into the intake; start; if it starts, runs, then shuts off, remove and clean the carburetor. If it doesn't start at all the problem may not be fuel.
- fix: Clean the carburetor (most mechanical homeowners can) or have a repair shop do it.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: The tank vents through the cap; a clogged vent acts like a vacuum.
- confirm: Remove the cap, start, let it run; if it runs fine reinstall the cap and keep running; if it eventually shuts down with the cap on but restarts and runs as soon as the cap is removed, the cap is clogged.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap (doesn't always work); otherwise buy a new cap.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Unleaded, 87 octane minimum, 10% ethanol max.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Fuel can break down within 30 days; use fresh fuel right away; stabilizer/additive.
