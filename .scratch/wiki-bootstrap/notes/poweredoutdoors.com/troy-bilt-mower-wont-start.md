## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Troy-Bilt mower won't start"
- alias: "engine turning but not starting"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, wrong-fuel, old-fuel, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter, blown-fuse, incorrect-starting-procedure
- confirm: verify before diagnosing: fuel shut-off valve open (often closed for storage/transport); enough fresh fuel; parking brake or bail lever engaged; PTO off (riding, tractor, zero turn); choke closed/on for cold start.
- confirm (fuel test): remove air filter, spray carb cleaner in intake (author uses carb cleaner rather than starter fluid), try recoil or key; if it tries to start/starts, fuel system (dirty carb, clogged fuel filter, lines, pump if fitted); if not, most likely spark plug.
- confirm (air test): inspect filter; choke plate: manual choke lever ON = plate closed, OFF = plate open (on some models fast throttle position); newer Troy-Bilt push mowers have an automatic thermostat-controlled choke (closed cold, open warm). If not moving, check linkage and cable, lubricate with carb cleaner, replace bad cable.
- confirm (spark test): boot off plug, spark plug tester between wire and plug, crank; glow = spark; none: bad plug/coil; electric start: battery, wiring, solenoid.
- fact FAQ: Troy-Bilt won't start when fuel restricted (dirty carb, clogged fuel filter, old gas); air restriction and lack of spark (stuck choke, fouled plug, bad coil). First check fuel supply and maintenance items (air filter, plug, fuel filter, old gas). Engine cranks but won't start: dirty carb, clogged air filter, fouled plug, clogged fuel line.
- safety: remove spark plug wires before repairs.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Troy-Bilt 4-cycle engine requires regular unleaded gasoline, no higher than 10% ethanol, minimum 87 octane; least or no ethanol is best; read the pump label.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas best for about 30 days; ethanol collects moisture, causes corrosion and degradation; gummy residue clogs the system.
- fix: drain and flush tank; fresh fuel with Sea Foam stabilizer/cleaner.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain; replace; gap correctly; wires secure.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter makes engine work harder, overheat and shut down.
- step (paper): tap on hard surface; hold to light; replace if no light, damaged or oil-covered.
- step (foam): replace if brittle, torn, brown spots; else wash with mild dish soap, rinse until soap gone, squeeze water out, lay flat to dry (sun faster), lightly coat with clean engine oil.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: paper and foam steps above (foam: engine oil lightly).

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: old gas degrades plastic and internals; leaks at seams; replace if leaking.
- confirm: verify flow to the pump inlet via shut-off valve or clamps; then disconnect the hose from the carburetor into a container, start, watch for constant or pulsating flow; replace if none.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- tip: author replaces at least once a year; most push mowers don't use a fuel filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: use the shut-off valve under the tank or a clamp; start with the hose furthest from the tank and work toward the tank (saves time).
- fix: remove line, carb cleaner, compressed air; replace if it won't clear (hardware store or online). Recommends replacing dry or cracked lines even without a clog.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fix: take apart and clean with carb cleaner including the float bowl and needle; or small engine mechanic; replace if too dirty; many small parts.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: baking soda solution 2 cups water + 3 rounded tablespoons; wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fix: multimeter; about 12.7 volts needed; charge if less; replace if no hold.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: several switches: stop the deck running without operator; prevent starting without parking brake.
- confirm: multimeter or temporary bypass. Safety: never operate without it or bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: multimeter; replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; wire hot, smoking or melting.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid; starter can be removed and tested.
- pro: have a starter/alternator repair shop test and rebuild it if possible before buying a pricey new one.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: some Troy-Bilt push mowers use a rope recoil that wears out; rope may not retract.
- fix: try replacing the spring and restringing; if clips or pulley are damaged replace the whole recoil assembly.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fix: replace with same amperage; if it keeps blowing go to Troy-Bilt dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: drains the battery so it won't start next time; bad stator or alternator; have a dealer or shop check; guessing parts is expensive, electrical parts typically non-returnable.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: remove the cap and start; if it runs, refit the cap and let it run; sputters and dies = bad cap.
- fix: clean; replace if it won't vent.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Troy-Bilt safety precautions require certain steps; e.g. not leaving the choke on restricting air for a cold start; see the operating manual.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: add fresh fuel.
