## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Bad Boy mower blades won't engage or turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-or-stretched-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: Bad Boy blades won't engage due to a worn or stretched deck belt, a belt off the pulleys, a bad PTO switch, a faulty clutch, a weak battery, a bad safety switch or a bad fuse.
- safety: Never reach under the deck until operator's-manual precautions are done: remove ignition key, remove spark plug wire(s), wait for all parts to stop moving.
- safety: Consult a professional if you lack the skills/knowledge/condition to do the repair safely.
- pro: Blown fuse that keeps blowing -> take to a Bad Boy service dealer or repair shop to find root cause of the electrical failure.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: worn deck belt has cracks, fraying or a shiny glazed look; may sit deep in the pulley grooves (problems/blades-wont-engage).
- fix: replace a worn belt; replace it as soon as it shows wear even if it is not the main reason blades won't engage.
- fact: belt grip on the pulleys affects blade speed; fast blade speed creates the suction under the deck that lifts grass for an even cut.
- fact: a stretched belt is listed among reasons the belt comes off the pulleys.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- symptom: belt loose and vibrates off the pulleys (problems/blades-wont-engage).
- fact: a tensioner arm and spring hold the idler pulleys inline; on most decks the pulley is on one side of the bracket and the spring on the other.
- fact: the bracket hole where the spring attaches can wear, or the spring itself can wear, letting the belt loosen. A missing spring is also named.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: pulley wobbles; one side of pulley sits higher off the deck than the other (problems/blades-wont-engage).
- fact: each pulley has a bearing; wear lets the pulley sit non-parallel to the deck and move, which can roll the belt off.
- fix: replace a pulley whose bearing is bad.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch is usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch; when it fails the blades won't turn on.
- confirm: check the switch for continuity.
- fix: replace a switch with a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO (Power Take Off) clutch transfers engine power to the blades by engaging the drive belt; a worn/faulty clutch must be replaced when it fails to power the blades.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: an electric clutch uses battery power; a weak battery can't give the clutch solenoid enough power to engage the blades.
- confirm: multimeter; a fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V. Charge if lower.
- fix: if it won't hold a charge, replace it (12 V batteries at hardware/automotive stores or mower dealers). Bring the old battery; core fees average $20 otherwise.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: zero turn has a seat safety switch under the seat as part of the operator presence system; blades won't engage without the operator in the seat. A failed switch may not sense the operator and blocks the blades.
- confirm: test with a multimeter, or temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one.
- safety: Do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed; always keep safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: if no battery power reaches the clutch you may have a blown fuse; the fuse protects the electrical system.
- fix: replace with the same size fuse.
- pro: if fuses keep blowing, take it to a Bad Boy dealer/repair shop.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver (to uncover battery), protective gear
- safety: wear protective gear for skin (shock) and eyes; do not touch anything without a rubber coating.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (may need a screwdriver to uncover; under the hood or under the seat). Do not remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect charger cables positive first (red / plus sign) to the positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach the negative (black) cable to the negative terminal.
- step 4: Set charger voltage and amperage. Average lawn mower battery is 12 V. More amps charge faster; start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best.

## page: guides/how-clutches-work
