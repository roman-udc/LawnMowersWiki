## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: Routine service: change engine oil, scrape deck, clean air filter, remove debris. 15 steps.
- tools: socket or spanner wrench (drain plug if present), deck scraper or wire brush, plastic funnel, oil drain pan, rags/paper towel, cardboard or newspaper, spray lubricant.
- parts: engine oil SAE30 or 10W-30; spark plug; air filter; fuel filter (if used); mower blade if not resharpening; fuel stabilizer if storing.
- safety: Rubber gloves, heavy work gloves and safety glasses; engine may be hot; always wear heavy gloves around the blade; remove spark plug wire before maintenance and don't reattach until done.
- step 1: Gather materials.
- step 2: Park on a flat level surface (workbench or level ground); lay cardboard/newspaper; secure the mower if on a bench.
- step 3: Change engine oil (see procedures/change-the-engine-oil).
- step 4: Check spark plug and gap. Clean if dirty. Fouled plug can make mower quit; intermittent running/starting from dirty or wrongly gapped plug. Author replaces plug annually.
- step 5: Remove dirt and debris packed around engine, recoil and other components.
- step 6: Air filter. Author changes annually at service. Paper: remove, tap against hard surface; reuse if light visible through element, else replace. Foam: wash with mild dish soap and water, rinse thoroughly, lay flat to dry, lightly coat with filter oil, replace; replace if torn, brown or brittle. A filter too dirty to pass air can cause internal engine damage; check and clean regularly through season.
- step 7: Replace inline fuel filter if used; arrow on the side shows fuel flow direction; install accordingly.
- step 8: Check fuel system for leaks: hoses secure at hose clamps, no leaks (from punctures/cracks, components not sealing, degrading plastics). Replace dry, brittle fuel lines.
- step 9: Check belt: replace if glazed, worn, cracked, stretched or shredded.
- step 10: Flip mower on side or back on handle (secure handle); remove blade and sharpen/replace; scrape deck with scraper or metal brush (clogged deck affects cut; moisture in debris corrodes deck prematurely); reinstall blade.
- step 11: Check the safety system per owner's manual.
- step 12: Replace damaged, worn or missing parts; hardware securely in place.
- step 13: Lubricate cables and linkages; reattach the spark plug wire.
- step 14: Wash with a garden hose and let dry completely (moisture causes premature corrosion).
- step 15: If storing for off-season: remove fuel or add stabilizer (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment); run a couple of minutes to circulate stabilized fuel; store dry, use waterproof cover if exposed; use rodent control (pet-safe product in packet or spray form, packets on mower and spray on wires) to stop rodents chewing wires.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- step 1: Warm the oil by running a few minutes (warm oil flows better), shut off.
- step 2: Remove spark plug wire.
- step 3: Clean around the oil fill area to keep dirt out of the crankcase.
- step 4a: No drain plug: place drain pan on the side opposite the air filter, remove dipstick, tilt mower toward the pan, let oil run out.
- step 4b: With drain plug: tilt on side or back on handle to reach the deck bottom, clean around plug, place pan, remove plug, drain all oil, replace plug.
- step 5: Return mower upright.
- step 6: Fill with fresh oil per owner's manual; most push mowers use SAE30 or 10W-30.
- step 7: Check level on the dipstick; too much or too little oil causes engine problems.
- step 8: Wipe spills; dispose of used oil by putting in empty containers to a recycling center (some auto parts stores accept it).
- tip: An oil evacuator (vacuum-style pump) removes oil through the fill tube without tipping the mower.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- fact: Inspect and gap; clean if dirty; author replaces annually.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Paper: tap, hold to light, reuse if light visible. Foam: dish soap and water, rinse, dry flat, lightly coat with filter oil; replace if torn, brown or brittle.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter has an arrow showing flow direction; install following the arrow.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Remove blade, sharpen or replace, scrape deck while off; links to Cub Cadet blade article.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Service list: oil, plug, air filter, fuel filter, fuel leaks, belt, blade/deck scrape, safety system check, worn parts, lubrication, wash and dry.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Remove fuel or add stabilizer and run a couple minutes; store dry with waterproof cover if outdoors; rodent control for wires.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Stabilized fuel (Sea Foam) for storage; run engine a couple of minutes to circulate.
