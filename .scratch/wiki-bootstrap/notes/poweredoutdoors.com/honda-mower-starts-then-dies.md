## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Honda lawn mower starts then dies"
- alias: "mower dies in the middle of the lawn"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: headline: bad fuel clogs fuel system; plugged air filter or wrong choke restricts air; bad plug or coil; overheating from wrong oil level or plugged deck.
- safety: follow owner's manual safety guidelines.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can degrade as soon as 30 days; ethanol (corn-based) attracts moisture, corrosive to fuel system; as it evaporates leaves gummy substance; ethanol/water mix separates and sinks to tank bottom and runs hotter in the engine, potentially damaging it.
- fix: unleaded gas with no more than 10% ethanol; if gas sat a long time, siphon tank, refill with fresh fuel plus stabilizer. Author adds Sea Foam Motor Treatment to all gas (opinion).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: runs sluggishly and dies; restricted airflow shuts down engine.
- tip: replace filter annually and clean several times during mowing season.
- fix paper: carefully remove; wipe dirt from housing without letting it fall into intake; tap on solid surface; hold up to a light and check you can see light through the paper; reuse if light shows, else replace; reinstall and secure cover.
- fix foam: remove, wipe housing; replace if dry, brittle, brown spots or torn; wash in mild dish soap and water; rinse until water runs clear; lay flat to dry (sunny or breezy outdoors speeds it); lightly saturate with foam filter oil, fully covered but not dripping; squeeze out or blot excess with paper towel.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1 (paper): remove carefully; wipe housing; tap; check light passes through; reinstall.
- step 1 (foam): remove; inspect for dry/brittle/brown spots/tears; wash in soap and water; rinse clear; dry flat; lightly oil with foam filter oil; remove excess.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: gummy substances from old fuel plus dirt clog it.
- fix: replace; arrow on filter housing must point in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: start/stop fuel flow with shut-off valve or pinch pliers while checking lines; to check a section stop flow, detach the end of the section furthest from the tank, put in a container, restore flow and look for good flow.
- fix: remove section, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air, repeat; reinstall; replace the line if blockage won't clear; also good time to replace if dry and brittle.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: mower has a pump only if the carburetor sits higher than the tank; a push mower without one may not need one.
- confirm: confirm flow to the pump inlet; then stop fuel flow, remove line from carburetor into a container, restore flow, start mower; expect steady or pulsating flow; if not, replace pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: verify fuel reaches the carb via line flow; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner into air intake and start. If it starts, runs fine, then shuts down, likely the carburetor.
- pro: a small engine mechanic can clean or rebuild it.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: crankcase must always be at manufacturer's recommended level, "not just a suggestion".
- fact: low oil: friction raises heat, can burn oil and melt internal parts; if the mower shut down from lack of oil, engine damage is most likely; adding oil probably won't fix it.
- pro: have a small engine mechanic diagnose, get an estimate; weigh estimate, mower age and replacement price.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil raises crankcase pressure; engine can overheat and shut down as crankshaft and rod push through extra oil; oil in the cylinder can hydro-lock the engine.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: carbon on tip prevents spark and mower dies.
- fix: clean tip; replace if tip is very dark, burnt electrode or cracked porcelain; gap to manufacturer spec; make sure plug wires are secure.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: windings can separate and short when the mower is hot, so plug gets no voltage; mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: ohmmeter check for a break in continuity; replace if broken.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts air and adds fuel to start cold; leaving it on after warm-up kills the engine.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: runs a while then shuts down; cap vent plugged makes tank vacuum.
- confirm: run it for a while with and without the cap; fine without but dies after a while with cap on = cap problem.
- fix: try cleaning the cap to unclog the vent; replace if can't be fixed.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed clippings under the deck give a bad cut and strain the engine, causing heat, power loss, and possibly dying. Scrape regularly; keep blades sharp because dull blades magnify the strain.
