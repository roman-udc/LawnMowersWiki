## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Cub Cadet won't turn over"
- alias: "Cub Cadet won't crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: Won't turn over when power isn't reaching the starter motor. Causes in Source order: bad battery; loose or corroded wiring/terminals; bad fuse; bad ignition key switch; faulty safety switch; bad starter solenoid; faulty starter motor.
- brand: Cub Cadet has an operator presence control system (safety switches).
- pro: If uncomfortable working on starting system, Cub Cadet dealer or knowledgeable mechanic.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: clicking sound, or no click at all, when turning key; won't turn over.
- confirm: Source links a battery test article (not reproduced).
- fix: Charge; if it won't hold a charge, replace (dealer, hardware or automotive store). Bring old battery to avoid core fee.
- tip: Keep battery charged especially during storage to extend life.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: battery charger (12-volt battery); protective gear for eyes and skin.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (may need screwdriver to uncover). Do not remove battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect charging cables, positive first (red / plus sign) to positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach negative cable (black / minus sign) to negative terminal.
- step 4: Don't touch anything without a rubber coating (avoid electrocution).
- step 5: Set charger voltage and amperage. Average mower battery is 12 volts. More amps charges faster; start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best.
- safety: protective gear for eyes and skin.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: Constant vibration loosens wires/connections; outdoor moisture causes corrosion at connections.
- fix: Secure connections, verify continuity; remove corrosion with wire brush and baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); replace terminals if corrosion is severe.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- confirm: Multimeter probe on each prong of fuse measuring resistance: near 0 = good; infinity = bad.
- fix: Replace with same amperage fuse. If fuses keep blowing, take to dealership or repair shop to find root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: Multimeter on resistance, probe each fuse prong.
- step 2: Near 0 good; infinity bad.
- step 3: Replace with same amperage.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned and nothing happens; Cub Cadet fails to turn over.
- confirm: Multimeter continuity test. Find prongs marked B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid). Insert key, turn to start, multimeter on resistance, one probe on B and other on S. Good = near 0 ohms; bad = infinite resistance, replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- confirm: Test with multimeter; may temporarily bypass switch for troubleshooting only.
- safety: Never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run a mower with a safety switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Starter solenoid is an electromagnetic switch that engages the starter motor. Goes bad when spring weakens or copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause solenoid failure.
- confirm: Need a fully charged battery before testing. Source points to a separate solenoid-testing article.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: After ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid, remove starter and test it.
- tip: Author recommends a repair shop that specializes in starter/alternator repair test and rebuild the starter if possible before buying an expensive new one (opinion).
