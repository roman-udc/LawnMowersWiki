## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Change blades once a year (annual service); more often with dirt/gravel/sand exposure. If old blades aren't damaged or excessively worn, sharpen and keep as spares. Sharpen after every 25 hours of use (about twice a year for average homeowner); more often in sandy/gravel conditions.
- fact: New blades need no sharpening; sharpened at manufacture, then painted; paint wears off the edge with use.
- tools (remove/install): socket wrench, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, block of wood.
- safety: Disconnect spark plug boot, remove key; wear gloves. Access underside with ramps or a lift designed for the zero-turn; make sure deck is secure and won't drop; use brake and wheel chocks.
- step 1: Remove blade bolts and washers with socket wrench, with a block of wood lodged between blade and deck housing to stop blade turning.
- step 2: Remove blades.
- step 3: Clean the area under the deck where the blade mounts.
- step 4: Reused sharpened blades must be balanced.
- step 5: Install blades with bolts and washers.
- step 6: Torque bolts to the manufacturer's spec in the owner's manual (wrong torque risks blade coming off); block of wood prevents rotation.
- tools (sharpen): 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves.
- step (sharpen) 1: Clean blade with rag; wire brush for stubborn buildup.
- step 2: Secure blade in a vise; flip and re-secure for the other side.
- step 3 file: push file at an angle in one direction; no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges.
- step 3 drill sharpener: flat guide along flat part of blade with cutting edge in the bevel slot; move up and down the edge length.
- step 3 grinder: hold perpendicular to the blade edge, run along length, keep moving to remove a little metal at a time.
- step 4: Balance before reinstall.
- fact: Corrosive lawn chemicals can corrode blades and deck, fusing blades to deck components (nearly impossible to remove); remove blades regularly to reduce risk.
- tip: Walk the lawn first and remove toys, sticks, rocks. Sand/debris wears blades; thicker grass cover reduces sucked-in dirt.
- pro: Seek a professional if not comfortable sharpening/balancing; unbalanced or worn blades can damage crankshaft/engine. Author has the dealer sharpen (cleaner edge). Reasons: safer; sharpening often cheaper than new blade; pro can grind out large nicks and gouges; proper balance reduces vibration.
- mistake: Failing to balance causes vibration and crankshaft damage.
- fact: Balanced = equal weight each side; use a blade balancer, or a nail in the wall with head about 3/4" to 1" out; center the blade on the nail; blade should sit level; if not, shave metal from the side that hangs lower.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- confirm (remove method): rock the blade at each end to check spindle housing; movement or knocking -> disassemble housing and replace damage. Remove blade and match to a new blade; gap = bent.
- confirm (no removal): mower on level flat surface; measure ground-to-blade at one end, rotate 180 degrees, measure other end at same ground spot; difference greater than 1/8" = bent, replace. Check spindle/bearing damage before removing.
- safety: remove spark plug wires, protect hands.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull or gouged edge, worn tips and sail, bent blade, unbalanced blade hurt cut. Dull blades can brown the lawn a couple days after mowing (grass beaten, not cut). Small chips smoothed by sharpening; large nicks/gouges -> replace. Bends from hitting tree roots or rocks.
- fact: Cracked blade must be replaced right away; can break off and fly out, endangering people, pets, structures.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: Sail is the high side of the blade; creates air movement under the deck; with deck shell and baffles creates suction that stands grass up for a precise cut. Dirt wears it; when thinner than the center of the blade, replace blade.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- fact: Check blade condition (dull, worn, bent) for bad/uneven cut.
