## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- fact: general rule: avoid using an electric mower in the rain; risks are harm to yourself, damage to the mower, poor cut. Even if the manual says waterproof, mow when dry and skies are clear. Check the owner's manual first; virtually every company warns about mowing in rain.
- fact: many modern electric mowers are rated to keep moisture out, but that does not make rain use a good idea
- safety: a standard 120v outlet can potentially kill; a corded electric mower in rain on a household outlet risks shock
- tip: mitigate by using a UL-listed extension cord rated for outdoor use with three prongs (grounding); better still a battery-powered mower with no cord. Battery-powered is generally safer than corded.
- safety: wet footing - easier to slip and fall pushing a heavy mower through wet grass; falling on a running mower can lead to injury or death
- fact: mowers shed most rain; water runs off and is diverted from critical parts. Washing with a hose is fine if you avoid spraying the engine.
- fact: motor cases have vents to let hot air out; in long rain, water entering vents reaches motor and wiring and harms the electrical system
- fact: battery casing is often hinged for access; small gap lets water in after long exposure; a large amount of water in the battery compartment can damage the battery and connectors
- symptom: damaged electrical system - mower may not turn on, or runs with less power and cuts poorly
- symptom: moisture can short out the mower; electricity diverted; possible electrical fire or harmful fumes; smoke is one sign
- fact: electric mowers run on insulated copper wiring; with enough water the insulation may fail, allowing a short and risking shock to the operator
- tip: wet-grass effects: uneven cut; tire ruts and footprints; fungus/disease from ragged tears; deck clogging with clinging clippings (worse when mulching or side-discharging); wet clippings can cause corrosion/rust and mold under the deck; clogged clippings disrupt blade rotation and stress the engine; wet grass bends over and stays bent; bag gets wet/heavier and inlets jam; leftover clippings can kill grass; mulching less effective; soil compaction and uprooted grass
- tip: if you must mow wet grass: clear the yard of debris; wear non-slip shoes, long pants and long sleeves; remember stiff grass cuts better than bent grass; keep blades sharp; use gas stabilizer (gas mowers; moisture may get in the gas); raise the deck; use the side chute instead of bag or mulcher; mow narrower passes; clear beneath the deck between passes; watch battery charge (wet mowing drains more power; spare battery or recharge halfway); rake clippings afterward; clean the mower when done (underside of deck, blade, air inlets, filters) to prevent mold
- tip: moisture/gas stabilizer advice appears generic to all mowers

## page: guides/mower-got-wet
- fact: if left out in a serious rainstorm, dry the mower before using it again; do not try to turn it on until sure it is dry
- step 1: take it apart to expose the motor
- step 2: dry the motor and wiring with a handheld blower, fan or similar
- step 3: letting it sit in the sun on a dry breezy day also helps
- tip: optionally use a special cleaner for electrical connections; optionally check for lost insulation resistance in wiring with a megohmmeter
- fact: a brief shower is most likely fine if exposure is reasonably short; thorough drying may take a good amount of time

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- symptom: mower won't turn on or runs with less power after rain exposure; smoke if shorted
- fix: dry the motor and wiring, clean connections, check insulation resistance
- fact: water entering motor vents or the hinged battery compartment gap is the path
