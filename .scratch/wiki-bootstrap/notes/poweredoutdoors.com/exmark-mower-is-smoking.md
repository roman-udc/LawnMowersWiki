## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Exmark mower smoking / smoke rolling out"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, spilled-oil-on-engine
- fact: Exmark may have Kawasaki, Kohler, Briggs or Exmark engine.
- fact: black smoke = engine running very rich (more fuel than air); check/clean/replace air filter first, then other airflow restriction.
- fact: blue or white smoke = burning excess oil; check oil level, then piston rings, valve train, engine gasket.
- tip: check oil level and air filter before each mowing.
- pro: internal problems (rings, valves) need small engine mechanic teardown and tests; dealer or certified engine dealer (Kawasaki, Kohler, Briggs).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: black smoke (rich, lack of air). Story: author found a friend's Exmark filter plugged with dirt and grass.
- fact: can run without filter but never should (debris shortens engine life; repair cost >> savings). If filter plugs completely, engine may draw air and oil from crankcase into cylinder; oil burning makes smoke.
- tip: check filter at least a couple of times per season; more often in dry dusty conditions or heavy use.
- step: remove cover (thumbscrews or clips); remove filter without knocking dirt into intake; wipe housing with dry clean rag; tap on solid surface; never compressed air (damages); hold to light - replace if no light shows through, reuse if it does.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1-4 as above (cover thumbscrews/clips; wipe housing; tap; light test); mistake: compressed air.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: oil should be at full mark on dipstick, not lower or higher.
- fact: low oil -> friction, heat melts parts and burns oil -> smoke.
- pro: adding oil usually won't work once it smokes; take to Exmark dealer or small engine shop to assess damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: crankcase pressure pushes oil into cylinder and up to air intake through the valve train; oil burning off makes smoke.
- fix: remove oil: (a) pull drain plug and quickly replace for a little oil, drain pan ready; (b) remove oil filter and take oil from it, replace filter; (c) turkey baster from fill area (don't reuse for cooking); (d) oil evacuator. Then check level and top off to full.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step: four methods listed above; recheck dipstick, top off to full if needed.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- confirm: remove spark plug with 3/4" or 5/8" socket (depends on engine); oil on plug may indicate ring or valve train problem. Mechanic may find cylinder wall scoring letting oil into combustion chamber.
- fact: only teardown/tests find root cause.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: burnt valve (overheated); another sign: muffler gets red hot (burnt valve or timing problem).
- confirm: remove cylinder head and leak-down test; best done by mechanic.
- fix: mechanic cuts valve and seat at correct angles so valve completes combustion chamber.

## page: causes/spilled-oil-on-engine
- fact: bad engine gasket leaks oil which can drip on hot muffler and smoke when burned off; gasket replacement difficulty depends on location.
- fix: replace gasket.
