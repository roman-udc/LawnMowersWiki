## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "push mower smoking"
- alias: "white / blue / black smoke from my mower"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, oil-in-air-filter-or-tipped-mower, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, bad-head-gasket, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train
- fact: Smokes from insufficient oil, oil spilled on the engine, plugged air filter, wrong choke, bad engine gasket, valve train or piston ring problem. Internal damage may need a pro or a new mower.
- tip: After repair, run the mower 10-15 minutes to burn off remaining oil until smoking stops.
- fact: Black smoke: engine running rich (more fuel than air); look for plugged air filter, incorrect choke, other airflow restriction.
- fact: White or blue smoke: usually burning oil: wrong oil level, worn piston rings, valve train problem, bad engine gasket, incorrect oil viscosity. Two-cycle push mower: oil-to-fuel ratio may be wrong; check the owner's manual.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Restricted air makes the engine run rich, black smoke. Also the engine may pull air (and oil) from the crankcase into the cylinder, burning as smoke.
- mistake: Never run without an air filter, even briefly until a replacement arrives.
- tip: Install a new filter each season at annual maintenance; clean a couple of times during the season.
- step 1 (paper): Remove from housing; wipe housing with a clean dry cloth without dirt entering the intake; tap on a solid surface (no compressed air, it can damage the filter); hold to light; reuse if light shows; replace if not, damaged, extremely dirty or oily; refit cover.
- step 2 (foam): Remove; wipe housing; inspect - replace if brittle, dark spots or tears; wash with mild dish soap and water until clear; lay flat to dry (sun speeds it); when dry, lightly saturate with foam filter oil, squeeze excess (also for a new foam filter); install and secure cover.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Wrong choke gives black smoke. Manual choke sticking in choke position: lubricate the cable. Automatic choke faulty: have a repair shop fix it.

## page: causes/oil-in-air-filter-or-tipped-mower
- fact: Hillside use or tipping runs engine oil into the air filter and carburetor, creating smoke.
- fix: Replace an oil-covered filter after wiping oil out of the housing; clean the carburetor.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil from underfilling at last change, wrong viscosity or a leak. Friction heat can burn the oil (smoke) and melt parts. Adding oil probably won't help as damage is likely done.
- pro: Small engine mechanic to diagnose; decide repair vs replace the mower.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Extra oil raises crankcase pressure, pushes oil into the cylinder or up to the air intake through the valve train; burns off hot, smoking.
- fix: Replace an oil-covered air filter. Correct oil to the full mark on the dipstick: quickly remove and replace the drain plug to release a little; if no drain plug tip the mower on its side to let a little out of the fill area, keeping the air filter and carburetor on the high side. Author prefers a turkey baster or oil evacuator through the fill area (easiest, cleanest).

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: Quickly remove and replace the drain plug to let a little out, or
- step 2: no drain plug: tip on its side, air filter and carb up, and drain from the fill area, or
- step 3: suck out with a turkey baster/oil evacuator.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: Failing engine gasket leaks oil which smokes when burning off the hot engine or muffler. Locate and replace; difficulty depends on the gasket location.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- confirm: Look for oil on the spark plug; excessive oil suggests a piston ring or valve train problem; both mean taking the engine apart. May find cylinder wall scoring or worn rings letting oil into the combustion chamber.
- pro: Take to an experienced small engine mechanic; may have to choose between replacing the engine or buying a new mower.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: Detect only by removing the cylinder head and doing a leak-down test, by an experienced mechanic. Burnt valve from overheating (valve edge falls apart); indication: a muffler glowing red from extreme heat or timing problem. Valve and seat must be cut at certain angles for good seating against the block.
