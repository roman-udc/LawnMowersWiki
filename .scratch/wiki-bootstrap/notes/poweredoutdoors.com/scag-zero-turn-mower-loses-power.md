## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "SCAG zero turn loses power and bogs down"
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- safety: Remove ignition key and spark plug boots before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old fuel develops gummy deposits that block the fuel system. Buy only what you can use in 30 days; add stabilizer. Drain the bad fuel into a container for recycling; refill. SCAG gas mowers: unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; higher ethanol is bad for small engines.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches the carburetor; remove air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into the intake; start; if it runs strong then loses power or dies, clean the carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Dirt and old-fuel residue plug the filter; replace a plugged filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Stop/start fuel flow with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank or by crimping the line; remove the end furthest from the tank of one section into a container; restore flow; check each section.
- fix: Remove the section, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air through; replace if it can't be cleared or the line is dry and cracked.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Plugged filter causes sluggish running, overheating, possible quit.
- step (paper): remove filter; wipe housing with a dry cloth without dirt into the intake; tap on a solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light passes and not damaged or extremely dirty; replace if no light.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Fouled plug gives intermittent spark and power loss; look for carbon buildup, wear, damage. Replace; or wire-brush and reuse if good and not very dark. Gap must meet engine manufacturer spec; wires securely attached.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil: friction heat, extremely hot engine, power loss; if uncaught internal parts melt (big repair or replacement). Check before each mowing. Add oil to manufacturer's level; continued problems may mean internal damage needing a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Overfill raises pressure; oil pushed into cylinder through valve train; bluish-white smoke. Continued running can cause seal damage, hydrolock, and a bent piston rod. Remove a little oil. Smoke can plug the air filter; check air filter and spark plug and clean/replace.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: Poor air circulation: remove clippings, dirt and debris collected under the engine shroud.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins can plug with mud and grass clippings; clean around them and replace broken fins.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: Slow down in thick lawns and on inclines (engine works harder).

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- tip: Mow dry grass; mow regularly; for tall grass use the highest deck setting for the first cut then lower for the next; if unavoidable slow ground speed.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Buildup under the deck makes the engine work hard turning blades and also causes a bad cut. Scrape regularly; avoid mowing wet.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull blades magnify a clogged deck's power loss; sharpen or replace.
