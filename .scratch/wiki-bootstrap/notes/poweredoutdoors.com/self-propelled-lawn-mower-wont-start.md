## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "self-propelled lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, fuel-valve-off, old-fuel, incorrect-starting-procedure, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, weak-or-dead-battery, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: caused by lack of fuel, plugged air filter, clogged line, plugged fuel filter, dirty carburetor, bad plug, bad ignition coil or faulty recoil; electric-start models add battery, ignition switch, starter solenoid.
- safety: follow operator's manual, remove the spark plug before repairs.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: check level and look for leaks; repair leaks; fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- fact: some mowers have a shutoff valve, usually near the bottom of the fuel tank; fuel may have been shut off during storage, transport or repair. Make sure it is open.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: breaks down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol attracts moisture, leaving varnish and gummy deposits causing restrictions (sluggish running or hard starting).
- fix: drain with a fuel siphon pump; add fresh fuel mixed with an additive like Sea Foam. Use gasoline with minimum octane 87 and ethanol content 10% (max).
- fact: Sea Foam stabilizes fuel, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system; STA-BIL also named.
- fact: two-cycle engines need a gas and oil mix; 4-cycle engines use gasoline not mixed with oil.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: self-propelled mowers have a bail lever/handle that stops the engine immediately when released; must be pulled back and held against the handlebar to start and run.
- fix: hold the bail lever against the handlebar while turning the key on an electric-start mower or pulling the recoil on a manual start mower; release only to stop.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace. Inline filter: arrow on side points in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.
- fact: some self-propelled mowers have no inline filter; look up the parts diagram; some use a filter or screen at the bottom of the fuel tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop flow with shutoff valve or fuel pinch-off pliers; remove hose from the carburetor into a container lower than the tank (fuel can't flow uphill without a pump); restore flow.
- fix: no good flow -> stop flow, remove the line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air; repeat; replace if can't clear.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: with fuel verified at the carburetor, remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into the intake, start; if it starts, runs and shuts down, disassemble and clean the carburetor.
- fix: remove and clean; replace damaged parts with a carburetor rebuild kit or replace carburetor assembly.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: replace annually; check/clean regularly.
- paper: remove; wipe housing and cover without letting dirt into intake; tap on solid surface; light test; replace if no light, very dirty, wet or damaged; reinstall.
- paper with foam pre-cleaner: clean paper as above; wash foam pre-cleaner in mild detergent and warm water; rinse until water runs clear; air dry; DO NOT add oil to a foam pre-cleaner.
- primary foam: remove; wipe housing; wash in mild detergent and warm water; rinse until clear; air dry; lightly coat with foam filter oil or motor oil; squeeze out excess (fully covered, not dripping).
- tip: if unsure of filter type, see operator's manual.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- see causes/clogged-air-filter above (paper, paper with foam pre-cleaner, primary foam).
- mistake: oiling a foam pre-cleaner used with a paper filter.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: dirty tip, cracked porcelain, burnt electrode.
- fix: clean deposits with wire brush; replace if very dark or damaged or burnt electrode. Gap per operator's manual; wrong gap or loose plug boot/wire causes starting problems; boot must make good connection.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- confirm: make sure plug is good first; check continuity with ohmmeter/multimeter; break in continuity -> replace.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: rope unstrung, broken pulley, springs or clips.
- fix: sometimes restring only; otherwise replace broken parts; price a full recoil assembly first, it may be cheaper than repairing components.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap vent clogged -> vacuum in tank restricts fuel.
- confirm: run with and without the cap; starts without but shuts down after refitting -> replace cap.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: electric start: if mower starts with the manual recoil, the problem is in the electric start.
- fact: some mowers have an external charger that plugs into a standard outlet; others use a battery charger.
- fix: charge, check fuses, remove corrosion, ensure good contact; replace battery if fuses fine; if still not charging, an experienced mechanic.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: battery that fails to charge -> check the fuses.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fix: test and replace if bad (electric start).

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic switch that actuates the starter motor. Test it if you hear a click or hum when turning the key or wires attached to the solenoid get hot and smoke.
