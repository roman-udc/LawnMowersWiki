## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Cub Cadet shuts off in the middle of the yard"
- alias: "Cub Cadet starts then dies"
- causes-named: old-fuel, wrong-fuel, dirty-carburetor, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dies when unable to regulate the air-to-fuel mixture: dirty carburetor, bad fuel, plugged air filter, broken fuel pump, plugged fins, other restrictions.
- pro: Source says when to take to a professional; carburetor cleaning: otherwise a repair shop; continued problems after fresh fuel -> small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Most gas contains ethanol (corn-based). Ethanol attracts moisture, begins to separate from gasoline and sinks to the bottom of the tank; water/ethanol solutions damage the mower and must be removed.
- tip: Use fuel within 30 days of buying; only buy from a busy gas station.
- fact: A fuel additive stabilizes fuel against ethanol separation and moisture buildup but must be added to fresh fuel; it cannot reverse separation that has already occurred.
- fix: Drain the tank (fuel into a container for recycling), flush the tank, fill with fresh fuel treated with stabilizer. Author prefers Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizer plus fuel system cleaner); follow bottle directions.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Small engines require unleaded gasoline with octane rating of 87 or greater and no greater than 10% ethanol. Read the pump sticker for octane and ethanol content.
- fact: Most stations carry E10 or E15; avoid E15 and especially E85 (85% ethanol; yellow pump handle in the author's anecdote; handle colors are not always consistent between stations).
- fact: Anecdote: a customer's mower died because a grandchild grabbed the yellow (E85) handle when filling the can.
- fix: Drain tank, flush, fill with fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam. If problems continue, small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Deposits from bad fuel build up; the carb can no longer regulate gas/air mixture.
- fix: Most likely can be cleaned, but buildup may be too bad to clean enough. Hard to remove all buildup no matter how hard you try. If mechanically inclined, clean it yourself; otherwise take to a lawn mower repair shop.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Pump moves gas to the carburetor; broken or cracked pump can't hold the pressure.
- confirm: Use shut-off valve or clamps to start/stop flow while checking lines. Once fuel reaches the pump, remove the line from the carburetor, put in a container, start the mower, watch for steady or pulsating flow. Replace a cracked pump or one that no longer pumps fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture; evaporation leaves a gummy substance that blocks lines and filter.
- fix: Replace a dirty filter or one not passing fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove line, spray carb cleaner then compressed air, reinstall; replace the line if clog can't be removed.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Blocked vent creates a vacuum in the tank restricting fuel flow.
- confirm: Remove the cap and start and run the mower; if it no longer dies, suspect cap. Be careful not to let dirt/debris enter the fuel system when testing without the cap. If it shuts off after running a while with the cap on, the cap is at fault.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap to vent; otherwise replace.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Plugged with grass, dirt, debris can make the engine overheat; can cause extensive engine damage ("spending a little to keep a clean filter can save a large repair bill").
- fix: Remove filter without knocking dirt into the intake; paper filter: tap against a solid surface; hold up to light; replace if no light shows through. Other filter styles have different cleaning procedures (linked article).

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins packed with dirt, grass, oil, or broken can't dissipate heat around block and cylinder head.
- fix: Remove the engine cover, clean the fins, replace damaged fins, ensure the heat shield is attached correctly.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: Replace a plug that is very dark, worn, or damaged; if good and only a little dirty, clean with a small wire brush and reuse. Gap correctly and attach the wire securely (either can cause quitting).
- tip: Author replaces the plug when troubleshooting to rule it out (inexpensive).

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: When hot it can stop working; windings separate and short; insufficient voltage to plug.
- confirm: Ohmmeter test for a break in continuity; replace if bad.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Too much oil can make the engine smoke and die while mowing; smoke can clog the air filter so the engine looks elsewhere for air. Can cause significant damage including internal engine damage and possible engine replacement.
- fix: Change oil per Cub Cadet recommendations; fill to correct level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Too little oil: additional friction, engine overheats and dies.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: Deck packed with grass and dirt, or dull blades, make engine work harder; blades may hit debris each turn, drawing on the engine, overheating and shutting down.
- fix: Inspect deck for damage, scrape debris, sharpen blades. Always run at full throttle when cutting; avoid cutting wet grass.
