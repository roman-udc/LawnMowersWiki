## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "my mower smells like gas"
- alias: "gas smell in the garage from the mower"
- causes-named: bad-gas-cap-seal, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-primer-bulb, cracked-fuel-tank, old-fuel
- safety: First roll the mower out of the garage into a well-ventilated area.
- fact: Gas fumes rise about 7" before dissipating; odor shouldn't be heavy in the garage. If you can't find a leak, take it to a repair facility.
- fact: Gas shelf life about 30 days; older gas develops a stronger odor. Buy from a busy, high-traffic station with fresh fuel, not a small remote one. If not used quickly add stabilizer to fuel cans before filling (also for winter storage or summer heat).

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- confirm: Cap is normally vented so some smell is normal but not enough to fill the garage. Remove the cap and inspect for cracks and the gasket inside. Gaskets get dry and brittle; check for wrinkled, torn or shrunk.
- fix: Wipe gasket and cap; replace gasket if needed; replace a cracked cap.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- confirm: Follow lines for leaks; residue around the clamps may be holding fuel; check for punctures or debris buildup.
- fix: Remove and install new clamps and fuel lines.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Plastic filter housing often cracks; replace a leaking filter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- confirm: Check the pump isn't cracked and fuel isn't running down the line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carb has a vent but shouldn't leak fuel. Leaks if dirty or the float is stuck; a stuck float needle lets fuel overflow. Float floats on the fuel in the bowl to fill without overfilling; sticks from gummy buildup from gasoline chemicals; right gas minimizes deposits.
- fix: Remove and clean the carburetor (steps below).

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step 1: Remove the air filter, spray carb cleaner into the air intake, start the engine. If it fires up but won't stay running, get inside the carb.
- step 2: Gather pliers, screwdrivers, sockets, ratchets.
- step 3: Photograph the carburetor (linkage and springs) for reassembly.
- step 4: Remove the throttle cable and choke cable if any.
- step 5: Undo the filter housing and the nuts/screws holding the carburetor.
- step 6: Slowly remove springs so they don't stretch (may need to twist the carb); watch the gasket between the engine block and carb so it doesn't tear.
- step 7: Remove the bottom screw of the float bowl (rag ready for gas).
- step 8: Remove the bowl without damaging the o-ring around it. Caution: no carb cleaner or chemicals on the o-ring; it stretches and can't be reused.
- step 9: Inspect the stem hanging down from the center of the carb for clogged holes; plugged holes won't draw fuel to the jet. Clean with a thick wire (flashlight helps), then rinse with carb cleaner.
- step 10: Inspect for hard crusty white buildup (fuel additives including ethanol); remove as much as you can; nearly impossible to get all.
- step 11: Reassemble in reverse using the photo.
- step 12: Add fresh fuel plus stabilizer, let the bowl fill, start; pull cord may take several pulls.
- tools: pliers, screwdrivers, sockets, ratchets, thick wire, flashlight, carb cleaner, rag.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: Residue around the bottom of the carb (bowl): replace the gasket.
- fix: Get make, model and spec number off the engine and take it to the local dealer for the correct o-ring gasket. Shut off fuel, unscrew the screw, drop the bowl, replace gasket, reinstall bowl and screw, turn fuel on, wipe down the bowl and check for leaks.

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- fact: Rubber primer bulbs get dry and brittle; replace if dry or starting to crack.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Tanks are polyethylene (resists fuel chemistry); hold up well but can crack on impact (something falling on it or hitting an object). Check for damage and leakage.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: See problem section; bad gas smells stronger.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Mower may smoke if the wrong fuel is used or fuel is mixed with another substance. Use correct, fresh gas; stabilizer if not used within 30 days. Other reasons: clogged filter, insufficient oil, valve train or piston ring problem.
