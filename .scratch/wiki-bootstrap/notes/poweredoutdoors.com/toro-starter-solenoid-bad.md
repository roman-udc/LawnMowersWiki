## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnet on/off switch that engages the starter motor. Often mounted on the starter, but may be on the frame nearer the battery.
- fact: Every electric-start engine has one; round or square, 3 or 4 posts. Positive (+) battery wire attaches to one side; following the battery positive wire finds it.
- fact: Inside: a spring and copper plate. Fails if the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes. Can also fail as a result of a weak starter, bad battery or bad ground. Electrical items can fail at any time.
- symptom: Click or hum when the key is turned and mower does not start; or a wire gets hot and begins to smoke or melt.
- fact: Not all wire schematics are the same; the basic 3/4-post schematic omits options like lights and 12 V ports.
- confirm: See procedure.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches (for loose wires), needle nose pliers (if screwdriver does not work), optional battery charger
- step 1: Make sure the battery has a full charge: at least 12.3 volts on a voltmeter.
- step 2: Set the parking brake, make sure the mower is in neutral, turn the key on.
- step 3: Bypass: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the cable from the battery and the cable to the starter. A spark is common. If the engine turns over while bypassed, the solenoid is probably bad. Needle nose pliers can be used if the screwdriver does not work.
- mistake: You may only have loose wires or a bad ground; check them before replacing the solenoid.
- step 4: Bench test (solenoid mounted on starter): remove the starter, attach the negative (-) charger clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid.
- safety: Bypassing can spark.
