## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Average mower life about 10 years (also stated as 7 to 10 years with proper maintenance); service life 200 to 1000 hours; poor maintenance can cut life expectancy in half.
- fact: Push mower single-cylinder engine life 500 to 700 hours; larger riding mower engines up to 1000 hours; commercial engines often 2000 hours and beyond.
- fact: Mower battery typically replaced every 3 to 5 years. Keep dry and free of corrosion, and give it regular charges so it doesn't crystallize.
- fact: Replace tires when excess wear or dry rot; tires cost $20 to $80, about an hour at home (extra tools); shop charges labor to dismount/mount.
- fact: Replacement belt roughly $20 to $60, under an hour to replace.
- fact: Spark plugs can cost as little as $2; replacement batteries typically start around $40.
- fact: Repair vs. replace guide: consider replacing with extensive engine or transmission damage, constant repairs outside warranty, or repair cost near new mower cost. Repair labor $40 to $150 per hour; new mowers $180 to $4000. Opinion: "do I want to put 50% of replacement cost into a mower that may last a couple more years".

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- tip: Use gasoline within 30 days, don't let it sit in the machine; sediment builds in the carburetor and fuel line, making it hard to start.
- tip: Change oil at least once each mowing season, using manufacturer-recommended oil; heavy use or large lawn: a couple of times per year.
- tip: Check air filter regularly; check the oil filter too, and replace it rather than clean it.
- tip: Check tire pressure and tread; max pressure is on the tire sidewall; look for worn spots and dry rot.
- tip: Sharpen or replace blades about once a year; before changing blades remove the spark plugs to avoid accidental starting; find replacement blades by OEM number (in the manual).
- fact: Maintenance trade-in value: clean, runs, no oil leaks; dealers check deck, belts, pulleys, blades, mechanical and safety functions, engine and hydraulic pumps for leaks.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- step: Add fuel stabilizer (e.g. Sea Foam) to a fresh tank and run the mower so it circulates.
- step: Remove the battery and charge it a couple of times over winter; fully charged batteries won't freeze but store in a cool dry place away from flammables for safety.
- step: Clean/check air and fuel filters; replace if very dirty.
- step: Sharpen or replace blades in the off-season.
- step: Store indoors dry, away from rain/snow; set rodent traps to protect wires.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- causes-named: overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, bad-head-gasket
- fact: Blue or white smoke usually means burning oil. Check oil isn't overfull; also incorrect oil grade, obstruction in the breather tube, blown head gasket, or tilting the mower to one side so oil leaks out. Drain and refill to right oil/amount; rarer cases need a professional.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Most common reason: low oil (unlubricated friction heats parts). Debris/built-up grass blocks airflow; keep mower clean.

## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, dirty-carburetor, old-fuel, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Grass/dirt clogging the air filter or blocking cooling fins starves/overheats the engine; dirt in fuel system builds up in the carburetor (drain, replace with fresh fuel, additive). Cutting very thick grass or climbing steep hills can bog down the engine.

## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, cracked-fuel-tank, weak-or-dead-battery, fouled-spark-plug
- fact: First check the fuel tank (most common reason), and the tank for leaks or cracks. Spark plugs: loose, dirty or old ones should be replaced. For battery models check battery damage or charge after sitting all winter.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- causes-named: bad-tire, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Often a damaged wheel or dull blade. Make sure all wheels are at the same height; replace a bad wheel. Sharpen the blade with a metallic file; chipped or broken blades need replacing.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: starter rope hard to pull or stuck.
- fact: Blade connects to the crankshaft, so a blade that can't turn (debris) means the starter won't turn. Clean old grass from blade and underside of housing. If clear, remove housing and inspect recoil; a bound or broken spring needs replacing.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: mower slowing down: usually a loose or damaged belt. Loose: reattach. Damaged or worn: replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: A crack or leak can make the mower seem to use excess fuel. Check tank exterior. Excess fuel consumption also a symptom of a dirty air filter.
