## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "riding mower leaking gas / wet spot on ground / burnt grass / smell of gas in garage"
- fact: hard to find leak point once gas evaporates.
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: leak sources in carb: bowl gasket failure, stuck float, stuck float needle. Bowl is on bottom of carb. Gasket (looks like a rubber band) dries and gets brittle from heat/cool cycles next to engine.
- fix gasket: get engine model and spec number for correct gasket (local engine dealer or online). Steps: shut off fuel at valve under tank; rag for fuel in bowl; remove screw at bottom of bowl and bowl; swap gasket; reinstall bowl, tighten screw.
- confirm stuck float: leak near air intake port; float regulates fuel into bowl; stuck = overflow. Take carb apart; may clean to unstick or rebuild.
- fix stuck needle: needle works with float; carb must be taken apart and rebuilt (DIY or shop). Tapping carb with rubber mallet or rubber end of hammer may temporarily free it (works a time or two) but eventually replace needle.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: gas sitting leaves gummy crusty deposits causing parts failure and leaking.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: stuck float needle -> rebuild.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: gas degrades plastic housing, soft and weak, leaks at seam or when cracked. Replace. Careful: soft plastic ends can break off inside fuel hose. Replacement online or hardware store.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: gas damages pump; check seams for leaks; leaking pump must be replaced (riding mower retailer or online).

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: metal tank (older) rusts through; replacements often no longer made, may have to patch hole. Newer tanks are high-density polyethylene, weak at seams and leak; replace tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: valve at bottom of tank, metal or plastic, known to leak; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: old lines dry, brittle, crack; also leak at clamps (loose or puncturing hose). Replace cracked lines; good time to replace pinch-style clamps; author prefers worm gear clamps (less pinching).

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: cap seal fails, gas seeps around cap; evaporates so may not see wet spot.
- confirm: rock mower back and forth to splash fuel around cap; wet spot outside of cap = replace cap.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- tip: replace clamps; worm gear clamp preferred.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- mistake: plastic ends may snap off inside fuel hose.
