## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Gravely won't start"
- fact: a Gravely needs air, fuel and spark; missing any one prevents starting.
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil
- fix: table: no gas -> fill fresh fuel. Bad/old fuel -> drain tank, refill fresh, add fuel additive like Sea Foam. Clogged cap vent -> clean cap and remove clogged vent, replace if needed. Bad spark plug (dirty/broken/bad connection/wrong gap) -> replace, secure connection, gap to manufacturer spec. Plugged air filter -> clean or replace. Plugged fuel filter -> replace. Bad/leaking fuel pump -> replace (no longer makes pressure). Blocked fuel lines -> carb cleaner and compressed air, replace if needed. Dirty carburetor (bad fuel/ethanol deposits) -> clean, replace if needed. Bad battery / loose or dirty terminals -> charge, replace if it won't hold charge, tighten cables, clean corrosion. Bad safety switch -> test and replace. Bad ignition switch or coil -> test and replace.
- fact: Source also lists a bad ignition switch, bad battery and a faulty charging system among start-preventers.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Gravely starts then dies" / "Gravely dies while mowing"
- fact: many no-start causes also shut an engine down after running; additionally the mower can overheat and shut down from a plugged air filter, plugged or broken cooling fins or insufficient oil.
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, faulty-charging-system, bad-gas-cap-seal, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fix: table: bad fuel -> drain and refill, Sea Foam additive. Plugged air filter -> clean/replace. Dirty or broken cooling fins (packed with dirt, oil, grass) -> replace broken fins and clean. Clogged fuel lines/filter -> carb cleaner + compressed air, replace plugged filter. Dirty carb -> clean all components, replace if needed. Too much engine oil (raises crankcase pressure, overheats and dies) -> remove a little oil to correct level. Bad spark plug (fouls, intermittent running) -> clean/replace, secure wires, correct gap. Faulty ignition coil (windings can separate and short) -> check continuity with an ohm meter, replace if bad. Choke in wrong position (continues restricting airflow after engine warms) -> set choke correctly. Battery not charging -> test battery and charging system, replace bad electrical components. Bad gas cap (plugged vents restrict fuel) -> replace a cap that no longer vents. Clogged mower deck (strains engine) -> periodically scrape the deck.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Gravely blowing blue, black or white smoke"
- fact: can be as simple as spilled oil from an oil change burning off a hot engine/muffler; or a more serious valve or piston ring problem.
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-head-gasket, bad-valve-train, spilled-oil-on-engine
- fix: table: plugged air filter -> clean/replace. Too much oil (pressure builds, oil gets into the cylinder through the valve train) -> drain a little oil to manufacturer's level. Low oil (friction, extreme heat) -> take to small engine shop to inspect damage; adding oil is often too late. Internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) -> small engine repair shop.
- pro: internal engine problems should go to a local small engine repair shop.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Gravely won't move or go forward" / "weak hydrostatic transmission"
- fact: check drive bypass valves first: valves at the rear that disengage the hydros so you can push the mower; when bypassed the mower free-rolls but won't drive; they must be in the operating position. Then check drive belt, tensioner spring, tensioner pulley; then hydraulic system (hot fluid, low level, air).
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fix: table: drive release lever in wrong position -> operating position. Drive pump belt worn/off -> replace worn/cracked belt, seat securely on pulleys. Bad tensioner pulley (bearing can fail) -> replace. Missing/broken idler arm spring -> replace. Low or old hydraulic oil -> change at Gravely-recommended intervals, check level periodically. Hot hydraulic fluid (can signal hydrostatic damage) -> let cool; if it runs fine cold but weak when hot, have the Gravely dealership check it. Air in hydraulic system -> bleed after fluid change per Gravely's procedure in the owner's manual.
- fact: weak hydrostatic transmission section: bad drive belt (frayed, cracked, worn -> replace), bad tensioner pulley (replace and grease the tensioner arm), low/old hydraulic oil.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "Gravely won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- fact: check the tracking; if not tracked correctly the mower veers to one side.
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, (tracking adjustment)
- fix: table: unequal tire pressure -> set to manufacturer spec; tires on each side need equal pressure. Bad dampers on zero turn (mower jerks to one side going forward) -> replace damper. Tracking adjustment needed (one tire faster than other) -> adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change tire rotation speed per owner's manual.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- fact: adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the rotation speed of the tire; refer to owner's manual.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: unequal pressure makes the mower pull to one side and also can make cut uneven (a low tire can stop the mower sitting level). Tires on each side need equal pressure.

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fact: bad dampers on a zero turn make it jerk to one side when moving forward; replace the damper.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Gravely vibrating"
- fact: small debris lodged in mower, worn engine mounting bolts or bad deck components cause vibration.
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fix: table: loose/missing engine mounting bolts (reduce vibration) -> secure/replace. Bad clutch (bearing failure, falls out; manual engagement clutch can have bushing/linkage failures) -> replace clutch. Unbalanced/damaged blades -> replace; blades must be balanced before installing. String/weeds wrapped on blade shaft -> remove all, check spindle damage. Bad spindle housing bearings -> replace bearing or whole spindle housing assembly. Worn pulleys or deck belt -> replace. Debris lodged in deck or mower -> check every place, remove ("don't overlook the small stuff").

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Gravely cuts uneven"
- fact: not always the deck; a low tire can stop the mower sitting level; check tire pressure first, then the deck.
- causes-named: worn-blade-sail, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-tire-pressure, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell, (operator error)
- fact: Source lists as a 3-column table: worn blade, bent blade, blade installed wrong, clogged deck / uneven tire pressures, unleveled deck, bent spindle or bearing, worn deck pulleys or belt / engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, damaged deck shell, operator error.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "Gravely leaking gas"
- fact: leak location hard to find; you may notice only a trail of burnt grass or a strong odor in the shed; fuel may have evaporated. Old gas degrades components, makes mechanical parts fail and plastic housings weak.
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fix: table: dirty/failed carb (residue from old gas) -> clean, replace damaged parts. Old fuel filter (plugged) -> replace. Bad fuel pump (leaks at seams) -> replace. Bad fuel tank (poly tank leaks at seams) -> replace. Bad shut-off valve (metal and plastic prone to leak) -> replace. Cracked fuel lines (rubber dries and cracks) -> replace. Bad gas cap seal (gas splashes out around cap) -> replace cap.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Gravely upkeep: regularly clean the mower, check engine oil and hydraulic oil, clean the air filter, sharpen blades and scrape the deck; do routine oil changes as required.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil raises crankcase pressure, overheats and kills the engine; oil can reach the cylinder through the valve train and cause smoke. Fix: remove a little oil to the manufacturer's level.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins packed with dirt, oil and grass keep air from cooling the engine block; replace broken fins and clean.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke is for cold starts; if left restricting airflow after warm-up the engine can shut off.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: windings can separate and short out; check continuity with an ohm meter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: seal can fail, letting gas splash out around the cap; replace the gas cap.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vent in the cap can clog, creating a vacuum in the tank and restricting fuel flow; clean the cap/vent or replace.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: clogged mower deck strains the engine, may shut it down; scrape the deck periodically.
