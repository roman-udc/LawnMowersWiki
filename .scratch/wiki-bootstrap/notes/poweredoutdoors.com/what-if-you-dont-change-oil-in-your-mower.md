## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: Author is a former outdoor power equipment service manager who has seen many engines fail from running dirty oil.
- fact: Without oil changes the oil gets very dirty and breaks down, losing cooling agents and detergents; significant engine damage.
- fact: Average mower engine holds about 3/4 to 1 quart of oil, usually SAE30. Engine is air-cooled only (if 80 degrees outside, the oil is already that warm).
- fact: Oil functions: cooling agent for internal parts; buffer between moving parts; cleaner that keeps carbon and broken-down oil from clogging internal oil passages.
- fact: Most small engine manufacturers: change oil every 25 to 50 hours, depending on engine capacity and how hard it's used. Harder/longer use raises temperature; weak oil can fail. Overheated oil turns black and thick and bakes onto internal parts, no longer flowing as designed.
- fact: Oil selection: most small gas engines use SAE30 except Kohler, which needs 10W-30 because of the hydraulic lifter. Most engines take 3/4 quart, up to 2 quarts for larger.
- tip: Check oil before every mow. Always adding oil = oil leak, burning oil, or using oil.
- fact: Oil leak: look over engine for signs; replace the gaskets leaking; check filter seal. Burning oil: check for plugged air filter (makes engine work harder and burn oil); clean/replace. Using oil: overheating, or valve/ring problem; take to a repair center for engine diagnosis.
- fact: Replacement engine costs $200 to $2,700 depending on manufacturer and horsepower; need the model and spec number to buy a compatible engine (same numbers for parts); consider mower's condition (frame, shell, deck) vs buying a new mower. (Part buying/economics, borderline.)

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Old/dirty oil breaks down; Kohler needs 10W-30; SAE30 for most; always adding oil indicates leak/burn/use.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- tools: socket or wrench for the oil plug (some square, some hex in varying sizes); funnel; filter wrench if the engine has a filter; drain pan (or leak-proof container) plus closed container for used oil; cardboard/newspaper; rubber gloves; rags.
- fact: Don't use milk or soda containers; empty oil containers OK; never dispose of oil in landfill, drain or on property; take to a recycling center.
- parts: filter if equipped: OEM per owner's manual; aftermarket possible via cross-reference websites; author always uses OEM; customers had good results with Stens brand.
