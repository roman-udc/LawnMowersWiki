## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "riding mower won't start after winter storage"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, weak-or-dead-battery, wet-electrical-components
- fact: after winter, old gas corrodes the fuel system and restricts flow with clogs in the fuel lines or a dirty carburetor; a bad plug, dead battery and corroded electrical components/wires can also prevent starting.
- safety: protect hands, skin and eyes when working with the battery, fuel and electrical system.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: fuel breaks down over time, leaves a sticky substance and corrodes fuel components.
- fix: drain the tank, add fresh fuel, add additive like Sea Foam Motor Treatment to clean and remove moisture.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove an end of the line into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump), restore flow; good flow - reinstall; poor flow - shut off, remove that section.
- fix: spray carburetor cleaner into the line to loosen the clog, blow compressed air through; repeat until clear; reinstall. Replace with new line of same length and diameter if unclogable or cracked and prone to leaking.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: fuel bowl holds a little fuel; old fuel gums up with sticky, crusty deposits; fuel jet clogs and small parts stick. If fuel reaches the carburetor but not the engine, no start.
- fix: take apart and clean with carburetor cleaner; rebuild or replace if it can't be cleaned or parts are damaged.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: unused plug can corrode. Remove with a socket wrench and inspect for corrosion. Replace if corroded, very dark or damaged; if good but dirty/carboned, wire brush. Gap to engine manufacturer spec before installing, attach plug wire securely.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: a battery left in the cold can freeze and no longer hold a charge; fully charged batteries won't freeze.
- fact: freezing chart (charge / 12 V / 6 V / freezing temp): 100% 12.78 V 6.39 V -80 F; 92% 12.66 6.33 -71.3 F; 85% 12.57 6.28 -62 F; 62% 12.27 6.13 -16 F; 50% - - -10 F; 40% 11.97 5.98 +5 F; 20% 11.67 5.84 +19 F; zero charge +20 F.
- confirm: Source says check charge level "using an ohmmeter" (sic; voltage reading); most riding mowers use 12 V; fully charged reads 12.7 V; less - charge; won't hold a charge - replace.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: put on safety gear for eyes and skin (acid or electric shock).
- step 2: access the battery and terminals (may need a screwdriver to uncover).
- step 3: leave the battery in its casing with the terminal cables attached.
- step 4: connect the positive charging cable first (red, plus sign), then negative (black). Mnemonic: positive (red +) is the last one off and the first one on.
- step 5: only touch the rubber coating on the cables and clamps.
- step 6: set charger voltage (usually 12 V) and amps; more amperage charges faster; start at 2 amps, up to no more than 10 amps.
- step 7: if the charger has a gauge, keep connected until fully charged. A 10 amp charger usually takes about one hour for a full charge.
- tools: battery charger and cables, screwdriver

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- fact: storing the mower with debris or moisture causes corrosion; check safety switches and wiring for good connections and no corrosion.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: battery: remove it or fully charge it for winter storage (a fully charged battery will not freeze); if unsure, remove and store in a cool, dry place away from moist or humid areas.
- fact: fuel: either empty the tank or add stabilizer.
- step (empty tank) 1: empty the tank with a fuel siphon pump into a fuel container.
- step (empty tank) 2: run the mower until it stops from fuel starvation to remove most fuel from the system.
- step (stabilize) 1: add fuel stabilizer per bottle instructions (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment).
- step (stabilize) 2: run the mower a few minutes so treated fuel works through the system before storing.
