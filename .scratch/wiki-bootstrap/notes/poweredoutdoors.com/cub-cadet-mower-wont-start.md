## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Cub Cadet mower won't start"
- alias: "riding mower turning over but not starting"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, wrong-choke-setting, fuel-valve-off, bad-ignition-coil, low-compression
- fact: Cub Cadet article covers lawn tractors, riding mowers and zero-turns (models named: XT1, XT2, SLT1550, Ultima XT).
- fact: engine needs air, fuel, compression and spark; check fuel flow, airflow, spark.
- confirm (fuel test): remove air filter cover and filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake (author uses carb cleaner, not starter fluid), try to start by pull rope or key. If it attempts to start or starts, the fuel system is the problem (most common: dirty carb, clogged fuel filter, clogged fuel lines; fuel pump if used). If it does not attempt to start, most likely spark plug problem.
- confirm: before fuel checks, if the mower has a fuel shut-off valve make sure it is open (often closed for storage/transport).
- confirm (airflow test): remove filter, inspect it; check the choke plate: manual choke lever on = plate closed, lever off = plate open (on some models off is the fast throttle position); auto-choke: closed cold, opens when warm (many Cub Cadet push models). If choke not working, check linkage and cable; loosen stuck choke with carb cleaner on linkages and shaft; replace a bad choke cable.
- confirm (spark test): pull plug wire boot, attach spark plug tester to the plug wire, attach tester's other end to the plug (still installed), crank by pull cord or electric start; glow in the tester's transparent section means spark. No glow: bad plug or bad ignition coil; on electric start also battery, wiring, starter solenoid.
- tip: guessing and throwing parts at a mower gets expensive.
- fact (FAQ): click but no start = starting electrical failure, starter solenoid clicking and failing to turn over engine; check weak battery, loose wiring, corroded terminals, bad solenoid.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with fresh fuel; inspect fuel system and repair leaks; make sure fuel gauge works (if equipped); gas minimum 87 octane, no more than 10% ethanol.
- fact: Source text elsewhere says "minimum of 10% ethanol content" in the buying tip, which appears to be a typo for max 10%.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- fact: if mower has a fuel shut-off valve it is often closed during storage or transport; ensure it is open before diagnosing fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas deteriorates as quickly as 30 days after purchase; may lose volatility and no longer combust. Ethanol attracts moisture leaving varnish and gummy residue in lines, filter and carb.
- confirm: strong foul odor or yellowish color.
- fix: wipe around the fuel cap, remove, siphon fuel into an approved container, refill with fresh fuel plus additive like Sea Foam Motor Treatment (cleans system, removes moisture, stabilizes); start and let it run so treated fuel works through.
- tip: consume within 30 days; store in dry location away from moisture.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: on a Cub Cadet the tank vent is built into the fuel cap; clogged vent forms a vacuum that stops fuel to the carb.
- confirm: wipe around cap, loosen it, try to start. If it won't start, tighten and keep troubleshooting. If it starts, tighten cap and let it run; if it dies after running a while, the cap is not venting.
- fix: replace the cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: carbon buildup or oil on the tip causes misfire and no start; loose plug wire also.
- fix: replace very dirty/damaged plug; twin-cylinder engines have two plugs.
- step 1: remove plug wire (boot); wipe around the plug.
- step 2: remove with spark plug socket, 3/4 inch or 5/8 inch depending on engine.
- step 3: inspect for damage, wear, dark carbon tip; replace; or wire-brush if lightly dirty.
- step 4: check gap to engine manufacturer spec; install; attach wire.
- tip: replace spark plug(s) annually (author's practice, inexpensive).
- tools: spark plug socket (3/4 in or 5/8 in), wire brush, gap tool.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- step: see steps above (remove boot, wipe, socket 3/4 or 5/8, inspect, gap to spec, install, reattach).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: starves engine; may overheat and draw air through the crankcase causing extensive damage. Replace annually, clean several times per season.
- fix: paper: remove; wipe housing and cover (don't let dirt fall into intake); tap to loosen dirt; light should be visible through a good filter; replace if very dirty, damaged or not sealing; reattach cover.
- fix: primary foam: remove; wipe housing; inspect; clean in soapy water, rinse until water runs clear; squeeze and dry; coat in clean engine oil, squeeze out excess (not all foam filters are oiled; check manual); install.
- mistake: never oil a foam pre-cleaner used with a paper filter; it damages the paper.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- see paper and foam steps above; replace annually, clean several times per season.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- confirm: fuel in should equal fuel out; if not, filter is plugged.
- step 1: shut off fuel with shut-off valve or clamp the line.
- step 2: needle-nose pliers to remove clamps and slide filter out of the lines.
- step 3: filter may have a directional arrow; arrow points in direction of fuel flow.
- step 4: install new filter and clamp securely.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: shut off fuel supply (valve or clamp).
- step 2: remove clamps with needle-nose pliers and slide filter out.
- step 3: arrow points toward fuel flow (toward carb).
- step 4: install new filter and clamp.
- tools: needle-nose pliers, line clamp.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow with compressed air until free of dirt and gummy residue; or replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump needed when tank is below carb; vacuum pump uses crankcase pressure; old fuel can deteriorate components.
- confirm: inspect for cracks or fuel outside the pump (then it can't use crankcase pressure). Verify fuel reaches the inlet port first. Shut off fuel; remove line from carb into a container; start mower; expect steady or pulsating flow; replace pump if not.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: crusty buildup and gummy deposits from ethanol fuel; engine runs rough or won't start.
- fix: clean it yourself if mechanical, otherwise a repair shop; replace if very bad using engine model and spec to get the correct part.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: trace the positive cable to the solenoid and the cable from solenoid to starter; loose or corroded ends break continuity.
- fix: tighten, secure wiring, remove corrosion with a water and baking soda mixture.
- safety: remove negative battery cable first, then positive.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: check after terminals and cables are good; test with a multimeter, about 12.7 volts needed; charge if lower; replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence control uses several switches, kill engine when operator leaves seat; faulty switch may not recognize the operator.
- confirm: can temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one.
- safety: do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed; replace bad switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: turn key or press button and nothing happens. Test with a multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- confirm: after plug is verified good, check coil continuity with an ohm meter; break in continuity means replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; a wire attached to the solenoid getting hot, smoking or melting.
- fix: test and replace.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: contributes to a weak battery; bad stator or alternator among possible parts; author suggests letting a Cub Cadet dealer or small engine mechanic identify which part.
- confirm: charging system can be tested with an ohmmeter (see author's battery article).

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: on a Cub Cadet riding mower or zero-turn the brake must be engaged, PTO switch off and user in the seat for the engine to start; check manual for other interlock safety switches.

## page: causes/low-compression
- fact: blown head gasket, valve train problem, piston ring issue or damaged cylinder cause poor compression; engine runs rough and may not start.
- pro: have an experienced small engine mechanic or Cub Cadet servicing dealer diagnose engine problems.
