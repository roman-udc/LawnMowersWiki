## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Husqvarna won't crank or turn over"
- alias: "turn the key and it won't even turn over"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: Most likely an electrical or starting system problem.
- safety: Remove the negative battery cable before electrical repairs. If unsure of skills, take it to a Husqvarna dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: A fully charged 12-volt battery reads about 12.7 volts; lower, charge it.
- tip: Keep charged, especially in storage, to extend life.
- fix: Charge; replace if it won't hold a charge; bring old battery to avoid core fee.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear protective gear for eyes and skin.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed); don't remove battery from casing.
- step 2: Connect positive (red / plus) cable to positive terminal first.
- step 3: Attach negative (black / minus) cable to negative terminal.
- step 4: Don't touch anything without a rubber coating.
- step 5: Charger at 12 volts typical; start at 2 amps, up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge best.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: Vibration loosens wiring (poor continuity); moisture corrodes terminals.
- fix: Clean with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); secure loose wires; replace damaged or severely corroded parts.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- confirm: Multimeter probes on each prong measuring resistance; near 0 good; infinity bad.
- fix: Same amperage replacement; if fuses keep blowing, take to Husqvarna dealer or repair shop.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step: Probe prongs with multimeter on resistance; near 0 good, infinity blown; replace with same amperage.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned, nothing happens.
- step 1: Multimeter continuity test; find prongs marked B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid).
- step 2: Insert key, turn to start.
- step 3: Multimeter on resistance, one probe on B and the other on S.
- fact: Good near 0 ohms; bad infinite, replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Operator presence control system includes switches that prevent starting unless conditions are met, like engaging the brake.
- confirm: Test with multimeter; temporary bypass for troubleshooting only.
- safety: Never operate without the safety switch or with it bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Electromagnetic switch that engages the starter. Goes bad when spring weakens or copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause failure. Needs a fully charged battery before testing. Article links to its Husqvarna solenoid article.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: After ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid. Can be removed and tested.
- tip: Have a starter/alternator specialist test and rebuild before buying new (author's recommendation).
