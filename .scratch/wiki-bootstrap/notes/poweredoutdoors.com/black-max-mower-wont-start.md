## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Black Max mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, fuel-valve-off, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, incorrect-starting-procedure, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Start diagnosis with the three requirements for a combustion engine: air, fuel, spark.
- fact: Source summary: plugged air filter, wrong choke setting, bad spark plug, faulty ignition coil, plugged fuel filter, clogged fuel line, dirty carburetor, or old gas.
- safety: Follow all owner's manual safety guidelines before working; consult a professional if unable to repair safely.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: A fuel leak or a stopped gas gauge may make you run out sooner than expected.
- fix: Check fuel system for a leak or faulty gauge, repair, fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Valve may have been shut when working on the fuel system or before storage; no fuel reaches the engine when closed.
- fix: Put the fuel shut-off valve in the open position.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Gas can start breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase; gummy deposits clog fuel system components.
- fix: Drain old fuel (a fuel siphon pump works well); mix gas with fuel stabilizer and refill.
- tip: Use fuel within 30 days or add stabilizer. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes fuel, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system).
- brand: Black Max 4-cycle engine: gas minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Clogged vent in the cap makes a vacuum in the tank that restricts fuel flow.
- confirm: Run the mower with and without the fuel cap. If it starts without the cap but shuts down after the cap goes back on, the cap cannot vent.
- fix: Clean the cap or replace it with a new Black Max gas cap.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- brand: Black Max push mowers have a blade control lever as a safety feature; it must be pulled back and held against the handlebar to start and run the mower.
- fix: Hold the blade lever against the handlebar while turning the key (electric start) or pulling the recoil (manual start). Releasing the lever stops the engine and the blade.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Filter strains dirt; can plug enough to starve the carburetor.
- fix: Remove and replace. Many filters have an arrow on the side; install with the arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Dirt and deposits from old fuel can block the line.
- confirm: step 1: stop fuel flow with the shut-off valve (or clamp the line if no valve).
- confirm: step 2: remove the end of the hose farthest from the tank and put it in a container.
- confirm: step 3: restore fuel flow and verify fuel reaches the container. The container must be lower than the tank (fuel cannot run uphill).
- fix: If no good flow, stop flow, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner in it, blow through with compressed air; if the clog will not free, replace the line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- confirm: After confirming fuel gets to the carburetor, remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and start. If it starts, runs and then shuts down, disassemble and clean the carburetor.
- fact: Gummed carburetor components may not release the gas needed to start.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Best practice is replace the air filter annually; check and clean regularly.
- fix: Paper filter step 1: remove filter from housing.
- fix: Paper filter step 2: wipe out dirt in housing and cover; do not let dirt fall into the intake.
- fix: Paper filter step 3: inspect, tap filter to loosen dirt.
- fix: Paper filter step 4: hold up to a light; if good light shows through, reuse; if not, or very dirty, damaged, or no longer seals, replace.
- fix: Paper filter step 5: install filter and reattach cover.
- fix: Foam primary filter: remove, wipe housing, inspect for damage and replace if needed; if good wash in soapy water and rinse until water runs clear; squeeze and let dry; lightly saturate in clean engine oil and squeeze out excess; install and reattach cover.
- mistake: Do not add oil to a foam pre-filter used over a paper filter; it damages the paper filter. Check the operator's manual for which fluid to use.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove filter from housing; wipe out the housing and cover without letting dirt fall into intake.
- step 2: Paper: tap to loosen dirt, hold to light, reuse if light passes, else replace. Foam: wash in soapy water, rinse clear, squeeze dry, lightly oil with clean engine oil, squeeze out excess.
- step 3: Reinstall filter and cover.
- mistake: Never oil a foam pre-filter that sits on a paper filter.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Fouled when the tip is dirty, porcelain cracked or electrode burnt.
- fix: Clean deposits off the tip; replace if tips are very dark or damaged. Gap per the Black Max operator's manual.
- mistake: Incorrect gap or a loose spark plug wire also cause starting problems.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Check the spark plug first; coil supplies voltage to the plug.
- confirm: Check coil continuity with an ohm meter; a break in continuity means replace the coil.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Rope may be unstrung; pulley, springs or clips may be broken.
- fix: Restringing may be enough; otherwise replace broken parts. Price a full recoil assembly first, since replacing the assembly may be cheaper than tearing down.
