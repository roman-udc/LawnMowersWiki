- alias: "Bolens lawn mower won't start"

- fact: Source headline list: bad spark plug, plugged air filter, wrong choke setting, dirty carburetor, plugged fuel filter, sheared flywheel key, bad safety switch, old gas (14 causes total).
- safety: remove spark plug boot(s) before any repair.
- tip: guessing and throwing parts at the mower gets expensive.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- brand: Bolens small fuel tank needs refilling more often.
- fix: fill with fresh unleaded gas, min 87 octane, ethanol no greater than 10%.
- brand: most Bolens in use are 4-cycle (straight gasoline); a very old Bolens may be 2-cycle needing gas-oil mix, identified by only one fill port for a gas/oil mix.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; varnish and deposits clog the fuel system.
- confirm: pungent strong smell or yellow darkened fuel; compare color to fresh fuel.
- fix: siphon old fuel out with a fuel siphon pump; mix fresh fuel with Sea Foam or STA-BIL; once started, run engine a while so treated fuel works through the system.
- tip: consume fuel within 30 days; store fuel cool and dry.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: clogged cap vent makes a vacuum in the tank; won't start.
- confirm: use a gauge for vacuum, or loosen the cap and see if the engine starts. If it does, install cap, let it run and listen for it to bog down and shut off.
- fix: replace cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: fouled plug causes misfire/no start.
- confirm: check for spark with a spark plug tester; remove plug and inspect.
- fix: replace if damaged, extremely dirty or burnt electrodes; if slightly dirty clean with wire brush without damaging the metal; check gap to manufacturer spec; securely attach plug wire.
- tools: spark plug tester, wire brush, gap gauge.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: ignition coil (ignition module) supplies voltage to the plug.
- confirm: after verifying plug is good, inspect coil wire: cracked, worn or exposed wires mean replace (exposed wires can ground out the coil). Check continuity with a multimeter or verify spark with a spark tester. Replace if continuity is broken.

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- fact: sheared key throws engine timing off so spark isn't at the optimal time.
- confirm: inspect blade; if damaged, the impact may have sheared the key (remove the spark plug wire first). Pull the starter rope: a sheared key makes the rope spring back instead of pulling smoothly.
- fix: replace the key (procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: very dirty filter starves engine of air so it will not start; blocked airflow may cause the engine to overheat and draw air from the crankcase, causing extensive damage.
- fix: paper filter: remove carefully without dropping dirt into the intake, wipe out housing with clean dry cloth; inspect for damage (needs good seal); replace if very dirty, damaged, or covered in fuel or oil; if slightly dirty tap against a solid surface (do not damage paper element); reinstall and secure cover.
- fix: foam filter: inspect (dry, brittle or torn means replace); wash in bucket of water and mild dish soap; rinse and dry completely (sun speeds it); lightly saturate with clean engine oil (not dripping); install.
- mistake: never coat a foam pre-cleaner in oil; it can damage a paper element (the foam instructions are for the primary foam filter).

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1 (paper): inspect for damage; replace if very dirty, damaged or fuel/oil soaked. Wipe housing with clean dry cloth.
- step 2 (paper): tap filter against a solid surface to knock dirt out without damaging the element; reinstall and secure cover.
- step 1 (foam): inspect; replace if dry, brittle or torn. Wash in water and mild dish soap; rinse; dry completely (sun helps); lightly saturate in clean engine oil; install and attach cover.
- mistake: never coat a foam pre-cleaner in oil.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke plate closed to start a cold engine, open for a warm engine. Manual-choke models use a throttle lever to open/close choke; auto-choke models use a temperature gauge near the muffler.
- confirm: remove air filter to view choke plate; verify it opens and closes.
- fix: if stuck, lubricate linkages and choke shaft with carburetor cleaner.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: filter clogs with very dirty fuel or if not replaced regularly.
- fix: replace if fuel flow stops at the filter.
- confirm: if no filter visible, there may be one at the bottom of the tank (shine flashlight in; drain tank and remove fuel line to access); some engines have a strainer at the top of the tank and/or a sediment bowl under the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, spray carb cleaner in, blow through with compressed air until clear of dirt and gummy residue, or replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: crusty buildup and gummy deposits from old ethanol fuel; carb can't regulate fuel/air so engine runs rough or won't start.
- fix: remove and clean with carb cleaner; make sure float bowl, float needle and jet work; replace if poor condition.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: safety switch is part of operator presence control; stops engine when bail lever (safety bar) is released. Faulty switch may not recognize lever squeezed to handle.
- confirm: follow cable from bail lever down to the side of the engine; switch is designed to ground when lever is released. Check manual for additional safety switches on the model.
- safety: never use a mower with a bypassed safety switch.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- brand: Bolens push mowers use a recoil starter.
- fix: if rope is no longer wrapped, may restring; may have broken pulley, spring or clips; price parts vs whole recoil assembly; replacing may be more cost-effective.

## page: causes/low-compression
- fact: indicates head gasket, valve, cylinder wall or piston ring damage.
- confirm: quick check: remove spark plug wire and slowly turn the blade backward to the compression stage; feel some resistance. Does not prove sufficiency; do a compression test or leak-down test.
- brand: on engines with automatic compression release (ACR), do a leak-down test unless manufacturer gives ACR compression specs.

## page: procedures/check-compression
- step 1: remove spark plug wire; turn blade backward slowly to compression stage and feel for resistance (quick check only).
- step 2: do a compression test or leak-down test for real numbers; ACR engines: leak-down unless manufacturer gives ACR compression specs.
