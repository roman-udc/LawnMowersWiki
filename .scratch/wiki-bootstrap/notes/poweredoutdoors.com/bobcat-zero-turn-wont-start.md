## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Bobcat zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, bad-gas-cap-seal, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure, wrong-choke-setting, bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Bobcat won't start when not getting enough air, fuel and spark: plugged air filter, clogged fuel line, bad fuel pump, dirty carburetor, bad spark plug, faulty ignition coil, bad battery, faulty switch or loose connections.
- fact: Troubleshoot order in article: lack of fuel (empty tank, old fuel, plugged filter, clogged line, bad pump, dirty carb); spark (damaged/dirty plug); lack of air (plugged filter, wrong choke/stuck choke, wrong starting procedure); electrical (bad/weak battery, loose cables/wiring, faulty safety switch, bad ignition switch).
- safety: take all safety precautions including removing the spark plug wire.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, faulty-charging-system
- fact: if you turn the key and the Bobcat doesn't start or even turn over, the ignition switch could be the problem.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: forgot to check; possible undetected fuel leak, forgotten fill-up or faulty fuel gauge.
- fix: check tank has enough fuel; check for a leak or faulty gauge and repair; add fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas starts breaking down as quickly as 30 days after purchase.
- fact: most gas has ethanol (often corn-based), which attracts moisture from air, causes premature corrosion and leaves sticky deposits restricting fuel flow.
- fix: drain tank with a fuel siphon pump; mix fresh gas with Sea Foam or STA-BIL.
- tip: buy only what you can use in 30 days; otherwise add a stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment. It must go in fresh fuel; it cannot reverse old fuel.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- brand: Bobcat requires unleaded gas, minimum octane 87, maximum ethanol 10%. Don't use E15 or E85 (up to 15% / 85% ethanol).
- fact: ethanol-free fuel is best but a little pricey.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Bobcat mowers: unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; no E15/E85; ethanol-free is best.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gas goes bad in about 30 days; buy what you'll use in 30 days or add Sea Foam / STA-BIL to fresh fuel. Stabilizer can't fix already-old fuel.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Bobcat gas cap vents air; a plugged/damaged vent makes a vacuum in the tank and starves the engine.
- confirm: mower won't start until you loosen/remove the cap. If it starts after loosening, retighten and run it a while to see if it shuts off and won't restart until the cap is loosened again.
- fix: replace with a new Bobcat fuel cap.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- same as bad-gas-cap-seal note above: plugged cap vent creates tank vacuum; loosening cap lets it start; replace cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: fouled/damaged plug gives intermittent spark; incorrect gap or loose plug wire can contribute.
- confirm: remove with socket wrench; look for cracked porcelain, burnt electrode, dirty plug.
- fix: damaged or excessively dirty/dark plug -> replace. Slightly dirty -> clean with wire brush. Gap to engine manufacturer spec before installing; attach plug wire securely.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- step 1: Remove plug with a socket wrench and inspect.
- step 2: Gap new/cleaned plug to engine manufacturer's specification.
- step 3: Install and securely attach plug wire.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: zero-turn mowing stirs lots of dust; dirt plugs the filter and restricts air so mower won't start. Running plugged can overheat the engine.
- safety: never run without an air filter, even briefly.
- tip: start each season with a new air filter; check/clean several times a season, more often when dusty.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- tools: dry clean rag
- step 1: Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe dirt out of housing with dry rag; don't let dirt fall into intake.
- step 3: Tap filter on a solid surface to knock dirt out. Do not use compressed air (damages filter).
- step 4: Hold paper element to light; if light shines through, reuse.
- step 5: If no light (oily, very dirty, damaged), replace.
- step 6: Reinstall filter and housing cover.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: filter strains dirt from fuel; clogs if not changed regularly or with dirty gas; prevents steady flow so mower won't start.
- fix: replace; good practice to replace annually. On an inline filter the arrow on its side must point in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: replace annually; inline filter arrow points in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop flow with fuel shut-off valve or fuel pinch pliers; remove the end of a hose section furthest from tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't flow uphill without the pump); restore flow and watch the stream. Good flow -> reattach.
- fix: stop flow, remove line, spray carburetor cleaner into it, blow compressed air through; repeat until clear. If can't clear, replace the line.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: old gas sitting in the pump can degrade it; pump uses engine vacuum to draw fuel from tank to carburetor.
- confirm: check for leaks; verify fuel reaches pump inlet; disconnect the hose from the carburetor into a container, start mower and watch for constant or pulsating flow.
- fix: replace the pump if no constant/pulsating flow.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old fuel leaves deposits that clog the fuel jet and gum small parts; carburetor regulates gas mixed with air.
- fix: disassemble and clean gummy/crusty buildup; or replace, or have a small engine mechanic clean it. If too dirty to clean, replace: find engine model and spec number and contact the dealer (e.g. Bobcat with a Kawasaki engine -> Kawasaki engine dealer).

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: terminals not corroded and attached securely.
- fix: clean corroded terminals with baking-soda solution of 2 cups water + 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda; scrub with a wire brush.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter, about 12.7 V; charge if lower; replace if it won't hold a charge.
- fact: a battery that won't hold a charge prevents starting.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Bobcat has safety switches preventing starting unless conditions are met, and one that disengages the deck and shuts off the mower if the operator isn't sensed.
- fix: follow correct starting procedure from the operator's manual first; if still no start, test switches with a multimeter. Temporary bypass only for troubleshooting.
- safety: never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: test with a multimeter. fix: replace if bad.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: not the primary no-start cause but contributes to a weak battery. If battery keeps dying and needs repeated charging, suspect it. Bad stator or alternator can be the problem; test charging system with an ohmmeter (article links to a battery-drain article).
- pro: have a Bobcat dealer mechanic or small engine shop check it; parts stores usually won't take back electrical parts, so guessing gets expensive.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: cold engine needs choke engaged (restricts air, richer mix); after warming, set choke to off so enough air mixes with fuel.
- fix: use choke to start cold, then move to off; check choke linkages if choke lever isn't working properly.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- same as above: choke must be off after engine warms; check linkages.
