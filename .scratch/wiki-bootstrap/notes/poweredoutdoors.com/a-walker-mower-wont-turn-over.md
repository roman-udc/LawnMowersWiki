## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Walker mower won't turn over"
- alias: "Walker mower won't crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- safety: Always remove the black negative battery cable before repairing the electrical system. Follow the Walker operator's manual safety precautions.
- tip: Order given: battery first, then cables/connections, fuse, ignition switch, safety switch, solenoid, starter. Solenoid test requires a fully charged battery.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: A weak battery doesn't give the solenoid enough power to energize the starter and turn the engine (problems/engine-wont-crank).
- confirm: Test the battery (Source points to its battery-drain article for test steps).
- fix: Charge with a charger; a new battery must be bought if it won't hold a charge.
- tip: Keep the battery charged, especially in cold weather; a battery can freeze and be damaged when not fully charged.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: battery charger (12-volt), screwdriver (to uncover the battery), protective gear for eyes and skin
- step 1: Access the battery and terminals; may need a screwdriver to uncover it. Do not remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect the charging cables, positive first: red cable / plus sign on the positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach the negative cable (black / negative sign) to the negative terminal.
- step 4: Do not touch anything without a rubber coating, to prevent electrocution.
- step 5: Set charger voltage and amperage. Average lawn mower battery is 12 volts. More amps charge faster; start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best.
- safety: Wear protective gear for eyes and skin before charging.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: Mower won't crank after battery is confirmed good and charged; cables/wiring come loose from engine vibration and bouncing over uneven ground.
- confirm: Check connections are secure and for corrosion buildup that breaks continuity. Moisture on connections starts corrosion.
- fix: Clean corrosion with a small wire brush and a baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda). Disconnect the battery and remove components before cleaning. Replace terminals/components if corrosion is severe and can't be cleaned sufficiently.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- symptom: Won't turn over; the fuse protects the electrical system.
- confirm: Multimeter probe on each prong of the fuse, measure resistance. Near 0 = good; infinity = bad fuse.
- fix: Replace with a fuse of the same amperage.
- pro: If fuses keep blowing, take it to a dealership or repair shop to find the root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: Multimeter on resistance; probe on each prong of the fuse.
- step 2: Near 0 = good; infinity = bad.
- step 3: Replace with the same amperage.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: Insert and turn the key and nothing happens; Walker fails to turn over/start.
- confirm: Multimeter continuity. Find prongs marked B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid). Key in START position, meter on resistance, one probe on B and the other on S. Good switch reads near 0 ohms; bad reads infinite resistance and must be replaced.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: Walker fails to turn over; mower has an operator presence control system.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter. May temporarily bypass the safety switch to identify a bad one, for troubleshooting only.
- fix: Replace a bad safety switch.
- safety: Never operate a mower without the safety switch. Never run a mower with a safety switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Starter solenoid is an electromagnetic switch that engages the starter motor.
- fact: Solenoid goes bad when the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes. A weak starter, bad battery, or bad ground can also cause it to fail.
- confirm: Battery must be fully charged before testing the solenoid; then follow the Source's linked solenoid diagnosis guide.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: Rule out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid first; the starter can then be removed and tested.
- pro: Author recommends a local shop that specializes in starter/alternator repair test and rebuild the starter if possible before buying a pricey new one (opinion).
