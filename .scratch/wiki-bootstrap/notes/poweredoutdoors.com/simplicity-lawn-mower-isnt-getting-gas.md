## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Simplicity mower runs sluggish, almost dies, or dies"
- alias: "mower isn't getting gas"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: sluggish running, nearly dying or dying - points to the mower not getting the gas it needs
- safety: follow operator's manual safety precautions

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: most gas contains ethanol (described as corn-based) that attracts moisture; water/ethanol mix leaves gummy deposits restricting flow and can cause premature failure of fuel components; gas can break down in as little as 30 days
- fix: siphon old fuel from the tank into an approved fuel container (to recycle later); add fresh fuel plus an additive to stabilize, reduce moisture and clean the fuel system (Sea Foam Motor Treatment used by author)
- brand: Simplicity gas mowers require unleaded gasoline, minimum octane 87, maximum 10% ethanol

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: strains dirt and sediment; replace annually at full service
- fix: replace; arrow on side of filter must point in the direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: gummy deposits from old fuel block the line
- confirm: stop/start flow with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the fuel tank, or hose pinch pliers to clamp; check each section for fuel still running out
- fix: remove clogged section, spray carburetor cleaner, blow with compressed air, repeat; replace if can't clear or dry and cracked

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: needed when the carburetor sits higher than the tank; most Simplicity mowers use a vacuum-style pump that builds pressure off the crankcase
- confirm: step 1 stop fuel flow; step 2 remove fuel line from pump inlet port; step 3 put it in a container lower than the tank and restart flow - fuel out confirms flow to the pump, otherwise find blockage in lines/filter; step 4 reattach to inlet; step 5 remove the line from the carburetor into a container; step 6 start fuel flow and start the mower - steady or pulsating flow expected
- fix: replace the pump if no flow, cracked or leaking

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: confirm fuel reaches carburetor (lines, filter, pump); remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner in the intake, start; if it starts but won't keep running, remove and disassemble the carburetor for cleaning (article links own steps)

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: air passes through the vent in the Simplicity fuel cap; plugged vent makes tank act like a vacuum
- confirm: run with and without the cap; runs OK without but dies or sluggish with it = plugged cap
- fix: try cleaning the cap vent; otherwise new cap

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: use a fuel additive to reduce moisture and stabilize fuel to minimize clogging from old fuel
