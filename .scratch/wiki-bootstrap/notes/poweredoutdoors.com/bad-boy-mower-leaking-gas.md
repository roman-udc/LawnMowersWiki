## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "smell of gas fumes in the garage from my mower"
- alias: "Bad Boy mower leaking gas"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- safety: Open the garage door to ventilate and remove fumes before working; working in an enclosed area with gas fumes can be extremely dangerous.
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety instructions; consult a professional if not skilled/in condition.
- fact: Bad Boy may leak from carburetor, fuel tank, fuel lines, fuel pump, shut-off valve, fuel filter. Often from old fuel degrading components or leaving residue that gums up and sticks; components also dry and brittle with age.
- fact: Fix leaks because fuel can damage the lawn and wastes money.
- pro: If unsure how to diagnose/repair safely, have the local Bad Boy dealer or repair shop do it.
- brand: Bad Boy zero turn.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: Fuel leaking from the carburetor (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas).
- fact: Bowl gasket sits between the carb bowl (at the bottom of the carb) and carb, looks like a rubber band. Dries out and loses sealing from extreme temperature changes; near the engine it heats while running and cools when off. Replace when it fails.
- confirm: Stuck float: look for a leak near the air intake. Float acts as gatekeeper for fuel into the carb; old-fuel deposits make it stick, so fuel keeps flowing and overflows out of the carb. Need to take the carb apart to find the reason; may need to clean carb and float or replace float.
- confirm: Stuck float needle works with the float to keep gas flowing into the bowl; if it sticks, replace. Tapping the carb side with a rubber mallet may free it but is only temporary.
- fix: Replace carburetor bowl gasket (steps in procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor).
- tip: To order carb parts you need the engine model and spec from the engine housing, not the mower model and serial number.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: (Bowl gasket replacement, Bad Boy.) If the gasket isn't the leak or carb still leaks, check the float.
- step 1: Shut off fuel using the shut-off valve or crimp the fuel line.
- step 2: Wipe dirt from around the carburetor so foreign material does not get in.
- step 3: Remove the screw at the bottom of the carb and remove the bowl; keep a rag to catch fuel left in the bowl.
- step 4: Remove the old gasket.
- step 5: Fit the new gasket; take care not to get carburetor cleaner or other material on it.
- step 6: Reinstall the bowl and tighten the screw holding bowl and carb together.
- step 7: Wipe down the carburetor.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Dirty carb can make components stick; stuck float/needle leads to overflow (see causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat).
- fact: Source mentions steps to clean the Bad Boy carburetor exist elsewhere.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: Fuel filter soft/cracked, leaking at seams.
- fact: Fuel makeup can degrade and soften the filter. Replace annually to minimize risk.
- fix: Replace; don't break off the filter ends when removing from fuel lines since they may be soft. Install with the arrow on the side facing the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: Leak from vacuum fuel pump seams.
- fact: Old fuel sitting in the pump degrades its plastic housing, softening it and causing seam leaks. Replace a vacuum pump that is leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Bad Boy most likely uses a high-density polyethylene tank whose seams can fail over time; replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Bad Boy may or may not have a fuel shut-off valve; if it does, check it, as they are prone to leaking. Replace if it is the cause.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Fuel lines dry with age, crack and leak. Clamps can loosen or puncture the line.
- fix: Replace dry/cracked lines. Good time to convert pinch-style clamps to worm gear clamps, which are less likely to leak since they don't pinch the line.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: Gas sloshes and leaks around the cap when seal fails. Check if you smell a leak and everything else is ruled out.
- confirm: Wet spot may be missed because fuel evaporates leaving no trace. Rock the mower back and forth with the cap installed to splash fuel to the cap area and watch for a wet spot; if so replace the cap.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old fuel degrades fuel components and leaves residue that gums them up (see problem).
