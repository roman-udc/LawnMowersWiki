## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Exmark shaking", "Exmark vibrating", "vibration more than just bumpy ground"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Author felt vibration beyond mole-tunnel bumps and isolated it step by step. Listed causes: loose engine mounting bolts, electric clutch, unbalanced blades, foreign matter wrapped around the blade shaft, bad spindle housing bearings, bad pulleys, debris wedged in the mower.
- safety: Prevent starting: turn off ignition, remove key, remove spark plug boot(s).

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- fact: Engine mounting bolts (motor mounts) reduce vibration; make sure they are tight; replace any missing, broken or worn bolts. On a push mower check them when vibration appears once the blades are engaged.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: Electric clutch has a ball bearing that can fail and cause vibration. Manual-engagement clutch (belts/pulleys) has bushings and linkages that can fail. Push mowers have no clutch; check the blade engagement lever.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Exmark blades must be balanced; an unbalanced blade (worn more on one side, e.g. by sand wearing unevenly, or incorrect sharpening) shakes the mower.
- fact: After recent blade replacement, verify correct blade length and center hole size, and correct hardware (some blades use washers and bolts, others also use splined bushings).
- confirm: Blade balancer, or nail on the wall: put the center hole on the nail; blade should stay level.
- fix: if weighted to one side, continue sharpening that side until it hangs level.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- symptom: vibration; wire or string wrapped on the blade shaft.
- confirm: inspect the top of the blade/shaft for foreign material such as wire or string.
- fix: remove it and check for damage to the blade shaft (spindle).

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: wear heavy work gloves, grab both ends of the blade and rock up and down; there should be no movement.
- fix: movement = bad bearing. Exmark uses sealed bearings in the cutter housing assembly and does not offer replacing only the bearing; replace the whole cutter housing assembly.
- opinion: Some owners find a matching bearing and repair the housing; in the author's experience it doesn't last as long as replacing the whole assembly.
- brand: Exmark cutter housing assembly is sold as a unit.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: remove belt covers and lift the floor pan to reach belt and pulleys; hand-turn each pulley, feel for resistance and listen for bearing noise; also check bearing and bushing on the engagement linkage; inspect belt for wear.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Small debris (acorn, wood chip) lodged in the mower can shake the whole machine; a small piece of wood caught under a deck pulley makes the belt vibrate the entire deck each pass. Look over every part of the mower.
- tip: Keep the belt/pulley area clean during regular cleaning; also prevents fire from belt heat plus dry debris.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Clean regularly, do regular maintenance during the year, and prep for storage. Author references separate guides for Exmark hydraulic oil/filter change and winterizing (not in this Source).
