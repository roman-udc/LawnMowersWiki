## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- safety: Safety glasses; well-ventilated area.
- fact: Old gas must be drained and replaced; check fuel filter and lines for restrictions; carburetor is most likely culprit and must be cleaned, rebuilt or replaced.
- step 1: Remove old gas with a fuel siphon pump into a fuel container. Fill with fresh gas with additive (author: Sea Foam Motor Treatment, mix per bottle). Start and run until not sluggish; if not, continue below.
- step 2: Check fuel filter for clog; replace; install inline between fuel lines with arrow in direction of fuel flow.
- step 3: Check fuel line flow: stop fuel (shut-off valve near bottom of tank, under the seat on some zero-turns; or fuel pinch pliers); remove the end of line furthest from tank into a container lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump); start flow; good flow = not clogged, reinstall. If clogged: remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air, alternate until open; else buy line of same length and diameter, cut to length. Reinstall, start flow, test start.
- step 4: Carburetor: fuel stored in bowl; old fuel can clog the jet or freeze small parts; clean (mechanical, OK with small parts) or rebuild/replace; or take to small engine mechanic/dealer.
- fact: Fuel pump: needed if carburetor sits higher than tank. With fuel confirmed to pump, remove carburetor line into container, start, watch for steady or pulsating flow; replace if not.
- fact: Fuel cap: broken/plugged vent forms vacuum; test with cap on and off; starts with cap off, quits after a short time with cap on = replace cap.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can sit in a mower up to 30 days before breaking down; leaves varnish that gums the fuel system; mower bogs and runs sluggish. Buy and use within 30 days; stabilizer if more than you'll use.
- fact: Ethanol (often corn-based) attracts moisture; ethanol and moisture leave gummy deposits, can separate over time and sink to the bottom of the tank; the mix runs hot in the cylinder, potentially engine damage.
- mistake: Mixing old gas with new gas doesn't solve the problem; gas loses combustibility and leaves varnish as soon as 30 days after purchase. Dispose of old gas first, add additive (reduces moisture, cleans, stabilizes).

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: 30-day rule; stabilizer extends; Sea Foam reduces moisture, cleans, stabilizes.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Clogged by dirt and sticky deposits from old fuel; restricts flow; no start.
