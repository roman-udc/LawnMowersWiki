## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "Husqvarna engine smells hot / overheats"
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, mowing-too-fast
- fact: when you smell the engine running hot, stop the mower and fix it before heat damages the engine.
- safety: follow the Husqvarna operator's manual: stop mower, remove ignition key, wait for engine to cool, remove spark plug boots before repairs.
- pro: if consistent trouble after oil change, take to a Husqvarna servicing dealer or small engine dealer; technician runs additional tests.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: oil lubricates and also helps cool a gas Husqvarna engine; air-cooled small engines (Briggs & Stratton, Kohler, Kawasaki used on Husqvarna push, riding, zero-turn) need air-cooled engine oil with a high concentration of zinc; zinc is a cooling agent; car motor oil has little zinc because liquid-cooled engines use water.
- brand: most small-engine makers on Husqvarna recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; may use 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures and 5W-30 in cold. Source includes a Kawasaki viscosity/temperature chart (image, not transcribed).
- fix (wrong oil): drain wrong oil and add correct air-cooled engine oil.
- symptom (low oil): friction builds heat; oil thickens if run low; significant engine damage possible.
- tip: check oil before using the mower; add a little if low.
- confirm: if consistently low on oil look for: oil leak (find leak, replace gaskets it comes from; inspect oil filter sealing if equipped); burning oil (plugged air filter makes oil burn and engine work harder: clean or replace filter); using oil (overheating, or valve or ring problem; take to a repair center for diagnosis).
- fix: check and correct a slightly low level; if the cause of hot smell, do a full oil change (heated oil may have thickened).

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins push air to the exterior of the cylinder head and block; fan on top of the engine pulls cool air in and blows it down toward the bottom, passing all hot parts and out the bottom.
- symptom: fins plugged with dirt or damaged fail to dissipate heat.
- fix: remove engine cover, clean out fins; replace damaged fins; remove dirt from engine cover and around the engine; make it part of annual maintenance.
- mistake: washing the engine pushes dirt and water into the engine area; don't spray water into the engine when washing the mower.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- step 1: remove the engine cover/shroud; clean out cooling fins; replace damaged fins; clean engine cover and area around engine. Annual maintenance.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: the engine guard (shield) must be in place so cool air circulates around the engine; without it cool air exits the area.
- fix: check guard is correctly positioned; tighten or replace mounting screws; replace missing or damaged guard.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter starves the engine of air; engine runs hot because it works harder to breathe.
- confirm: remove filter, shine a flashlight through the paper element; good amount of light and good condition: tap against a solid surface and reinstall; dull/no light, damaged, or very dirty dark: replace.
- brand: some Husqvarna engines use an inner air filter (narrow filter inside a canister-style paper filter) or a foam pre-cleaner wrapped around the paper filter.
- tip: replace annually, clean several times a season; more often in dust or heavy use. Author: don't try to make the filter last; far cheaper than engine repair.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: deck, baffles and blades are designed for airflow and suction; buildup hurts cut quality and makes engine work harder.
- fix: scrape with a deck scraper (wire brush or putty knife also work); check frequently. Deck coatings reduce buildup but are not miracle products (author mentions a DuPont spray).
- tip: to reduce clumping avoid wet grass and always run at full throttle.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify a plugged deck; extra strain overheats engine.
- fix: check regularly and sharpen; homeowners sharpen twice a season or about every 25 hours (balance blades as well).

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: long, thick or wet grass puts the engine under load, may overheat it.
- tip: avoid wet grass; double or triple cut (highest deck setting first, then lower a little for next pass).

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: slower ground speed in long, thick, or wet grass; keep engine speed fast (full throttle).

## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- tip: let engine cool before storing in a garage; best to park outdoors; on a riding mower lift the hood to expose the engine; let it run under no load so normal cooling begins.
