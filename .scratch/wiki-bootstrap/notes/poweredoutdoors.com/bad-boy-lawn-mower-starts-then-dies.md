## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Bad Boy lawn mower starts and then dies"
- alias: "mower shuts down after mowing / dies in the middle of the lawn"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug wires before working; careful around hot engine; wear eye protection; protect hands from sharp components; follow owner's manual.
- pro: if unsure how to diagnose/repair safely, use local Bad Boy dealer or repair shop.
- brand: Bad Boy; Source also links a "Common Bad Boy Lawn Mower Problems & Solutions" guide.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas that sits long causes corrosion and varnish in the fuel system. Most gasoline contains ethanol (corn-based).
- fact: ethanol gasoline can begin to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase.
- fact: ethanol attracts moisture; the ethanol/moisture mix separates and sinks to tank bottom; running it burns hot and can damage the engine; when it evaporates it leaves gummy deposit that clogs fuel lines, filter, carburetor.
- fix: drain tank with a fuel siphon, refill with fresh fuel, add a fuel additive to stabilize, reduce moisture and clean the system (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: engine needs air; restricted airflow makes mower die; also sluggish running and overheating. Never run without a filter (dirt = engine damage).
- tip: replace air filter annually, clean regularly through season.
- step 1: remove filter from housing without letting dirt fall into intake; wipe housing with dry cloth.
- step paper: tap filter on solid surface; hold to light - if light shows through reuse, otherwise replace; reinstall and cap.
- step foam: inspect for brown spots, tears, dry brittle condition (replace if any); wash with mild dish detergent and water; rinse, lay flat to dry; coat with foam filter oil, wring out excess (should not drip); new foam filter also needs oil; reinstall.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- see causes/clogged-air-filter above for paper test (light through element) and foam steps (inspect brown spots/tears/brittle; mild dish detergent and water; rinse; dry flat; foam filter oil, not dripping; new foam filter needs oil too).
- tip: replace annually, clean regularly during season.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: filter strains dirt; dirt and gummy deposits plug it over time. Replace annually.
- fix: replace; arrow on side of filter must point in direction of fuel flow, i.e. toward carburetor and away from tank.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step: arrow on filter points toward carburetor, away from tank.
- tip: replace annually.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: gummy deposits from old fuel clog lines; mower dies while mowing.
- confirm: shut flow with fuel shut-off valve at bottom of tank (or pinch pliers to crimp line); remove line from fuel pump, put in container; start flow (container must be lower than tank since fuel can't run uphill without pump; push mower may have no pump). Good flow = line OK, reattach, check other components.
- fix: isolate blocked section, shut off flow, remove section, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow compressed air through, repeat; if can't clear, replace the line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old fuel in carb forms gummy hard deposits restricting fuel.
- confirm: first confirm fuel reaches carb; remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake, start; if it runs then dies, disassemble and clean.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: starts then dies when engine gets hot (problems/engine-starts-then-dies).
- fact: fins on flywheel push air around block and head; plugged or broken fins restrict airflow.
- fix: careful (hot engine); remove debris from fins, replace broken fins, clean debris around engine and shroud, ensure heat shield attached securely.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil builds crankcase pressure; can overheat and shut down.
- fix: remove oil via drain plug or oil filter, or evacuator/turkey baster via fill; check dipstick; adjust until at full line.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low oil (leak or engine burning oil) = friction/heat, overheat and die; heat can melt internal parts.
- pro: if engine shut down for lack of oil, significant damage likely; take to experienced small-engine mechanic to tear down and test.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step: remove oil through drain plug or oil filter, or oil evacuator/turkey baster through fill area; check dipstick; repeat until full line.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: runs sluggish then dies. Also damaged plug, wrong gap, loose wire can stop engine.
- fix: clean tip; replace if very dark, burnt electrode, or damaged; gap per engine manufacturer spec; attach wires securely. Plugs inexpensive; replace annually.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: winding can separate and short when hot; plug can't get voltage; mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: ohmmeter check for break in continuity; replace coil if break.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke cuts air for cold start; must be moved to correct position once engine is warm or mower dies.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: gas cap or rollover vents on tank that can't vent make a vacuum, blocking fuel flow.
- confirm: run with and without cap; if it runs with cap off but eventually dies with cap on, suspect vent.
- fix: determine whether tank vented through cap or rollover vents; replace that part.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: plugged deck makes engine work harder, overheat and shut down.
- tip: scrape deck regularly; avoid mowing wet (clumps stick); deck spray can minimize buildup but is not a miracle product.

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "Bad Boy battery not charging"
- fact: run at high engine speed; don't idle long - engine power charges battery.
- confirm: if battery and wiring good and secure but constantly needs charging, suspect charging system; (links to author battery article).
- fix: replace bad battery.
- pro: charging system fault - have experienced mechanic diagnose; many components can fail, parts-swapping is costly and electrical parts usually non-returnable.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: see above; mechanic recommended.
