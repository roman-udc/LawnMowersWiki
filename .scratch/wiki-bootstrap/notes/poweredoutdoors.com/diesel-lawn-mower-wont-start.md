## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- causes-named: bad-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-starter-solenoid, blown-fuse, bad-safety-switch, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- symptom: click or hum when turning key (solenoid); wire attached to solenoid hot/smoking/melting.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Let a hot engine cool a few minutes. Fill with diesel. If you ran out of fuel, bleed air from the fuel system by letting it run about a minute.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Use diesel within 6 months of purchase. Long storage causes sediment and gummy solutions from exposure to oxygen and water.
- symptom: fuel darkening, increased sediment, filter clogging more often than normal, filter appearing dark.
- tip: Buy diesel from a busy fuel station that is busy with diesel users (author found a black filter after buying at a station mostly used by gas vehicles); diesel in station storage tanks sitting a long time develops sludge.
- fix: Remove old diesel, fill with fresh; if still no start, check fuel components for restrictions from dirty old fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Filter strains fuel from tank to keep dirt and sediment out. Replace plugged filter. Small arrow on the filter side must point in the direction of fuel flow.
- fix: If filter is very dark from old fuel, drain tank, refill with fresh diesel, start mower and let run about a minute to bleed air.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: Install with arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow. After replacing, run engine about a minute to bleed air from the fuel system (diesel).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: sediment and gummy deposits block fuel to engine.
- confirm: Use the fuel shut-off valve to start/stop fuel flow, check each section of line for good flow.
- fix: Remove fuel line, spray carburetor cleaner into tube to loosen clog, blow compressed air through; repeat as necessary. Replace line if clog can't be removed. Start mower and run up to a minute to bleed the fuel system.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- fact: Excess water sinks to the bottom of the tank and causes starting problems. Best to drain the tank and refill with fresh diesel. Running the mower about a minute removes air and water from the system; may add Sea Foam to help clean the fuel system and reduce moisture.

## page: causes/air-in-fuel-system
- fact: Air trapped in the fuel system hurts starting and running. Start mower and let it run a minute to remove air.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Without a vent the tank acts like a vacuum and restricts fuel flow; old fuel can clog the vent.
- confirm: Start and run the mower for a while with cap off and then with it on to see if the cap affects running.
- fix: Replace the fuel cap.

## page: causes/bad-glow-plugs
- fact: Glow plugs assist a cold engine: they electrify a heating element at the tip; hot tip ignites compressed air in cylinder with injected fuel.
- fix: Test glow plugs and replace if not working (source links General Technologies Corp guide on testing glow plugs).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Never run mower without an air filter, even briefly. Plugged filter -> won't start. Check regularly; inexpensive part, prevents expensive engine repair.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove filter from housing carefully, don't knock dirt into the intake.
- step 2: Wipe out remaining dirt in the housing.
- step 3: Tap filter against a solid surface to knock out excess dirt.
- step 4: Hold filter up to a light source; check for light through the paper element.
- step 5: Reuse if light passes through; otherwise replace.
- step 6: Install filter and attach cover.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Can fail from old fuel; sediment, dirt and water deteriorate it.
- confirm: Ensure good fuel flow to pump (through filter and lines, no blockages or kinks). Get correct pressure spec from operator's manual; attach a pressure gauge to the pressure hose coming out of the fuel pump. Reading lower than manufacturer's spec = replace.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- confirm: Check cables and terminals secure. Clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda). Test with multimeter: about 12.7 volts; charge if lower; replace if dead or won't hold charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Secure cables/terminals; clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water : 3 heaping tbsp baking soda).

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum when turning the ignition key; wire attached to solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts.
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnetic on/off switch that actuates the starter motor. Source links its solenoid test article.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse block location varies; start looking around battery (under or next to it) or behind the dash. Replace with same amperage. If it keeps blowing, take to repair shop or dealer for root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- fact: Replace with same-amperage fuse; repeated blowing = professional diagnosis.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Several switches in operator presence system: kill the engine when operator leaves the seat with deck engaged; allow start when parking brake set and PTO disengaged. Faulty switch may not recognize operator in seat or brake engaged. Refer to manual for switch types and test procedures.
- confirm: Can temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one.
- safety: Do not operate a mower without safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: wires secure, battery charged, but mower won't start until battery is charged each time.
- fact: Bad stator, alternator or other electrical parts can be cause; test with an ohmmeter (linked battery article).
- pro: Recommends a professional dealership with factory-trained mechanics; many pricey electrical parts, stores often don't accept returns of electrical parts; author says some mechanics just throw parts at mowers.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Safety features prevent start unless procedure followed: set brake, put in neutral, wait for glow plugs to warm the engine. Varies by mower; check operating manual.

## page: problems/engine-hard-to-start-when-cold
- fact: Glow plugs warm a cold engine; waiting for glow plugs is part of the diesel starting procedure.
