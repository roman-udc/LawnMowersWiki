## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "strong fuel fumes in the garage, can't see a leak"
- alias: "Snapper mower smells of gas"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: A leak is hard to spot after the mower has sat because fuel evaporates and leaves no wet area. Old gas degrades components; aging fuel parts get dry and brittle.
- fact: Source list: dirty carburetor, old/cracked fuel filter, old/cracked fuel pump, rusted tank or failed seam, shut-off valve not sealing, dry/cracked fuel lines, cracked primer bulb, bad gas cap seal.
- tip: When ordering parts have the mower model and serial number; for engine parts (carburetor, fuel pump) get the model and spec number from the engine itself; sometimes only a QR code scan gives it.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Gasket between bowl (bottom of carburetor) and carburetor looks like a rubber band; heat cycling next to the engine makes it dry, brittle and unsealed so the bowl leaks.
- fix: Gasket: step 1: shut off fuel (valve under the tank, or clamp the line). step 2: clean around the bowl, rag ready for fuel, remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and the bowl. step 3: replace the old gasket. step 4: reinstall bowl and screw.
- fact: Stuck float: leak near the air intake port; float regulates gas into the bowl; when stuck, gas overflows. Fix: disassemble the carburetor, clean or rebuild.
- fact: Stuck float needle: needs replacing. Gentle tapping on the carburetor side with a small rubber mallet or hammer handle may free it but is a temporary fix that works only a time or two; take the carburetor apart and replace the needle.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Gumming and crusty buildup from old fuel; several small parts can freeze up.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Damaged filter or plastic that weakens and softens leaks at the seams.
- fix: Replace; careful removing the ends from the line (soft plastic can break off inside the hose). Arrow on the side points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Used when the carburetor is higher than the tank; pump components degrade and leak.
- fix: Replace; do not break soft plastic off the pump ports.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Current Snapper tanks are high-density polyethylene; check the seams; replace the tank when leaking. Older mowers may have metal tanks that rust and develop holes; hard to find replacements.
- tip: Try a petroleum-resistant epoxy such as TankWeld by J-B Weld to patch a hole in a metal tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Located at the bottom of the tank, plastic or metal, prone to leaking.
- fix: Replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Lines dry and crack with age, or are punctured by clamps.
- fix: Replace cracked lines. Consider replacing pinch-style clamps; author prefers worm gear clamps (less likely to pinch the line).

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Bulb can leak when overfilled with fuel, where it meets the fuel line, or when dry and brittle. Relatively easy to replace.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- confirm: Rock the mower back and forth to splash gas around the cap area and watch for wet spots forming around the cap.
- fix: Replace the cap if wet spots form.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Snapper: ethanol no more than 10 percent; lower is better; add stabilizer since gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase.
