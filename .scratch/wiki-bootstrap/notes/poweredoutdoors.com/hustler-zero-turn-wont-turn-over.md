## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Hustler zero turn won't turn over or crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: look for items that keep the starter solenoid from energizing the starter.
- safety: remove the negative cable from the battery before electrical repairs. If unsure, take to a Hustler dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: a fully charged 12-volt battery reads about 12.7 volts; lower: charge it.
- tip: keep battery charged, especially in storage.
- fix: if it fails to hold a charge, replace; take the old battery along (core fee otherwise).

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for eyes and skin; don't touch anything without a rubber coating.
- step 1: access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover); do not remove battery from its casing.
- step 2: connect positive (red, plus sign) charger cable to the positive terminal first.
- step 3: attach negative (black) cable to the negative terminal.
- step 4: set charger voltage and amperage; mower batteries usually 12 volts; more amps charges faster; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge is best.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: vibration loosens wiring and connections; moisture corrodes terminals.
- fix: remove corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); secure loose wires; replace damaged or severely corroded components.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: loose wires lose continuity; see above.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- confirm: multimeter probes on each prong of the fuse, measuring resistance: near 0 is good; infinity is bad.
- fix: replace with same amperage; if it keeps blowing, take to a Hustler dealer or repair shop to find root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: multimeter on resistance, one probe on each fuse prong; near 0 good, infinity bad.
- step 2: replace with same amperage.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: turn the key and nothing happens.
- confirm: multimeter on resistance; find prongs marked "B" (battery) and "S" (starter solenoid); key in start position; one probe on B, one on S; good switch near 0 ohms; bad switch infinite resistance, replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Hustler has an operator presence control system with safety switches preventing starting unless conditions are met (e.g., brake engaged); seat switch, brake switch and PTO switch may be bad; consult operator's manual for others.
- confirm: test with multimeter; may temporarily bypass a safety switch to identify a bad one, for troubleshooting only.
- safety: never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run with a safety switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic switch that initiates the starter motor; goes bad when spring weakens or copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause it to fail.
- confirm: needs a fully charged battery before testing; source refers to its solenoid-test steps in another article.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid, remove and test the starter.
- tip/pro: author recommends a starter/alternator repair shop test and rebuild it if possible before buying a pricey new starter.
