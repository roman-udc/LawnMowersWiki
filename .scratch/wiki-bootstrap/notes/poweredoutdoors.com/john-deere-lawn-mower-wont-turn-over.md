## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "John Deere mower won't turn over"
- alias: "nothing happens when I turn the key"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: won't turn over when battery dead/won't hold charge; wires, cables or connections loose; fuse blown; ignition or safety switch bad; starter solenoid faulty; or starter bad.
- safety: careful working on the electrical system to avoid electrocution; follow operator's manual; consult a professional if unsure.
- fact: check order: dead battery; loose wires/connections/corrosion/bad ground; blown fuse; safety switch; ignition switch; starter solenoid; starter motor tested.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; lower -> charge.
- fix: replace if it won't hold a charge; buy at mower dealer, hardware or automotive store; bring old battery to avoid a core fee.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for eyes and skin; touch only rubber-coated parts.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover); don't remove battery from casing.
- step 2: Connect charger cables positive first (red / plus) to positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach negative (black) cable to negative terminal.
- step 4: Set charger voltage and amps: usually 12 V; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; a slow charge is best.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: vibration can shake wires loose, losing continuity; moisture causes corrosion affecting continuity.
- fix: remove corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water : 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); secure loose wires/connections; replace damaged or severely corroded wires, connections or terminals.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- confirm: place multimeter probes on each prong of the fuse and measure resistance: near 0 = good; infinity = bad fuse.
- fix: replace with a fuse of the same amperage; never a different amperage.
- pro: if fuses keep blowing, take it to a John Deere dealer or repair shop to find the root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: multimeter on resistance; probe on each fuse prong.
- step 2: near 0 resistance = good; infinity = blown.
- step 3: replace with same amperage fuse.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: insert key and turn, nothing happens; mower won't turn over.
- confirm: multimeter continuity: find prongs marked "B" (Battery) and "S" (Starter Solenoid). Insert key, turn to start, multimeter on resistance, one probe on B and the other on S. Good switch near 0 ohms; bad switch infinite resistance -> replace.

## page: procedures/replace-the-ignition-switch
- fact: test B-to-S resistance in the start position (near 0 ohm good, infinite bad -> replace).

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: several safety switches stop starting unless conditions are met, like engaging the brake; a defective one stops the John Deere turning over.
- confirm: test with a multimeter; temporarily bypass only for troubleshooting.
- safety: never operate without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic switch that initiates the starter to turn the engine. Fails when internal spring weakens or copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause failure.
- confirm: battery must be fully charged before testing the solenoid (article links a solenoid test guide).

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid, remove and test the starter.
- tip: have a shop that specializes in starter/alternator repair test it and rebuild if possible rather than buying a pricey new starter.
