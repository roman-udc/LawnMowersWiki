## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "Kubota mower running rough"
- alias: "sputtering" / "bogs down"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, mowing-too-fast, wrong-engine-speed, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: rough running = not enough air, fuel or spark, or overworked engine.
- safety: wait for engine to cool and parts to stop; remove spark plug wire before repairs; follow Kubota manual.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- tip: replace once a year, clean several times per season; more in dust or heavy use.
- fix: clean if a little dirty; replace if extremely dirty, damaged, or no longer seals over the intake.
- step 1: remove filter from housing; step 2: wipe housing without letting dirt into the intake; step 3: tap on a solid surface; step 4: hold to light, reuse if light shows through and undamaged; step 5: reinstall and attach cover.
- fact: foam pre-filter: wash with mild detergent and water, rinse, air dry. DO NOT APPLY OIL to the pre-filter.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: paper steps and foam pre-filter wash (mild detergent, no oil) as above.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke closed for cold start, open for warm running; stuck open or closed or wrong position causes rough running and bogging.
- fix: verify lever position; check the choke plate opens and closes with the lever; use carb cleaner to free a stuck choke; replace a worn choke cable.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down in about a month; ethanol and water separate and sink to the bottom of the tank, leaving sticky deposits and corrosion.
- fix: replace old fuel; add fresh gas with fuel cleaner/stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment; gas minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; ethanol-free best.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop/start flow with fuel shut-off valve or hose crimp pliers; collect fuel in a container lower than the tank.
- fix: shut off fuel, remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air, repeat; replace with same length and diameter if it won't clear or has cracks.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- tip: change annually, sooner if plugged/damaged; arrow points in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: ensure fuel reaches the carb (else check line, filter, pump); remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake and run; if it runs well then goes sluggish again, clean the carburetor.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: on Kubota the tank vent is in the gas cap.
- confirm: when running rough, loosen the cap; if better, retighten and let it run until sluggish again, loosen again to confirm.
- fix: replace with a new Kubota gas cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: dirty plug, wrong gap or loose wire give intermittent spark.
- fix: replace if very dark, burnt electrode or damaged; gap to engine manufacturer spec; attach wire securely.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: slow down when engine bogs; for tall grass make multiple cuts: highest cutting height first, then lower.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: run the engine at highest speed (throttle lever; rabbit icon on many mowers) when engaging blades.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fix: scrape with deck scraper or wire brush; avoid wet or damp grass; deck spray helps but isn't a miracle.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades tear grass leaving brown tips a couple of days later and magnify plugged-deck strain. Remove, sharpen and balance.
