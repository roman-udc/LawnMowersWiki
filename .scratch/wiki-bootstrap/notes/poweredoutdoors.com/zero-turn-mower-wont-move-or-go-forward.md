## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "zero turn won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "zero turn stops driving forward"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open (drive release lever), worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: 8 reasons: drive release lever not in operating position; worn/loose/broken drive or pump belt; bad tensioner pulley; missing idler arm spring; low hydraulic fluid; hot hydraulic fluid; air in hydraulic system; non-hydraulic items.
- fact: non-hydraulic things that stop the engine/mower: battery and charging system; plugged fuel filter and fuel lines; plugged air filter; dirty carburetor (if engine sputters or shuts off you can't drive). Link to "zero turn quits when hot".
- pro: experienced mechanic or brand dealer.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: zero-turns have drive-release buttons, levers or knobs to release the hydros so the mower free-rolls (e.g. push onto trailer). Won't drive forward unless release is in "operating" position. Form (lever, push/pull button, knob) varies by manufacturer and model: check owner's manual.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- confirm: belt not fallen off, correctly positioned on pulleys; replace at any sign of wear.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: tensioner pulley bearing or pulley can break; most tensioner pulleys are hard plastic that wear or break. Replace pulley worn or with bad bearing; keep tensioner arm greased so it does not seize.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: idler spring tensions the drive belt; replace if broken or fallen out.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: some entry-level zero-turns have sealed (non-serviceable) hydraulic systems; contact dealer for problems. Others need oil changes at manufacturer intervals; old/low oil makes mower not move or seem weak.
- fact: most manufacturers have a "break-in" period: initial oil and filter change sooner than normal.
- fix: add oil until full level on overflow tanks, measured when oil is cool. Check for leaks if low.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: bad/low fluid doesn't lubricate, friction, overheating; hot fluid can lead to more extensive damage.
- pro: take to dealer if hydraulic pump leaks or mower runs fine cold but stops when hot.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: trapped air makes mower slow or not move; bleed after fluid change.
- fix (most zero-turns): raise rear drive tires off ground; with mower running move drive levers forward then reverse so tires rotate both ways; repeat until no excessive noise and normal speed. Procedures vary by manufacturer and model: follow operator's manual.

- see above.
