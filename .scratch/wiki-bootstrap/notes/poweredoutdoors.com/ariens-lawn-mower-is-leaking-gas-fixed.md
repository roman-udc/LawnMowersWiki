## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "strong fuel odor around my mower"
- alias: "dead spots in the grass where gas leaked"
- symptom: strong fuel smell or dead grass spots from gas leaking onto lawn; small leaks evaporate and leave no evidence, so the leak can be hard to find
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- safety: follow owner's manual safety precautions; gas fumes are harmful so work in a well-ventilated area
- pro: if unsure how to diagnose/repair safely, have a professional (dealer or repair shop) do it
- brand: Ariens; replacement fuel pump available from an Ariens dealer or online for Briggs, Kawasaki or Kohler engines

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: leak at the carburetor bowl gasket (between bowl and carburetor; looks like a rubber band). Problem: mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: gasket hardens and turns brittle because it sits next to the engine and constantly heats up/cools down, losing sealing ability
- symptom: leak near the air intake port suggests a stuck float, which no longer regulates fuel into the bowl; fuel keeps flowing and overflows out of the carb
- symptom: stuck float needle (works with the float to meter fuel into the bowl) also causes a leak
- fix: replace the bowl gasket (pretty simple); for stuck float/needle take the carb apart to find the cause, clean, or rebuild; or have a small engine mechanic rebuild it
- tip: some Ariens owners tap the carb gently with a rubber hammer handle or rubber mallet to free the needle; works a time or two but is only temporary
- tip: find the replacement gasket by the engine's model and spec number, not the mower's model/serial number

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carburetors get gummy/crusty buildup that stops parts (float) working correctly, causing leaks; cleaning may keep the float from sticking, otherwise rebuild
- fact: author links a separate article for cleaning the carb (poweredoutdoors.com/fix-lawn-mower-starts-then-dies/)

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: if the float is stuck, take the carburetor apart to determine actual cause; may need cleaning or a full rebuild

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: inline filter leaks at the seams or from cracked plastic. Problem: mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: fuel degrades plastics like the inline filter body
- fix: replace the filter (online or local hardware store)
- mistake: be careful removing filter ends from fuel lines; plastic gets soft and can break off

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: leaking inline filter must be replaced; careful removing it from lines as plastic can soften and break off

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: vacuum fuel pump leaks at plastic seams. Problem: mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: modern fuel makeup degrades plastics; compromised plastic means the pump can't build the pressure needed to feed the carburetor
- fix: replace the pump

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: tank seams fail and leak
- fact: Ariens tanks are high-density polyethylene
- fix: best solution is to replace the tank

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: valve at the bottom of the fuel tank (starts/stops fuel flow) leaks, whether plastic or metal
- fix: replace the shut-off valve

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: lines dry, turn brittle and crack with age; leaks also at hose clamps (loose clamp, or clamp punctures hose)
- fix: replace cracked lines; good time to replace pinch-style clamps
- tip: author prefers worm gear clamps because they are less likely to pinch the line and cause leaks

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- fact: replace any cracked fuel lines; replace pinch-style clamps at the same time (worm gear clamps suggested)

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: leak around gas cap leaves a wet spot briefly then evaporates; check it if you smell gas and everything else checked out
- confirm: rock the mower back and forth to splash gas up around the cap area and watch for wet areas around the cap
- fix: replace the cap when you see signs of gas on the outside of the tank around the cap
