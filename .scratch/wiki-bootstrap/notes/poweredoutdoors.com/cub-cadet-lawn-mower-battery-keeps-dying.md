## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "Cub Cadet battery keeps dying"
- alias: "Cub Cadet battery keeps draining"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, faulty-charging-system, parasitic-battery-drain, bad-battery
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety. Always disconnect the negative (black) cable from the battery before any electrical repairs.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: Check cables to battery and other components are not loose: follow the red (positive) cable from the battery to the solenoid, then on to the starter; all must be secure with good connection.
- symptom: Green or white corrosion on connections causes loss of continuity so the battery keeps dying.
- step 1: Disconnect the negative cable first to break the circuit (if you touch frame/metal with the wrench while the circuit is live, the battery may arc and throw sparks and could potentially blow up).
- step 2: Then remove the positive (red) cable. Memory aid: positive cable is last one off and first one on.
- step 3: Remove other cables to clean/replace.
- step 4: Clean with baking soda and water: 4 cups water with about six rounded tablespoons baking soda to make a paste; spread on corrosion, it foams; rinse with water (messy, rinse outdoors); wire brush speeds it; cola soda also works.
- safety: Always wear safety glasses and gloves when working on the battery.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: Most Cub Cadets have an internal alternator.
- confirm: Test with a volt-ohms meter set to read 12 V.
- step 1: Engine off, red lead on positive, black on negative: battery voltage should be 12.4-12.8 V (some read higher depending on charge/condition). Write it down.
- step 2: Set parking brake, start engine, run at about 3/4 throttle, read again: should be 13.2-13.9 V or higher. Higher than the first reading means alternator is charging.
- step 3: If reading doesn't change from the first, charging system issue: possibly bad regulator or bad stator/alternator.
- fact: Some systems are 15 amp, others 20 amp, depending on manufacturer and engine.
- pro: Author recommends a professional mechanic (Cub Cadet servicing dealer or independent shop) to find root cause; many expensive engine parts can be at fault, throwing parts at it gets costly.
- tip: If cables are clean you can run this test at any time; it may also reveal a bad connection.

## page: causes/parasitic-battery-drain
- symptom: Ignition key left on after mowing; won't affect battery much unless there's an oil light on the dash or a 12 V accessory cord plugged into an accessory port.
- fix: If battery is dead, charge it.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- confirm: On a charger an hour or two and still won't charge above 12 V means bad battery; replace.
- fix: 12 V lawn and garden batteries from Cub Cadet dealer, automotive store, home improvement store. Bring old battery; core fee $15-$25 unless you return old battery. New batteries are usually fully charged, but if shelf-stored a long time, charge first (charger or jump-start).
- fact: Most batteries last at least 3 years if cared for; replacing more often means a connection or battery problem.
- fact: Most batteries fail from vibration (plates break up inside) - make sure battery sits securely in tray and doesn't bounce. Cold weather: a battery that isn't fully charged can freeze and be damaged.
- fact: Battery size: at least 300 cold cranking amps (cca) if deck uses an electric clutch; without electric clutch 250 cca can do, bigger is better. Make sure dimensions fit the battery holder.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Red cable to positive (+), black to negative (-). Disconnect battery cables first (positive last off, first on).
- step 1 (Method One): If battery has removable caps, check water level; if plates aren't covered add distilled water (well water not good; city water a little better; distilled best).
- step 2: Hook up charger. Check voltage with meter: 11.8 V needs little charging; 10.1 V needs a long time; lower reading = longer.
- step 3: Author's "slow bake" for a very low battery: 2 or 4 amp setting (chargers may have 6/4/2 amp switches) about an hour; recheck voltage is rising; if so continue another hour.
- step (Method Two): If reading isn't too low and you need to mow: charger up to 6 amps about half an hour to reach about 12 V; remove charger, start mower; keep throttle 3/4 to full so it charges while running; check reading after mowing.
- tip: Author likes a charger with a trickle-charge setting for a very weak battery.
- fact: Charging-times article linked (not in source text).

## page: procedures/jump-start-the-battery
- fact: Use a good 12 V car battery; confirm both batteries are 12 V first.
- step 1: Park car next to mower and shut off car engine (do not start the car; its battery provides enough power).
- step 2: Red positive cable first to mower positive terminal, then to car battery positive.
- step 3: Black negative cable to car battery negative terminal; connect the other black end to the mower frame.
- step 4: Set mower brake, start mower, let it run a few minutes.
- step 5: Remove cables in reverse order of installation. The mower alternator keeps the battery charged; check with volt meter.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- confirm: See faulty-charging-system voltage test above (12.4-12.8 V engine off; 13.2-13.9 V at 3/4 throttle). Bad battery: won't charge above 12 V after an hour or two on charger.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- tip: Keep cables clean; cables tight and battery securely held down; don't let battery freeze, keep fully charged in cold weather.
- fact: Battery life at least 3 years; vibration and freezing are the main killers; cca guidance 300 (electric clutch) / 250.
