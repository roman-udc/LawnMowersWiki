## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: change Exmark blades at least once a year, more often if bent or damaged; start each season with a fresh set and sharpen as needed during the season.
- fact: sharpen every 25 hours of use (about twice a year for an average homeowner); more often in dusty conditions or lawns with gravel.
- fact: new Exmark blades do not need sharpening; they are sharpened and paint-coated at manufacture; paint wears off the edge in initial use.
- tools (removal): ratchet and socket, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional, for removal only)
- tools (sharpening): 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or a nail on the wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves
- safety: remove the ignition key and the spark plug boots to prevent the mower starting and the deck engaging; wear gloves and safety glasses.
- step 1: remove ignition key and spark plug boots.
- step 2: remove the bolt securing the blade to the spindle with socket and ratchet; use a cordless impact gun if stubborn. Stubborn bolt: place a block of wood over the bolt and hit it with a hammer to shock it loose.
- step 3: remove bolt, washer and blade. Many Exmark mowers also have a splined bushing in the center hole that comes off with the bolt.
- step 4: reinstall new or sharpened blade in the reverse order.
- step 5: torque the bolt to 50-60 ft-lb with a torque wrench. Do not use an impact gun to tighten: it can overtighten the bolt and damage the spindle.
- step (sharpen) A: clean blade with a rag; wire brush for stubborn buildup.
- step (sharpen) B: secure the blade in a vise; flip and re-secure to do the other side.
- step (sharpen) C file: push the file at an angle in one direction along the blade, no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges; flip and repeat.
- step (sharpen) D drill sharpener: place the flat guide along the flat of the blade with the cutting edge in the bevel slot; move up and down the edge; flip and repeat.
- step (sharpen) E angle/bench grinder: hold perpendicular to the blade edge and run along its length to level rough spots/nicks; keep moving so a little metal is removed at a time; flip and repeat.
- step (balance): after sharpening, balance the blade: use a blade balancer, or hang the blade by its center hole on a nail that sits about 1 inch off the wall; the side that hangs lower is heavy; remove metal from the heavy side until the blade sits level. Unbalanced blades shake the mower and can damage the spindle housing and engine.
- mistake: using an impact gun to tighten the blade bolt.
- tip: lawn chemicals can corrode deck components and fuse blades to the deck; remove blades periodically so they don't form a tight seal.
- tip: sticks, rocks and toys bend or gouge blades and can damage spindle and housing; walk the yard first. Sand wears blades; sharpen more often until grass fills in.
- tip: professional sharpening at the Exmark dealer costs about $7-$10 per blade; author drops them off and picks up a couple of days later, keeping a second sharpened set on hand. Reasons given: safer, often cheaper than buying a blade, a pro can grind out large nicks and gouges, and the blade is properly balanced.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: replace when the top of the sail looks thinner than the center of the blade.
- fact: chips and dings are usually removed in sharpening; deep gouges mean replace the blade.
- confirm (bent, removed): place the removed blade on top of a new blade; any gap where they do not fit together means replace.
- confirm (bent, installed): with the mower on a level hard surface, measure from the ground to the blade tip on one side, rotate the blade 180 degrees and measure from the same ground spot to the tip; replace if the difference is greater than 1/8".
- safety: a cracked blade is a safety risk (can break off while mowing, injure someone or damage a structure); replace.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: uneven cut (problems/uneven-cut); bent blade from hitting a hard object.
- fix: replace if bent beyond 1/8" difference or gapped against a new blade.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: sail wears from constant movement of dirt under the deck; replace when top of the sail is thinner than the center.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: before removing the blade, with work gloves grab each side of the blade and rock it up and down. Play or movement means remove the spindle housing assembly and look for damage; also remove it when you hear knocking (possible bad bearing).
- fact: Exmark offers the spindle housing only as an assembly (sealed bearing); a bad bearing means replacing the whole cutter housing assembly.
- brand: Exmark spindle housing is sold as an assembly only.
- fact: a significant blade impact can damage the spindle and housing, causing vibration and uneven cut.
