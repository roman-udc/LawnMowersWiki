## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- tools: engine oil (and filter on riding/zero-turn), drain pan, socket or allen wrench set (per drain plug), plastic funnel, rubber gloves, cardboard to cover work surface; filter wrench (riding/zero-turn); oil extractor optional.
- step 1: Run engine 2 to 3 minutes to warm oil (flows better), shut off; careful of hot engine. Put cardboard down and park on it.
- step 2: Disconnect spark plug boot(s) (twist off) so engine can't start; on riding/zero-turn also remove key.
- step 3 (push/self-propelled, no drain plug): drain pan beside mower; clean around fill cap, remove cap; gently tip mower on its side with carburetor on the high side so fill hole lines up with the pan; let oil drain, wipe drips; return upright; refill per manufacturer to full level; reattach spark plug boot.
- step 3 (push, with drain plug): tip on side, carburetor high (oil must not run into carburetor or air filter); find square or hex plug on the bottom of engine under the deck; scrape deck debris from around plug; pan under plug; unscrew counterclockwise; loosen fill cap or dipstick to speed draining; replace plug, return upright; refill; reattach boot.
- step 3 (riding/zero-turn): find plug or drain hose on side of engine near the bottom (not underneath), often near the frame; older riding mower may need side panel removed. Clean around plug; pan under plug/hose; drain.
- step 4 (riding/zero-turn): Unscrew filter with filter wrench; put a film of engine oil on the new filter's seal; spin on.
- step 5: Reinstall drain plug or hose after fully drained.
- step 6: Fill with oil type and capacity from operator's manual; verify with dipstick/gauge.
- step 7: Replace spark plug boot, run engine a few minutes, shut off, cool, recheck level, top up if needed.
- tip: An oil extractor avoids tipping and spills (cleaner). Some engines use a drain hose instead of plug (cleaner); drain hoses can be added to many engines, ask engine dealer.
- fact: Drain plug can look like a large bolt head hidden by dirt and grass. Many push mower engines have no drain plug (tip or extract); some use a dipstick with a plug on the end.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- fact: Remove oil if above manufacturer's recommended level. Remove key and spark plug wire first.
- step: Drain plug or valve port (side of engine by dipstick or bottom of oil pan): remove plug briefly, replace, check level, repeat if needed.
- step: Oil filter: loosen or remove filter to release a little oil, rag ready.
- step: Fill hole: tip mower with carburetor on high side so oil doesn't run into carburetor or air filter.
- step: Oil extractor pump: tube inserted into fill hole.
- step: Turkey baster for small amounts (don't reuse for cooking).

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil in crankcase causes smoking, overheating, engine seal damage or blown gaskets, and can hydrolock the engine.

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- fact: Hydrolock: oil gets past piston into cylinder and fills combustion chamber; piston compresses air, not oil/water, so it can't reach top of its stroke.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Overfilled oil can make engine smoke and overheat. causes-named: overfilled-oil
