## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "riding mower belt keeps falling off"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: belt comes off when brackets, belt keepers or idler springs are worn/damaged; bearings in pulleys or spindle housings are bad; or debris interferes with deck belt tension.
- fact: also covers shredded belt: rubbing on brackets or wrong routing.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: normal wear stretches and loosens the belt; replace when worn, cracked or glazed/shiny.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: belt keepers are rods sitting 1/8" to 1/4" away from the pulley; the belt doesn't sit against the keeper; it only prevents the belt slipping off the pulleys.
- symptom: keepers bend when a slack belt hits them, letting the belt off.
- fix: look for damage, adjust to the correct position; straighten with a hammer or other tool; replace if that fails.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: slowly spin each pulley; feel for restriction and listen for bearing noise.
- fix: restriction, bearing noise, or pulley not flat and parallel to the deck -> replace the pulley.
- confirm (spindle): with work gloves, grab each end of the blade and rock up and down; movement or knocking = bad bearing. Remove spindle housing, inspect bearing, replace. Repeat for each spindle.
- brand: some manufacturers sell only the whole spindle housing assembly, not the bearing.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: tensioner bracket and spring hold the idler pulley in line; usually pulley one side of the bracket and spring on the other; spring or the bracket hole it attaches to wears; causes deck vibration or loss of tension. Replace the bracket or spring when broken or worn.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: debris stuck under the tensioner or blocking its movement upsets tension. Debris in pulley grooves stresses the belt (it stretches around extra material) and can make it come off or snap; clean mud/debris so the belt sits in the grooves.
- tip: clean the deck after each mowing; also a safety matter: dry debris on the deck can catch fire from belt friction heat.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: rust from deck/pulleys collects on the belt, drying it and making it prone to cracking and coming off; oil from a leak makes the belt slip around pulleys and come off.
- fix: repair the oil leak or remove rust; replace badly rusted pulleys; then install a new deck belt.

## page: causes/wrong-belt
- fact: shredded belt can also come from wrong routing: check the diagram decal on the mower or owner's manual. Also find a bracket that is smooth/shiny from belt rubbing and adjust it away; if it has a bushing replace the bushing or the whole bracket assembly.
- fact: OEM riding mower deck belts run about $80 to $250.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: route per decal/manual diagram; fix the cause of shredding (rubbing bracket) first so you don't go through multiple belts.

## page: guides/belt-types
- fact: deck belts about $80 to $250 for a good OEM belt.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: belt, belt keepers, idler spring/bracket, pulley and spindle bearings wear.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- tip: to extend belt life: clean deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off the belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix/replace damaged brackets or belt keepers.
