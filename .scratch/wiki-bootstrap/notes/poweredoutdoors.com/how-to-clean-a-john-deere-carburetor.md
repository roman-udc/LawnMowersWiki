## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- difficulty: a little mechanical, comfortable with small parts; otherwise have a repair shop do it
- fact: ethanol and fuel additives form gummy substance clogging carb components
- symptom: won't start; starts then dies; backfires (running lean); sputtering/rough; surging; consuming too much fuel
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers
- step 1: decide if it needs disassembly: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into the intake, start engine; if it starts then shuts down, take the carb apart
- step 2: take photos of each stage for reassembly
- step 3: shut off fuel with shut-off valve; remove fuel line from carb
- step 4: remove throttle and choke cables if fitted
- step 5: remove hardware attaching carb to air filter housing
- step 6: remove springs slowly without stretching them; may need to twist the carb
- mistake: don't tear the gasket between engine block and carb; if torn buy a new one
- step 7: clean around the bowl, have a rag for remaining gas, remove screw at bowl bottom and take off the bowl
- mistake: don't damage the o-ring between bowl and carb (looks like a rubber band); keep carb cleaner/other substances off it or it stretches and can't be reused
- step 8: check the stem (piece hanging from the carb centre); holes clog from old fuel and stem won't draw fuel to the jet; use flashlight and clean holes with thick wire
- step 9: check for white crusty buildup from ethanol/additives; remove as much as possible with carb cleaner; almost impossible to remove all
- step 10: make sure float and float needle move freely; reassemble using photos; reattach springs, filter housing, throttle/choke cables, fuel line
- step 11: turn on fuel; fill with fresh fuel plus additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment; let bowl fill; start engine
- fact: if corroded or damaged parts, replace carb; have engine model and spec number to order from engine dealer or online

- fact: rebuild kit may be available from local John Deere dealer; compare its cost with a new carb, may be little price difference
