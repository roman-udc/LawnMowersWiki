## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Honda lawn mower is smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, bad-head-gasket, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train
- fact: Smoke from a plugged air filter, inefficient oil level, an engine leak, or internal problem (piston ring or valve train). A smoking mower may indicate a larger internal engine problem. Stop running it to avoid further damage.
- fact: Blue or white smoke = excess oil burning (damaged rings, valve train, gasket leaks, or oil pushed into the cylinder by a plugged air filter). Black smoke = excess fuel burning from a plugged filter (runs rich); if not the filter, look for another air restriction.
- confirm: Check spark plug tips for oil; points to internal problem (ring or valve train) without pinpointing it; engine teardown and tests needed.
- pro: Low-oil smoking usually means a significant engine problem for a small engine mechanic; burnt valve work needs a mechanic.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Black smoke from running rich; filter too plugged to draw air makes the engine draw air (and oil) from the crankcase, which burns; smoke can plug the filter further. Always run a filter; replace once a year with annual maintenance; check and clean through the season.
- fix (paper): remove from housing, wipe out housing without knocking dirt into intake, tap on a solid surface, reuse if light shows through, else replace; reinstall with cover. (Foam instructions in a separate article.)

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil: friction heat can melt internal parts and burn off oil, causing smoke. Check oil before every use; helps spot leaks or oil consumption. When smoking occurs, usually too late for a quick fix like adding oil.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil raises crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and up to the air intake through the valve train; burns and smokes.
- fix: Remove oil until the level is within the full lines on the dipstick; drain plug, or author's preference an oil evacuator or turkey baster.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: Bad engine gasket gives an oil leak that drips onto the muffler and smokes; inspect to find the leak and repair.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Piston ring problem or cylinder wall scoring lets oil into the combustion chamber to burn off; requires engine teardown.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: Burnt valve from overheating; a red hot muffler can be a sign of a burnt valve or timing problem. Confirm only by removing the cylinder head and leak-down test; valve and seat are cut at certain angles to complete the combustion chamber; send to a small engine mechanic.
