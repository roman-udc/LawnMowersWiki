## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Bad Boy zero turn won't start"
- fact: a Bad Boy zero-turn will not start when it is not getting air, fuel and spark; often a plugged air filter, bad fuel, dirty carburetor, plugged fuel components, bad spark plug, faulty electrical parts, or bad battery.
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- brand: article covers gas Bad Boy mowers; diesel Bad Boy has a separate guide.
- pro: if unsure how to diagnose/repair safely, have a professional (Bad Boy dealer or small engine shop) do it.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- symptom: key turned and mower does not start or even turn over: ignition switch or ignition coil could be the problem; also bad battery, loose cables/corroded terminals, failed safety switches.
- causes-named: bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: easily forgotten; may also be a fuel leak.
- fix: check for and repair leak, add fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can start breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase, causing running and clogging problems.
- brand: Bad Boy gas mowers require unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10 percent ethanol.
- fact: ethanol attracts moisture; over time the solution separates and sinks to the bottom of the tank; when it evaporates it leaves a gummy residue that can clog the fuel system, corrode it, and run hot causing engine damage.
- tip: if fuel can't be used within 30 days, add a stabilizer such as Sea Foam to stabilize and reduce moisture.
- fix: drain the tank with a fuel siphon; add fresh fuel containing additive to stabilize and clean the fuel system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: clogged vent makes the tank a vacuum, fuel won't flow, mower won't start.
- fact: vent may be in the fuel cap or a rollover vent on the tank.
- confirm: loosen cap and start; if it starts and runs fine, reinstall cap and keep running; if it shuts off after a while, vent likely plugged.
- fix: determine cap vs rollover vent; replace with a new gas cap or rollover vent.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: no spark from dirty/fouled plug; burnt electrode, broken porcelain insulator, unsecured wires, or incorrect gap also cause no-start.
- fix: remove plug, inspect for carbon buildup or cracked porcelain; replace with new plug, correctly gapped, wires secure.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: grass clippings and dirt plug the filter; engine can't get air; may overheat and shut down.
- safety: running a dirty filter or no filter risks serious engine damage; always use an air filter.
- tip: check more often in dry dusty conditions; start each season with a new filter and clean routinely.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: PAPER filter:
- step 1: remove filter from housing.
- step 2: wipe dirt out of housing with a dry clean rag; don't let dirt fall into the air intake.
- step 3: tap filter against a solid surface to knock out loose dirt. Do not use compressed air (damages the filter).
- step 4: hold up to a light; if light shows through the paper, reuse; otherwise replace.
- step 5: install filter, reattach housing cover.
- fact: FOAM filter:
- step 1: remove filter; wipe housing with dry clean rag, no dirt into intake.
- step 2: inspect; dark spots, tears, or dry/brittle foam means replace.
- step 3: wash in water and mild dish soap; rinse until water runs clear and soap is gone.
- step 4: squeeze out excess water, lay flat to dry (sun speeds drying).
- step 5: coat fully with foam filter oil, but not dripping.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: replace the fuel filter once a year during annual maintenance.
- symptom: dirt plugs the filter, restricts fuel flow, starting problems.
- fix: replace; the arrow on the filter side must point in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: install the new filter with the arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow (annual replacement recommended).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: clog not visible. Stop/start fuel flow with the fuel shut-off valve (under the fuel tank) or pinch pliers. With flow stopped, remove one end of the hose into a container, start flow, check for fuel. Container must be lower than the tank; fuel cannot flow uphill.
- fix: remove line, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow compressed air through; repeat until clear. If it can't be cleared, replace the line.
- tip: replace fuel lines, even without a clog, if they are dry and beginning to crack.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: old gas sitting in the pump degrades it; pump can't build pressure to push fuel to carburetor.
- confirm: check for leaks; verify fuel reaches the pump (shut-off valve/pinch pliers); disconnect hose from carburetor into a container, start mower, watch for constant or pulsating flow.
- fix: replace the pump if no constant or pulsating flow.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: common from running old fuel; gummed-up carb can't give the right fuel/air mixture.
- fix: if mechanical, disassemble and clean with carb cleaner including float bowl and needle; or have a small engine mechanic clean it; or replace. Replace if too dirty to clean.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: battery won't charge or fails to hold a charge.
- confirm: test with multimeter; need about 12.7 volts; below that, charge it.
- fix: replace the battery if it doesn't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: confirm terminals aren't corroded and are secure; clean with baking soda solution of 2 cups water + 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda; scrub with a wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: safety switches prevent starting without the parking brake engaged; another disengages the deck and shuts off the mower if operator presence isn't sensed; there may be others. Failed switches stop it starting.
- confirm: test with a multimeter. May temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch, for troubleshooting only.
- safety: never operate a mower without the safety switch or with it bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned, doesn't start or even turn over.
- confirm: test with multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- confirm: after confirming the spark plug is good, check coil continuity with a multimeter; replace if there is a break in continuity.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: not the main no-start reason but can leave a weak battery. Bad stator or alternator among possible parts; article links a charging system test with an ohm meter.
- pro: have a Bad Boy dealer or small engine shop check; without experience you'll likely replace parts until one works, which gets expensive because parts stores often won't take back electrical parts.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Bad Boy safety features require certain steps to start/operate; refer to the operating manual so you don't trip safety shutoffs.
