## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "John Deere zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, wrong-fuel, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, faulty-charging-system, wrong-choke-setting, incorrect-starting-procedure
- safety: always remove ignition key and spark plug wire before repairs
- pro: charging system faults: bring to John Deere dealer; otherwise parts get thrown at it; electrical parts expensive and usually non-returnable

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: faulty fuel gauge, a fuel leak emptying tank early, or forgetting to check
- fix: check for leaks and repair; add fresh fuel

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: John Deere gas zero-turns need unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; high ethanol can cause significant engine damage

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: old gas is often the main culprit for sluggish, no start, or dying after running
- fact: ethanol (plant-based) attracts moisture; water/ethanol leaves varnish that restricts flow and corrodes components
- fact: gasoline breaks down as soon as 30 days; consume within that; use stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment if you can't
- fix: drain tank (an inexpensive manual siphon pump works well); add fresh gas plus additive to clean system, reduce moisture, stabilize

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: annual maintenance part
- fix: replace; arrow on filter points in direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, carb cleaner then compressed air; if still clogged buy new line of right diameter and length

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump works against gravity to move fuel to the carb; most John Deere zero-turns use a vacuum-style pump; fuel degrades it over time; leaking pump must be replaced
- confirm 1: shut off fuel (shut-off valve under the tank or clamp/pinch line)
- confirm 2: remove hose from pump inlet port, place in container lower than tank
- confirm 3: start fuel flow; flow into container confirms fuel reaches the pump; no flow means restriction upstream of pump
- confirm 4: shut off fuel, replace inlet hose
- confirm 5: remove hose from carb into a container
- confirm 6: start fuel flow and engine; watch for steady or pulsating flow
- confirm 7: shut off mower, reattach hose to carb
- fix: replace pump if no constant or pulsating flow

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old fuel clogs passages and leaves varnish that sticks internal parts
- fix: clean (author links John Deere carb cleaning article) and replace stuck/damaged parts; or small engine shop, or replace assembly

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: tank vents through the cap on John Deere zero-turn
- confirm: if it won't start with cap on, loosen cap (may hear vacuum release); if it starts, tighten and run until it shuts off and again won't restart until loosened
- fix: replace the cap

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: inspect for carbon or cracked porcelain; replace if damaged, worn or very dark; if good clean the tip with small wire brush; gap per spec; wires secure

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- tip: replace annually, check regularly
- step (paper) 1: remove from housing; 2: wipe housing and filter cap with dry cloth, no dirt into intake; 3: tap on solid surface; 4: hold to light; 5: reuse if light shows, replace if not, extremely dirty or damaged; 6: install

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: disconnect battery, remove corroded components; clean with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) or cola plus small metal brush; secure connections

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter; below 12.7 volts put on charger
- fix: replace if it won't hold a charge

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence control system; defective switch stops starting
- confirm: multimeter, or temporarily bypass for troubleshooting only
- safety: never operate without the safety switch; never run with it bypassed

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: multimeter on resistance; B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid) prongs; key at start; near 0 ohms good, infinite bad
- fix: replace

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates starter
- symptom: click or hum when turning the key; wire on the solenoid getting hot, smoking or melting
- confirm: author links John Deere solenoid test

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: bad stator or alternator or other electrical parts leave a weak battery
- confirm: ohmmeter test (author links article)

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts airflow for cold start; wrong position means no start

## page: procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower
- step 1 (cold start): choke lever to full choke
- step 2: throttle halfway between slow and fast
- step 3: key to start, release once engine starts
- step 4: move choke to off
- step 5: idle at half throttle a few minutes before full throttle to mow
- fact: safety interlock requirements: sit in operator's seat; steering control levers in the outward position to engage the brake (or engage separate brake lever); PTO off

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: mower won't start unless safety interlock requirements met (seat, brake engaged via levers outward, PTO off) and choke set correctly

## page: guides/zero-turn-safety
- safety: operator presence control / interlock system exists to keep you safe; never bypass beyond troubleshooting
