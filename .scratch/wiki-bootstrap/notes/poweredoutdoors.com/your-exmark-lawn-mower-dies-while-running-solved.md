## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Exmark starts then dies while mowing"
- alias: "zero turn just stops running in the middle of the yard"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, dirty-carburetor, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Source lists 14 reasons; fuel/air restriction (plugged filters, clogged lines, bad pump, dirty carb), low oil, clogged fins, plugged deck.
- safety: remove spark plug wire before repairs.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: check fuel gauge (visual or on the display); fill with the appropriate fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: about 30-day shelf life; ethanol attracts moisture; when moisture evaporates gumming clogs the system; ethanol and water can separate and sink to the tank bottom and damage the engine.
- tip: buy and use gas within 30 days; buy from busy stations; don't leave fuel outdoors (attracts moisture); store in a dry place; add stabilizer if not used within 30 days. Sea Foam Motor Treatment: petroleum-based, won't harm the engine, stabilizes, removes moisture, cleans the system.
- fix: drain the tank; refill with fresh gas plus additive; dispose of old gas at an appropriate recycling facility; do not dump on the ground.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: stations sell E10, E15, E85; diesel; REC-90 recreational fuel (non-ethanol). Read pump decals for octane and ethanol. Exmark gas mower needs unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; less is better; more ethanol can compromise engine life.
- fix: drain, flush the tank, refill with fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam or other additive.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: gummy and white crusty deposits from old/ethanol fuel keep components from regulating the mixture.
- fix: disassemble and clean with carb cleaner to free parts and unclog the jet; rebuild or replace if too dirty or parts broken. Source links a carb-cleaning article covering Briggs, Kawasaki, Kohler or Exmark engines.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: fuel pump present because the carburetor is higher than the tank; vacuum-pressured pumps can stop building pressure. Look for small cracks or fuel leaking from the pump.
- confirm: confirm flow to the pump, then steady or pulsating flow from the outlet using the shut-off valve or hose clamps.
- fix: replace if no steady/pulsating flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: filters differ; confirm the correct one for your engine and correct installation direction (arrow on the plastic shows flow direction).
- tip: author changes his yearly; inexpensive.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, carb cleaner, compressed air; reinstall; replace if dry and cracked or won't clear (online or hardware store).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: run with and without the cap; fine without but dies with cap = clogged cap. Don't let dirt into the fuel system with cap off.
- fix: clean vent; else replace.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter starves the engine; extremely dirty filter may pull air and oil from the crankcase into the cylinder causing smoke and overheating. Author checks his about once a month; dusty conditions foul sooner.
- fix: remove paper filter; tap on solid surface; light test; reuse if light shows, replace if not; wipe housing first without letting dirt into the intake.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: clean if dirty; replace if very dark or damaged; gap per engine manufacturer; wires secure.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: windings can separate and short when hot; no spark; dies after running a while.
- confirm: ohmmeter for break in continuity; replace.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: a fan on top of the engine pushes air down the block and cylinder head; dirty fins overheat the engine.
- fix: remove the engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: the full line is not a suggestion; high or low oil can make the engine smoke and die; smoke can clog the air filter; too high causes internal damage.
- fix: remove excess by: evacuator; unscrew filter and drain from it; remove drain plug and quickly replace; turkey baster from the oil-fill area.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: too little oil: friction and heat build, parts begin to melt, oil gets very hot and thick.
- fix: change oil per engine manufacturer; fill to correct level; if problems continue take to an authorized Exmark dealer or good small engine mechanic.
- pro: dealer/mechanic for internal engine problems.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck (mud/dirt) with dull blades loads the engine, overheats, shuts down. Inspect deck for damage; scrape; sharpen blades; always full throttle; avoid wet or very tall grass.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- fact: evacuator, filter, drain plug quick open, turkey baster.
