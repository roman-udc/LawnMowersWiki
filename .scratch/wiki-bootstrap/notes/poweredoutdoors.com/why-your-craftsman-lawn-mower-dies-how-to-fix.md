## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Craftsman lawn mower starts then dies"
- alias: "stuck in the middle of the lawn with a dead mower"
- causes-named: old-fuel, wrong-fuel, dirty-carburetor, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: engine needs air and gas; dies when the air/fuel mix can't be regulated: dirty carburetor, bad fuel, plugged air filter, broken fuel pump, plugged fins, other fuel/air restrictions. Author starts with fuel and air restrictions.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas good only 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture, separates over time; ethanol/water sits at the bottom of the tank and isn't easily seen; ethanol is corrosive, dissolves plastics, leaves deposits causing buildup and blockages.
- fix: drain tank into a container for recycling; flush tank; fill with fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam (stabilizes and cleans fuel system).
- tip: use fuel within 30 days; add Sea Foam Motor Treatment to fresh fuel as prevention.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: small engines need unleaded gasoline, octane 87 or greater, no more than 10% ethanol; read the pump sticker for octane and ethanol percent; handle color varies by station so can't be trusted.
- fact: most stations carry E10 and E15; avoid E15 and especially E85.
- fix: drain, flush, refill with fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: 87+ octane unleaded, max 10% ethanol; avoid E15 and E85; read the pump sticker, not the handle color.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: deposits from bad fuel build up; carburetor can't regulate air/gas mix.
- fix: usually can be cleaned; if deposits too bad, replace; DIY if mechanically inclined or use a repair shop.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump is needed where the fuel tank sits lower than the carburetor. Check seams for leaks; internal damage needs tests.
- confirm: use shutoff valve (or clamp lines) to start/stop flow; check fuel flows to the pump; if not, check lines and filter. Then remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start the mower; steady or pulsating stream = pump OK; otherwise replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: no flow to pump -> check fuel lines/filter for obstruction.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: same flow test as the pump section.
- fix: remove line and blow through with compressed air; reinstall; or replace with a new line (online or hardware store).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: blocked vent creates a vacuum in the tank restricting fuel flow.
- confirm: remove the fuel cap and run the mower; if it no longer dies, suspect the cap. Be careful not to let dirt into the fuel system during the test.
- fix: try cleaning the vent; otherwise replace the cap.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter starves engine; also can overheat and cause internal engine damage.
- fix: remove; paper: tap on a solid surface; hold to light; replace if no light, very dark or damaged.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: packed with dirt, grass and oil or broken, fins can't dissipate heat from block and cylinder head.
- fix: remove engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: clean with wire brush if good and only a little dirty; replace if very dark, broken porcelain or damaged electrodes. Gap before installing; secure plug wires. Wrong gap or loose wires cause running problems.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: dies after running a while: windings separate and short out when hot; plugs get no voltage.
- confirm: ohmmeter for break in continuity; replace if break.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: too much oil -> engine smokes and dies while mowing; smoke can clog air filter; can cause internal engine damage and possibly engine replacement.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: too little oil -> friction -> overheat and die.
- fix: change oil per Craftsman recommendations; always fill to the correct level.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: packed deck or dull blades make engine work harder; blades hitting debris each rotation draws on the engine, which can overheat and shut down.
- fix: inspect deck for damage; scrape; sharpen blades; run at full throttle; avoid cutting wet grass.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fix: sharpen the blades (with scraping the deck).
