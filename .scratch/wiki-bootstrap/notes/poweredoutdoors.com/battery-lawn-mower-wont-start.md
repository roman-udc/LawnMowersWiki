## page: problems/battery-mower-wont-start
- alias: "battery lawn mower won't start"
- alias: "battery-powered mower won't turn on"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, battery-not-seated, bad-charger, overheated-battery, incorrect-starting-procedure, bad-safety-switch, bad-wiring-or-ground, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bad-motor, clogged-motor-vents
- safety: Before repairing or working on the electrical system or deck, remove the battery to prevent starting and avoid electrocution. Follow owner's manual safety guidelines.
- fact: Won't start when battery isn't charged or installed with good contact; also bad charger, hot battery, pinched/loose wires, bad switch or motor, plugged deck.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: Insufficient power; use the correct type and size battery for the mower.
- confirm: Check charge via LED lighting system (varies by model; on the battery, on the mower indicator panel, or by placing the battery on the charger to read status).
- symptom: Battery going bad: fully charged but no longer holds charge as long; becomes very hot; charges very slowly.
- fix: Buy a replacement battery if it can't hold a charge, charges very slowly, or gets extremely hot. Batteries fail from age, extreme temperatures, or mechanical failure.

## page: causes/battery-not-seated
- confirm: Check battery is securely in place making good contact. If there are grooves along the battery sides, line them up when sliding in. Most batteries snap into place.

## page: causes/bad-charger
- confirm: Check charger cord to outlet connection (standard 120 V outlet; plug style must match; do not modify the plug). In an external charger, battery pack contacts must engage the charger. Most chargers have a light indicator of charging level.
- fact: Battery chargers are among parts regularly replaced on battery mowers (with batteries, blades, power cords).

## page: causes/overheated-battery
- symptom: Mower shuts down when battery gets extremely hot; wait for the battery to cool before starting.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- symptom: Safety features prevent starting without proper procedure, e.g. depressing the safety bar while starting/operating, or inserting a key. Also handle must be extended to the correct position and locked (some mowers have a safety switch in the handle that blocks starting unless extended; some have no adjustable handle).
- fix: Read the operator's manual and follow manufacturer starting steps.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- confirm: Test by temporarily bypassing with a jumper wire. Replace if bad.
- safety: Never operate the mower without all safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- symptom: Loose or pinched wires make a short; mower won't start or cuts out while mowing.
- confirm: Wiggle wires or use a multimeter; start at pinch points, e.g. in a push mower where the handle folds.
- fix: Repair with a wire nut after removing battery.
- tip: Check warranty first; repairing a damaged wire yourself can void it.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Deck packed with dirt/clippings stops blade rotating, overloading the motor so it won't start.
- fix: Avoid cutting wet grass; raise cutting height in thick/tall grass; reduce ground speed.

## page: causes/bad-motor
- fact: Switch and motor failures need an experienced mechanic. Consult dealer about repair vs. replace considering mower age/condition, part cost and labor.
- pro: Take to an experienced repair shop if not experienced with electric components.

## page: causes/clogged-motor-vents
- symptom: Vents keep the motor cool; clogged vents raise overheating risk; most electric mowers won't turn on if a vent is blocked.
- fix: Wipe with a clean dry cloth; no liquids (they disintegrate motor lubrication); if a rag fails use compressed air can to dislodge debris. Be extremely cautious not to damage the motor.

## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- tip: Don't leave the mower in rain; damp causes corrosion in electrical system.
- tip: Remove dirt and clippings from under the deck.
- tip: Track battery age and charge; charge an hour or two before use so it is fully charged.
- fact: A battery left in the mower loses some charge over time.
- fact: Battery mowers quieter and lower maintenance than gas (no oil changes or gas runs).
- fact: Parts regularly replaced: batteries, blades, battery chargers, power cords.
