## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Troy-Bilt bad or uneven cut"
- alias: "cut turns brown a couple of days later"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, wrong-tire-pressure, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-deck-chute, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: fix table: worn blade -> sharpen/replace; low tire -> correct pressure; bent blade -> replace; blade incorrectly installed -> sail up; clogged deck -> scrape; unlevel deck -> side-to-side and front-to-back; damaged spindle/housing -> replace; bad belt/pulleys -> replace; low engine speed -> full throttle; fast ground speed -> correct speed; wrong paths -> overlap; damaged shell -> repair/replace.
- safety: remove ignition key and disconnect spark plug wire(s) before working on the deck; work gloves around sharp blades.
- tip: check tire pressure first, else deck adjustments may have to be undone.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: worn blades leave strips; dirt wears blades (thin grass cover with more dirt wears sooner). Dull sign: cut turns brown a couple days later; grass blades bruised with brown torn tips.
- fact: bends from rocks, stumps etc.; sometimes jolt and grinding, sometimes subtle; seen blades bent 90 degrees and slightly bent; even slight bend gives uneven cut and vibration. Walk the yard and remove objects first.
- confirm (method 1): remove blade and lay on a new Troy-Bilt blade.
- confirm (method 2): park on flat level surface; measure ground to blade tip one side, rotate 180 degrees, measure the other; greater than 1/8 inch = replace.
- mistake: never straighten a bent blade (weakened; can break off and fly out at high speed).
- fact: balance: equal weight each side; if a sharpened blade is reinstalled it must be balanced; shave the heavy side; most accurate = blade balancer (inexpensive); or nail on wall with head sticking out 3/4 to 1 inch and remove metal from the end hanging lower until level. Unbalanced blades can cause significant damage.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: one tire lower than the other makes the deck unlevel; use a gauge, manufacturer pressures, equal on each side.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: sail is the high side of the blade and must point upward toward the deck; upside-down blades beat and tear grass and can damage spindle housings. Some blades are stamped "BOTTOM" or "THIS SIDE DOWN". Remember "Sail Up!".

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: suction and airflow under the deck produce the cut; buildup also affects blade speed and engine load. Don't mow wet grass; a silicone-type spray under the deck reduces buildup and scraping frequency (does not prevent it).

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- fact: have the owner's manual for model specs; steps vary.
- procedure: park on hard level surface. Side to side: set deck lift/knob to a middle position; rotate blade so the tip is at the right side; measure ground to blade tip; repeat on left side; adjust if different.
  - Riding mower: loosen (do not remove) the hex cap screw on the deck hanger bracket on the left side; turn the adjustment gear behind it with a wrench clockwise to raise, counterclockwise to lower; retighten the hex cap screw.
  - Zero turn: loosen the jam nut on the adjustment bolt (one on each side of the deck; do not remove); adjust bolt up/down until measurements match; retighten the jam nut.
- procedure: pitch (front to rear): front must be lower than rear. Measure on the right side with the blade closest to the discharge chute rotated parallel to the mower; measure ground to blade tip at front and at rear.
  - Riding mower: front 1/4 in to 3/8 in lower than rear. Adjust at the stabilizer bracket: loosen (don't remove) the jam nut on the front; tighten the lock nut to raise the front, loosen to lower; retighten the jam nut.
  - Zero turn: front 1/16 in to 1/4 in lower than rear. Lock nut and jam nut on the front hanger bracket (may be behind a cap); loosen the jam nut; tighten lock nut to raise, loosen to lower; retighten the jam nut.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- step 1: park on hard level surface. step 2: set deck lift/knob to middle. step 3: blade tip to right side, measure ground to tip; same on left. step 4: adjust side to side (riding: hex cap screw + adjustment gear clockwise raises; zero turn: jam nut + adjustment bolt). step 5: measure front vs rear at blade nearest the discharge chute; riding front 1/4-3/8 in lower, zero turn front 1/16-1/4 in lower; adjust stabilizer bracket lock nut (riding) or front hanger bracket lock nut (zero turn); retighten jam nuts.
- brand: Troy-Bilt specs above.
- tools: wrench, tape/ruler, owner's manual.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: heavy gloves, rock blade ends up and down; movement or knocking = remove spindle housing, inspect spindle and bearing (bearing most likely bad).

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: bad belt looks glazed/shiny, frayed, stretched or cracked (age or rubbing deck parts). Replace with compatible belt; author uses only manufacturer belts.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: rotate each pulley slowly; resistance or bearing noise = replace.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: full throttle; if full throttle yet weak, take to Troy-Bilt dealer or small engine shop.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: slow down for thick, tall, wet grass.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: fewer passes risk strips; takes experimenting with a new mower to avoid overlapping too much.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: repair or replace; ask the dealer to also quote a complete deck assembly; depending on age and labor, a new deck may be worth it.

## unmapped
- lawn conditions: high spots and shallow ditches cause scalping; wide decks can't follow dips; use a smaller push-mower deck for ditches (lawn-care advice; skip).
