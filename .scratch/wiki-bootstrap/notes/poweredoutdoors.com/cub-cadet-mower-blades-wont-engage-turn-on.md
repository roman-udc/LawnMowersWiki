## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Cub Cadet blades won't engage", "blades won't turn on"
- causes-named: worn-drive-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: Summary: worn deck belt; worn tensioner arm or spring; bad pulley; faulty PTO switch; bad clutch; weak battery; blown fuse.
- safety: Follow the Cub Cadet operator's manual; always remove the ignition key and spark plug wires before repairs.
- pro: Repeated blown fuses: take to a Cub Cadet service dealer or repair shop for the root electrical cause.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- symptom: belt slips on pulleys and fails to turn blades.
- confirm: look for cracks, wear, fraying, shiny appearance.
- fix: replace; replace as soon as it shows wear even if it isn't the main cause. Belt grip affects blade speed, which is needed for the suction that lifts grass.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Tensioner arm and spring hold the idler pulley in line; the bracket typically has a pulley on one side and a spring on the other. The spring-attachment hole in the bracket or the spring itself can wear, loosening the belt so it vibrates off the pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Bad bearings let a pulley move so it no longer sits flat/parallel to the deck; one side higher than the other; the belt can roll off. Replace the pulley.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch is usually a knob allowing battery voltage to power the clutch; failure = blades won't turn on.
- confirm: check continuity; replace if there is a break.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to the blades by engaging the drive belt; replace a worn/faulty clutch.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: Manual-engagement mower has clutch lever and cable; check lever, cable, spring, bushings, linkages; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Electric clutch uses battery power; weak battery can't energize the clutch solenoid.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12 V reads about 12.7 V; charge if lower.
- fix: if it won't hold a charge replace with a new 12 V battery (hardware/automotive store or dealer); bring the old battery to avoid a core fee averaging $20.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for skin and eyes; don't touch anything without rubber coating.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (under hood or seat; screwdriver may be needed); don't remove battery from casing.
- step 2: Connect charger positive (red/plus) cable to positive terminal first.
- step 3: Connect negative (black/minus) to negative terminal.
- step 4: Set charger to 12 V; start at 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge is best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Seat switch under the seat is part of the operator presence system; blades won't turn on without the operator in the seat; a bad switch prevents engagement.
- confirm: test with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: do not operate without the safety switch installed.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: No battery power to the clutch may be a blown fuse; replace with the same size fuse.
