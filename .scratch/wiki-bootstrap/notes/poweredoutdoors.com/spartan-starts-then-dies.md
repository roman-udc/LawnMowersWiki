## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Spartan zero turn starts, stalls and dies"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- safety: Remove the spark plug wire and ignition key before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Ethanol (from corn or another high-starch plant) attracts moisture, corroding the fuel system; evaporation leaves gummy deposits; the separated mixture runs extremely hot and damages the engine. Don't use fuel above 10% ethanol; use fresh unleaded.
- fix: Siphon old fuel into an approved container; refill with correct gasoline plus stabilizer. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment in each tank (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans; petroleum-based).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace; arrow on the housing points in the direction of fuel flow. Symptom: runs sluggishly then dies.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Use the shut-off valve to start/stop flow while checking each section.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove the line, carburetor cleaner then compressed air; replace with a new line if it won't clear.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most Spartan pumps are vacuum pumps; they can wear or degrade from fuel.
- confirm: Confirm flow to the pump; then fuel off, line off the carburetor into a container, fuel on, start; steady or pulsating flow = OK, else replace.
- confirm: Electronic fuel injection pump: measure pressure with a fuel pressure gauge; the spec is in the operator's manual; replace if below the engine manufacturer's specification.
- brand: Spartan models with EFI use a gauge test instead of the container test.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Confirm fuel flow to the carburetor (from the pump check); remove the air filter; spray carburetor cleaner in the intake; start. Starts, runs fine, then shuts down = carburetor.
- fix: Remove and disassemble to clean, or take it to a small engine repair shop.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dust and grass pulled in without a filter wear the engine; keep a clean filter installed at all times. Replace annually for average homeowner use; more often for commercial or frequent use. Check before each use and clean when required (takes a minute or two); skipping can overheat the engine.
- step: Clean paper filter: remove the element; wipe dirt from the housing with a clean dry cloth without letting it into the intake; tap on a hard surface; no compressed air (damages paper); hold to light, replace if no light or damaged, else reuse; install and reattach the cover.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: Engine overheats after running a while and dies.
- fix: Clean dirt off fins, replace broken fins, clean around the engine block and shroud, make sure the heat shield is correctly installed for air circulation.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- tip: Check the oil level before each use with the dipstick (many owners skip it).
- symptom: Low oil: friction builds heat in the crankcase, burning oil and parts, overheat and shutdown.
- pro: Adding oil seldom fixes it after a shutdown; likely significant damage; have an experienced small engine mechanic assess.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Excess oil builds pressure in the crankcase.
- fix: Remove a little oil via drain plug, oil filter, or evacuator/turkey baster at the fill; repeat until correct.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: Clean with a wire brush; replace if very dark or damaged; gap per the engine manufacturer; plug wires secure.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Windings separate and short when hot; dies after running a while.
- confirm: Ohmmeter continuity; break = replace.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts airflow for cold starts; if left on after warm-up, the mower dies. Set the choke lever to open/off once warm.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Mower shuts down and won't restart until the cap is loosened to let air into the tank.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Plugged deck makes the engine work hard, overheat and shut down; dull blades magnify it. The deck uses blades and the area under the deck to create air movement to lift and cut grass.
- fix: Scrape the deck regularly and sharpen blades.
- safety: Protect hands, remove the ignition key, disconnect the spark plug wire, follow the operator's manual around sharp blades.

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- fact: Spartan battery not charging: run at high engine speed; don't idle for long periods since charging needs engine power. If you keep having to charge it and battery and wiring are good and secure, suspect the charging system.
- fix: Check battery and charging system (Source links a test article); replace a bad battery.
- pro: Have an experienced mechanic diagnose and repair the charging system; many components can fail, parts get thrown at it, electrical parts usually can't be returned.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: See above; idle too long = no charge; professional diagnosis recommended.
