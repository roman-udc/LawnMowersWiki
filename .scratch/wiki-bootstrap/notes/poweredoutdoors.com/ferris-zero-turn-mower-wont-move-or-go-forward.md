## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Ferris zero turn won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "zero-turn moves slowly"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: Won't move when drive levers are not in operating position and bypass valve is open; also worn/loose transmission drive belt, bad tensioner pulley, stretched/missing idler arm spring, hot or low hydraulic oil, trapped air.
- fact: Owners often fear a costly transmission problem (wheel motor or hydraulic pump); often it's just fresh oil or a belt/pulley/spring.
- tip: Non-hydraulic checks if engine sputters or shuts off so you can't drive: battery and charging system, plugged fuel filter and lines, plugged air filter, dirty carburetor.
- pro: Take to the Ferris dealership if the hydraulic pump leaks or the mower runs fine cold but stops when hot.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- symptom: Ferris drive release levers release the transmissions so the mower free-rolls (for pushing to garage/trailer). They are on the transmission cradle between the rear wheels. Must be in the operating position.
- safety: Never tow your mower.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- confirm: Check the transmission drive belt is in good condition and correctly positioned on pulleys.
- fix: Replace if broken, loose, or worn.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: Tensioner pulley or its bearing can break/wear.
- fix: Replace worn pulley; keep the tensioner arm greased so it doesn't seize.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- symptom: Idler spring tensions the drive belt; broken or fallen out.
- fix: Replace the spring.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- symptom: Old or low hydraulic oil can cause no movement, slow movement, or weak feeling.
- fix: Add oil until the overflow tanks reach the "FULL COLD" mark (on each transmission overflow tank).
- fact: Ferris transmissions have a break-in period; for most Ferris zero-turns hydraulic oil changed after 100 hours; after that oil and filter every 400 hours using 20W-50 conventional detergent motor oil. Confirm in operator's manual.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Intervals above (100 hours break-in; then every 400 hours oil and filter; 20W-50 conventional detergent motor oil).

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- symptom: Bad or low fluid can't lubricate efficiently: more friction, overheating, more extensive damage.
- pro: Dealer if pump leaks or mower stops when hot.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- symptom: Slow or no movement, especially after changing hydraulic fluid; must bleed air out.
- fix: See procedure.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Chock the front wheels, raise the rear drive tires off the ground.
- step 2: Open bypass valve with drive release levers (transmission cradle between rear tires), start the mower, release parking brake.
- step 3: Move drive levers forward then reverse, 6 times.
- step 4: Shut off, set parking brake, close bypass valve; repeat the process (start, release brake, levers forward/reverse).
- step 5: Keep repeating until the transmission stops making excessive noise and wheels move at normal speed forward and reverse. Refer to operator's manual for model-specific procedure.
