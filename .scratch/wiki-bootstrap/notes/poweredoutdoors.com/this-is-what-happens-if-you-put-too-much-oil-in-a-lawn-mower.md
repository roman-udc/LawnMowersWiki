## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: engine very hot/overheats; blue or white smoke; leaking engine oil; slow starting; runs rough; clogged air filter; fouled spark plug.
- fact: effects: increased crankcase pressure, overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets, hydro-locked engine. If caught soon may fix; otherwise significant engine work or replacement.
- fact seals: oil seals can blow out; if oil runs out you can blow up the engine: heat may spin a rod or weld to crankshaft, break rod out of block; piston can weld to cylinder (seize).
- fact overheating: crankshaft and rod can't rotate freely through extra oil; pressure loads internal parts; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil may not flow; aluminum engines get very hot; can pop out valve guide or valve seat (engine then needs to be scrapped out, as stated).
- fact hydrolock: oil in cylinder (past worn rings); combustion chamber should hold only air and a little fuel; piston can't compress oil; engine turns over hard; can bend piston rod when starting.
- fact smoke/fouling: oil reaches spark plug and fouls it; oil into valve train burns in cylinder -> thick whitish blue stinky smoke; air filter clogs as engine pulls crankcase air and oil; oil can get into carburetor via valve train and through to air filter -> engine won't run.
- fact twin-cylinder: same problems; excess pressure may blow gaskets; gasket replacement costs more than push mower because engine must be removed.
- fact: small engine oil pan is small, little room for error.
- fix: remove spark plug wire first; remove excess to dipstick level: (1) drain plug or valve port (side of engine by dipstick or bottom of oil pan): drain pan, remove plug for a quick second, replace, check level; (2) oil filter: loosen/remove to drain a little, rag ready; (3) fill hole: many push mower engines no drain plug, tip mower to drain from fill hole; (4) oil extractor pump/evacuator via tube in fill hole; (5) turkey baster (don't reuse for cooking). After correcting, wipe spilled oil off mower to prevent smoking.
- tip prevent: check owner's manual for crankcase capacity (or search online); most push mower engines about 3/4 qt; v-twin about 2 qt; larger commercial "big block" about 3 qt (engines about 34 hp or larger); add only 1/2 qt at a time, then smaller amounts near full, checking dipstick.
- pro: if draining a little doesn't fix, small engine mechanic to inspect; tearing engine down yourself may cause more damage. Dealer or engine-maker repair center (Honda, Kawasaki, Kohler, Briggs & Stratton).

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: remove spark plug wire.
- step 2: choose method: drain plug/valve port (quick remove and replace); oil filter; tip mower and drain from fill hole; oil evacuator; turkey baster.
- step 3: check dipstick; repeat until correct; wipe up spilled oil.

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- fact: see overfilled-oil hydrolock: oil in cylinder, piston can't compress it; pulling rope or starter won't move piston; can bend piston rod.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: blue/white thick smoke from oil burning after overfill.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- fact: overfill -> overheating.

## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- fact: overfill -> fouled plug, runs rough.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-oil
- fact: overfill symptom: leaking oil (seals, blown gaskets).

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: capacities: push about 3/4 qt; v-twin about 2 qt; big block (34 hp or larger) about 3 qt; add 1/2 qt at a time.
