## page: causes/safety-key-missing
- symptom: most mowers need a safety key or button depressed to start (problems/battery-mower-wont-start).
- fix: push key in further, or remove and reinsert. If still no start, a bad switch that doesn't recognize the safety device.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Safety button/key is meant to prevent children accidentally depressing the safety bar and starting mower; not following procedure prevents starting. Check user manual of new/used mower.
- step 1: securely insert the battery.
- step 2: insert safety key and press safety button (not all models have a key).
- step 3: with safety button depressed, pull the safety bar to meet the handle; this starts the mower.
- step 4: release the safety button.
- step 5: mower shuts off when you release the safety bar.
- fact: Push mower foldable handle must be unfolded and secured in the operating position; if side rails on handle, extend to max length and lock the clamps; rails can slip out of position -> no start: release clamps, check handle extension, secure clamps. Safety switches enforce this.

## page: causes/battery-not-seated
- fact: battery must be inserted correctly and make good contact with the holder.
- step 1: wipe around battery cover before opening so dirt doesn't fall in.
- step 2: remove battery.
- step 3: wipe battery contacts; clear dirt from compartment.
- step 4: slide battery into guides; on most models you hear a click as it snaps in and the release button springs into place.
- step 5: close battery compartment cover.
- fact: for cordless, start with battery connection, then battery and charger.

## page: causes/overheated-battery
- symptom: excessively hot battery makes mower shut down and not start.
- fix: let battery cool before restarting. Clogged vents are why most mowers won't turn on with hot battery.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: some mowers have a charge display; otherwise charger may use LEDs to show charging status.
- fact: failing battery: fully charged but doesn't hold charge as long, charges very slowly, or gets extremely hot -> buy replacement. Batteries fail from age, extreme temperatures, or mechanical failure.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- symptom: wires loose or pinched develop a short; push mowers commonly pinch wires where handle folds into the machine for storage.
- confirm: wiggle wires; multimeter to find a short.
- fix: repair loose/pinched connections with a wire nut; remove the battery first.
- safety: check warranty first; DIY wire repair can void it.
- fact: plug-ins can come loose (esp. large riding mowers): with mower off and safety engaged, open it up around the battery and check all plug-ins connected.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- confirm: test each switch by temporarily bypassing with a jumper wire. Replace bad switch.
- fact: push mower switches: foldable handle; side rails fully extended (if sliding rails); safety bar (must be pulled into handle; release shuts mower off); safety button. Owner's manual for additional switches (EGO mentioned).
- safety: never operate without all safety switches working.

## page: causes/bad-bagger-or-full-bag
- symptom: full cutting bag prevents mower turning on (cannot collect more) (problems/battery-mower-wont-start).
- confirm: remove detached bag, check status; empty, reattach, retest.
- tip: empty bag routinely.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- symptom: grass/debris lodged in deck inhibits blade rotation; if blades can't turn properly, safety features prevent starting (protect motor).
- confirm: check under deck near blades (push or riding).
- fix: clean deck after every use; keep blades sharp (dull blades magnify the problem and strain motor).

## page: causes/clogged-motor-vents
- fact: vents keep motor cool; clogged vents increase overheating risk.
- fix: wipe with clean dry cloth; no liquids (they disintegrate the motor's lubrication); if rag fails use compressed air can to dislodge debris.
- safety: be cautious; can damage motor while cleaning.

## page: causes/bad-motor
- fact: damaged motor or start switch: pro job (see pro above).

## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- fact (corded): average U.S. lawn about 1/4 acre; owner needs extension cord. Corded push mower needs extension cord supplying 13-15 amps; 6-10 amp cord may cause trouble turning or staying on; mismatched cord increases overheating of both, damage and fire hazard; use cord rated for the mower.
- confirm (corded): check outlet and cord connections fully plugged in; outlet capable of needed power; check household breakers; test outlet with a simple appliance like a lamp.
- fact: common replacement parts: power cords, batteries, blades, plug-ins.
- tip: prevention: empty bag and clear deck after every use; track battery age and charge; charge rechargeable battery an hour or two before use; a fully charged battery left sitting loses some charge over time.
- fact: manufacturer contact table: EGO 855-346-5656 (egopowerplus.com/store-locator); Greenworks 888-909-6757; RYOBI 800-525-2579; STIHL 800-467-8445; Toro 888-384-9939; Sun Joe 866-766-9563; SKIL 877-754-5999.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- tip: charge battery an hour or two before use; full batteries left sitting lose charge; replace when it can't hold a charge/charges slowly/runs hot.
