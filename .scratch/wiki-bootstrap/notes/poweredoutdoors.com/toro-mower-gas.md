## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Toro mowers need unleaded gasoline, octane 87 or higher, ethanol no greater than 10% (E10); low-ethanol or ethanol-free is best. Many Toro push mowers with 2-cycle engines still in use need a gas and oil mix.
- fact: regular gas can be used if ethanol is 10% or less; regular generally has octane 87 (US Energy Information Administration, cited by Source).
- fact: ethanol level is on the fuel pump decal; typically 10% to 15%; avoid E15.
- fact: ethanol facts: corn-based, works in vehicles but damaging to small gas engines; attracts moisture so gas separates from ethanol; separated ethanol and water sink to tank bottom and get fed through the engine causing ignition problems; leaves gummy deposits damaging fuel components; corrosive and can burn very hot, overheating the engine.
- fact: gasoline breaks down and loses effectiveness after 30 days; buy at busy stations; buy only what can be used in 30 days.
- fact: ethanol-free options: recreation fuel REC-90 (not all stations; pure-gas.org lists locations in US and Canada); ethanol-free 4-cycle canned fuel from hardware stores or online. Can cost up to $2.00 more per gallon at the pump, more in canisters. No stabilizer needed for ethanol-free fuel (doesn't hurt if added).
- opinion: author recommends higher quality fuel if affordable.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: store unused gas in an approved fuel container in a dry area away from water; use a quality plastic gas can designed for gasoline (some plastics break down).
- tip: add a fuel additive to the can; author likes Sea Foam Engine Treatment.
- tip: avoid more than a 30-day supply; empty the current can before adding new gas; use stored gas first so fresh isn't added to old.
- tip: at season end discard leftover gas appropriately (can often go in a car or other machines); don't let a can sit months after the season.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: a gas leak can be smelled but hard to spot because fuel evaporates, unlike oil which leaves a stain; check fuel lines, gas cap and carburetor (links a "smells like gas" article).

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: wrong gas damages the fuel system and engine; see guide notes.
