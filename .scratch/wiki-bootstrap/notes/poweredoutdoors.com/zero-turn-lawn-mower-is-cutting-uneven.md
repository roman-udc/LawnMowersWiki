## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "zero turn mower cuts uneven"
- alias: "strips of uncut grass"
- alias: "scalping over high spots"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, dull-or-bent-blade, worn-blade-sail, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, clogged-deck-chute, unlevel-deck, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- safety: remove spark plug boots and ignition key; protect hands from blades; see owner's manual.
- tip: if engine no longer has its old power, have a small engine mechanic test it.
- pro: if you can't find the cause or aren't comfortable, take it to a dealer or an experienced small engine shop.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: a low tire makes that side of the deck sit closer to the ground; check first (otherwise you will have to undo adjustments made before finding it).
- fix: inflate to pressure on the tire sidewall; both drive tires equal.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- confirm (dull): look for worn rounded blade ends; strips of uncut grass.
- fix: sharpen if fairly good condition without large gouges; replace if gouges or edges thin from prior sharpening.
- confirm (bent): flat level surface, measure ground to blade end, rotate 180, measure same point; more than 1/8 inch difference = bent, replace. Or lay old blade on a new one and look for gaps. Check spindle/bearing first before removing.
- fact: bending commonly from impact with stump or rock (jolt or grinding noise); smaller impacts may go unnoticed.
- safety: never straighten a blade; metal weakened, more prone to cracking; cracked blade at speed endangers people, pets, objects.
- fact: unbalanced blade: weight heavier on one side; from dirt wearing one side more or from uneven sharpening. Unbalanced blade damages spindle housing and vibrates mower. Check with a blade balancer (inexpensive tool) or nail on the wall: nail head about 3/4 to 1 inch from wall, blade center on nail; shave metal from the side that hangs lower until level.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: worn sail (high side of blade) can't create enough airflow/suction under the deck to lift grass.
- confirm: sail should be same thickness as center of the blade; when thinner than center, replace the blade.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fact: bad bearing or spindle causes blade wobble then significant vibration at speed.
- confirm: grab each end of the blade, rock up/down for movement or knock; disassemble housing and inspect.
- fix: replace bearing or spindle; some manufacturers use sealed-bearing spindle housings, so the whole housing assembly must be replaced.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: worn belt slips; replace belts with glazed shiny look, riding deep in pulley grooves, stretched, cracked, shredded.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: failed bearing means pulley not flat/parallel to deck or doesn't turn smoothly.
- confirm: turn each pulley by hand for restriction or bearing noise; check it sits securely, not wobbly; replace if any symptom.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: debris interferes with airflow and suction under deck; engine works harder (overheating risk).
- fix: scrape routinely; deck spray reduces how often (not a miracle); avoid wet mowing (wet grass clumps and plugs faster; leaves more pronounced tire tracks that look like a bad cut).

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- confirm: measure ground to blade on each side on a level surface; also front-to-rear (rake).
- fact: most zero-turn decks must sit with front slightly lower than rear; follow owner's manual measurements and procedure (varies by manufacturer/model).

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: full throttle with deck engaged for fast blade tip speed.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: too fast pushes over grass; thick, tall, heavy, or wet grass needs slower speed; slow on inclines (extra engine strain).

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: author spaced passes as wide as possible to save time and left strips of uncut grass; overlap paths.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: hitting a tree or post can damage the shell.
- fix: try to repair or replace; price a full replacement deck versus shell swap because labor to move all components can be surprising; depends on remaining mower life.

## page: causes/unbalanced-load
- fact: lawn uneven: high spots from burrowing critters cause scalping; roll the yard; incline or ditch scalping with a wide deck (can't flex/float); a smaller deck like a push mower is best there.
