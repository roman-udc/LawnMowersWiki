## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "Craftsman mower engine smells hot"
- safety: when you smell the engine getting hot, stop the mower to avoid significant damage. Remove spark plug wires before repair; let the engine cool; follow operator manual.
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- pro: if problems continue after an oil change, low oil may have caused extensive damage; take to a Craftsman servicing dealer or small engine dealer for further tests.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: air-cooled small engines on Craftsman riding mowers, tractors, zero-turns use air-cooled engine oil with a high concentration of zinc (zinc as cooling agent). Car motor oil has little zinc because liquid-cooled engines use water.
- fact: most small-engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; may change to 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures and 5W-30 in cold. Chart: Kawasaki engine oil viscosity and temperature chart; consult operator manual.
- fix (wrong oil): drain and add correct air-cooled oil.
- symptom (low oil): friction builds and creates heat; oil thickens if you keep running low; significant engine damage.
- tip: check oil before each use; add a little if low.
- fact: if consistently low, look for: oil leak (find source, replace gaskets, check oil filter seals if fitted); burning oil (plugged air filter burns oil and makes engine work harder: clean/replace); using oil (from overheating, or valve/ring problem: repair center diagnosis).
- fix: correct slightly low level; if it's the cause of the hot smell do a full oil change (heated oil may have thickened).

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins push air to the exterior of the cylinder head and block. A fan on top of the engine pulls cool air in and blows it down through hot parts, hot air exits at the bottom.
- symptom: fins plugged with dirt or damaged cannot dissipate heat.
- fix: remove engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins, clean dirt from cover and around engine. Make it part of annual maintenance.
- mistake: washing the mower can push dirt and water into the engine area; don't spray water into the engine.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: engine guard/shield keeps cool air circulating around the engine; without it cool air exits the area.
- fix: check it is in correct position; tighten or replace mounting screws; replace missing/damaged guard.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: plugged with dirt, engine starved of air, runs hot because it works harder to breathe.
- tip: don't stretch an air filter's life; filter cost is far less than engine repair (author's opinion). Consult manual for filter type.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: PAPER:
- step 1: remove from housing.
- step 2: wipe out housing; no dirt into intake.
- step 3: tap against solid surface.
- step 4: hold to light; light visible means reuse, else buy new.
- step 5: reinstall, attach cover.
- fact: FOAM:
- step 1: remove; wipe housing.
- step 2: inspect; dark spots, tears, dry/brittle means replace.
- step 3: wash with water and mild dish soap; rinse out soap.
- step 4: lay flat to dry (sun speeds).
- step 5: coat completely with foam air filter oil, not dripping; wring out or blot excess with paper towel.
- step 6: reinstall, attach cover.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: clippings, dirt build under the deck; deck, baffles, blades are designed for airflow and suction; buildup hurts cut quality and makes the engine work harder.
- fix: scrape with a deck scraper; wire brush or putty knife also works; check frequently.
- tip: deck coating sprays (e.g. DuPont) reduce but don't eliminate buildup. Avoid cutting wet grass and run at full throttle to reduce clumping.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: dull blades magnify a plugged deck, straining the engine to overheating.
- fix: sharpen about twice a season or about every 25 hours for the average homeowner; balance when sharpening.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- symptom: long, thick, wet grass loads the engine and may overheat it.
- fix: slow ground speed; avoid wet grass; double/triple-cut long grass (highest deck setting first, then lower a little for a second cut); full throttle.

## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- fact: let the engine cool before putting the mower in a storage unit or garage; best to place in an outdoor area; on a riding mower lift the hood to expose the engine; let it run under no load so normal cooling begins.
