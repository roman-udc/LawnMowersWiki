## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Toro has a bad or uneven cut"; "stair-step cut"; "strips of uncut grass"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade (worn/dull), wrong-tire-pressure, dull-or-bent-blade (bent), blade-installed-upside-down, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing (and worn belt), wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- tip: Check tire pressure first before measuring deck level or blade bend, otherwise you may make adjustments you later have to reverse.
- fact: Extra: an uneven lawn (high spots, shallow ditches) makes a wide deck scalp; for ditches use a smaller format deck like a push mower.
- fact: Solution table in Source: dull/worn blade - sharpen or replace; tire pressure - correct level; bent/damaged blade - replace; blade incorrectly installed - sail upward; deck packed - scrape; deck not level - side-to-side and front-to-back; spindle/housing damage - replace; bad belt/pulleys - replace; low engine speed - full throttle; ground speed - correct for conditions; path overlap; damaged deck shell - repair or replace.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: Worn blades leave strips of uncut grass; very worn ones get thin and can curl at the end; tips turn brown a couple of days after mowing (bruised/torn rather than cut).
- confirm: Bent: (1) remove the blade, lay on a new Toro blade; (2) park on flat level surface, measure ground to blade tip, rotate 180 degrees, measure again; more than 1/8" difference = replace. Remove key and disconnect spark plug wire(s) before working under the deck. Even a slightly bent blade can cause uneven cut.
- mistake: Never straighten a bent blade; weakened metal can break off and fly out at high speed.
- fix: Sharpen or replace.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- symptom: A low tire makes one side of the deck sit lower; stair-step cut.
- fix: Inflate to the manufacturer's recommendation; both sides equal.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: Every Toro blade has a sail (high side) that must point toward the deck. The blade may have "BOTTOM" or another term stamped to show which side faces down; otherwise "Sail Up". Upside-down installation beats up the grass and can damage spindle housings.
- fact: Balance: an unbalanced blade (one end heavier) vibrates aggressively, felt in the seat; shave metal off the heavy end. Use a blade balancer (relatively inexpensive) or a nail on the wall (head sticking out about 3/4" to 1"), remove metal from the end hanging lower until level.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Grass under the deck kills the air movement/suction that lifts blades for a clean cut.
- tip: Don't cut wet grass (clumps on deck). A silicone-type spray on the underside reduces buildup and scraping frequency but doesn't prevent it.
- fix: Scrape the deck.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- fix: Park on a level surface; measure each corner from the ground to the bottom of the deck and record; check against the owner's manual specs for side-to-side and front-to-back levelness. Deck leveling varies by Toro model.
- fact: Correct pitch: the front of the deck sits slightly lower than the back for best cutting.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- step 1: Park on a level surface.
- step 2: Measure each deck corner from ground to bottom of deck; record.
- step 3: Compare with the owner's manual specification for side-to-side and front-to-back; adjust per the manual.
- fact: Front slightly lower than back.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Heavy gloves, grab both sides of the blade and rock up and down; listen for knocking and feel for movement; if found, remove the spindle housing and inspect; bearing likely bad.
- fix: Replace damaged spindle or housing.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: Slowly rotate each pulley; resistance or noise = bad pulley; replace.
- fact: Bad belt: glazed or shiny worn look, frayed or cracked; replace deck belt. Belt that fails to move around pulleys at high speed gives a bad cut.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: Run at full throttle to keep blades at top speed. If at full throttle and the engine doesn't sound or feel as powerful, take it to a Toro dealer or small engine repair shop.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Move slower through heavy, thick or wet grass than light dry grass; too fast pushes grass over and the mower can't cut efficiently.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: Fewer passes finish quickly but leave uncut strips without proper overlap. On a new mower, experiment with overlap so you don't overlap too much either.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- symptom: Hit an obstruction (e.g., moving too fast) and damaged the shell.
- fix: Repair or replace the shell; have the dealer quote a complete deck assembly as well since labor to swap components may make a new deck worth it.
