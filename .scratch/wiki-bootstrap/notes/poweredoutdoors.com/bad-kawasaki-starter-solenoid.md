## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: clicking or humming when turning the ignition key (problems/engine-wont-crank); wiring may smoke or melt.
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnet on/off switch that engages the starter motor to turn the engine over. Often mounted on the starter but may be on the frame closer to the battery.
- fact: Found on every electric-start engine; may be round or square with 3 or 4 posts. Find it by following the positive (+) battery wire; the positive wire attaches to one side of the solenoid. Typical 3- and 4-post wiring schematic shown; not all schematics are the same, some include lights and 12-volt ports.
- fact: Inside is a spring and copper plate. Fails when the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes. Can also fail as a result of a weak starter, bad battery or bad ground. "Electrical items can fail at any time."
- confirm: Loose wires or a bad ground can mimic it; check those before replacing the solenoid.
- brand: Source is about Kawasaki engines; zero-turn with Kawasaki shown.
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety instructions; consult a professional if you lack skills.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohms meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches (to check loose wires), needle nose pliers (if screwdriver doesn't work), battery charger (optional)
- step 1: Make sure the battery is fully charged: at least 12.3 volts on a voltmeter.
- step 2: Get mower ready to start: set parking brake, make sure in neutral, key to on.
- step 3: Bypass the solenoid: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the two cables (cable from battery and cable to starter). A spark is common; don't be alarmed. If the engine turns over while bypassed, solenoid is likely bad. Needle nose pliers can be used if screwdriver doesn't work.
- step 4: Bench test (solenoids mounted on starter): remove starter from engine, attach battery-charger negative (-) clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid. Quick bench test to see if starter works with solenoid.
- safety: Bypass can spark (said to be normal); be careful.
- tip: Check loose wires/bad ground before replacing.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- fact: Use voltmeter; battery should have at least 12.3 volts (in the solenoid test context).
