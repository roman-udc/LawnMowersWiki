## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Exmark zero turn won't turn over or crank"
- alias: "nothing happens when I try to start"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: Troubleshoot items that keep the starter motor from getting power.
- safety: Remove the negative battery cable before electrical repairs. If uncomfortable, consult an experienced mechanic.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Keep fully charged, especially in cold storage, since batteries can freeze when not fully charged.
- fix: Charge 12-volt battery with a charger; replace if it won't hold a charge. Buy at a dealer, hardware or auto store; bring old battery to avoid core fee.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear protective gear for eyes and skin.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover). Do not remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect charger cables positive first: red cable or plus sign to positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach negative (black / minus sign) cable to negative terminal.
- step 4: Don't touch anything without a rubber coating.
- step 5: Set charger voltage and amperage. Typical 12 volts. Start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best; more amperage charges faster.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: Constant vibration can shake wires and components loose and break continuity; corrosion also affects continuity.
- fix: Remove battery cables first, then corroded components. Clean with wire brush and baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda). Replace terminals if corrosion is too severe.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse protects against power surge or short.
- confirm: Multimeter probe on each prong of the fuse, measuring resistance. Near 0 = good; infinity = bad.
- fix: Replace with a fuse of the same amperage. If fuses keep blowing, take to a dealer/repair shop to find the root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step: Probe each prong with a multimeter on resistance; near 0 good, infinity blown; replace with same amperage.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: nothing happens when key is inserted and turned.
- confirm: Multimeter continuity. Prongs marked B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid). Key to start position, multimeter on resistance, one probe on B and one on S. Good = near 0 ohms; bad = infinite resistance, replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Exmark has an operator presence control system; a defective safety switch can prevent turn-over.
- confirm: Test with multimeter; may temporarily bypass for troubleshooting only.
- safety: Never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Electromagnetic switch that engages the starter motor. Goes bad when the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes; a weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause failure.
- confirm: Battery must be fully charged before testing the solenoid (article links to its solenoid test article).

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: Consider only after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid. The starter can be removed and tested.
- tip: Have a starter/alternator specialist shop test and rebuild it if possible before buying a pricey new starter (author's recommendation).
