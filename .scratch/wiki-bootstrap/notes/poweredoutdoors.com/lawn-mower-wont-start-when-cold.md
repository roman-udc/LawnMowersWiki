- fact: covers cold-weather problems plus general no-start items that may merely have appeared on a cold day. (Title says 13, heading 15; about 14 listed.)
- safety: follow operator's manual precautions.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts air to the carburetor, giving a richer mixture needed for a cold engine. Linkage can stick and the cable can be out of adjustment so the choke doesn't open/close properly.
- fix: remove the air filter housing, find the choke valve, move the choke lever to see it open and close; adjust the cable and lubricate the linkage so the choke is closed when the lever is engaged and open when not.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: oil too thick keeps internal parts from moving freely and affects starting; the right viscosity depends on ambient temperature. Most manufacturers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30, but 5W-30 may be needed in colder temperatures, especially riding mowers used for snow maintenance. Source references a Kawasaki Motors oil viscosity vs temperature chart.
- fix: check the operator's manual; if the wrong oil, drain and refill with the correct oil for outdoor temperature.
- brand: Kawasaki viscosity/temperature chart referenced (not reproduced).

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: all mowers except manual-start recoil push mowers need a battery. Battery can freeze and fail to hold a charge when left in the cold without a good charge. If ambient temperature falls under 20 F and the battery has less than 20% charge it may freeze; colder temps need more charge.
- fact: table (charge / 12 V / 6 V / freezing temp): 40% = 11.97 V / 5.98 V / 20 F; 20% = 11.67 V / 5.84 V / 5 F; zero charge = freezes at 20 F (Source's row as printed; likely a typo, reported as is).
- confirm: multimeter, about 12.7 V; charge if lower; replace if dead or won't hold a charge.
- fact: Source links to a "Winter Lawn Mower Battery Care" guide.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: secure cables and terminals; clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) and a wire brush.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: ethanol (corn-based) and the moisture it attracts leave a sticky substance when it evaporates; gas breaks down as quickly as 30 days after purchase; consume or stabilize.
- fix: remove old fuel with a fuel siphon; add fresh fuel with an additive (clean system, reduce moisture, stabilize), author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: dirty or incorrectly gapped plug can stop a cold start; carbon buildup can be cleaned with a wire brush but author prefers replacing (inexpensive).
- confirm: inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, burnt electrode. Gap to manufacturer spec and attach plug wires securely.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dirt in the engine can cause cylinder scoring and gasket failures. Never run without a filter, even briefly.
- fix (paper): tap to remove dirt; hold to light, reuse if light passes; else replace; reinstall with cover. Carefully remove the filter so dirt doesn't fall into the intake; wipe dirt out of the housing with a clean cloth.
- fix (foam): decide if reusable: dark spots or dry and brittle = replace. If good, wash with mild dish soap and water, rinse, lay flat to dry (outdoors in sun speeds it), then add clean engine oil to lightly saturate (not dripping), install and cover.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: paper vs foam procedures as above.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: typically inline between fuel lines after the tank; replace annually; arrow must point in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, spray carburetor cleaner into the tube, blow compressed air through until clear of dirt and gummy residue; repeat as needed; replace if you can't clear it.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: used when the carburetor sits higher than the tank; fails from old fuel deteriorating the components.
- confirm: inspect the vacuum pump for cracks or fuel outside the pump (cannot create pressure). Use the shut-off valve or pinch pliers to stop/start flow and confirm fuel reaches the pump; then remove the fuel line from the carburetor into a container and start the mower; steady or pulsating flow = good. Replace a bad pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: ethanol and additives form gummy substances clogging small parts.
- fix: clean it if somewhat mechanical or have a repair shop do it; replace if very bad; shop can clean or rebuild.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; wire on solenoid hot, smoking or melting.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence control switches kill the engine when the operator leaves the seat with the deck engaged; a faulty switch may not recognise the operator in or out of the seat so the mower won't start.
- confirm: can temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch; never operate without safety switches installed.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: affects the battery; a bad stator, alternator or other electrical parts can drain the battery; if you must charge it every time you use the mower, suspect the charging system. Test with an ohmmeter (Source links to an article).
- pro: author recommends a lawn mower dealership identify charging-system faults; electrical parts often can't be returned if not the cause.
