## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter, bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Summary list in the Source: old fuel, dirty carburetor, bad fuel pump, dirty spark plug, dead battery, faulty safety switch or bad ignition switch. Many issues are prevented by annual maintenance.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: Fuel breaks down over time, less efficient and prone to clogging the fuel system (also engine damage). Applies to won't start and dies-while-mowing.
- fix: Drain the tank and fill with fresh fuel. Use a fuel additive like Sea Foam to stabilize the fuel and help clean the fuel system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Vent in the cap clogs, tank forms a vacuum restricting fuel flow.
- fix: Clear the vent with a thin piece of wire; replace the cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Excessively dirty or broken plug, bad connection, or not gapped correctly.
- fix: Replace with a new plug, secure connections, gap to manufacturer specification.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Dirt and grass plug the filter, preventing airflow (won't start; dies while mowing; smoke from rich running).
- fix: Remove and clean; replace if in bad condition.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: A dirty filter restricts fuel flow; replace the fuel filter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: Failed or leaking pump no longer creates pressure to pump fuel. Also leaks at seams as it wears.
- fix: Replace the fuel pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Bad fuel deposits and dirt build up and block the line (won't start; dies while mowing).
- fix: Carb cleaner and compressed air to clear the clog; replace line if necessary.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Clogged from bad fuel and ethanol deposits, preventing fuel flow (won't start; dies while mowing).
- fix: Clean all carburetor components; replace if needed.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fix: Charge the battery; replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: Loose cables or corroded terminals cause starting problems.
- fix: Make sure cables are tight and clean corrosion from terminals.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Safety switches prevent starting in certain situations; a failed switch may stop the mower starting.
- fix: Test and replace the faulty switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fix: Mower won't start when the ignition switch is bad; test and replace.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fix: Mower won't start when the coil is bad; test and replace.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: Pull-start mower with damaged or broken recoil won't start the engine.
- fix: Check whether it can be restrung or broken parts like the pulley replaced; replace the recoil assembly if needed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: Ignition switch hums when turning the key.
- fix: Test the solenoid and replace if bad.

## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- fact: Not specifically, but see engine-starts-then-dies / engine-loses-power section below.

## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "lawn mower loses power and dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor
- fact: Dies while mowing when fuel or air filtration is clogged: plugged air filter, dirty carburetor, bad fuel. Also check anything failing to keep the engine cool, like dirt and grass packed around the engine or broken cooling fins.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: Fins packed with dirt, oil and grass don't let air cool the engine block (loses power and dies).
- fix: Replace broken fins and clean the cooling fins.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "lawn mower is smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Smoke develops when the engine runs too rich (burning more fuel than air). Another cause is engine oil burning off due to an oil leak, insufficient oil level, or an internal engine problem.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Too much oil in the crankcase builds pressure, letting oil get into the cylinder through the valve train (smoke).
- fix: Drain a little oil until at the manufacturer's recommended level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Low oil increases friction causing extreme heat (smoke).
- fix: Bring to a small engine repair shop to inspect engine damage. Adding oil may be tried but it is often too late.
- pro: yes, shop for engine damage.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- symptom: Internal problems including the piston ring, valve train or engine gasket cause smoke.
- pro: Take to a local small engine repair shop for troubleshooting; they are harmful if not caught soon.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: See engine-cranks-but-wont-start list; Source does not distinguish crank vs no-crank in its table.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "lawn mower won't move or go forward"
- causes-named: bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper (drive release lever in wrong position; see unmapped), missing-axle-key, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: Hydrostatic transmissions are used in most current riding and zero-turn mowers; service steps differ by type; follow owner's manual.

## page: causes/missing-axle-key
- symptom: A small key in the axle of a riding mower may have fallen out of place. Fix: replace the key in the axle.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- symptom: Drive pump belt fallen off or worn; mower won't move (also weak hydro transmission: pump works inefficiently).
- fix: Replace a worn, frayed or cracked belt; make sure the belt is securely around the pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: Tensioner pulley bearing can fail / pulley fails to keep tension on the drive belt.
- fix: Replace the tensioner pulley (and grease the tensioner arm in the weak-transmission case).

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- symptom: A broken or missing idler arm spring can no longer tension the drive belt. Fix: replace the spring.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- symptom: Low or old oil fails to lubricate the hydraulic system so it seems weak or won't move.
- fix: Change hydraulic oil at manufacturer-recommended intervals; check periodically that it is full.
- fact: Some transmissions are non-serviceable (sealed; oil can't be changed) - take to a repair shop.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- symptom: Hot fluid can be a sign of hydrostatic transmission damage. Runs fine cold but weak when the hydraulic system gets hot.
- fix: Let oil cool. If it behaves that way, have it checked at a local dealership.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- symptom: After changing hydraulic fluid, air must be bled or the mower can't move at normal speeds.
- fix: Follow the manufacturer's procedure for changing fluid and bleeding air (owner's manual).

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "lawn mower won't drive straight"; "mower won't turn left or right"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, loose-steering-linkage
- fact: Table: incorrect tire pressure (unequal pressure pulls to one side; keep each side's tires equal and at manufacturer spec); bad zero-turn dampers (mower jerks to one side moving forward; replace damper); tracking adjustment on zero-turn (when one tire moves faster, adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change tire rotation speed, see owner's manual); worn or loose steering components in a riding mower (worn bushings, washers and gears; inspect and replace failed parts).

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- symptom: Unequal tire pressure pulls to one side; also a low tire can make the mower not sit level, giving uneven cut. Check tire pressure first.

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- symptom: Bad zero-turn dampers jerk the mower to one side moving forward. Fix: replace the damper.

## page: causes/loose-steering-linkage
- symptom: Worn bushings, washers and gears in a riding mower steering system stop it steering one way or the other. Fix: inspect and replace failed parts.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- fix: Zero-turn tracking: adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the rotation speed of the tire (refer to owner's manual).

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "lawn mower is vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- tip: Look the mower over closely; the littlest items can cause a big vibration.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- symptom: Engine bolts reduce vibration when the engine runs. Fix: secure and replace missing mounting bolts.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- symptom: When a bearing fails and falls out of the clutch it causes vibration; a manual-engagement clutch can have a bushing and linkages that fail. Fix: replace the clutch.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Damaged or unbalanced blades send vibration through the mower; blades must be balanced before installing. Replace damaged blades and balance.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- symptom: String or long weeds wrapped around the blade shaft cause vibration. Fix: remove all debris; check for spindle damage.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fix: Replace the bearing in the spindle housing or replace the whole spindle housing assembly.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Debris lodged in the deck or mower; check everywhere small items can lodge. Check deck belt and pulleys for wear and bearing failure; replace damaged or worn belts and pulleys.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "lawn mower has a bad or uneven cut"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-deck-chute, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: Table of reasons: worn blade, bent blade, blade installed wrong, clogged mower deck, uneven tire pressures, unleveled deck, bent blade spindle or spindle bearing, worn deck pulleys or belt, engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, damaged deck shell, operator error. Uneven cut doesn't always mean a deck problem; a low tire can keep the mower from sitting level. Check tire pressure first, then the deck.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "lawn mower is leaking gas"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter (old degraded filter), bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- tip: Leaks are hard to find because fuel evaporates; the only clue may be a strong odor in the garage. Follow the fuel lines starting at the tank and check every fuel system component.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: A metal tank can develop a rust hole; a poly tank can leak at the seams. Fix: replace the tank; a rust hole can sometimes be repaired if no replacement is available.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: Metal and plastic shut-off valves are prone to leaking. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: Rubber fuel lines dry out and crack and leak. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: Primer bulb rubber cracks or isn't attached well to the hose and leaks. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: Seal fails and gas splashes out around the cap. Fix: replace the gas cap.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Leak variant: an old filter degrades and its plastic seams leak. Replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Leak variant: buildup from old gas causes blockages or parts failures; clean and replace damaged parts.

## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- fact: Weak hydrostatic transmission (hydro seems weak) is a separate problem in the Source: causes bad drive belt (replace frayed/cracked/worn belt), bad tensioner pulley (replace, grease the arm), low or old hydraulic oil. See causes/worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, low-hydraulic-oil.
