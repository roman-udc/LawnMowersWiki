## page: causes/old-fuel
- brand: Bad Boy offers gas and diesel mowers; most are gas.
- fact: gasoline can begin to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Most gasoline today includes ethanol (corn-based); ethanol attracts moisture from air, causing gummy buildup and corrosion in the fuel system and engine damage when the solution separates and runs hot.
- fix: use only unleaded gas, minimum 87 octane, max 10 percent ethanol; lower ethanol is better.
- fix: if gas will not be used within 30 days add a fuel stabilizer additive. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment in each tank (opinion).
- fact: diesel also breaks down from air and moisture, forming gummy and solid substances. Bad diesel looks dark and causes corrosion and blockages.
- tip: buy diesel at a busy station that is busy with diesel customers (author bought from a busy station and found a black fuel filter later). Store diesel away from moisture, light and heat; add a stabilizer such as Sea Foam.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: filter strains fuel before it enters the fuel system/engine.
- confirm: check filter for blockage; plugged or dirty/dark means replace.
- fix: when installing a new filter, the arrow on its side must point in the fuel-flow direction, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: gummy residue from old fuel blocks lines (problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start).
- confirm: stop/start fuel flow using the fuel shut-off valve or hose pinch pliers, checking each section. To test a section: stop flow, remove the end furthest from the tank, put it in a container, turn on fuel and watch for flow.
- tip: container must be lower than the tank; fuel cannot run uphill without a fuel pump.
- fix: remove the restricted section, spray carburetor cleaner into the hose, then blow compressed air through; repeat as needed. Replace the line if the clog will not clear or the line is dry and cracking.
- tools: fuel shutoff valve or hose pinch pliers, container, carb cleaner, compressed air.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- brand: Bad Boy uses a vacuum-style fuel pump (builds pressure off the engine) because the carburetor sits higher than the tank.
- confirm: look for leaks (fuel degrades the pump over time); replace if leaking.
- step 1: confirm fuel reaches the pump inlet line.
- step 2: shut off fuel supply and remove the fuel line from the carburetor; put it in a container.
- step 3: open fuel flow, start the mower, watch for flow. Steady or pulsating flow means the pump is fine; otherwise replace the pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old fuel leaves deposits that make carb components stick or clog.
- confirm: first rule out line, filter and pump restrictions; then pull the air filter, spray carb cleaner into the air intake and start the mower. If it starts, runs, then shuts off, the restriction is most likely in the carburetor.
- fix: disassemble and clean; or take to a small engine repair shop.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: tank vents through the gas cap or rollover vents; a clogged vent makes the tank act like a vacuum and fuel stops flowing.
- confirm: mower runs fine with cap removed but stalls after running a while with cap installed.
- fix: determine which vent type you have (cap or rollover vent) and replace it.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gasoline can degrade as soon as 30 days; use stabilizer if unused in 30 days; diesel stored away from moisture, light, heat.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol.
