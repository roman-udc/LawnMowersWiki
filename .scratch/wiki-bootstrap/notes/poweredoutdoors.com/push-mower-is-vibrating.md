## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "push mower shakes my arms"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage (engagement lever, loose/worn), bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, dull-or-bent-blade (bent blade), unbalanced blade, bent-crankshaft, sheared-flywheel-key, loose hardware
- safety: never work under the deck without ensuring the mower can't start; remove spark plug wire.
- fact: most "normal" push mower vibration is the engine, absorbed by engine mounting bolts (motor mounts).
- fix: engine bolts: tighten; replace missing/worn/broken.
- fix: engagement lever (engages the blade): check for loose/worn parts, tighten, replace worn.
- fact: loose hardware and worn parts elsewhere can vibrate; check.

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- symptom: a significant impact with a stationary object, or a series of smaller impacts, damages the blade adapter; adapter also wears from a damaged crankshaft, wrong blade installed, or unbalanced blade.
- fix: replace worn/broken adapter; check crankshaft for damage; install new blades too when replacing the adapter.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- confirm (bent): park on flat level surface; measure ground to blade tip; rotate blade 180 degrees and measure from same ground spot to blade tip; more than 1/8 inch variance means replace.
- confirm: or remove the blade and match to a new one; gaps remain means bent.
- mistake: don't straighten and reuse a bent blade; straightening weakens the metal, prone to cracking, and a cracked piece can fly out at high speed.
- symptom (unbalanced): heavier on one end wobbles and vibrates. Causes: uneven wear (dirt drawn into the deck); incorrect sharpening removing more metal from one end.
- fix: check with a blade balancer; remove a little metal from the heavier end. Without a balancer: drive a nail into the wall with the head about 1 inch from the wall, hang the blade by center hole; balanced blade sits level; if not, remove a little metal from the side that hangs lower until level.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- step 1: flat level surface; measure ground to blade tip.
- step 2: rotate blade 180 degrees, measure from the same ground spot.
- step 3: more than 1/8" variance: replace blade.

## page: causes/bent-crankshaft
- confirm: spark plug removed, clamp holding the safety lever to the handle to disable the flywheel brake; carefully spin the blade and watch the center bolt; if it moves all over the place, crankshaft most likely bent. Protect hands from sharp blade.
- confirm: if unsure remove blade and blade adapter, turn the crankshaft and measure that it stays centered (dial indicator or a stationary object as gauge).
- fix: if it shifts off-center it's bent; replacing the crankshaft usually costs more than a new mower. Bending it back is a significant safety risk (author highly recommends buying a new mower).

## page: causes/sheared-flywheel-key
- confirm: remove the nut off the top of the crankshaft to inspect the flywheel; small key in the keyway; replace if damaged.

## page: procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key
- step 1: remove the nut on top of the crankshaft; inspect the key in the keyway; replace if damaged. (Source gives no more detail.)
