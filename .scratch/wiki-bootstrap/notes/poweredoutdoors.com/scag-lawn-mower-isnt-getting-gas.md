## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Scag mower isn't getting gas"
- alias: "Scag running rough and sluggish, no fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: lack of fuel makes mower run rough and sluggish, or not start/run at all

## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- fact: same fuel-system causes (see above)

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: ethanol attracts moisture; water/ethanol leave gummy deposits, corrode fuel components; gas breaks down in as little as 30 days
- brand: Scag: unleaded, min octane 87, max ethanol 10% (sold E10 or regular); read pump label
- fix: siphon old fuel with fuel siphon pump into approved container to recycle; add fresh fuel with stabilizer (Sea Foam Motor Treatment)

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: replace annually, more often with dirty fuel
- fix: shut off fuel, remove filter, install new with arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow, between fuel lines

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: check each section of hose; stop flow with shut-off valve or pinch pliers; remove end furthest from tank into a container; start flow and confirm good flow; reattach
- fix: fuel supply off; remove section; carb cleaner then compressed air; repeat until clear; replace with same length and diameter if cannot clear or line dry and cracked

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump needed since carburetor sits higher than tank; most Scag use vacuum-style pump (pressure off crankcase); some models use electric pump; electric pump can fail from lack of power: bad fuse, relay or module; use multimeter to verify power/connection before testing pump
- confirm: verify fuel to pump; remove line from carburetor into container, start fuel flow and mower; steady or pulsating flow expected; none = replace pump
- confirm (electric): measure fuel pressure with a pressure gauge against operator's manual spec; replace if below spec
- fact: cracked or failing pump must be replaced

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: verify fuel reaches carburetor (else check line, filter, pump); remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake, start; if it starts but won't keep running, remove and clean carburetor

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap vent equalizes tank pressure; plugged vent makes vacuum
- confirm: remove cap and run; if it runs, put on a new cap with mower running and let run a while; if sluggish and dies, then restarts with cap removed, cap likely bad
- fix: replace gas cap
