## page: problems/battery-mower-wont-start
- alias: "Toro electric battery mower won't start"
- causes-named: bad-battery (insufficient power), battery-not-seated, bad-charger (bad connection with charger/outlet), overheated-battery, weak-or-dead-battery, incorrect-starting-procedure, bad-safety-switch, bad-wiring-or-ground, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, mowing-too-fast / overloaded
- safety: Remove safety button (key) and battery before repairs; precautions for electric currents and sharp blades. If under Toro warranty, consult Toro before repairs; wiring repairs and modifications outside an authorized Toro repair center can void all warranties.
- fact: Intro: may not start due to insufficient power supply, bad electrical connection, overworking the motor, or incorrect operating procedure.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fact: Use a battery compatible with the mower; wrong size may fail to start and cause more severe electrical problems.
- symptom: fully charged but doesn't hold charge as long as before; charges very slowly; gets extremely hot. Replace.
- fact: Batteries fail when old, exposed to extreme temperatures, or fail mechanically.

## page: causes/battery-not-seated
- fix: Wipe the battery compartment lid before opening (keep dirt out). Remove and reinstall: battery slides into guide rails and snaps into place; lid must close securely; good contact.

## page: causes/bad-charger
- fact: Toro batteries are removed and charged in an external charging station made for that battery. Check outlet and cord connections; use standard 120V household outlet with sufficient power; plug style must match outlet (don't modify; 3-prong into 3-prong). Clean vents; keep charger out of extreme heat or cold.
- fact: Charger LED codes (refer to charger manual for your model): blinking green = charging; solid green = charged; blinking red = battery may be defective; solid red = too cold or too hot, move charger to location without extreme temperature.

## page: causes/overheated-battery
- fact: Hot battery shuts down the mower and it won't start. Clean mower vents; let battery cool before restarting.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Safety key/button prevents children from accidentally pressing the safety handle. 
- step 1: Ensure battery securely in place and safety button inserted.
- step 2: Pull bail (safety bar) toward the handle and press safety button to start the motor.
- step 3: Release the safety button.
- step 4: Mower shuts off when you release the safety bar.
- brand: Toro push mower and self-propelled mower.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- confirm: Test each switch with multimeter or temporarily bypass with a jumper wire. Replace bad switch.
- fact: Switches on Toro push/self-propelled: safety bar/bail lever switch (bar must be pulled to handle to run; release shuts off); momentary switch for on/off button; check manual for others.
- safety: Never operate without all safety switches working.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: Many wires run through the mower and along the handle; loose or pinched wire can short. Wiggle wires to find a short; use a multimeter. Common pinch point near the handle fold; keep wires on outside of handle when folding.
- fix: Repair pinched wire with a wire nut after removing battery and safety button.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Grass lodged under deck can inhibit blade rotation, causing no-start. Remove battery and key first.
- fact: Overload: cutting thick, tall or wet grass at once; raise cutting height, slow down, avoid wet grass.

## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- fact: Electric mower needs less maintenance (no oil changes) but not problem-free.
- fact: Don't use an electric mower in the rain: water can short it out and prevent starting.
- pro: If nothing found (motor or other parts may be damaged), authorized Toro service center.

## page: guides/mower-got-wet
- fact: Water can short out electric mower and not start.
