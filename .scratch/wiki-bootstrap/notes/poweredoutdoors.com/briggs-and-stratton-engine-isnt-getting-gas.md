## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "engine isn't getting gas, acts sluggish almost like it's going to die"
- alias: "Briggs engine sputters"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plenty of fuel in tank but engine not getting enough gas.
- safety: Work in a well-ventilated area, wear goggles and gloves, remove the spark plug wire before repairs.
- brand: Briggs & Stratton.

## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: Old fuel can make the engine sputter or not start because it isn't getting gas (same six causes as above).

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can begin to break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase; use within this time. Old gas leaves varnish and gummy deposits causing restrictions and component failure.
- fix: Drain the tank into an approved fuel container; refill with fresh fuel containing an additive such as Sea Foam (acts as fuel cleaner and reduces moisture).
- tip: Add Sea Foam or STA-BIL to fresh fuel to keep it stable.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Filter is a maintenance item to replace at least annually. A plugged filter restricts fuel.
- fix: Replace with a Briggs and Stratton fuel filter; install with the arrow on the plastic housing pointing in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Gummy deposits from old fuel or dirt restrict or fully block the line.
- confirm: Use the fuel shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers to start/stop flow while checking a section.
- fix: Replace the line if it cannot be cleared; also a good time to replace a dry, cracking line before it leaks.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- fact: Find and remove a restriction (clearing procedure):
- step 1: Stop fuel flow with the shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers.
- step 2: Remove the end of the fuel line furthest from the tank and put it in a container placed lower than the tank so fuel runs downhill.
- step 3: Restart fuel flow.
- step 4: If flow into the container is poor, stop flow and remove the fuel line from the mower.
- step 5: Spray carburetor cleaner into the hose and blow the line out with compressed air; repeat until open.
- step 6: Reattach the line.
- step 7: If it can't be cleared, replace the fuel line.
- tools: pinch-off pliers, carburetor cleaner, compressed air, container.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: A plastic or metal fuel pump is fitted when the tank sits lower than the carburetor. A vacuum pump builds pressure off the crankcase to push fuel up to the carb.
- symptom: Cracks, damage or leaks: replace.
- confirm: First confirm fuel reaches the pump inlet port (the fuel line check). Then remove the fuel line from the carburetor, put it in a container, start the mower and watch for steady or pulsating fuel flow. No good flow: replace the pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carb regulates air/fuel mixture to the cylinder; mounted on top or side of engine block, usually below or behind the air filter. Dirty carb can clog/stick components including the fuel jet.
- confirm: Remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, start the engine. If it starts but won't keep running, remove and disassemble the carb for cleaning.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Tank vents through the fuel cap; failed vent makes a vacuum that stops fuel reaching the engine.
- confirm: Run the mower with and without the cap. If it runs well with the cap loose/removed but runs sluggish or dies after reinstalling the cap and running a while, the cap is bad. Replace a bad gas cap.
