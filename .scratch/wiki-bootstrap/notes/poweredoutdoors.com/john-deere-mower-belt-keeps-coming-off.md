## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "John Deere mower belt keeps coming off"
- alias: "deck belt keeps breaking / shredding"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Belt falls off when worn or shredded; bad bearing in spindle housing or pulley; worn brackets and springs; debris on top of the deck.
- safety: Remove the key and disconnect spark plug wires; wear gloves for sharp edges.
- tip: Extend belt life: clean the deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off the belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix or replace damaged brackets or belt keepers.
- fact: Deck belts can be pricey so longer life is better.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- confirm: Belt should sit tight on pulleys and not look shiny or cracked; if so replace.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Belt guides are metal rods next to pulleys; belt doesn't actually touch them; added protection against slipping off. Slack in the belt hitting a guide bends it so it may no longer retain the belt.
- fix: Bend the guide back with a hammer or vice grip.
- symptom: Shredded belt edges - belt rubs on a guide or bracket, or is routed wrong. Check routing against the owner's manual or an online parts diagram; then find the rubbing bracket/guide (smooth or shiny spot on the metal) and adjust/repair it. Some brackets bend into place; others have a bushing needing replacement and may only be sold as a bracket assembly; check with the John Deere dealer for available parts.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: Bad pulley bearing means the pulley doesn't sit flat/parallel to the deck; belt runs wrong and comes off.
- confirm: Rotate each pulley slowly by hand; restriction or noise means replace.
- fact: Some pulleys allow bearing replacement; idler pulleys have a sealed bearing so replace the whole pulley.
- fact: Debris or mud in the pulley grooves makes the belt stretch around it, stressing the belt so it rolls off or snaps; clean the grooves.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Gloved, grab each end of the blade and rock it up and down; movement or knocking noise suggests a bad bearing.
- fix: Take the spindle housing apart, inspect and replace the bearing if needed.
- fact: A bad spindle bearing causes significant deck vibration with blades engaged, which can throw the belt.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Idler tensioner bracket holds the idler pulleys in line; typically a pulley on one side and a spring on the other. Weak spring lets the belt come off. The spring attachment hole in the bracket can wear larger causing extra vibration. Replace the worn bracket or spring.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Debris under pulleys and tensioner stops the tensioner moving to keep belt tension; belt loosens and falls off.
- safety: Debris on top of the deck is also a fire hazard: dry debris plus belt/pulley heat from friction can start a fire on the deck.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil left on a belt a long time makes it swell and slip off. Oil comes from an engine oil leak dripping onto belt/deck. Rust from old pulleys/brackets dries out the belt so it cracks and may break.
- fix: Remove rust from pulleys/brackets; replace very rusty pulleys; check oil level before each mowing (low oil may mean a leak); repair leaks; then replace the deck belt.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- fact: Failed pulley and spindle housing bearings, an unlevel deck and worn blades can cause uneven cut on a John Deere (Source points to another article).
