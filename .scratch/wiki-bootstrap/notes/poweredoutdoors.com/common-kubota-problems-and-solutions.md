## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Kubota lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Start by looking for an air or fuel restriction: plugged air filter, sufficient fuel, clogs in fuel components, plugged fuel filter. Old gas run through the mower can cause clogged/failing fuel components. Then check spark plugs and ignition coil (no spark = no start), then battery, safety switches, charging system; make sure all wires secure.
- fix: no gas -> fill with fresh fuel. Old fuel -> drain tank, fill fresh, use fuel additive like Sea Foam to stabilize and help clean fuel system. Clogged fuel cap vent -> clear with thin wire, replace cap if needed. Bad spark plug (dirty/broken/bad connection/not gapped) -> replace, secure connections, gap to manufacturer spec. Plugged air filter -> remove and clean, replace if bad. Plugged fuel filter -> replace. Bad fuel pump (failed or leaking, no longer creates pressure) -> replace. Blocked fuel lines (bad fuel deposits/dirt) -> carb cleaner and compressed air, replace line if necessary. Dirty carburetor (bad fuel, ethanol deposits) -> clean components, replace if needed. Bad battery/loose or dirty terminals -> charge, replace if won't hold charge, tighten cables, clean corrosion from terminals. Bad safety switch -> test and replace. Bad ignition switch or coil -> test and replace.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Kubota starts and dies", "dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, overfilled-oil, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, faulty-charging-system, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Many starting causes also cause dying: engine can't get air, fuel and spark so it runs sluggish and may quit. Overheating can kill it: use correct oil, maintain level, clean cooling fins and around engine. Electrical system or clogged deck can cause dying after running; check charging system, switches, ignition coil, spark plugs.
- fix: old fuel breaks down and clogs fuel system. Cooling fins packed with dirt, oil, grass don't cool block -> clean fins, replace broken fins. Fuel line clog -> carb cleaner and compressed air; replace plugged fuel filter. Carburetor dirty (ethanol deposits) -> clean all components, replace if needed. Too much oil raises crankcase pressure and overheats -> remove a little oil. Ignition coil windings separate and short -> check continuity with ohm meter, replace if bad; clean/replace plugs, secure plug wires, gap plug correctly. Choke: used for cold starts; if it keeps restricting airflow after warm-up engine shuts off -> make sure choke in correct position. Battery that won't hold charge or bad charging system -> test battery and charging system, replace bad parts. Gas cap vent clogged -> replace cap that no longer vents. Clogged deck strains engine -> scrape deck periodically.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket, spilled-oil-on-engine
- fact: Clogged air filter or incorrect choke restricts airflow, engine runs rich, more fuel burned = black smoke. Insufficient oil or internal problems (piston ring failure, valve train, gasket failure) also smoke. Oil dripping on muffler or spilled on engine during an oil change burns off and smokes.
- fix: Plugged air filter -> clean or replace. Too much oil -> drain a little until at manufacturer's recommended level (excess pressure forces oil into cylinder through valve train). Too little oil -> friction causes extreme heat; may be too late; take to small engine repair shop to inspect damage; adding oil may not fix it. Internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) -> local small engine repair shop.
- pro: Internal engine problems -> small engine repair shop.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Kubota mower won't move or go forward"
- causes-named: drive-release-lever-wrong-position (unmapped), missing-axle-key, worn-or-stretched-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring (tensioner pulley), worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fix: Drive release lever (lets hydrostatic mowers roll freely) must be in operating position. Missing key in axle -> replace the key. Drive pump belt fallen off, worn or cracked -> replace, make sure securely around pulleys. Tensioner pulley bearing failed -> replace tensioner pulley. Idler arm spring broken/missing (no tension on drive belt) -> replace. Low/old hydraulic oil -> let hydraulic oil cool; if mower runs fine cold but weak when hydraulic system gets hot, have it checked at local Kubota dealership. Hot hydraulic fluid can signal damage to hydrostatic system -> follow Kubota procedures for changing fluid and bleeding air (owner's manual). Air in hydraulic system -> must be bled after changing fluid or mower won't move at normal speed; follow Kubota's owner's manual procedure.
- pro: Fluid-temperature-related weakness -> Kubota dealer.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "won't drive straight or steer correctly", "pulling to the right or left"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, loose-steering-linkage
- fix: Unequal tire pressure pulls to one side (source's solution cell says to adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change tire rotation speed; see owner's manual). Bad dampers on zero-turn jerk to one side when moving forward -> replace damper. Tracking adjustment needed on zero-turn: when one tire moves faster than the other mower pulls to one side. Worn or loose steering components in riding mower: worn bushings, washers, gears -> inspect and replace failed steering parts.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Can begin when clutch bearing fails; engine mounting bolts worn; blades unbalanced; spindle or pulley bearings failed; deck belt worn; debris lodged. "Don't overlook the small stuff": an acorn or stone stuck in the mower can cause significant vibration.
- fix: Loose/missing engine mounting bolts (bolts reduce vibration) -> secure, replace missing. Bad clutch (bearing failing and falling out of clutch; manual-engagement clutch can have bushing/linkage failures) -> replace the clutch. Unbalanced/damaged blades -> replace damaged blades, ensure balanced before installing. String or long weeds wrapped around blade shaft -> remove all, check spindle damage. Bad spindle housing bearing -> replace bearing or the whole spindle housing assembly. Worn pulleys or deck belt -> replace damaged parts. Debris lodged -> check deck and mower and remove.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- causes-named: worn blade (dull-or-bent-blade), blade installed wrong (blade-installed-upside-down), clogged deck (debris-on-deck-or-pulleys), wrong-tire-pressure, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt (deck pulleys/belt), wrong-engine-speed (too low), mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell (damaged deck shell), operator error / incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: Kubota decks normally give a nice cut. Operator error: not overlapping paths correctly, fast ground speed or low engine speed. Source's table lists: worn/bent blade, blade installed wrong, clogged deck, uneven tire pressures, unlevel deck, bent spindle or spindle bearing, worn deck pulleys or belt, engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, damaged deck shell, operator error.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Gas evaporates so the leak can be hard to find. Leak points: fuel filter, fuel pump, fuel lines, fuel shut-off valve, fuel cap.
- fix: Dirty/failed carburetor (buildup from old gas, blockages or part failures) -> clean and replace damaged parts. Old fuel filter plugged -> replace. Bad fuel pump wears, leaks at seams -> replace. Bad fuel tank: metal tank can develop rust hole; poly tank can leak at seams -> replace; sometimes a rust hole can be repaired if no replacement tank is available. Shut-off valve (metal and plastic prone to leaking) -> replace. Rubber fuel lines dry and crack -> replace. Gas cap seal fails and gas splashes out around cap -> replace gas cap.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- fact: Overheating can cause dying; use correct oil, maintain level, clean cooling fins.
- causes-named: dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Low or old hydraulic oil fails to lubricate the hydrostatic system, making it seem weak. Change at intervals recommended by Kubota; check level periodically. Some transmission systems are sealed and can't be serviced; see dealer.

## page: causes/bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: Weak hydrostatic transmission causes listed: bad drive belt (pump works inefficiently; replace frayed/cracked/worn belt), bad tensioner pulley (fails to keep belt tension; replace pulley and grease the tensioner arm), low or old hydraulic oil.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel breaks down over time, less efficient, prone to clogging the fuel system. Drain tank, fill fresh, use fuel additive such as Sea Foam to stabilize and help clean the fuel system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cap vent can clog, forming vacuum in the tank and restricting flow. Use a thin piece of wire to clear it; replace cap if needed.

## page: causes/missing-axle-key
- fact: Riding mower may have a small key in the axle that can fall out; replace it. (mower won't move)

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: Broken or missing idler arm spring can no longer tension the drive belt; replace.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: Must bleed air after changing hydraulic fluid or mower can't move at normal speeds; follow Kubota owner's manual procedure.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: Can be a sign of damage to the hydrostatic system; follow Kubota's fluid change/bleeding procedures.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Windings can separate and short out. Check continuity with an ohm meter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: Listed as internal problem causing smoke; take to repair shop.
