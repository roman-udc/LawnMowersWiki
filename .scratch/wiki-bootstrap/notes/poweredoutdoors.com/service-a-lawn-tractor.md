## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: a full service includes changing engine oil and filter; replacing fuel filter, air filter and spark plugs; replacing hydraulic fluid if required; replacing or sharpening blades; scraping the deck and removing debris. Source gives 21 steps.
- tools: socket or spanner wrenches, filter wrench, rubber gloves, work gloves, safety glasses, plastic funnel, rags/paper towel, deck scraper or wire brush, oil drain pan, tire pressure gauge, torque not mentioned
- parts: engine oil filter, engine oil, spark plugs, inner and outer air filter, fuel filter, mower blades if not reusing, fuel stabilizer if storing
- step 1: gather tools and materials.
- step 2: park on a flat level surface; put cardboard or newspaper under the tractor; run a few minutes to warm the oil (warm oil drains better); shut off, remove key, disconnect spark plug wires (gas), set parking brake; follow owner's manual safety.
- step 3: change oil and filter (see procedures/change-the-engine-oil).
- step 4: remove old plugs with a 3/4" or 5/8" socket (gas only; diesel has no spark plugs); replace; don't attach plug wires until service is complete so it can't start accidentally.
- step 5: remove dirt and debris from cooling fins and engine area.
- step 6: clean debris from hydro fans and transmission area; residential tractor hydrostatic transmissions are most likely sealed and non-serviceable (confirm in owner's manual); follow manual if it permits changing hydraulic oil and filters. Hydro fans keep the transmission cool.
- step 7: clean battery terminals (baking soda solution, 2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda, wire brush); check it is fully charged, trickle charge if not; replace a battery that won't hold a charge.
- step 8: remove debris around electrical components and wiring (debris holds moisture and corrodes).
- step 9: replace air filters (author replaces inner and outer); start each season with new, clean several times.
- step 10: replace the fuel filter; install the inline filter with the arrow in the direction of fuel flow.
- step 11: check the fuel system for leaks: hoses secure, no leaks from punctures, cracks, bad seals or degrading plastics; replace dry cracked lines.
- step 12: check tire pressure with a gauge; uneven pressure makes the deck unlevel and a bad cut; a low tire can cause steering issues; confirm lug nuts are secure.
- step 13: remove the mower deck for cleaning/service (per owner's manual; varies by make and model).
- step 14: clean dirt and debris from the top of the deck, including under belt covers; debris restricts the tensioner bracket and belt movement; dry debris plus belt friction heat is a fire hazard.
- step 15: check pulleys and belts: replace the belt if glazed/shiny, cracked, stretched or shredded; rotate pulleys by hand, they should sit parallel to the deck and spin smoothly; replace if resistance or bearing noise; clean rust off pulleys or replace if very rusty (rust prematurely dries out the belt).
- step 16: scrape the deck and check blades: rock each blade up and down to check spindle bearings (movement or knocking - remove the spindle housing and replace bad bearings or the housing assembly); replace blades with a new set, or sharpen and balance reused blades; scrape clippings and dirt with a deck scraper or metal brush (a clogged deck hurts cut and makes the engine work harder; moisture corrodes the deck).
- step 17: reinstall the deck (reverse order) and check level (see procedures/level-the-deck).
- step 18: check the safety system per owner's manual.
- step 19: replace damaged, worn or missing parts; all hardware secure.
- step 20: grease front wheels, front axle and deck wheels (a grease zerk on each); lubricate lift linkages, pivot points and brake; author uses high-performance grease and a spray lubricant.
- step 21: wash with a garden hose; if using a pressure washer do not use high pressure (damages sealed bearings); let dry completely (sun, or a leaf blower); reattach spark plug wires if the mower must be moved.
- tip: off-season storage - remove fuel or add stabilizer; fully charge the battery or remove it and store cool and dry.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: tractors have either a drain plug or a drain hose; author prefers a drain hose (easier, fewer spills; cap twists off); engine manufacturers may offer one that fits the drain port, installable after draining.
- step 1: put an oil drain pan under the plug or hose.
- step 2: remove the drain plug and let oil drain.
- step 3: remove the dipstick so oil flows faster.
- step 4: reinstall the drain plug once fully drained.
- step 5: remove the oil filter with a filter wrench; put a light film of fresh oil on the new filter's seal; install.
- step 6: add new oil to the correct level using the dipstick; don't overfill (hurts the engine; remove a little if you do).
- step 7: wipe spilled oil off (spilled oil burns off and smokes when hot); dispose of used oil at a recycling center or auto parts store that accepts used oil.
- tools: oil drain pan, filter wrench, funnel

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- step 1 (side-to-side): deck on a flat level surface, deck lift in the middle position; measure from the ground to the blade tip on the right side and on the left; if different, follow owner's manual leveling instructions (vary by model).
- step 2 (pitch front-to-rear): on a flat level surface, lift in the middle position, blade parallel to the mower; measure ground to blade tip at front and rear. Most tractor decks sit slightly lower in front, about 1/4" to 3/8" lower than the rear; verify the owner's manual spec.
