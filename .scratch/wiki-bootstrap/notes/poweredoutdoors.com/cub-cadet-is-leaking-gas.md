## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "storage area smells of gas but I can't find a leak"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, bad-gas-cap-seal, cracked-primer-bulb
- fact: Cub Cadet can leak from carburetor, fuel cap, fuel lines, fuel filter, fuel pump, fuel tank, fuel shut-off valve, and primer bulb. Gas evaporates so small leaks are hard to spot.
- confirm: Check order summary: fuel tank, cap and shut-off valve first, then follow fuel lines out of the tank and check every component fuel passes through: filter, fuel pump, carburetor.
- confirm (gas cap): if you smell gas but find no source, shake the mower to slosh gas up to the cap and watch for a wet spot around the cap; if so replace cap. Gas may splash and evaporate before a wet area is noticed.
- tip: Non-ethanol (recreational) fuel minimizes problems from old fuel.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: (problems/mower-is-leaking-gas) Leak at carburetor bowl: a very thin o-ring (looks like a rubber band) between the base of the carburetor and the bowl dries and fails to seal; heat cycling hot to cold makes it hard.
- fix: Get engine make, model and spec number and take to local Cub Cadet dealer for the correct o-ring gasket. Shut fuel off at shut-off valve (or use clamps to stop fuel flow if no valve); unscrew the screw at the bottom of the carb and drop the bowl; replace gasket; reinstall bowl and tighten screw; turn fuel back on; wipe the bowl and inspect for leaks.
- symptom: Stuck float: fuel leaking from the air intake port; the float regulates gas in the bowl; if stuck it can't shut off fuel so gas keeps flowing out. Take carb apart to find why float is stuck.
- symptom: Stuck float needle: gently tap the side of the carburetor with a soft mallet or hammer handle end to free it; short-term fix only, eventually rebuild the carburetor.
- pro: If not mechanically inclined have a repair shop fix a stuck float; author has a separate Cub Cadet carburetor cleaning article.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: Follow lines from tank; dry cracked lines must be replaced. Check where hose is secured by hose clamp: many manufacturers use pinch-style clamps that damage hoses faster than worm-gear clamps; consider replacing pinch clamps with worm-gear style.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: Small rust hole on metal tank with age; molded plastic tank seams can separate. Fix: replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: Valve on bottom of the fuel tank starts/stops fuel flow; plastic and metal both prone to leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: Plastic inline filter left unchanged past normal intervals becomes weak and soft; check for cracks; replace. Bad gasoline is corrosive and damages the filter element.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: Most mower fuel pumps are plastic and deteriorate from bad gas; gas sitting for years rots the pump; diaphragm and seams may leak.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: Cap and seal failing; wet spot around cap when mower is shaken. Fix: replace cap.

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: Some smaller Cub Cadet mowers have a primer bulb that can fill with fuel and leak, or dry out and crack; replace when it no longer works and seeps gas.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Cub Cadet best fuel is non-ethanol, but regular gas is OK: unleaded, octane 87 or greater, no more than 10% ethanol.
