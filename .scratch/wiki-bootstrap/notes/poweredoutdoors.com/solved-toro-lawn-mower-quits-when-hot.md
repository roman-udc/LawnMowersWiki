## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- alias: "Toro lawn mower quits when hot"
- alias: "dies after running for a while"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- safety: Wait for the engine to cool; remove ignition key (if used) and spark plug wires.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Extremely dirty filter blocks fresh air; engine shuts down and may overheat.
- tip: Start each season with a new filter; check regularly; clean/replace more often for commercial, heavy or very dusty use.
- safety: NEVER run a mower without an air filter. Do not clean a paper filter with compressed air (damages it).
- step 1: Remove cover.
- step 2: Carefully remove filter so loose dirt doesn't fall into intake.
- step 3: Wipe housing without letting debris into intake.
- step 4: Tap paper element on a solid surface.
- step 5: Hold to light; reuse if light shows; replace if no light, wet, damaged or very dirty.
- step 6: Install and reattach cover.
- fact: Foam pre-filter (used with some paper filters; don't confuse with a primary foam filter; DO NOT oil it): inspect for tears, brittleness or dark spots (replace if present); wash with mild detergent and water; rinse until water runs clear and soap gone; squeeze (don't wring, may tear); lay flat to dry; install once dry.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture; the ethanol-water mix evaporates leaving sticky residue that clogs the fuel system, and the mix separates and sinks to the bottom of the tank; can make the engine quit.
- brand: Toro mowers require unleaded gasoline, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; lower ethanol better, ethanol-free best.
- fix: Drain the tank into an approved fuel container with a siphon pump; refill with fresh fuel plus additive (author recommends Sea Foam Motor Treatment).
- brand: Old Toro push mowers may use gas and two-cycle oil mix (see push mower gas article).

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old gas varnish clogs passageways and makes small parts stick; lack of fuel makes the mower quit.
- confirm: Confirm fuel reaches the carburetor; remove air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into intake and start; if it runs a bit then shuts down, disassemble and clean.
- fix: Clean it (links to Toro carburetor cleaning article) or have a repair shop do it.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Best is air-cooled engine oil with high zinc concentration (cooling agent). Cars cool with liquid; small engines with air. Most small-engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; 20W-50 for higher ambient temperatures. Use the Kawasaki chart and operator's manual. Wrong oil can overheat the engine and shut it down.
- fix: Drain wrong oil and refill with appropriate fresh air-cooled oil.
- fact: Low oil: check before each use; friction builds heat; oil and parts can burn and cause significant damage.
- pro: Once the mower shut down from low oil, extensive damage is most likely; have a small engine mechanic test; may need engine replacement.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Excess oil raises crankcase pressure and makes crankshaft and rod push through oil, putting load on internals so the engine gets hot and quits. Oil pushed into the cylinder via the valve train burns as thick smoke, which can plug the air filter and starve the engine of air.
- fix: Disconnect spark plug wire(s) first. Drain a little through the drain plug, oil filter or oil fill hole; or use an oil evacuator or turkey baster at the fill. Check dipstick and adjust. Check the air filter for smoke plugging. Reattach plug wire(s).
- pro: If problems continue, have an engine mechanic check.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins help dissipate heat from block and cylinder; clean annually; overheated engine quits.
- fix: Remove debris around fins, replace broken fins, remove dirt around the engine shroud, make sure the heat shield is firmly in place.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Carbon buildup, broken porcelain or burnt electrode can let a plug start the mower but not keep it running.
- fix: Clean tip if needed; replace if extremely dark or damaged; make sure plug wires are securely attached.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: When hot, the coil can stop working; windings separate and short out; insufficient voltage to the plug.
- confirm: Ohmmeter test for a break in continuity; replace if bad.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plugged vent makes a vacuum so fuel can't flow to carburetor; mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: Run with and without the cap; if it shuts off with the cap on but runs fine without, suspect the cap.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap; author replaces with a new cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Packed deck makes engine work harder; dull blades magnify it.
- fix: Scrape the deck periodically, sharpen blades, don't mow wet.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull blades magnify the plugged-deck load on the engine.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
