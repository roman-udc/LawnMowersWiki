## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Bad Boy zero turn won't turn over"
- alias: "nothing happens when I turn the key"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- symptom: engine won't even turn over; look for items keeping the starter motor from getting power
- brand: Bad Boy zero-turn
- safety: always remove the black (negative) battery cable before starting repairs on the electrical system
- safety: follow the operator's manual
- pro: if unsure how to diagnose safely, have the Bad Boy dealer or repair shop do it

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: weak or dead battery won't give power to turn the engine over
- tip: keep zero-turn battery fully charged, especially in cold; an uncharged battery stored in cold can freeze
- fix: if the battery fails to hold a charge replace it; bring the old battery to avoid a core fee
- fact: article points to its own battery-drain article for testing

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: 12-volt battery charger; screwdriver to uncover battery; protective gear for eyes and skin
- step 1: access the battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover); do not remove the battery from the casing
- step 2: connect the charging cables, positive first (red / plus sign) onto the positive terminal
- step 3: attach the negative cable (black / minus sign) to the negative terminal
- step 4: do not touch anything that is not rubber-coated, to prevent electrocution
- step 5: set charger voltage and amperage; lawn mower batteries are usually 12 volts; more amps charges faster; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge is best
- safety: wear protective gear

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: wires and components vibrate loose from constant mower vibration and lose continuity; corrosion also affects continuity
- fix: if corrosion found, remove battery cables first, then the corroded components; clean with wire brush and baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); replace terminals if corrosion is too severe

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: check wiring and components are securely attached so there is no break in continuity; check for corrosion
- fact: a bad ground can make the starter solenoid fail

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- symptom: no crank; fuse protects the electrical system
- confirm: place multimeter probes on each prong of the fuse and measure resistance; near 0 = good; infinity = bad
- fix: replace with a fuse of the same capacity
- pro: if fuses keep blowing, take it to a dealer or repair shop to find root cause

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: set multimeter to resistance, probe each prong of the fuse
- step 2: near 0 = good, infinity = blown
- step 3: replace with same-capacity fuse

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: nothing happens when key inserted and turned; won't turn over or start
- confirm: multimeter on resistance; find the prongs marked "B" (battery) and "S" (starter solenoid); insert key and turn to start; one probe on B, other on S; good switch reads near 0 ohms; bad reads infinite resistance and needs replacing

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: engine fails to turn over; defective operator-presence switch
- confirm: test with multimeter; can temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch, for troubleshooting only
- safety: never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic switch that when engaged initiates the starter motor to turn the engine
- symptom: can go bad when the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause failure
- confirm: battery must be fully charged before testing; article links its own solenoid test article

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: only suspect after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid; starter can be removed and tested
- pro: author recommends a shop that specializes in starter/alternator repair test and rebuild the starter before buying a new one (opinion)
