## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Husqvarna zero turn starts then dies"
- alias: "mower just shuts off while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: often lack of air, fuel or spark, or a loose wire/component.
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug wires before repairs.
- brand: Husqvarna zero-turn shown with a Kawasaki engine; for an engine problem go to Husqvarna service center or the engine maker's dealer (Kawasaki, Kohler, etc.).

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can go bad in a month; ethanol attracts moisture leaving gummy deposits and varnish.
- fix: if fuel sat more than 30 days without stabilizer, drain tank; Husqvarna zero-turns need unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol. Mix fuel with Sea Foam Motor Treatment before adding; run about 15 minutes to work it through.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace; arrow on filter housing points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: gummy substance narrows the line.
- confirm: use fuel shut-off valve to start/stop flow, checking each section.
- fix: turn off fuel, remove line, carb cleaner then compressed air; if still blocked, install new line of same length and diameter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most Husqvarna zero-turns use a vacuum pump that uses vacuum off the engine block; old fuel can degrade it.
- confirm: verify flow into the pump; turn off fuel, remove line from the carb into a container, turn on fuel, start; steady or pulsating flow = good; else replace pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: verify fuel to carb; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner (not starter fluid) in intake; if it starts, runs fine, then bogs and shuts off, suspect carb.
- fix: remove, disassemble, clean; or small engine shop.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dusty conditions of zero-turns load the filter; replace annually and clean several times a season; more often in very dusty conditions or commercial use.
- fix paper: remove without letting dirt into intake; wipe housing with clean dry cloth; tap on hard surface; do not use compressed air (damages the paper element); hold to light; replace if no light shows, damaged, or oil-covered; reinstall and attach cover.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: a fan at the top of the engine pushes air to cool it; fins dissipate heat.
- fix: remove debris from fins, replace broken fins, clean around engine block and shroud; make sure the heat shield is installed correctly for air circulation.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- tip: check oil level with dipstick before each use.
- fact: low oil: friction heat in crankcase, burns oil and parts; adding oil usually won't fix it since damage likely done.
- pro: have an experienced small engine mechanic assess.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil builds crankcase pressure and shuts engine down; can cause overheating and smoke, damaged seals, blown gaskets, hydrolocked engine.
- fix: remove a little oil via the drain plug, oil filter, or oil fill using an oil evacuator or turkey baster; repeat until correct.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: clean if dirty; replace if very dark or damaged; gap to engine manufacturer spec; wires secure.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: windings separate and short when hot; dies after running a while. Ohmmeter continuity check; replace if break.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fix: choke on/closed to start cold, off/open once warm.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: loosen cap and see if engine runs better; retighten and run a while to replicate; replace bad cap. Starts fine but shuts down after a while.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck strains engine, overheats, shuts down; scrape deck regularly and sharpen blades; deck uses blades and under-deck area to create airflow that lifts grass.

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "Husqvarna zero turn battery not charging"
- causes-named: faulty-charging-system, bad-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: run at high engine speed; don't idle long; engine power is needed to charge battery.
- fact: if battery and wiring are good and tight but it keeps needing charging, suspect charging system.
- pro: author highly recommends an experienced mechanic for charging system faults; electrical parts are expensive and usually non-returnable.
