## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: Cleaning extends longevity and performance; remove debris after each mowing; wash periodically.
- fact: Pressure washer OK but not at a high setting; be careful around the engine and deck bearings.
- step 1: Remove grass and debris: leaf blower (outside, safety glasses), or compressor with blow gun, or soft brush that won't scratch finish.
- step 2: Never wash while engine is hot (don't spray cold water on a hot engine). Use mild soap that won't harm paint/plastic; if no soap dispenser on pressure washer, wet mower, then wipe with sponge and soap. Author uses mild grease-cutting dish soap (Dawn). Do NOT add dish detergent to a pressure washer.
- step 3: Engine top and around recoil housing: low pressure or stand back; water and carburetor don't mix.
- step 4: Top of deck and wheels: most mowers' wheels aren't greaseable so lubricate after. On riding mowers/tractors, deck covers protect the belt; bearings are sealed but not against high pressure: no pressure washer around spindle housings and pulleys, use a garden hose.
- step 5: Underside of deck (messy; away from driveway and visible yard). Push mower: tip front up on a jack stand or something firm. Riding mower/tractor: lift with floor jack and jack stands, car ramps or trailer tailgate; otherwise pressure washer wand under deck until next deck service/blade sharpening. Zero-turn: hard access; author uses a Jungle Jim's lift jack.
- fact: Deck washout port (some decks, some push mowers): often plugged, very messy. Use: hook garden hose, water on, start mower, engage the deck; blades spin water off; use away from structures.
- step 6: Rinse, let air dry or use leaf blower; author leaves in hot sun to evaporate.
- step 7: Lubricate to prevent rust. Push mower: WD40 or equivalent on wheel axles and cables that got wet. Riding/zero-turn: WD40-type lubricant on cables and seat hinges; grease steering and deck before storage.
- fact: Routine maintenance: at minimum 2 services per mowing season for average homeowner; large mowers with many hours: change engine oil every 50 hours; check owner's manual for hydraulic fluid and filter interval.

- fact: Service at least 2 times per season; engine oil every 50 hours on large mowers with heavy use; hydraulic fluid/filter per owner's manual.
