## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "lawn mower isn't running, carburetor not getting fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Carburetor doesn't get fuel when old gas corrodes fuel components and leaves gummy deposits; plugged fuel filter; clogged or kinked fuel lines; faulty fuel pump; stuck float and float needle; or bad gas cap.
- safety: Work in a well-ventilated area, follow the operator's manual, take caution around a hot engine.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Most gasoline contains ethanol (corn-based), which attracts moisture; ethanol plus water forms sticky deposits that block the fuel system and can make components fail prematurely.
- fact: Gas can break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase; use the right fuel and consume it within this time frame.
- fix: Use a siphon pump to drain the old fuel into an approved fuel container (to recycle later). Add fresh fuel plus a fuel additive to stabilize fuel, reduce moisture and clean the fuel system. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment.
- tip: Run fresh gasoline after fixing flow so restrictions don't redevelop.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Most mowers use an inline filter with a fuel line on each end. Plugs if fuel is very dirty or filter not changed annually.
- fix: Replace it. There is an arrow on the filter side; install with the arrow pointing in the fuel-flow direction, toward the carburetor and away from the tank. (Source says "plugged air filter" in the fix line; context is the fuel filter.)

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Check each section by stopping and starting flow. Control flow with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the gas tank; without one, pinch pliers crimp the line.
- fix: Remove the clogged section, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow out with compressed air, repeat until open. Replace the line if you can't unclog it or it's dry and cracked.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most gas mowers use a vacuum fuel pump when the carburetor sits higher than the tank; it builds pressure off the crankcase and moves fuel to the carburetor because fuel won't run uphill. A cracked or failed pump must be replaced.
- confirm: step 1: confirm fuel reaches the pump: stop flow, remove the fuel line from the pump inlet port, put the line in a container lower than the tank, restart flow; fuel in container = flow confirmed, otherwise find blockage in lines/filter. step 2: reattach line to inlet. Remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start fuel flow and start the mower. Expect a steady or pulsating flow; if not, replace the pump.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Stuck float or float needle won't let fuel into the carburetor bowl; the float and needle regulate bowl fuel level.
- tip: Temporarily unstick by tapping the carburetor with a rubber mallet or rubber end of a hammer; works a time or two, but the carburetor must be disassembled, cleaned and repaired.
- fix: Take the carburetor apart to find the cause; may clean it so the float stops sticking, or rebuild/replace.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plugged cap vent makes the tank act like a vacuum.
- confirm: Run the mower with and without the cap; if it runs okay without the cap but eventually shuts off or runs sluggish with it, the cap is plugged.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap vent; otherwise buy a new cap.
