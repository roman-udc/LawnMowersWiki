## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: dull blades beat up grass instead of cutting cleanly; the torn part turns brown a couple of days later
- fact: Cub Cadet blades available at Cub Cadet dealer or online
- fact: change blades at least once a year; more often if yard has sand, gravel, rocks (prematurely dull and ding blades)
- fact: sharpen every 25 hours or about twice a year for the average homeowner
- tools (remove): ratchet and sockets, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional)
- safety: always remove spark plug boot(s) and ignition key before getting under the deck so mower can't start
- step 1: remove spark plug boot and ignition key
- step 2: remove blade bolts with socket and ratchet; cordless impact gun helps if stubborn
- tip: very stubborn bolt: put a block of wood over the bolt head and hit the block with a hammer to loosen it
- step 3: inspect blade (see below)
- confirm: sail wear thinner at the ends than centre is normal wear from sand and dirt
- confirm: chips and dings (from hitting objects) can usually be removed by sharpening; large gouges mean replace
- confirm: check for bends; if unsure compare old blade to a new one; bent blade must be replaced
- mistake: never straighten a bent blade; it weakens the metal and cracks can develop and break off
- safety: replace any cracked blade; a cracked blade can break off while mowing and send metal flying, injuring a person or damaging house siding
- tools (sharpen): 10 inch flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or a nail on the wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves
- step 4: wear safety glasses and gloves; clean blade with rag (wire brush if needed)
- step 5: secure blade in vise; after sharpening one side flip and secure again
- step 6 (file): push the file at an angle equal to the slope of the blade in one direction along the blade; do not use a sawing motion; remove rough spots/uneven edges then flip and repeat
- step 6 (drill sharpener): power the drill with the sharpener attached; put the flat guide along the flat of the blade with cutting edge in the bevel slot; move up and down the edge; flip and repeat
- step 6 (angle/bench grinder): hold grinder perpendicular to the blade edge; run along the length to level rough spots/nicks; keep it moving so it removes a little metal at a time; flip and repeat
- mistake: don't sharpen to a sharp pointed edge, it makes the blade wear faster
- step 7: balance: use a blade balancer and shave more metal from the heavy side; or hang blade by centre hole on a nail on the wall and see which side hangs lower; remove metal from the lower side until level
- fact: balancing reduces vibration and prevents deck damage
- pro: author has blades sharpened professionally (clean edge, balanced); reasons given: safer than doing it yourself, sharpening often cheaper than a new blade, a pro can grind out large nicks/gouges, blade properly balanced
- tip: remove blades frequently when using lawn chemicals so corrosion doesn't make them seize on the deck

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: blade design rotates air at high speed forming suction in deck so grass stands up; suction also draws in grass, dirt, debris that wear/damage blades
- fact: sticks, rocks, toys damage blade/deck; a damaged blade must be replaced since it can cause bad cut, spindle housing damage or vibration
- fact: sand and dirt (thin lawns) dull blades prematurely
- fact: lawn chemicals can get on deck/blades and corrode them
- tip: walk the yard before mowing to remove objects

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: brown torn grass tips after mowing; bent/chipped/cracked blades
- fix: sharpen dull or nicked blade; replace bent, cracked or badly gouged blade

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: compare an old blade to a new one if unsure whether it's bent
