## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: old fuel breaks down, causing buildup/clogging that prevents starting.
- brand: gas-powered Kubota mowers: unleaded, minimum octane 87, max ethanol 10%.
- fix: remove with a fuel siphon into an approved fuel container; flush the tank; add fresh fuel with an additive with cleaning agents (Sea Foam Motor Treatment stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans).

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Kubota gas mowers: unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; ethanol (corn-based) is not good for the small engines.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: cap is designed to vent; clogged vent creates a vacuum in the tank restricting fuel.
- confirm: run mower with and without the cap; if it runs fine without but shuts off after a while with the cap reinstalled, suspect the cap.
- fix: try cleaning/unclogging; otherwise replace with new Kubota cap.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- same as above (vacuum in tank; run with/without cap test; clean or replace).

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: remove plug with a 3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on the engine model; look for carbon buildup, broken porcelain, burnt electrode; gap to manufacturer spec.
- fix: new plug when it can't be cleaned effectively, tip dark from buildup, burnt electrode, or damaged porcelain insulator. After install ensure the wire is secure.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- tools: 3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on engine model
- step 1: remove plug, inspect; step 2: gap per manufacturer spec; step 3: install and attach wire securely.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: restricts air; can cause extensive internal engine damage if not corrected. Change annually at full service; check/clean regularly through season; more often in dusty dry conditions.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove paper element from housing without dropping dirt in the intake.
- step 2: tap against a solid surface to loosen dirt.
- step 3: hold to light; if light shows through paper, reuse; otherwise replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump moves fuel from tank to carburetor; housing and components degrade from old fuel.
- confirm: inspect for cracks, damage or leaking fuel -> replace.
- confirm: (flow to pump) shut off fuel via shutoff valve at bottom of tank or crimp the line; disconnect line from pump inlet port; put the line in a container lower than the tank (fuel won't flow if line is higher than tank); open the flow and watch for fuel; if none, the restriction is before the pump (blocked line or plugged filter). Reattach.
- confirm: (pump output) with flow to pump verified, remove fuel line from the carburetor into a container, start mower and watch; pulsating or constant flow = pump fine; inconsistent flow -> replace pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: dirt and deposits from old fuel clog the filter.
- fix: replace; arrow on plastic housing must point in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: arrow on the housing points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove the line, spray carb cleaner into the tube, blow compressed air through until free of debris and gummy residue; or replace if the clog won't clear or the line is dry, brittle, cracking.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: regulates fuel/air mixture; gums up and clogs.
- fix: take apart and clean with carb cleaner including float bowl and needle.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter about 12.7 V; below that put on a charger; replace if won't hold. Cables secure, no corrosion.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence system uses several safety switches designed to kill the engine if the operator leaves the seat; bad switch can stop starting. See manual for locations.
- confirm: temporary bypass or multimeter.
- safety: never operate with a bad or bypassed switch; many people are injured annually rolling mowers or falling off; always keep switches installed and working.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: turn key, nothing happens. confirm: multimeter. fix: replace.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: provides voltage to the plug so it fires.
- confirm: after verifying the plug is good, check coil continuity with an ohmmeter; replace if a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor.
- symptom: a click or hum when turning the key; or a wire attached to the solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts.
- confirm/fix: test (linked article); replace if bad.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: not the main no-start cause but contributes to a weak battery. Bad stator or alternator among electrical parts; test with ohmmeter.
- pro: have a small engine mechanic identify the cause; don't throw parts at it.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Kubota safety procedures shut off the mower; e.g. getting off a zero-turn with the engine running without setting the parking brake first shuts it off. Follow the operator's manual.
