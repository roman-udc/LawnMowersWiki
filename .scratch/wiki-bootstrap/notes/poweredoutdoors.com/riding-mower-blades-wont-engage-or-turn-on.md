## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "engage the PTO switch and nothing happens"
- alias: "blades won't turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- safety: Remove ignition key and spark plug wires, wait for engine to cool and parts to stop moving; follow operator's manual.
- pro: If fuses keep blowing, take to a repair shop or dealer to find the root cause.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: stretched, cracked or frayed belt; shiny glazed appearance; sits deep in the pulley grooves.
- fact: Worn belt slips on pulleys and reduces blade speed, which can give a bad cut. Replace a worn belt even if it isn't the root cause.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Spring holds the idler pulleys in line; the bracket/deck hole it attaches to can wear, or the spring wears; the belt loosens and vibrates off the pulleys. Replace a worn or missing spring; replace the bracket if the hole wears larger and the bracket is replaceable.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: Turn each deck pulley slowly by hand; check for restriction, bearing noise, wobble; any of these means replace the pulley. A bad bearing lets the pulley wobble and the belt come off.
- fact: If the belt keeps falling off or snaps, see the belt-keeps-coming-off article.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: Usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch. Check continuity; replace if there is a break.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to the blades by engaging the drive belt; replace a worn or faulty clutch.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: Manual-engagement clutch riding mowers have a lever and cable; lever, cable, spring, bushings and linkages wear; check and replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Electric clutch uses battery power; weak battery can't energize the clutch solenoid. A full 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; charge if lower.
- step (charge): wear protective gear (skin, eyes); access battery (under hood or under seat; may need a screwdriver); don't remove it from its casing; connect charger positive (red) first to the positive terminal; then negative (black) to negative terminal; touch only rubber-coated parts; set voltage (usually 12 V) and amperage (start at 2 A, up to no more than 10 A; a slow charge is best).
- fact: If it won't hold a charge replace it; 12 V mower batteries at hardware/automotive stores or mower dealers; bring the old battery to avoid a core fee, which averages $20.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Seat switch under the seat tells the operator-presence system the user is seated; blades won't run without the operator in the seat; a failed switch stops the blades.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter or temporarily bypass.
- safety: Don't operate a mower without the safety switch installed.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse protects the electrical system; blows from a short, bad wiring or failing components. Replace with the same capacity fuse.
