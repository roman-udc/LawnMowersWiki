## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: leak around the carburetor bowl (gasket); leak near the air intake port (stuck float); stuck needle.
- fact: Old gas sitting in carburetor forms gummy deposits that stick small components. Bowl is at the bottom of carburetor; bowl gasket looks like a rubber band; it is stressed by temperature swings (hot running, cool off), becomes dry and brittle and loses seal.
- fix (gasket): clean outside of carburetor, remove screw at bottom of bowl (rag ready for remaining gas), remove bowl, replace old gasket with new, reinstall bowl and tighten screw.
- fix (stuck float): float regulates fuel into bowl; stuck float lets fuel overflow. Disassemble carburetor to find cause; may be able to clean the carburetor to free it; may need to rebuild or replace.
- fix (stuck float needle): needle works with float to keep gas flowing into bowl; stuck needle -> rebuild carburetor. Temporary: tap carburetor with a rubber mallet (works once or twice). Proper fix is replacing the float needle.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: Needed when float needle is stuck.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- fact: Cleaning may free a stuck float.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: leaks at seams: old fuel degrades and softens the filter's plastic housing.
- fix: Replace; careful if plastic is soft because filter ends can break off in the hose. Replace filter annually during routine service.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Can degrade from old gas like filter; check pump seams for leaks; replace when leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Gravely fuel tanks are high-density polyethylene and can leak at the seams; best to replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Gravely has a fuel shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank; valves prone to leak over time; replace when leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Old lines dry out, crack, leak. Check line from tank to carburetor for cracks and punctures around hose clamps. Replace leaking lines; good practice to replace dry/cracked lines before they leak.
- tip: Replace pinch-style clamps with worm gear clamps; less likely to puncture the line.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- tip: Check clamps; replace pinch-style with worm gear clamps.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Seal dries out and no longer seals; fuel sloshes out around the cap. Gas evaporates so no visible sign, only fumes.
- confirm: Rock mower back and forth to splash fuel around cap area; wet area forming outside the tank around cap = replace the cap.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old fuel degrades the plastic of fuel filter and pump, causing leaks; forms gummy deposits in carburetor.
