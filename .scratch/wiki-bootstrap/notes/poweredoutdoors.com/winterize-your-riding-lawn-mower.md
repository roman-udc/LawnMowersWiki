## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Summary: remove debris; scrape the deck; wash and remove moisture; change engine oil; fully charge or remove the battery; add fuel stabilizer (or drain the tank) before storage.
- tools: deck scraper, metal putty knife or wire brush; gloves and rags; fuel stabilizer (or a gas container if draining); oil change supplies (oil, filter and filter wrench, socket or spanner wrenches, drain pan); cardboard under the work area to keep oil off the surface.
- step 1: Brush off large grass and debris; spray with a garden hose. Be careful with a pressure washer: don't use high pressure near bearings (sealed bearings aren't sealed against high pressure). If there's a deck wash port, follow the manufacturer's instructions to attach the hose and engage blades to wash the underside.
- step 2: Dry completely - don't store with moisture. Author lets it sit outside in the sun on a breezy day; a handheld blower helps. Moisture causes premature rust and corrosion of electrical components.
- step 3: Change the engine oil (see below).
- step 4: Prop the deck with jack stands or similar (pneumatic stands can shift: dangerous); if you can't get enough height, remove the deck per the manual. Scrape the underside with a deck scraper, small putty knife or wire brush; grass holds moisture and rusts the deck. Check blades and inspect bearings in the cutter housings.
- step 5: Fuel: either drain or stabilize (see below).
- step 6: Battery: stay installed fully charged, or remove (see below).
- step 7: Rodent prevention.
- step 8: Store in a cool dry place; cover with a tarp or large drop cloth indoors (dust); if outdoors, use a waterproof cover.
- tip: Rodents nest in riding mowers and chew wires; author saw dead rodents in filter housings, radiators and wiring harnesses at the dealership. Author uses Grandpa Gus's Mouse Repellent (all natural, packets placed around the mower and liquid spray on the wires; smells better than mothballs).

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- step 1: Park on a flat surface; warm the engine a couple of minutes (oil flows better warm).
- step 2: Shut off, remove the key, disconnect the spark plug lead(s). Engine may be hot.
- step 3: Drain the oil (drain plug near the bottom of the engine) and remove the filter.
- step 4: Put a light film of oil on the new filter's seal and attach it.
- step 5: Fill with the correct oil and quantity per the manufacturer; don't overfill.
- safety: Do not skip disconnecting the spark plug lead.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Untreated fuel sitting over winter can cause fuel system problems. Either option is OK.
- step: Drain - siphon into an approved fuel container (don't use unsuitable containers); start the mower and run until it shuts off and won't restart to burn off the rest.
- step: Stabilize - without a stabilizer, old fuel causes gumming and varnish; ethanol collects moisture. Author recommends Sea Foam Motor Treatment: stabilizes fuel for up to two years and helps remove moisture. Add the appropriate amount to fresh gas in the tank, start the engine and run a few minutes so treated fuel reaches the fuel system.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: A fully charged battery won't freeze; if left installed make sure it's fully charged. Many owners remove it and store it cool and dry.
- step: Remove: eye protection and gloves; engine off; remove the brackets or bungee straps; loosen the black negative terminal counterclockwise with an adjustable wrench and remove the cable; same for the red positive terminal; store away from combustibles in a cool dry place.
