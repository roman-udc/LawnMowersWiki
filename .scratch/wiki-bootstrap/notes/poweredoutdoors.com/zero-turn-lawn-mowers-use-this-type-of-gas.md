## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: zero-turns can be powered by gasoline, diesel, propane or battery; this article covers gas-powered ones.
- fact: best gas is unleaded with octane 87 or higher and ethanol 10% or less (lower is better).
- fact: pump options now include E15 (15% ethanol), E85 (85%), REC90 (ethanol-free) and more. Avoid anything over 10% ethanol, including E15 and E85.
- fact: ethanol is corn-based; it attracts moisture, leaves gummy deposits that clog the fuel system, and can separate and sink to the bottom of the tank; that mixture runs hot in the engine and can cause damage.
- fact: best option is ethanol-free (recreation fuel, REC-90): doesn't break down like ethanol fuels; more expensive, especially in hardware-store canisters; cheaper at the pump; find stations at pure-gas.org.
- tip: buy only what you can use in 30 days (gas can break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase); store in a dry area so water doesn't mix in; stabilize any excess.
- tip: buy the best-quality low-ethanol gas you can afford (expensive isn't always better).
- mistake: running wrong or old gas can damage the fuel system and engine, a significant repair bill.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: author adds a fuel additive to each tank: Sea Foam Motor Treatment, which stabilizes fuel for up to two years (author's claim), reduces moisture and cleans the fuel system. Use it when storing excess fuel and when storing the mower with fuel in the off-season.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: gas with ethanol over 10% (E15, E85) is wrong for a zero-turn.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: use within 30 days or stabilize.
