## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "engine acts like it's going to start but won't"
- fact: not getting air, fuel, spark, or compression.
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, low-compression

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: filter protects engine from dirt; a little dirt can shorten engine life; must be cleaned regularly and replaced when bad/damaged; check before each use.
- tip: new filter each season, clean several times.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: PAPER:
- step 1: remove filter from housing without letting dirt fall into intake.
- step 2: wipe housing with clean dry rag.
- step 3: tap against solid surface.
- step 4: hold to light; light visible: reuse; no light, extremely dirty or damaged: replace.
- step 5: install and reattach cover (if foam pre-filter, clean and install it first).
- fact: FOAM PRE-FILTER (extra filter used with a paper filter; not a primary foam filter):
- safety: NEVER add oil to a pre-filter or you damage the paper filter.
- step 1: inspect; replace if torn or brittle.
- step 2: wash with water and mild dish soap.
- step 3: rinse until soap gone and water clear.
- step 4: lay flat to dry (sun outdoors speeds it).
- step 5: install with the paper primary filter and reattach cover.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke lever/knob closes a plate controlling air mixed with gas; cold engine needs rich mixture (choke on, plate closed); once warm, choke off; if already warm, choke must be off.
- fix: set correctly; check choke plate isn't stuck and cable moves freely; carb cleaner frees stuck plate and linkages; replace worn choke cable if it can't be adjusted.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- tools: 3/4" or 5/8" socket wrench by engine model; two-cylinder engines have two plugs.
- symptom: dark tip with soot, wet plug, broken porcelain -> new plug. Wet plug: find why it is covered in fuel or oil.
- fix: good but dirty: small wire brush for carbon; verify electrode gap; attach wire.
- tip: new plugs each season.
- confirm: also check the plug wire isn't drying/cracking (could ground out).

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: if the coil fails there's no power to create spark. Check it.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: ethanol makes gas go bad quickly; engine makers recommend using within 30 days; varnish and sticky deposits. Use min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol.
- fix: drain with siphon pump; fill fresh with stabilizer like Sea Foam; run so it works through.
- fact: low fuel also a possible cause.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: inline filter between fuel lines; plugs if not replaced regularly. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: follow line tank to carb for kinks; shut flow (shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers); remove end furthest from tank into container; check flow per section.
- fix: carb cleaner then compressed air, repeat; replace if not cleared or aged and cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: not all mowers have a pump; used when carb is higher than tank; most are vacuum-style (crankcase vacuum).
- confirm: confirm fuel to inlet port (else check line/filter); remove line from carb into container; start flow and mower; steady or pulsating flow expected; else replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: good flow to carb; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner into intake and start; starts then runs sluggish/dies when cleaner burns off = dirty carb. Don't use starter fluid.
- fix: disassemble and clean, or replace.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: more often a problem when the mower ran a while, shut down, and won't restart.
- confirm: loosen cap and start; if it starts keep running while tightening; sputters/shuts down/won't restart until loosened = bad cap. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/low-compression
- fact: after verifying fuel, air, spark, do a compression check or leak-down test for valves, piston rings, etc.; need gauges and engine specs. Many small engines have automatic compression release; leak-down test is better for those.
- pro: take to a dealer with factory-trained support or reputable small engine mechanic for engine diagnostics/repairs.

## page: procedures/check-compression
- fact: compression check or leak-down test; needs correct gauges and the engine model's specifications; leak-down better for engines with automatic compression release. (No steps in source.)
