## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Toro mower loses power and bogs down"
- fact: loses power when engine lacks airflow/fuel flow or runs hot; or is under load (plugged deck, ground speed too fast).
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- tip: blocked air passageways: remove debris under engine shroud; heat shield securely in place.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: replace annually; clean several times per season, more in dusty conditions.
- step: paper filter: remove cover (clips, knobs, wing nuts); remove filter without dropping dirt in intake; wipe housing with dry rag; tap on hard surface; hold to light, reuse if light shines through, replace if not, damaged or oily; reinstall.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down as soon as 30 days; ethanol and moisture leave gummy deposits restricting flow.
- fix: drain tank and fill with fresh gas.
- brand: Toro gas mowers need unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; today's Toro mowers use 4-cycle with straight gasoline; some older Toro mowers are 2-cycle needing gas/oil mix.
- tip: Sea Foam Motor Treatment stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system; use when more gas than can be used in 30 days; author adds to every tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace annually; shut off with fuel shut-off valve or fuel pinch-off pliers; remove clamps, pull filter, install new; arrow toward carburetor, away from tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove end of a hose section into a container, restore flow, check flow.
- fix: remove line section, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air; replace with same length/diameter if can't clear or dry/cracked.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake and start; if it runs but won't stay running, clean the carburetor (link to Toro carburetor cleaning article).

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: intermittent spark; carbon, dirt, oil on tip.
- fix: replace; or if only dirty and not very dark, clean with wire brush and reuse.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low from underfilling at change, leak, or burning oil from wrong viscosity; lack of lubrication causes friction, heat, overheating, smoke, melting parts; possible engine replacement.
- confirm: check before each use: dipstick wipe, reinsert, pull, should be full; add oil, do not overfill.
- pro: if problems persist after refilling, small engine mechanic (internal damage possible).

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: bluish-white smoke from oil burning in cylinder; thick smoke can plug air filter (check filter and spark plug).
- fact: pressure builds, oil pushed into cylinder through valve train; continued running can cause seal damage, hydrolock, bent piston rod.
- fix: loosen and quickly tighten drain plug to release a little oil, or draw from oil filter/fill area with turkey baster or oil evacuator.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: air circulates around block and head; plugged fins (clippings, mud) limit airflow; replace damaged fins; clean around shroud and air intake.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: slow down in thick, wet, tall grass and on inclines.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: wet grass makes engine work harder, clumps and sticks under deck; mow dry. Tall grass: double or triple cut, highest deck setting first then lower.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck makes engine work harder turning blades; scrape regularly.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify a plugged deck problem.
