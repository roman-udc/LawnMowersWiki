## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Toro lawn mower smoking white, blue or black smoke"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket, rich-fuel-mixture
- fact: engine age and care correlate with problems; start with the easiest checks and move to internal ones.
- fact: black smoke: engine running very rich (more fuel than air); first check the air filter, then other airflow restrictions. Blue or white smoke: excess oil burning; check oil level first, then piston rings, valve train, engine gasket.
- tip: check oil level and air filter before each mowing.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter starves air; engine can overheat and pull air and oil out of the crankcase; that burns causing smoke.
- tip: check more often than at standard service.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove cover (usually one or two thumbscrews or knobs).
- step 2: remove filter without knocking dirt into intake; wipe housing with dry clean cloth.
- step 3: paper filter: tap against hard surface until loose dirt is gone; never use compressed air.
- step 4: hold to light; if no light through the paper, replace.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: either too high or too low smokes. Low: insufficient lubrication, friction heat, components melt and smoke; adding oil usually doesn't help as heat already caused damage; take to a Toro dealer.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil raises crankcase pressure, oil gets into the cylinder or up to the air intake through the valve train and burns off when hot; drain some oil out.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- confirm: remove the spark plug (3/4 in or 5/8 in socket depending on mower) and check for excessive oil buildup; oil means piston ring or valve train problem; either needs engine teardown; may find cylinder wall scoring letting oil into the combustion chamber.
- fact: may have to choose between replacing the engine or buying a new mower.
- pro: mechanic experienced with small engines if not mechanically inclined.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: a valve burned by overheating (edge falls apart: burnt valve); detect only by removing the cylinder head and leak-down test by a small engine mechanic; valve and seat must be cut at certain angles to seal against the block.
- symptom: muffler glowing red from extreme heat can indicate a burnt valve or timing problem.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: bad engine gasket leaks oil onto the hot muffler, smoke; locating and replacing can be difficult depending on gasket location.

## page: causes/rich-fuel-mixture
- fact: black smoke from running very rich.
