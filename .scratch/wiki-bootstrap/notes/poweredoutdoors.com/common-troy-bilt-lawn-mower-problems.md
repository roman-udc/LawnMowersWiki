## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Troy-Bilt won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Source lumps no-start causes: electrical, fuel or airflow problem. Charging system, ignition switch, battery, safety switches; also bad air filter, dirty carburetor, bad spark plug, clogged fuel components.
- brand: Troy-Bilt. Source links "Reasons Your Troy-Bilt Mower Won't Start".

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel breaks down over time, less efficient and prone to clogging fuel system (also engine damage when it dies).
- fix: Drain tank, fill with fresh fuel; use additive like Sea Foam to stabilize fuel and help clean fuel system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Vent in cap clogs, tank forms vacuum restricting fuel flow.
- fix: Clear vent with thin piece of wire; replace cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Excessively dirty or broken plug, bad connection, or not gapped correctly.
- fix: Replace plug, secure connections, gap to manufacturer spec.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dirt and grass plug filter and stop airflow. Plugged filter also causes: dying while mowing; smoke (not enough air so runs rich; or oil pulled from crankcase into cylinder with air and burns off).
- fix: Remove and clean; replace if in bad condition.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace fuel filter (dirty filter restricts flow).

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Failed or leaking pump no longer creates pressure to pump fuel; can leak at seams.
- fix: Replace fuel pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Bad fuel deposits and dirt clog lines.
- fix: Clear with carb cleaner and compressed air; replace line if necessary.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Clogged by bad fuel and ethanol deposits, or buildup from old gas causing blockages/part failures.
- fix: Clean carburetor components; replace if needed (replace damaged parts).

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Dead battery, loose cables or corroded terminals cause starting problems.
- fix: Charge battery, replace if it won't hold charge; tighten cables, clean corrosion from terminals.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Safety switches prevent starting in certain situations; failed switch = no start.
- fix: Test and replace faulty switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fix: Test and replace bad ignition switch (or coil).

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fix: Test and replace bad ignition coil (source: "ignition switch or coil").

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Pull-start mower with damaged/broken recoil can't start engine.
- fix: Check whether recoil can be restrung or broken parts (e.g. pulley) replaced; replace recoil assembly if needed.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Troy-Bilt loses power and dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor
- fact: Often a fuel or air restriction. Overheating contributors: dirty cooling fins and clogged mower deck.
- fix: Cooling fins packed with dirt, oil, grass: replace broken fins and clean. Other fixes as in cause sections above.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins packed with dirt, oil and grass don't let air cool engine block.
- fix: Replace broken fins and clean them.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Troy-Bilt lawn mower smokes"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Plugged air filter: not enough air so engine runs rich; or oil pulled into cylinder from crankcase and burns off.
- fact: Too much oil in crankcase: pressure builds, oil gets into cylinder through valve train. Fix: drain a little oil to the manufacturer's recommended level.
- fact: Oil level too low: more friction, extreme heat. Take to small engine repair shop to inspect damage; adding oil is often too late.
- fact: Internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket): take to small engine shop.
- pro: internal engine problems to a repair shop.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking).
- fix: Drain a little oil until at manufacturer's level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil raises friction and heat; often too late to fix by adding oil; send to small engine shop.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Internal problems incl. piston ring, valve train, engine gasket cause smoking; shop troubleshooting.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Troy-Bilt hydrostatic transmission is weak"
- alias: "Troy-Bilt won't move or go forward"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, missing-axle-key, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: Weak hydrostatic: check drive belt and tensioner pulley for wear, replace; old/low hydraulic fluid weakens transmission; follow manufacturer's oil and filter change schedule.
- brand: Some entry-level Troy-Bilt hydraulic systems are sealed (fluids can't be changed); contact dealer/repair shop for hydraulic problems on a sealed system.
- fact: Source links "9 Reasons Your Mower Won't Move or Go Forward".

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: Drive release lever lets hydrostatic mowers roll freely; must be in operating position.

## page: causes/missing-axle-key
- fact: Riding mower may have a small key in the axle that can fall out of place.
- fix: Replace the axle key.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: Drive pump belt: mower won't move if belt fell off or is worn; pump doesn't work efficiently.
- fix: Replace frayed, cracked or worn belt; ensure belt is securely around pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Tensioner pulley bearing can fail; it then doesn't keep tension on the drive belt.
- fix: Replace tensioner pulley and grease the tensioner arm.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: Broken or missing spring no longer tensions the drive belt. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Low or old oil fails to lubricate; system feels weak.
- fix: Change at intervals recommended by Troy-Bilt; periodically check level is full.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: Hot fluid can signal damage to hydrostatic system. Let oil cool. If runs fine cold but weak when hydraulic system gets hot, have it checked at Troy-Bilt dealer.
- pro: dealer.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: Air must be bled after changing hydraulic fluid or mower won't move at normal speed. Follow Troy-Bilt procedure in owner's manual.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "Troy-Bilt won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, loose-steering-linkage
- fact: Applies to riding mower and zero-turn.
- fact: Unequal tire pressure makes mower pull to one side; keep to manufacturer spec; both sides equal.
- fact: Bad dampers on zero turn make mower jerk to one side going forward; replace damper.
- fact: Tracking adjustment on zero turn: when one tire moves faster than the other mower pulls; adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change tire rotation speed per owner's manual.
- fact: Riding mower worn/loose steering components (worn bushings, washers, gears) cause failure to steer one way; inspect and replace failed parts.

## page: problems/loose-steering
- fact: Riding mower: worn bushings, washers, gears in steering system cause it not to steer one way or other; replace parts that failed.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: Unequal pressure pulls mower to one side; also a low tire keeps deck from sitting level (uneven cut).

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fact: Bad zero-turn dampers cause jerking to one side moving forward; replace with new damper.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- fact: Zero-turn: adjust speed adjustment bolt to change rotation speed of the faster/slower tire; refer to owner's manual.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Troy-Bilt shaking or vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Vibration from deck can be felt through the whole mower including seat.
- fact: Loose or missing engine mounting bolts (bolts reduce vibration): secure, replace missing.
- fact: Bad clutch: bearing failure/falls out of clutch causes vibration; manual-engagement clutch can have bushing and linkage failures; replace clutch.
- fact: Unbalanced/damaged blades: replace damaged, balance before installing.
- fact: String or long weeds wrapped around blade shaft: remove, check spindle damage.
- fact: Bad spindle housing bearing: replace bearing or whole spindle housing assembly.
- fact: Worn pulleys/deck belt: check belt wear and pulley bearing failure.
- tip: Debris lodged in deck or mower; "don't overlook the small stuff".

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Troy-Bilt bad or uneven cut"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-tire-pressure, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: Start with tire pressures: a low tire keeps deck from sitting level. Then inspect blades and components. Operator error: mowing too fast or throttle not on high.
- fact: Source table lists: worn blade, bent blade, blade installed wrong, clogged mower deck, uneven tire pressures, unleveled deck, bent blade spindle or spindle bearing, worn deck pulleys or belt, engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, damaged deck shell, operator error.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "Troy-Bilt leaking gas"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: small areas of burnt grass in lawn; gas smell in garage/shed.
- fact: Leak can be hard to find since gas evaporates; check all fuel components from tank to carburetor. Bad carburetor gasket, degraded plastic components, bad gas cap seal.
- fact: dirty/failed carburetor: buildup from old gas causes blockages or part failure; clean, replace damaged parts.
- fact: Old fuel filter plugged: replace. Fuel pump wears, leaks at seams: replace.
- fact: Fuel tank: metal tank can develop rust hole; poly tank leaks at seams; replace tank; a rust hole can sometimes be repaired if no replacement is available.
- fact: Shut-off valve: metal and plastic valves prone to leaking; replace.
- fact: Rubber fuel lines dry and crack: replace. Primer bulb rubber cracks or poorly attached to hose: replace.
- fact: Gas cap seal fails, gas splashes out around cap: replace gas cap.
