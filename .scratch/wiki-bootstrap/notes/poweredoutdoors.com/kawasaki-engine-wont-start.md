## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Kawasaki engine won't start"
- causes-named: wrong-fuel, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, wrong-choke-setting, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Engine needs fuel, air and spark; look for what blocks any of the three.
- safety: Remove ignition key and spark plug wires before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: sluggish running, starting problems, dying after running.
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture; water/ethanol separates and sinks, leaves varnish; parts degrade and restrictions form. Gas breaks down as soon as 30 days.
- fix: Drain tank (manual siphon pump is an inexpensive tool), add fresh gas and fuel additive to clean fuel system, reduce moisture, stabilize.
- tip: Sea Foam Motor Treatment must be added to fresh gas; can't reverse old fuel (author uses it in all small engines).

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Unleaded min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; avoid E15 and E85 (up to 15% / 85%); low-ethanol or ethanol-free preferred.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter between fuel lines; plugged filter reduces fuel; maintenance part replaced annually.
- fix: Replace; arrow pointed in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Sticky old-fuel deposits narrow the line. Author has seen pine needles and plastic shavings from new fuel tanks clog lines.
- fix: Remove line, spray carburetor cleaner, then blow compressed air; if not cleared, buy new line in right diameter and length.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Pump works against gravity to move fuel to the carburetor. Many mowers use vacuum-style pump on Kawasaki engines; fuel degrades pump over time. Replace if it can't draw fuel with engine vacuum or if it leaks.
- confirm step 1: Shut off fuel with shut-off valve under tank, or pinch line with clamps.
- confirm step 2: Remove hose from pump inlet port into a container below the tank level; start fuel flow; flow = pump receives fuel; none = restriction before pump.
- confirm step 3: Shut off fuel; reattach inlet hose.
- confirm step 4: Remove hose from carburetor into container; start fuel flow and engine; watch for steady or pulsating flow.
- confirm step 5: Shut off, reattach hose.
- fix: Replace pump if no constant or pulsating flow. Operator's manual for pressure specs on other pump types.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old fuel clogs fuel passages and leaves varnish making internal parts stick.
- fix: Clean carburetor and replace stuck/damaged parts, or shop cleaning, or replace the carburetor assembly.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Fouled plug, broken porcelain or worn electrodes give intermittent spark.
- confirm: Remove plug; look for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator; replace if damaged, worn or very dark.
- fix: If good, clean tip with small wire brush; correct gap per manufacturer; secure wires.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Restricted airflow may prevent starting. Author recommends replacing annually; check regularly in season, more often in dry dusty conditions.
- step (paper): remove filter; don't let dirt fall into intake; wipe housing and cap with dry cloth; tap on solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light visible; replace if no good light, extremely dirty or damaged; reinstall.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Disconnect battery, remove corroded components, clean with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) and small metal brush; reinstall securely.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Multimeter; reading below 12.7 volts = put on charger. Replace if won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Seat switch, brake switch and PTO switch can prevent engine start; see operator's manual.
- confirm: Test with multimeter; temporary bypass for troubleshooting only.
- safety: Never operate equipment without safety switch or with one bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: Multimeter on resistance; prongs B (battery) and S (starter solenoid); key at start; probes on B and S; near 0 ohms good, infinite = bad, replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum when turning key; wire on solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts.
- fact: Electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates starter motor.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts airflow for cold start; wrong lever position = no start.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- step 1 (cold Kawasaki): choke lever full choke.
- step 2: throttle halfway between slow and fast.
- step 3: key to start, release when engine starts.
- step 4: move choke to off.
- step 5: idle at half throttle a few minutes before full throttle to mow.
- fact: Interlock: riding mower, zero-turn or tractor starts only with operator seated, brake on, PTO off.

## page: procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower
- fact: Interlocks: sit in seat, brake on, PTO off; cold start choke steps as above.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step: multimeter; under 12.7 volts = charge.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- fact: Charge if multimeter reads under 12.7 V; replace if it won't hold charge.
