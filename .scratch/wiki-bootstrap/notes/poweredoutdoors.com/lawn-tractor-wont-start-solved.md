## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "lawn tractor won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, blown-fuse, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Won't start without sufficient air, fuel, or spark. Safety first per owner's manual.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Add fresh fuel; if using fuel unusually fast, look for a leak.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel can break down within 30 days of purchase (gas and diesel). Buy from busy stations. Diesel can form sediment/gummy solution from oxidation and microbial growth; moisture forms gummy solution in gasoline.
- fix: If not used within a month, stabilize with an additive like Sea Foam. Drain tank with a siphon; refill with fresh fuel with stabilizer/cleaner.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Gas tractor: unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; do not use E15 or E85. Both gas and diesel lawn tractors exist.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: Remove cap, start and run (keep contaminants out); if it runs fine, replace cap and keep running; if it dies after a while the vent is likely plugged.
- fix: Try cleaning the vent; replace if it can't vent.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Cracked porcelain, burnt electrode, heavy carbon/oil dirt. Clean if dirty; replace if very dark or damaged; gap per manufacturer; secure leads.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fix (paper): remove; wipe housing with dry clean cloth without dropping dirt in intake; tap on solid surface; hold to light, reuse if light shows else replace; reinstall cover. Plugged filter can also overheat the engine.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most lawn tractors use a vacuum fuel pump; fuel degrades plastic and internal parts. Check for leaks (replace if leaking). Otherwise check fuel flow to the pump (shut-off valve or clamps), then steady or pulsating flow from the outlet port; none = replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace annually as good practice. Arrow on the plastic inline filter must point in direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Use fuel shut-off valve at tank bottom or fuel hose crimp pliers to stop flow; remove the line end furthest from tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump); turn flow on and check flow.
- fix: Carb cleaner then compressed air; replace if dry/brittle or can't clear.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fix: Clean (DIY or small engine mechanic); rebuild or replace if too dirty or internal parts have failed. Runs rough and possibly won't start.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fix: Clean corroded terminals with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) and wire brush. Test with multimeter; about 12.7 volts needed; charge if lower; replace if it won't hold charge.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Switches: disengage deck when no operator in the seat; prevent starting without parking brake; others possible.
- confirm: Test with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify.
- safety: Never operate a lawn tractor with a bypassed or non-working safety switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: Key inserted and nothing happens, or key doesn't feel the same when turned.
- confirm: Multimeter on the switch; replace if bad. Ignition coil: after confirming plug works, check coil continuity with multimeter; replace if break.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fix: Fuse block location varies; look around battery (under or next to) or behind the dash. Replace with the same amperage; if it keeps blowing, take to repair shop/dealer for root cause.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Not a main cause but a weak battery results; bad stator or alternator and other parts. Have a shop isolate it; most parts stores won't take back electrical parts.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fix: Follow manual for throttle, choke and safety procedures so safety features don't prevent starting.
