## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Ariens mower quit running / sputtering like it's not getting fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, wrong-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Source says an Ariens isn't getting fuel when filter plugged, lines clogged, fuel pump stopped, carburetor dirty or fuel cap vent plugged; old fuel can cause restrictions and degrade fuel components.
- safety: Refer to the Ariens owner's manual for safety precautions before working on the mower.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: Fuel loses effectiveness over time; moisture gets into the fuel and forms deposits that clog the fuel system.
- fix: Drain the tank (siphon pump works well) into a fuel container; set the old fuel aside for disposal at a recycling facility. Refill with fresh fuel and add a stabilizer/cleaner additive.
- tip: Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment in every tank.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- brand: Ariens gas mowers require unleaded gasoline, minimum octane 87, maximum ethanol 10 percent. Avoid higher ethanol; ethanol-free fuel is best.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: Dirt builds up in the filter and fuel can no longer pass.
- fact: Fuel filter should be replaced at least once a year; it screens dirt and deposits after the tank and before the fuel system.
- fix: Replace the filter. Arrow on the plastic housing must point in the direction of fuel flow: toward the carburetor, away from the tank.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: Note the arrow on the plastic filter housing.
- step 2: Install the new filter with the arrow pointing toward the carburetor and away from the fuel tank.
- fact: Replace at least once a year.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Gummy deposits from old fuel clog the lines.
- confirm: Isolate the clogged section. Use the fuel shut-off valve (bottom of tank) to start/stop flow; if no shut-off valve, clamp lines with hose pliers.
- fix: Once a non-flowing section is found, shut off fuel and remove the line; spray carburetor cleaner into the hose and blow it out with compressed air. If it can't be cleared, replace the line.
- tip: If the line is dry and showing cracks, replace it before it starts leaking.
- tools: hose pliers, carburetor cleaner, compressed air

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Ariens has a plastic or metal fuel pump when the tank sits lower than the carburetor. A vacuum pump builds pressure using crankcase vacuum to push fuel to the carburetor.
- symptom: Cracked, damaged or leaking pump: replace.
- confirm: Step A confirm fuel reaches the pump: stop flow (shut-off valve or clamp), remove the line from the pump inlet port, put it in a container lower than the tank, start flow; fuel running into the container = flow OK (else check filter and lines), reattach. Step B remove the line from the carburetor, put in a container, start flow and start the mower; a steady or pulsating stream = pump working.
- fix: Replace a pump that is damaged or doesn't pump a stream of fuel.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor is mounted top or side of engine block, usually below or behind the air filter; clogged components including the fuel jet prevent fuel reaching the cylinder.
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches the carburetor. Remove the air filter from the housing, spray a little carburetor cleaner into the air intake, start the engine. If it starts, runs and then shuts off, the carburetor needs to be removed and cleaned. If it doesn't start at all, the problem may not be fuel.
- fix: Clean the carburetor (doable by a mechanical homeowner; otherwise a repair shop).
- pro: Local repair shop if not comfortable with small parts.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Tank must vent through the cap or it acts like a vacuum restricting fuel flow.
- confirm: Remove the cap, start and run mower. If it runs fine, reinstall the cap while running. If it eventually shuts down with the cap on but restarts and runs as soon as the cap is removed, the cap vent is clogged.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap; doesn't always work, then buy a new cap.

## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- fix: Drain old fuel by siphon, dispose at recycling facility, refill fresh, add additive.
