## page: problems/engine-only-runs-with-choke-on
- alias: "riding mower only runs with the choke on"
- alias: "engine dies when I push the choke off"
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: engine is getting too much air or not enough fuel. Lack of fuel: old gas, dirty carb, clogged line, bad pump, plugged filter, bad cap. Too much air: bad carburetor gasket, puncture in fuel line.
- fact: engine uses more fuel with choke engaged; mower may underperform or develop engine problems if not fixed.
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug boot before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas goes bad as soon as 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture, leaving varnish and deposits; corrosive. Riding mowers need unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; never more.
- fix: drain old gas, refill fresh with Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL; consume within 30 days.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: regulates gas-air mix; old gas clogs passageways and internal parts.
- fix: clean yourself if mechanical; if still not right after cleaning, rebuild or replace; or have a small engine mechanic do it.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: puncture lets air be sucked in so the engine gets too much air; choke compensates.
- confirm: follow the line from the tank up to the carb; look for punctures or cracks that introduce air.
- fix: replace with new section of same diameter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: shut off fuel at the valve, remove the end of a section into a container, restore fuel and watch flow. If poor, shut off and remove the line; carb cleaner then compressed air; repeat; replace if not clearable.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: keeps dirt out; replace annually; reduced fuel may require choke to correct the ratio.
- fix: insert new inline filter with arrow in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: used if carb is higher than tank; most mowers use a vacuum pump using engine vacuum.
- confirm: confirm fuel at inlet port; remove line from carb to container and test flow.
- fix: replace if insufficient flow, cracks or leaks.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: gasket behind the carburetor deteriorates and no longer seals; extra air drawn in, engine runs lean (more air, less fuel than needed); choke compensates.
- confirm: check carb bolts tight and gasket condition.
- fix: remove linkages and bolts, carb and gasket; install a new gasket; reattach carb, bolts, linkages; good time to clean the carb.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: riding mower tank vents through the gas cap; plugged vent forms vacuum so fuel can't reach carb.
- confirm: use pressure gauge for vacuum, or: loosen cap, put choke in off position; if engine continues with choke off, cap may be the problem; confirm by tightening the cap and letting it run with choke off; if it sputters after a while and won't run well until cap is loosened, bad cap.
- fix: replace cap.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke adjusts air to control fuel-air ratio for combustion; running only with choke on signals lean/air or fuel problems elsewhere.
