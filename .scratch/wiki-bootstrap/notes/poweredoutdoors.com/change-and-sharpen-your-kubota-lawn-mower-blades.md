## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Replace blades once a year; sooner if exposed to excessive dirt, rocks, gravel. Author installs new blades at the annual service and sharpens the old ones (if not too worn) to keep as a spare set.
- fact: Sharpen after every 25 hours of use (about twice a year for the average homeowner); more often in sandy conditions.
- fact: New blades need no sharpening: factory-sharpened and paint-coated; the paint wears off the edge while mowing.
- symptom: Dull blade sign: grass tips turn brown a day or two after mowing; grab a handful and the tips are brown with little tears (tearing instead of cutting).
- safety: Before working on the deck: prevent the mower from starting, protect hands and eyes, make sure mower doesn't move. Always remove spark plug boots and the ignition key. Wear work gloves (sharp edges) and safety glasses. See owner's manual.
- tools: ratchet and socket, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, block of wood, cordless impact gun (optional)
- step 1: Wedge a block of wood between the blade and deck to stop the blade turning.
- step 2: Remove the blade with a socket and ratchet; an impact gun if stubborn. Stubborn bolt: put a block of wood over the bolt head and hit it with a hammer to shock it loose.
- step 3: Remove the bolt, blade boss or flat washer, concave (cupped) washers and blade. Hardware varies by model.
- step 4: Check hardware; replace cupped washers if worn or flattened (a flattened washer lets the blade slip).
- step 5: Sharpen and balance the blade (below) before install.
- step 6: Install in reverse order. Cupped washers go with the cupped side facing the blade. Sails (high sides) face up, pointing toward the deck.
- tools: DIY sharpening: 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves.
- step: Clean blade with rag; wire brush for buildup. Clamp in vise; after one side, flip and clamp to do the other.
- step: File - push the file at an angle in one direction along the blade; no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges, then flip.
- step: Drill sharpener - flat guide along the flat of the blade with the cutting edge slotted into the bevel; move up and down the edge length; flip and repeat.
- step: Grinder - hold perpendicular to the blade edge, run along length to level rough spots/nicks; keep moving to remove a little metal at a time for an even result; flip and repeat.
- tip: Balance: a balanced blade has equal weight each side. Use a blade balancer; shave metal off the heavy side. Or hang the blade by its center hole on a nail in the wall (nail head 3/4" to 1" from the wall); balanced when blade is parallel to the ground; shave the heavier end.
- mistake: Unbalanced blades damage spindle bearings and cause severe vibration.
- mistake: Never straighten a bent blade and reuse it; straightening weakens the metal and it can crack and break off.
- tip: Small chips/gouges can be fixed by sharpening; larger gouges that sharpening can't fix mean replace the blade.
- pro: Option to have a dealership/professional sharpen: safer, often cheaper than a new blade, can grind out large nicks/gouges, and blades come balanced. Author takes blades to the local Kubota dealer (picks up in a couple of days) and keeps a spare set.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- confirm: Bent blade, installed: mower on flat level surface; measure from the ground to the blade at one end, record; rotate blade 180 degrees and measure from the same point on the ground. More than 1/8" difference = replace blade.
- confirm: Bent blade, removed: lay the used blade on a new blade; any bends or gaps = replace.
- confirm: Spindle/bearing check: with heavy work gloves, grab each end of the installed blade and rock it up and down; movement or knocking = problem in spindle or housing. If the blade is loose, remove blade and spindle housing and check spindle and bearing for damage; repair.
- fact: Worn sail: the sail is the high side of the blade that creates air movement/suction under the deck; replace the blade when the sail is not as thick as the blade center.
- fact: Cracked blade: replace immediately; pieces can break off and fly out of the deck.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: Brown torn grass tips after mowing (dull); a bend from hitting a tree root or rock can compromise the cut (problems/uneven-cut).
- fact: A bent blade may be visibly bent or only slightly bent.
- confirm: 1/8" rule (see measure-a-mower-blade).
- fix: Replace bent blades; do not straighten.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: Sail worn when thinner than the blade center; replace the blade.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: Hitting something that bends the blade can also damage spindle and bearing; vibration through the mower when the deck is engaged (problems/mower-is-vibrating).
- confirm: Rock the installed blade by hand up and down; movement/knocking.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades wear from sand, debris, rocks, sticks and toys; lawn chemicals can corrode and fuse blades to the mower so they are hard to remove. Remove and reinstall blades regularly to prevent fusing.
- tip: Walk the yard before mowing to remove items that damage deck and blades.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- causes-named: bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Unbalanced blade after sharpening causes severe vibration and spindle bearing damage; impact damage to the spindle bearing sends vibration through the mower when the deck is engaged.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- fact: Bad or uneven cut can come from dull, bent or worn blades; Source links to a separate Kubota uneven-cut article.
