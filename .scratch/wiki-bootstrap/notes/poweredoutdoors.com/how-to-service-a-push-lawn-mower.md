## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: service push mower annually or every 50 hours, whichever first.
- fact: checklist: check safety system; change engine oil; replace spark plug; inspect fuel lines and replace fuel filter if equipped; check tire pressures and lug nuts; clean or replace air filter if necessary; remove dirt/debris from unit and engine area; sharpen or change blade; scrape deck; lubricate control linkages, pivots, wheels.
- safety: remove spark plug wire so mower can't start; heavy work gloves for blade work, latex gloves for other work.
- tools: engine oil, air filter, fuel filter (if equipped), spark plug, socket or spanner wrenches, drain pan and funnel, rags, gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover, deck scraper / wire brush / metal putty knife.
- step 1: check safety system: blade control handle depressed = blades engage; released = blades stop; see manual for other safety features.
- step 2: change oil (see below).
- step 3: inspect spark plug for damage/buildup; author replaces annually; check gap when replacing.
- step 4: fuel lines and filter (see below).
- step 5: air filter (see below).
- step 6: remove engine shroud, clear dirt/debris around engine and exhaust (buildup stops cooling airflow, causes overheating).
- step 7: sharpen or replace blade; bent/damaged blade must be replaced.
- step 8: scrape deck with deck scraper or hard wire brush.
- step 9: lubricate control linkages, pivots, wheels (owner's manual for more points).
- step 10: secure all hardware, wash off dirt, dry in sun or with handheld blower; fully dry before storing.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: push mowers have no oil filter, so change more often than larger engines; every 50 hours or annually; new mower: first change after first 5 hours.
- fact: most push mowers use about 3/4 to 1 qt SAE30; keep at manufacturer's level (too much or too little can damage engine).
- step no drain plug: place drain pan beside mower; clean around fill cap with rag; remove cap; tip mower on its side with carburetor on the high side so fill hole lines up with the pan; let oil drain, wipe drips; return upright; fill to full per manufacturer; reattach spark plug boot.
- step with drain plug: tip on side, carburetor on high side (oil must not run into carb or air filter); find square or hex plug at bottom of engine, reached from under deck; scrape deck debris around plug first; drain pan under; turn counterclockwise to remove; loosen fill cap or dipstick to speed draining; replace plug, upright, fill, reattach spark plug boot.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- tip: inspect for damage/excess buildup; author replaces annually; check gap.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: not all push mowers have one; replace annually if present; plugged by gummy buildup from old fuel.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- step: remove fuel line; use clamps to stop flow if no shut-off valve; spray carb cleaner through line off mower; clear with compressed air (can of compressed air works); replace cleared line or buy new fuel hose at hardware store.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: push mowers use thin rectangular filter, paper or foam.
- step: remove filter from housing; wipe housing with clean dry rag without letting dirt into intake.
- step paper: tap off dirt; hold to light; replace if areas where light does not shine through.
- step foam: wash with dish soap and water; squeeze out water; lay flat in sun; lightly oil with filter oil (not dripping); replace if excessively dirty or torn.
- step pre-cleaner: replace if torn, excessively dirty, dry and brittle; clean with warm soap and water, dry flat in sun.
- step: reinstall filter and pre-cleaner (if used) and engine shroud.
- mistake: stretching a bad filter to save money risks engine damage.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: dull blades strain mower and tear grass (brown ends a couple of days after mowing). Replace damaged or bent blade.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: 50 hours or annually; initial oil change after 5 hours on new mower; push mowers no oil filter.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- fact: debris around engine prevents airflow and causes overheating.
