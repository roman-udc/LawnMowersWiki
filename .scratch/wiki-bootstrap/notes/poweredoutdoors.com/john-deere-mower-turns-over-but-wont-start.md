## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "John Deere turns over but won't start"
- causes-named: wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Engine needs air, fuel and spark.
- safety: Follow operator's manual; remove spark plug wire before repairs.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts airflow for cold starts. Cold engine: choke on/closed; warm engine: off/open. After a cold start, move choke to off once warm or the mower shuts down.
- fix: If setting is right, check choke plate isn't stuck and cable moves freely; carburetor cleaner frees a stuck choke plate and linkages; replace a stretched/worn choke cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- tip: Start each season with a new filter, clean several times per season; replace more often in dusty conditions.
- fix: see procedure.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove filter from housing; don't let dirt fall into intake.
- step 2: Wipe housing with clean dry rag.
- step 3: Tap paper filter on solid surface.
- step 4: Hold to light and look for light through element.
- step 5: Reuse if light seen; replace if no light, extremely dirty or damaged.
- step 6: Install and reattach cover (if push mower uses a foam pre-filter, clean and install it before cover).
- step 7 (foam pre-filter, if fitted; some engines use a different type): inspect, replace if torn or brittle; wash with water and mild dish soap; rinse until water clear; lay flat to dry (sun outdoors speeds it); install over paper filter.
- mistake: NEVER add oil to a pre-filter; it damages the paper filter. Don't confuse with a primary foam filter.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Remove with 3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on engine. Dirty or damaged plug gives intermittent spark.
- confirm: Check with a spark plug tester.
- fix: Very dirty/damaged = replace. Slightly dirty good plug: clean carbon with small wire brush; verify electrode gap before reinstall; secure plug wire.
- tip: Start each season with a new plug.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- fact: check gap; reattach wire securely.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old gas leaves varnish and sticky deposits. Buy fresh gas and use within 30 days; avoid high ethanol; 4-cycle engines: min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol. Older push mowers may be 2-cycle.
- fix: Drain with siphon pump, fill fresh with stabilizer such as Sea Foam (cleans, reduces moisture). If it starts, run 10-15 minutes so treated fuel works through the system.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Plugged when not replaced regularly; mower turns over, won't start. Replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Check for kinks. Stop fuel with shut-off valve or fuel pinch-off pliers; remove line from pump inlet (or carburetor if no pump); end in container lower than tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump); turn flow on and watch.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air; repeat until clear. Replace if clog persists or line is aged/cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Used where carburetor is above fuel tank; most mowers use a vacuum pump driven by crankcase vacuum.
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches pump inlet. Remove line from carburetor into container, start; need steady or pulsating flow, else replace.
- confirm: Electronic fuel injection pump: fuel pressure gauge, compare with operator's manual spec; replace if lower than engine manufacturer's spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old gas varnish plugs jet or sticks internals.
- confirm: Confirm fuel flow to carb; remove air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into intake and start; if it starts on it, carburetor probably dirty. Don't use starter fluid (source links an article on why not).
- fix: Clean, replace faulty parts, or replace carburetor.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: On John Deere the vent is in the gas cap; plugged vent creates vacuum, mower turns over but won't start.
- confirm: Loosen cap and try to start; if it starts, suspect cap. Keep running and tighten cap: sputters, shuts down, won't restart until loosened = cap.
- fix: Replace cap.
