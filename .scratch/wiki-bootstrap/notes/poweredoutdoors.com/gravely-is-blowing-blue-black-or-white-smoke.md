## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Gravely blowing black smoke"
- alias: "Gravely blowing blue or white smoke"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, bad-head-gasket, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, rich-fuel-mixture
- fact: Black smoke = engine running rich (gas-to-air ratio higher than normal); check for airflow restrictions (plugged air filter, wrong choke). White or blue smoke = usually burning oil: incorrect oil level, worn piston rings, valve train problem, bad engine gasket, or wrong oil viscosity.
- safety: Don't continue to run a smoking mower; a small problem can become a larger one.
- pro: Low-oil smoking likely means significant engine damage; take to Gravely dealer or small engine mechanic, then decide repair vs replace (age/hours). Oil on spark plug or suspected valve train -> experienced mechanic.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: black smoke (rich); also oil in cylinder.
- fact: Reduced air makes mixture rich. Restricted airflow may make the engine pull available air from the crankcase and pull oil into the cylinder, which burns off the hot cylinder as smoke.
- tip: Start each season with a new air filter; check and clean several times a season; never run without an air filter.
- step (paper filter): 1 remove filter from housing. 2 wipe housing and cover with a clean dry cloth; don't let dirt fall into intake. 3 tap against a solid surface; do not use compressed air (can damage filter). 4 hold to light. 5 reuse if you see light; install new filter if no light, covered in oil, or damaged. 6 reinstall and reattach cover.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: black smoke when choke stays on or sticks on (less air, runs rich).
- fix: Check the choke setting; lubricate choke cable so it moves freely; replace damaged or worn choke cable.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil from not enough at last change, wrong viscosity, or an oil leak. No lubrication -> friction -> heat so intense oil burns and smokes; engine parts may melt. Adding fresh oil probably won't help; probably significant damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil raises crankcase pressure, pushing oil into cylinder or up to the air intake via the valve train; burns off when hot and smokes.
- fix: Replace air filter if oil-covered; set oil to full on dipstick.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: Replace oil-covered air filter.
- step 2: Bring oil to the full mark on the dipstick.
- method A: quickly remove and replace the drain plug to release a little oil.
- method B: no drain plug -> tip mower on its side to remove a little from the oil fill area, keeping air filter and carburetor on the high side.
- tip: Author prefers a turkey baster or oil evacuator to suck oil from the fill area (easiest, cleanest).

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: "Bad engine gasket": failing gasket leaks oil; oil smokes burning off the hot engine or muffler. Locate and replace; difficulty depends on location.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- confirm: Oil on the spark plug indicates piston ring or valve train problem; engine must be taken apart. May find cylinder wall scoring or worn rings letting oil into the combustion chamber. May choose between replacing engine or buying a new mower.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: Detected only by removing cylinder head and doing a leak-down test (experienced mechanic). Burnt valve: overheated, valve edge falls apart; indication: muffler glowing red from extreme heat or a timing problem. Valve and seat must be cut at certain angles to seat correctly and contact the block to complete the combustion chamber.
