## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "lawn mower turns over but won't start"
- alias: "mower cranks but won't start"
- causes-named: wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cranks but won't start when not getting air, spark or fuel.
- safety: Remove the spark plug wire before repairs; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts air through the carb throat so a cold engine gets more fuel than air. A cold engine won't start without choke; a warm engine won't start with choke engaged. Once warm, the lever must be off.
- confirm: If setting is right but airflow still bad, check that the choke plate isn't stuck (carburetor cleaner frees it) and the choke cable moves freely (lubricant like Sea Foam Deep Creep to free linkages). Replace a damaged/worn cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Start each season with a new filter, clean several times in the season; more often for heavy use. Check operator's manual for the filter type.
- step 1 (push mower): Engine off, remove spark plug boot; engine may be hot.
- step 2: Remove the engine shroud (usually a thumb screw).
- step 3: Remove the filter without knocking dirt into the intake; wipe housing with a dry cloth.
- step 4 (paper): Tap to knock dirt out; hold to light; replace if areas block light or it's damaged. Do not blow with an air compressor.
- step 5 (foam): Wash with dish soap and water, squeeze out, lay flat in the sun to dry, lightly lubricate with filter oil (covered, not dripping). Replace if excessively dirty or torn.
- step 6 (foam pre-cleaner): Replace if torn, excessively dirty, dry or brittle; clean with warm soap and water and dry flat in the sun.
- step 7: Reinstall filter (and pre-cleaner), then the shroud.
- fact: Paper filter shroud is usually a cap held by a couple of clips or bolts.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: Remove with a 3/4" or 5/8" socket wrench (depends on engine model). Check for burnt electrode, broken porcelain, dark-colored tip.
- fix: Replace if any; if only a little dirty clean with a wire brush; check the gap; attach the plug wire securely. Start each season with a new plug.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can break down as quickly as 30 days; old gas leaves varnish and sticky deposits. Use gas with minimum 87 octane and max 10% ethanol; avoid high ethanol.
- fix: Drain with a fuel siphon pump, refill with fresh fuel plus stabilizer like Sea Foam, run the engine so it works through the system.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Most mowers use an inline filter between fuel lines. If none is inline, there's probably a filter at the bottom of the tank; drain the tank to replace it. Replace old/dirty filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Follow the line from the tank to the carb for kinks. Then shut off fuel with the shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers; remove the end furthest from the tank into a container and check flow.
- fix: Remove the line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air; repeat. Replace if not cleared or aged/cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Used when the carburetor is higher than the tank; most are vacuum pumps using crankcase vacuum. Replace if cracked or failing.
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches the inlet port first (else check line/filter blockage). Then remove the line from the carb into a container, start fuel flow and the engine; expect steady or pulsating flow; replace the pump if not.
- fact: Electronic fuel injection pump: measure with a fuel pressure gauge against the manual specification; replace if below the engine manufacturer's spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old gas leaves varnish that may plug the fuel jet or stick internal components.
- confirm: Confirm good fuel flow to the carb; remove the air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into the intake and start. If it starts then runs sluggish/dies once the cleaner burns off, the carb is dirty. Don't use starter fluid.
- fix: Disassemble and clean, or replace the carburetor.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Tank vents through the gas cap; plugged vent makes a vacuum.
- confirm: Loosen the cap and try to start; if it starts suspect the cap. Tighten while running: if it sputters, shuts down and won't restart until the cap is loosened, replace the cap.
