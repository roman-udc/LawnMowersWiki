## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: lawn mower batteries are smaller than car batteries; cold can damage or ruin them in winter storage
- fact: winter care options: covering, storing, packaging, or removing the battery from the equipment; removing it is called the best way
- fact: batteries can freeze when not fully charged or stored in extreme cold. A half-charged battery freezes into a block of ice at -10 F, overnight. A frozen battery is unlikely to charge again; frozen batteries are more likely to die and should be replaced.
- fact: freezing point by charge (12 V / 6 V reading / freeze temp): 100% 12.78 V / 6.39 V / -80 F; 92% 12.66 / 6.33 / -71.3 F; 85% 12.57 / 6.28 / -62 F; 62% 12.27 / 6.13 / -16 F; 50% / - / -10 F; 40% 11.97 / 5.98 / +5 F; 20% 11.67 / 5.84 / +19 F; zero charge +20 F. Higher charge = lower freezing point.
- fact: dirty terminals corrode faster (worse in cold wet winters); cleaning prolongs charge potential, storing ability and life; clean before storage and again before spring use
- tip: leaving the battery connected during long storage - energy drain over time plus cold ruins it
- fact: charging during winter: charge when not in use (needs an outlet near storage); depleted batteries are much harder to charge and don't last as long; charging occasionally is better than letting it drain fully
- fact: when to charge: early spring (hook up charger and test before first mow); during winter if using the mower - before each use (storing the mower in the cold while connected drains the battery quickly); if the battery dies
- fact: chargers: never charge a mower battery with more than a 10 amp charger; trickle charger recommended; 10 amp charger takes about one hour for a full charge; trickle units take eight hours or more, sometimes all day
- fact: products named: TOWER TOP 2/10/25a 12V smart charger (author's top pick; safety features over-voltage, overheat, overtime charging, short circuit, reverse polarity); LST trickle charger five-stage automatic (LED indicator, reverse polarity/overheat/overcharge/spark protection; cons: smaller clamps harder to use, reportedly lasts not much longer than two years of consistent charging)
- fact: dead battery after winter: check terminal connections, charge, test voltage before replacing; dead batteries may never charge again because they lose conductivity
- confirm: multimeter on DC; red to red/positive, black to black/negative; common riding mower battery 12 volts; fully charged about 12.7 volts
- fact: replace battery if: cracked or leaking case (acid hazard), bulged case, corroded or damaged terminals, won't hold a charge after charging, loose or broken cables (replace cables too; both if battery also damaged)

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- step 1: remove the battery for winter - rubber gloves and safety glasses on first
- step 2: open the body cavity (riding mower: battery under hood or seat; push mower: visible terminals)
- step 3: pick screwdriver, loosen the terminal clamp screws (one or two each)
- step 4: remove the negative cable/clamp first, then the other clamp
- step 5: clean the connection points with baking soda solution and Q-tips or metal brush
- step 6: wipe debris and moisture with a clean cloth
- step 7: gently lift battery out of casing and put in a cool, dry place
- tools: clean cloth; metal brush; phillips or flat screwdriver; water and vinegar solution; needle nose pliers; rubber gloves; safety glasses; soft cotton Q-tips
- fact: store indoors, cool and dry: storage closets, crawl spaces, spare bedrooms, armoires; avoid wet/humid places: under sinks, bathrooms, laundry room cabinets/shelves (humidity accelerates corrosion)
- tip: consider a battery terminal protectant product during storage

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: safety gear for eyes and skin (acid, shock)
- step 2: access battery and terminals (screwdriver to uncover)
- step 3: leave battery in casing with terminal cables attached
- step 4: connect charger positive (red/plus) first
- step 5: connect negative (black) next; positive is the last one off and the first one on
- step 6: touch only the rubber coating of cables/clamps
- step 7: set voltage (usually 12 V) and amps: start with 2, work up to no more than 10
- step 8: if the charger has a gauge, stay connected until full
- tip: a charging source can be a car battery or AC outlet (jumpstart or charge)
- tip: dies quickly after charging = replace

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step 1: multimeter set for DC; probes to matching terminals; fully charged about 12.7 volts (12 V)

## page: causes/bad-battery
- symptom: cracked/leaking/bulged case, corroded terminals, won't hold charge (see replacement list above)

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: vibration loosens terminal connections; winter moisture corrodes them
- fix: clean with baking soda and water (one part each) with wire brush (rusted/severe) or toothbrush (dirt/minor), scrub base and top of terminal, wipe damp then dry cloth; replace if damage severe; tighten screws and clamps

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: storing the mower connected in cold drains the battery; frozen if not fully charged
