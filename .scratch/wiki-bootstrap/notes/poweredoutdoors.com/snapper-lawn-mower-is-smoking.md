## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Snapper lawn mower smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, bad-head-gasket, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, rich-fuel-mixture
- safety: shut the mower off (if it didn't already quit); do not run it until you determine the type of smoking, to avoid more damage. Not all smoke is catastrophic: can be excess gas burning or melting internal parts.
- fact: smoke color: blue or white = excess oil burning (damaged piston rings, valve train, engine gasket leaks, or oil pushed into the cylinder by a plugged air filter). Black = excess fuel burning (plugged air filter raising fuel concentration; if not the filter, look for another air restriction).
- pro: internal engine problems need a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: engine runs rich, black smoke; or filter restricts all air and the engine draws air from the crankcase, pulling oil that burns in the cylinder; smoke itself can plug the filter.
- tip: always run with a filter; replace annually; check/clean several times a season; more often in dusty conditions.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step (paper): remove from housing; tap against solid surface; hold to light, reuse if light shows through paper, else replace; reinstall with cover.
- step (foam): remove; wipe housing with clean dry cloth without letting dirt into intake; inspect: brown areas, tears, dry and brittle: replace; if good, wash with mild dish soap and water, rinse until water runs clear and soap gone, lay flat to air dry (sun/breeze speeds), lightly saturate with foam filter oil (not dripping; squeeze out or paper-towel excess; new filters also get oil), reinstall with cover.

## page: causes/rich-fuel-mixture
- fact: engine unable to get good air runs rich and emits black smoke.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: engine maker's oil quantity is a requirement not a recommendation.
- symptom (low): friction, heat, oil burns, parts melt, smoke; likely engine damage already; adding oil may not solve it; see experienced small engine mechanic.
- tip: check oil before each mowing.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil raises crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and even the air intake through the valve train; burns off as smoke.
- fix: remove a little oil via drain plug or oil filter, or oil evacuator or turkey baster at the oil fill; recheck with dipstick; adjust up or down until correct; check the air filter for oil or smoke plugging and replace if so.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: a bad gasket lets engine oil leak; oil on a hot surface such as the muffler burns and smokes.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- confirm: simple check: remove spark plug(s) and look for oil on the plug; oil indicates possible piston ring or valve train problem (doesn't say which).
- fact: piston ring problem or cylinder wall scoring lets oil into the combustion chamber; requires engine tear-down to find.
- pro: leak-down and compression checks and teardown by small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: an overheated Snapper can develop a burnt valve; only determined by removing the cylinder head and a leak-down test; mechanic has to cut the seat and valve correctly.

## page: procedures/check-compression
- fact: Source only mentions leak-down and compression checks by a mechanic.
