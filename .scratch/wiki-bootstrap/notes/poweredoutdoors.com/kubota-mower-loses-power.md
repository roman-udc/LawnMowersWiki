## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Kubota mower bogs down / loses power"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: power loss happens when the engine isn't getting sufficient air or fuel (low oil, clogged air passages, plugged air filter, dirty carb, restricted fuel components), or is under load (plugged deck, too-fast ground speed). (Title says 13 reasons, heading says 14; 13 listed.)

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- tip: replace annually, clean several times in the season; more often in very dusty conditions.
- fact: the cover is often held by clips, knobs or wing nuts.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove the cover (clips, knobs, wing nuts).
- step 2: carefully remove the filter without letting dirt fall into the intake.
- step 3: wipe out remaining dirt from the housing with a dry clean rag.
- step 4: tap the filter on a hard surface.
- step 5: hold to a light; reuse if light shines through; replace if not, if damaged, or if oil-covered.
- step 6: install and reattach cover.
- fact: for a paper-style filter.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gasoline breaks down and is less effective after 30 days; ethanol and moisture leave gummy deposits that restrict flow.
- fix: drain tank, fill fresh. Most gas Kubota mowers need unleaded, minimum 87 octane, max 10% ethanol. Add additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans the fuel system). Use within 30 days or add additive.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: strains fuel leaving the tank; replace annually in routine maintenance; inexpensive part that prevents fuel supply issues and engine damage.
- procedure: shut off flow with the fuel shut-off valve or fuel pinch pliers if no valve; pull the filter from the lines and install a new one; the arrow on the filter must point in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: shut off fuel flow with the shut-off valve, or use fuel pinch pliers.
- step 2: pull the filter from the fuel lines.
- step 3: install the new filter with the arrow toward the carburetor (away from tank).
- tools: fuel pinch pliers

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove the end of a section of hose into a container, restore flow and check flow.
- fix: stop flow, remove the section, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow with compressed air; if the blockage stays or the line is dry and cracked replace with a line of the same length and diameter.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- fact: replace with same length and diameter.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: before tearing it apart, remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and start the engine; if it runs but won't stay running, clean the carburetor.
- fact: carb regulates fuel and air into the cylinder; residues from old fuel collect in it.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: inspect tip for carbon, dirt and oil buildup. Replace a dirty or damaged one (recommended); a plug only dirty and not very dark can be cleaned with a wire brush and reused. Intermittent spark causes power loss.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: can be low from underfilling at the last oil change, an oil leak, or burning oil from wrong viscosity. Low oil causes friction, extreme heat, a very hot engine, smoking from burning oil and melting parts; can mean major repair or engine replacement.
- confirm: remove dipstick, wipe with clean rag, reinsert, remove and read; must be at full on dipstick. Add fresh oil to full; don't overfill.
- tip: check oil before each use.
- pro: if problems continue after refilling, an experienced small engine mechanic should diagnose; low oil may have caused internal damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: causes smoking: pressure pushes oil into the cylinder through the valve train; bluish-white smoke when burned; thick smoke can plug the air filter; check air filter and spark plug and clean or replace. Continued running can cause seal damage, hydrolock and a bent piston rod (Source's wording).
- fix: remove a little oil with an oil evacuator, drain plug, or turkey baster. Drain plug: loosen and quickly tighten so only a little oil escapes. Also from the oil filter or fill area.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: loosen the drain plug and quickly retighten to let a little oil out; or remove oil via the oil filter or fill area with a turkey baster or oil evacuator.
- tools: turkey baster, oil evacuator

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins plug with mud and grass clippings; clean and replace broken fins. Also clear grass, dirt and debris around the engine and shroud, and make sure the heat shield is securely in place.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: extra load in thick, wet or tall grass; slow down, including on an incline.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: wet grass makes the engine work harder and clumps/sticks under the deck; mow when dry. Tall grass can bog the mower; double or triple cut: first cut with the deck at its highest setting, then lower the cutting height for the next cut.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: a packed deck makes the engine work harder turning blades through debris; scrape regularly; also helps cut quality.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades on top of a packed deck make power loss much worse; sharpen or replace.
