## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- fact: Electric mowers are simpler than gas but need basic maintenance. At season start and in heavy-use months inspect, clean and maintain: blades, battery, cables, springs, bolts and screws, cord.
- fact: Check blades often. Look for dirt/debris (brush off with dry cloth), rust (replace blades if starting to form), chips or cracks (probably replace). If blades are abnormally dull or make an unusual noise when cutting, stop using immediately.
- tip: Replace blades about every six months even if they look good (author's general advice).
- safety: Remove the power source first (take out battery or unplug cord) before working near blades; wear gloves and protective gear.
- fact: Battery: each season clean and inspect; make sure it is charged and holds a charge; clean dirt from terminals on both sides to ensure good contact with the mower's battery posts; check for corrosion on surface and contacts; buy a new charger if needed, especially if water-damaged.
- fact: Keep the battery charged to at least 20% at all times, including in winter when not in use. Lithium-ion batteries especially need frequent charging to avoid degradation.
- fact: Replace the battery if swollen, leaking, won't charge even with a new charger, or was drained to zero and doesn't seem to recharge.
- fact: Cord (corded mowers): extension cords and power cables. Inspect before each use for cracks in insulation or bare wires (shock and fire risk); replace a damaged cord. Keep mower and extension cord in a dry place in rainy seasons because wet cords have been known to cause fires.
- fact: Refer to owner's manual; model number can be found on manufacturer website.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: Keep mower battery at 20% charge or higher at all times, also in winter; lithium-ion needs frequent charging to avoid degradation. Replace if swollen, leaking, won't charge with a new charger, or drained to zero and won't recharge. Clean terminals, check for corrosion.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- tools: stone, sandpaper, file (or electric grinder, used carefully); gloves
- step 1: Remove power source (battery out / unplug cord).
- step 2: Sharpen with a stone (grind away spots and nicks, sharpen edge carefully), sandpaper (carefully grind each side until no flaws; don't grind too much; removes rust and build-up), and/or file (smooth and clean up edges, use after the other steps).
- step 3: Wipe blades down afterwards to remove metal filings.
- fact: Blades should be sharp before every use; sharpen every season or so; replace when too dull. A new coat of oil before winter storage keeps them fresh.
- pro: If unsure about sharpening, take the mower to a technician or just replace blades.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: Clean the underside after every use to prevent rust and keep performance; clippings and dirt cake onto the blade and make the mower ineffective.
- tools: can of compressed air, light machine oil spray or penetrant (WD-40 named), rags, sponges, brush, gloves.
- step 1: Remove battery or unplug cord. step 2: brush top of deck with stiff-bristle brush; rag/sponge on clippings areas. step 3: compressed air on the underside; all-purpose cleaner where needed. step 4: wipe underside with damp cloth.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Electric mower seasonal inspection list: blades, battery, cables, springs, bolts/screws, cord; clean underside after every use.
