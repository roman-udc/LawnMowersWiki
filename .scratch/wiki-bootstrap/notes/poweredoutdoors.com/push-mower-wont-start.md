## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "push mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, wrong-fuel, wrong-choke-setting, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-ignition-coil, bad-battery, bad-charger, blown-fuse, bad-recoil-starter, bad-safety-switch
- fact: Needs fuel, air, spark. Routine service: replace fuel filter, air filter, spark plug.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Most push mowers use a 4-cycle engine: unleaded gasoline octane 87+, ethanol 10% or less. Older mowers may use a 2-cycle engine requiring oil/fuel mix (gas cap often says; check manual). Never run straight gas in a 2-cycle needing mix; likely engine replacement. 2-cycle also needs 10% or lower ethanol.
- fix: Fresh unleaded 87+; 2-cycle: mix with 2-cycle engine oil at the manufacturer's ratio.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: No gas, no start.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts airflow so the mixture runs rich for a cold start; ON/CLOSED for cold engine, OFF/OPEN for warm. Starting warm with choke engaged won't start; forgetting to turn it off makes the mower quit. Check lever and make sure the choke plate isn't stuck. Some push mowers have automatic chokes.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas breaks down after 30 days; ethanol separates and sinks with moisture; evaporation leaves gummy residue clogging the fuel system. Buy fresh and use within 30 days; if more than a 30-day supply, stabilize (Sea Foam Motor Treatment). Fix: remove old fuel, add fresh.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plugged vent makes a tank vacuum. Replace cap if can't clean/open vent.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Loose connection or wrong gap can cause misfire/no start; carbon or oil fouling causes starting problems.
- fix: Remove and inspect for carbon or cracked porcelain insulator; replace if damaged or can't be cleaned; check connections and gap per manufacturer.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Push mower uses a thin rectangular paper or foam filter. Starved engine loses power or won't start; can cause extensive internal damage (maybe a new mower).
- step: Unscrew thumbscrews to remove the cover, remove filter without knocking dirt into intake, wipe with clean dry cloth.
- step (paper): tap on solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light shows; replace if no light, extremely dirty, or oil-covered; reinstall cover.
- step (foam): wash with water and mild dish soap; rinse; lay flat to dry (sun speeds); when dry lightly coat with filter oil; replace if brittle, brown spots, or torn; reinstall cover.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Mostly inline between fuel lines; some attach to the tank. Replace once a year (more often if dirty fuel or heavy use). Arrow on an inline filter shows flow direction.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow with compressed air until free of dirt/gummy residue; replace if dry and cracking.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Crusty buildup and gummy deposits from old ethanol fuel; engine runs rough or won't start. Clean yourself if somewhat mechanical or take to a repair shop.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Coil sparks the plug; bad coil = no start; can also cause start-then-die when hot.
- confirm: After confirming plug works, check coil continuity with a multimeter; replace if break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fact: Electric start push mowers have a small battery; some must be removed to charge, others have a wall-plug outlet. Replace battery and/or charger when it won't charge or hold charge.

## page: causes/bad-charger
- fact: See above; replace battery and/or charger.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fix: Replace with same amperage; if it keeps blowing, repair shop to find root cause.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fix: Pull-start may not start with broken recoil; restring rope; may have broken clips, pulley, or spring; sometimes replacing the whole assembly is most cost effective.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Operator presence control kills engine when operator releases the safety bar (bail lever); faulty switch fails to sense the operator and won't start.
- confirm: Can temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: Never operate without the safety switch installed. Replace a bad switch.
