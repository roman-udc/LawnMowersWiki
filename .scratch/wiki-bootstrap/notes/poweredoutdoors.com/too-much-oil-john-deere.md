## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: effects of too much oil: overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets, hydrolock, poor running/smoke, fouled plug (problems/engine-overheats, problems/mower-is-smoking, problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start).
- fact (overheating): crankshaft and rod push through more oil than designed; crankcase pressure rises, internal parts under load; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil may not flow properly; aluminum engine material intensifies it. Can pop a valve guide or valve seat out of the block; needs an experienced small engine mechanic.
- fact (seal damage): pressure can blow out engine seals so oil leaks; if not noticed engine can run out of oil and be permanently damaged: heat can spin a rod, weld it to the crankshaft, break the rod out the side of the block, or weld the piston to the cylinder (seize).
- fact (lock-up): oil gets into the cylinder; if oil gets past worn rings it can lock the engine = hydrolocked; piston can't compress oil; piston may bend when starting.
- fact (runs poorly/smokes): oil reaches the plug and fouls it; oil enters via the valve train and burns in the cylinder: thick whitish-blue stinky smoke; with a clogged air filter the engine may pull air and oil out of the crankcase.
- fact (gaskets): excessive heat may blow gaskets; gasket replacement on a twin-cylinder engine costs more than on a push mower because the engine must be removed (more labor).
- fact: oil can also get into the carburetor through the valve train, then into the air filter, preventing the engine from running.
- fact: small-engine oil pan is very small; little room for error.
- safety: remove spark plug boot(s) and let the engine cool before troubleshooting.
- fix: drain a little oil to the engine manufacturer's level; if lucky that fixes it; if not the engine may be damaged. Don't keep running when problems noticed; a technician can test whether it's repairable or should be replaced.
- tip: check twice; only add what the manufacturer requires.
- pro: technician if not fixed by oil change/simple repair.

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- symptom: engine turns over very hard or won't turn; oil gets past rings into the combustion chamber; pulling the rope or starting won't let the piston move; piston may bend.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 0: remove the spark plug wire for safety.
- step 1 (drain plug or valve port): find it on the side of the engine by the dipstick or bottom of the oil pan; have a drain pan; remove plug for a second, replace, check level.
- step 2 (oil filter): loosen or remove the filter to drain a little; rag ready.
- step 3 (fill hole): many push mower small engines have no drain plug; tip the mower to drain a little out of the fill hole.
- step 4 (oil extractor/evacuator pump): tube inserted through the oil fill hole.
- step 5 (turkey baster): for small amounts; don't reuse for cooking.
- tools: drain pan, rag, oil evacuator, turkey baster.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: to avoid overfill: find crankcase capacity in the owner's manual (or search online). Most push mower small engines take about 3/4 quart; v-twin about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" engines (about 34 hp or larger) take 3 quarts.
- tip: add only 1/2 quart at a time and check; near full add a little at a time and check the gauge/dipstick.

## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: too much oil can prevent running: hydrolock, fouled plug, oil through valve train into carb and air filter.
- causes-named: overfilled-oil, hydrolocked-engine, fouled-spark-plug

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: whitish-blue stinky smoke from oil burning in cylinder (overfilled oil).
- causes-named: overfilled-oil

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: capacities: push mower small engine ~3/4 quart; v-twin ~2 quarts; big block (~34 hp+) 3 quarts.
