## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Ferris deck belt keeps coming off the pulleys"
- alias: "belt keeps breaking"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, wrong-belt, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Belt breaks or comes off when worn, stretched, or shredded; belt keeper damaged; bearing failed in spindle housing or pulley; insufficient tension; rust or oil on belt; debris collecting on the deck.
- safety: Before working on or near the deck, remove the spark plug wire and ignition key; follow the Ferris operator's manual.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: Deck belt is a wear item. Worn belt slips, fails to turn blades at high speed (bad cut), can loosen and roll off pulleys. Ferris models have one or two deck belts.
- symptom: cracks, glazed shiny appearance, stretched belt, belt sitting deep in the pulley grooves -> replace.
- mistake: Buying a comparable belt at a hardware store; may look the same but not always right type, size or quality. Author prefers OEM belt.
- tip: Deck belt affects quality of cut.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Some Ferris deck belts have belt keepers sitting 1/8" to 1/4" from the pulley to stop the belt slipping off. Slack belt hits the keeper and bends the metal, increasing chance of coming off.
- fix: Check keeper and bracket; bend a bent bracket back into place (hammer or other tools); replace if you can't reshape it so it sits correctly.

## page: causes/wrong-belt
- fact: Shredding belt edges: first check you're using the right belt and it is routed correctly around deck pulleys. Decal on the mower shows belt layout; else operator's manual or parts diagram.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Mud and debris packed in pulley grooves stretches the belt and can roll it off or snap it. Remove dirt, stones, grass from grooves and under pulleys.
- fact: Debris on top of the deck can interfere with the tensioner pulley; dry debris on top of the deck can catch fire from belt heat. Keep belt area clean.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fact: Failed spindle housing bearing makes the blade wobble; at speed excessive vibration can knock belt off.
- confirm: Remove ignition key and spark plug wire, access under the deck, wear work gloves, hold each end of the blade and rock up and down; feel extra vertical movement or hear knocking while the blade is secure to the cutter housing -> bearing bad.
- fix: Remove blade, disassemble spindle housing (cutter housing), inspect spindle, replace bad parts. Buy complete spindle assembly or replace bearing in housing.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Pulley should sit flat and parallel to the deck; failed bearing lets it angle and wobble, with vibration rolling belt off.
- confirm: Slowly spin each pulley by hand; resistance, wobble, or noise from bearing = bad; replace pulley.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Ferris deck has tensioner bracket and spring. Too much tension damages deck components; too little makes belt vibrate, slip and come off.
- fix: Check tensioner, bushing and spring are in good condition; remove debris restricting the tensioner.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil spilled/leaked on the deck lubricates the belt so it slips off; prolonged oil makes the belt swell. Find and repair the leak, remove oil from deck and components, replace belt.
- fact: Rust transferring to the belt dries, cracks and breaks it. Remove rust; replace pulleys/components that can't be fully derusted; install new belt after cleanup.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: Confirm correct belt and routing (decal / manual / parts diagram); check for brackets the belt rubs against with deck engaged (shiny wear spot); adjust brackets so no rubbing; bracket with bushing may need bushing replaced; replace unadjustable bracket; install new belt after confirming no rub points.

## page: guides/belt-types
- fact: Prefer OEM belt over hardware-store belt.
