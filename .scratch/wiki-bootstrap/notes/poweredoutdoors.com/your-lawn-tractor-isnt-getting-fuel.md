## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact (gas): ethanol (corn-based) fine for most vehicles, not small engines; ethanol-containing gas can break down as quickly as 30 days; ethanol and moisture separate and sink to the tank bottom; burns hotter than gasoline -> potential engine damage; leaves gummy deposits that clog the fuel system.
- tip: stabilizer for gas not used within 30 days; Sea Foam Motor Treatment stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system.
- fact (diesel): diesel sitting a long time forms solids and condensation in the fuel system -> clogging, degradation. Old diesel looks dark, and the fuel filter looks dark as it filters out solids; dark filter = old fuel, replace with fresh. Solids can form in diesel tanks at fuel stations: buy from a busy station.
- fix: siphon old fuel into approved container for recycling; refill with fresh fuel + additive to stabilize and clean.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: dirt and sediment clog when not replaced. Replace; arrow on side points in the direction of fuel flow: away from tank and toward carburetor; wrong direction = flow problem.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: use fuel shut-off valve at bottom of fuel tank, or fuel hose pinch pliers; pick a section; stop flow; remove end furthest from tank into a container lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without pump); start flow, watch; poor flow -> shut off and remove section.
- fix: spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air; replace line if can't clear or if dry and cracked.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: fuel in pump degrades components or normal wear; can't draw pressure to pump fuel to the carb.
- confirm: confirm fuel reaches pump inlet port line; remove carb fuel line into container; start engine; steady or pulsating flow expected; none -> replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake, start; runs a bit then shuts down = clean or replace carb.
- fix: clean if mechanical; else replace or lawn tractor repair shop. When buying: have engine model and spec number (not mower model and serial number).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap vent plugged -> vacuum prevents fuel leaving tank.
- confirm: run with and without cap; runs fine without cap but shuts off with cap after a while = plugged.
- fix: try cleaning; else new cap.
