- fact: Won't start due to lack of air, fuel or spark.
- safety: Wait for the engine to cool and remove spark plug wire(s) before repairs; follow the Husqvarna operator's manual.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: Click or hum when turning the key points to the starter solenoid; also battery, safety switches, ignition switch (see cause sections).
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, incorrect-starting-procedure, faulty-charging-system

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: Fuel use may be higher than thought due to a fuel leak or faulty fuel gauge.
- fix: Check for leaks (repair/replace leaking component); add fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Old gas is often the main culprit when the mower runs sluggishly, won't start or dies. Ethanol draws moisture; ethanol/water leaves varnish and gummy deposits.
- fact: Gas breaks down as soon as 30 days. Use Sea Foam Motor Treatment if more gas than can be used in 30 days (author adds it to all small engine fuel - opinion).
- fix: Drain the tank (a manual siphon pump is inexpensive and works well); refill with fresh gas plus additive to clean the fuel system, reduce moisture and stabilize.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- brand: Husqvarna zero turns require unleaded gas, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Always use low-ethanol or ethanol-free fuel; high ethanol can cause significant engine damage.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter between fuel lines strains fuel out of the tank. Replace once a year; more often if a tank of very dirty fuel was used (better: don't use dirty fuel, drain it).
- fix: Replace; arrow on filter points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Sticky deposits from old fuel narrow the line.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner then blow compressed air; if not clearable, buy a new line of right diameter and length. Replace dry/cracking lines even without a clog.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Pump works against gravity to get fuel to the carb. Most Husqvarna zero turns use a vacuum-style pump; fuel degrades it over time; a pump that can't use engine vacuum to draw fuel must be replaced; leaking pump must be replaced.
- confirm: Shut off fuel with the shut-off valve under the tank or clamps pinching the line. Remove the hose from the pump inlet port into a container lower than the tank; start fuel flow. Flow = fuel reaches pump; none = restriction upstream. Shut off flow, refit hose. Remove the hose from the carburetor into a container, start fuel flow and the engine; look for steady or pulsating flow. Shut off, reattach.
- fix: Replace if no constant or pulsating flow.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old fuel clogs passages and leaves varnish that sticks internal parts.
- fix: Clean the carb and replace parts that can't be cleaned; needs some mechanical ability and small-parts comfort; otherwise take to a small engine shop or replace the carb assembly.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Husqvarna zero turn tank vents through the fuel cap; no venting forms a vacuum.
- confirm: Run with and without the cap. If it won't start with the cap on, loosen it (may hear vacuum release). If it then starts, suspect venting. Tighten cap and run to recreate (shuts off, won't restart until cap loosened).
- fix: Replace the fuel cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Loose wires, wrong gap or fouled plugs cause intermittent running/starting.
- confirm: Remove plug; look for carbon buildup or cracked porcelain insulator.
- fix: Replace if damaged, worn or very dark; else clean the tip with a small wire brush; gap correctly and secure wires.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Plugged filter can cause no-start and also overheating or smoke. Replace annually, check regularly.
- step 1: Remove the filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe housing and cap with a dry cloth, no dirt into intake.
- step 3: Tap on a solid surface.
- step 4: Hold to a light; reuse if light shows; replace if not, extremely dirty or damaged.
- step 5: Install.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Disconnect the battery, remove corroded components; use a baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) and a small metal brush; make sure wiring and components are secure with good connections.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Multimeter; reading under 12.7 volts means put on a charger. Replace if it can't hold a charge.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- confirm: Test switches with a multimeter; may temporarily bypass for troubleshooting only.
- safety: Never operate without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: Test with a multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor. Click or hum on key turn: check the solenoid. Another sign: a wire attached to the solenoid gets hot and smokes or melts.
- fix: Test (Source links a Husqvarna solenoid test); replace if bad.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts airflow for cold starts; after warm-up it must be off (push the choke knob down).
- fix: Cold start: full choke (pull choke knob up); throttle halfway between slow and fast; key to start, release when running; set choke to off; idle at half throttle a few minutes before full throttle.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Safety interlock requirements: sit in the operator's seat; set the parking brake if there is an independent brake lever (some models incorporate the brake into the steering: engaged when levers are in the outward position); steering control levers outward (neutral); PTO (blade) switch off.

## page: procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower
- step 1: Sit in the operator's seat.
- step 2: Set the parking brake (or control levers outward on models where that engages the brake).
- step 3: Steering levers outward in neutral.
- step 4: PTO (blade) switch off.
- step 5: Cold engine: full choke, throttle halfway, turn key to start and release.
- step 6: Choke off; idle at half throttle a few minutes, then full throttle to mow.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Not a main no-start reason but contributes to a weak battery. Bad stator or alternator among several electrical parts. Source links an ohmmeter charging system test.
- pro: Take to the local Husqvarna zero-turn dealer; otherwise you throw parts at it; electrical parts are expensive and usually non-returnable.
