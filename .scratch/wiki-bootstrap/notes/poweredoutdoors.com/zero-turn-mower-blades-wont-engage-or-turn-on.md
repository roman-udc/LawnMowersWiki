## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "zero turn blades won't engage"
- alias: "blades won't turn on when I pull the PTO switch"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: check the components that power the clutch plus deck failures.
- safety: never reach under the deck until precautions are met: remove ignition key and spark plug wires; wait for all parts to stop moving; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: deck belt rotates on idler pulleys; a worn belt fails to grip and turn pulleys, so blades don't turn or turn slowly. Belt grip affects blade speed, needed for suction under the deck.
- confirm: inspect for cracks, fraying, glazed appearance; sits deep in pulley grooves.
- fix: replace if worn, even if it isn't the main cause.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: belt can come off the pulleys. A tensioner arm and spring hold the idler pulley in line; bracket typically has a pulley on one side and a spring on the other. The hole where the spring attaches can wear, or the spring can wear, letting the belt loosen and vibrate off. (Source links to a separate article on why zero-turn belts come off.)

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: bad bearing lets a pulley sit not flat or parallel to the deck (one side higher); the belt can roll off. Replace a pulley with a bad bearing.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: usually a knob that sends battery voltage to power the clutch. Check continuity with a multimeter; replace if break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers power from engine to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace a worn or faulty clutch that fails to power the blades. (Source links "A Look into How Lawn Mower Clutches Work".)

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: electric clutch uses battery power; weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 volts; charge if lower.
- step (charge): access battery and terminals (under hood or seat; screwdriver may be needed to uncover; do not remove battery from casing); wear eye and skin protection; connect charger positive (red/plus) first then negative (black); don't touch anything not rubber-coated; set charger to 12 volts; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge best.
- fact: if it won't hold a charge replace (hardware, automotive store or dealer); bring old battery; core fees average $20.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- fact: steps above; start 2 A, max 10 A, slow charge best; positive cable on first.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: seat switch under the seat senses the operator; part of operator presence system; blades won't engage without the operator in the seat; a failed seat switch means blades won't engage.
- confirm: multimeter or temporary bypass.
- safety: do not operate without the safety switch installed.

## page: procedures/replace-the-seat-switch
- fact: seat switch is under the seat; test with multimeter first.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: no battery power to the clutch may be a blown fuse; replace with same size; if it keeps blowing take to dealer or shop for the root cause.

## page: guides/how-clutches-work
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to blades by engaging the drive belt; electric clutch fed through the PTO switch from the battery.
