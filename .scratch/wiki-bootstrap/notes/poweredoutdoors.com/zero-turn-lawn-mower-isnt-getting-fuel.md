## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: If every fuel component checks out and it still won't start, the problem isn't fuel; see a "won't start" list.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas: most contains ethanol (corn-based). Use no more than 10% ethanol. Gas breaks down as soon as 30 days; ethanol and attracted moisture separate and leave gummy residue on evaporation, clogging the system; ethanol softens plastics and corrodes metal.
- fact: Diesel zero turns: long-stored diesel forms solids in the tank and condensation that clog the system and damage components; old diesel degrades and looks dark. Buy from a busy station that cycles diesel often (author prefers separate diesel pumps with constant diesel truck traffic; had a bad experience at a station busy with gas cars - filter turned black).
- fix: Siphon out old fuel into an approved container for recycling; refill with fresh; add additive. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment (petroleum based, safe in diesel and gasoline zero turns); alternative STA-BIL if Sea Foam unavailable outside the US.
- tip: If you can't use fuel in the first month, add stabilizer.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Change annually at service. Install with arrow on the housing pointing away from the tank and toward the carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Gummy residue from old fuel clogs lines.
- confirm: Stop/start flow via the shut-off valve or by crimping. Disconnect the end furthest from the tank, put in a container, start flow; no flow = blocked. Hose must run downhill during the test.
- fix: With flow off, remove the clogged section, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air, reinstall. Replace if not cleared, or if dry and cracked.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most zero turns use a vacuum pump; needed because the tank is lower than the carburetor.
- confirm: First confirm fuel reaches the pump inlet; then remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start the engine, watch for pulsating or steady flow. Replace the pump if inadequate.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Remove air filter, spray carb cleaner in the intake, start. If it fires, runs, then shuts off, take apart the carb to clean and replace damaged parts.
- fix: Clean (if mechanical, small parts OK), have a small engine shop clean it, or swap in a new carburetor.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Small hole in cap lets air through; plugged vent makes a vacuum restricting flow.
- confirm: Run with and without the cap. If it runs well without it, replace the cap and let it run; if it shuts down, cap likely not venting.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap; if that fails buy a new cap.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: See old-fuel stabilizer notes; diesel stored long forms solids and condensation.
