## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "lawn mower starts then dies"
- alias: "mower won't stay running"
- alias: "mower dies in the middle of mowing"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, bad-ignition-coil, dirty-cooling-fins, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: applies to single- and twin-cylinder engines, push, riding and zero-turn
- tip: if it starts, you are getting spark, so replacing the spark plug may not fix anything; not the first place to start
- safety: remove spark plug wires before repairs
- fact (summary): starts but won't stay running because of fuel restriction, too much oil, plugged air filter, dirty plug, clogged carburetor, or engine running too rich; if all checked, go to repair shop

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: simple things get missed; check tank and fill with fresh fuel; may not have noticed if fuel gauge stopped working, a fuel leak developed, or you forgot last fill

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gasoline usually lasts only about 30 days before it breaks down; loses ability to run well and hot as chemicals decay
- fact: most gasoline has ethanol, which collects moisture from air; water can evaporate leaving residue in tank that clogs fuel system
- fix: drain tank, fill fresh, add additive to stabilize and clean
- tip: mix stabilizer with fuel in a fuel can before adding to tank; author likes Sea Foam (cleaning agents; good for carburetor and valve train; helps keep carbon down on top of piston)
- fact: 2-cycle engine: unleaded gasoline plus 2-cycle oil mix; 4-cycle: unleaded gasoline, do not mix with oil

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: customers bring mowers to the shop for a plugged air filter they could have changed; check air filter regularly (routine maintenance)

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: dirty/old/contaminated fuel clogs the filter and starves engine
- tip: use fresh clean fuel; replace the fuel filter annually

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: old fuel leaves varnish and gummy buildup; line can also be pinched or kinked; author has seen lines clogged with leaves, pine needles and plastic shavings from a new fuel tank
- fix: inspect for kinks; replace damaged, dry or cracked lines; for a clog remove line, spray carburetor cleaner to loosen, blow compressed air through, repeat as needed; if unable, install new line of same length and diameter

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump exists if tank sits lower than carburetor; most riding mowers have one, most push mowers don't
- symptom: leaking fuel at seams; internal damage hard to spot, so test
- confirm: need to start/stop fuel flow (fuel shut-off valve, or fuel pinch pliers on line)
- step 1: with fuel shut off, remove line from pump inlet port into a container lower than the fuel supply; start flow; fuel should run out; if not, look for blockage in line and filter; reattach line
- step 2: remove line from carburetor into a container; start mower; should get steady or pulsating flow; if none, shut off mower and fuel valve, check for blockage in the line to carburetor; if nothing plugged, replace pump

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carburetor regulates air and fuel for combustion; deposits from bad fuel; if beyond cleaning, replace
- confirm: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into air intake, start; if it fires then dies again, get inside the carburetor

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, sockets, ratchet, carb cleaner, thick wire, flashlight, rag
- tip: not very mechanically inclined: bring to repair center; take several photos during teardown
- step 1: spray carb cleaner in air intake with filter removed, try starting
- step 2: remove throttle cable and choke cable if present
- step 3: remove filter housing, then screws and nuts attaching carburetor
- step 4: remove springs slowly without stretching them (may need to twist carb); don't tear the gasket between engine block and carburetor
- step 5: remove bottom screw from float bowl, rag to catch gas; don't damage the o-ring (rubber band-like); do NOT get carb cleaner on the o-ring, it stretches and can't be reused
- step 6: check the stem hanging from center of carb for clogged holes (plugged holes won't draw fuel to the jet); clear with thick wire, flashlight, rinse with carb cleaner
- step 7: check for hard crusty white buildup (fuel additives including ethanol); remove as much as possible, nearly impossible to get all
- step 8: reassemble in reverse order using photos
- step 9: mix fresh fuel with stabilizer, fill tank, let carb bowl fill, start; pull cord may take several pulls

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil: smoke, runs terribly, eventually shuts down; engine may pull air and oil from crankcase, plugging the air filter; lack of air shuts mower down
- (details link: "what happens if you put too much oil")

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low oil: friction and heat overheat engine and it shuts down; heat can thicken oil and melt engine parts
- fix: if lucky, add oil and run; usually engine is severely damaged: have small engine mechanic diagnose; depending on age/model/repair cost may be better off buying new mower

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: may spark enough to start but can't keep running
- confirm: remove and look for carbon buildup or cracked porcelain
- fix: clean and reuse, or replace if damaged/excessively dirty; loose wire or wrong gap causes intermittent running/starting; check gap and wire attached securely

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts airflow for more fuel while engine heats up, used for cold starts; if left on after start engine gets more fuel and less air than needed and shuts down

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: fuel caps are vented; clogged vent or broken cap forms a vacuum in tank so no fuel
- confirm: loosen cap and start mower; if it runs with cap loose, replace the cap

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: coil winding can separate and short out when hot; plugs lose voltage; mower dies after running a while
- confirm: ohmmeter check for a break in continuity; replace if break found

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: damaged or packed with grass and mud, fins can't circulate air around block; mower overheats and dies mid-mowing
- fix: remove engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins, remove dirt around engine and cover

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: deck packed with grass/debris loads the engine; dull blades on packed deck compound it; engine under load can overheat and shut down
- fix: remove spark plug wire, scrape deck, sharpen blades
- tip: scrape deck regularly, avoid wet grass, always run mower at full throttle when cutting
- safety: blades sharp; remove spark plug wire first

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: replace annually
