## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "John Deere lawn tractor or zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Look first at air or fuel restriction. Sticky substances from old fuel clog fuel lines, fuel filter and carburetor. Use the correct gas and care for fuel properly. Also check battery, safety switches, charging system; a charging system that fails to keep the battery charged prevents starting.
- fix: Empty tank: fill with fresh fuel.
- fix: Old fuel: breaks down over time, prone to clogging the system; drain tank and refill with fresh fuel; use an additive like Sea Foam to stabilize fuel and help clean the fuel system.
- fix: Clogged cap vent: tank forms a vacuum restricting fuel flow; clear vent with a thin piece of wire, replace cap if needed.
- fix: Spark plug: excessively dirty/broken, bad connection, or wrong gap; replace, secure connections, gap to manufacturer specification.
- fix: Plugged air filter (dirt/grass): remove and clean; replace if in bad condition.
- fix: Plugged fuel filter: replace. Bad fuel pump (failed or leaking, no pressure): replace.
- fix: Blocked fuel lines (deposits and dirt): carb cleaner and compressed air to clear; replace line if necessary.
- fix: Dirty carburetor (bad fuel and ethanol deposits): clean components; replace if needed.
- fix: Bad battery or loose/dirty terminals: charge battery, replace if it won't hold charge; tighten cables, clean corrosion from terminals.
- fix: Bad safety switch: test and replace. Bad ignition switch or coil: test and replace the bad switch.
- fix: Broken recoil (pull-start): check whether it can be restrung or broken parts like the pulley replaced; replace recoil assembly if needed.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "John Deere clicks and won't turn over"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: Clicking and no turnover happens with a bad battery, ground, starter solenoid, or starter motor. Also ensure cables and wiring are securely attached, have good continuity and are not corroded.
- fix: Damaged/loose cables and wiring: remove corrosion, replace damaged parts, secure.
- fix: Weak battery: test, put on trickle charger if needed, replace one that won't hold a charge.
- fix: Bad ground: battery ground cable and starter-solenoid ground cable must make good contact and be corrosion free. (A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.)
- fix: Bad solenoid: test and replace if faulty. Bad starter motor: replace.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "John Deere starts then dies / dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, bad-electrical (unmapped: clogged-deck)
- fact: Many starting causes also make it lose power and die. Insufficient fuel/air makes it sluggish and may shut down; check fuel and air systems for restrictions. Overheating can cause dying: correct oil, correct level, clean cooling fins and engine surroundings. Electrical system or a clogged mower deck can also cause it to die after running.
- fix: Bad fuel: drain and refill; Sea Foam stabilizer helps clean the system.
- fix: Plugged air filter: clean/replace. Dirty or broken cooling fins (packed with dirt, oil, grass): clean, replace broken fins.
- fix: Clogged fuel lines or filter: carb cleaner and compressed air; replace plugged filter. Dirty carburetor: clean all components; replace if needed.
- fix: Too much engine oil: raises crankcase pressure, causes overheating and dying; remove a little oil to correct the level.
- fix: Bad spark plug: dirty/damaged plug fouls and gives intermittent running; clean or replace, secure plug wires, gap correctly.
- fix: Faulty ignition coil: windings can separate and short; check continuity with an ohm meter, replace if bad.
- fix: Choke in wrong position: choke is for cold starts; it can shut the engine off if it keeps restricting airflow after warm-up; make sure it is in the correct position.
- fix: Bad gas cap: vents plugged restrict fuel; replace a cap that no longer vents.
- fix: Clogged mower deck strains the engine and may shut it down; periodically scrape the deck.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "John Deere mower smokes"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-head-gasket
- fact: Black smoke is often a rich mixture (higher fuel-to-air ratio); a plugged air filter can cause it. Smoke can also be oil burning off due to oil in the cylinder or oil leaking onto a hot muffler.
- fix: Plugged air filter: clean/replace.
- fix: Too much oil in the crankcase: pressure builds and oil gets into the cylinder through the valve train; drain a little oil to the manufacturer's recommended level.
- fix: Low oil: lack of oil increases friction and extreme heat; take to a small engine repair shop to inspect damage; adding oil is often too late.
- pro: Internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket): take to a small engine repair shop for troubleshooting.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "John Deere zero turn won't move or go forward"
- causes-named: drive-release-lever-wrong-position (unmapped), missing-axle-key, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fix: Drive release lever in wrong position (lets hydrostatic mowers roll freely): must be in the operating position.
- fix: Missing key in the axle (small key can fall out): replace the key.
- fix: Drive pump belt worn or fallen off: replace worn/cracked belt; make sure it is securely around the pulleys.
- fix: Bad tensioner pulley bearing: replace the pulley. Missing/broken idler arm spring (no tension on belt): replace.
- fix: Low or old hydraulic oil: change at intervals recommended by John Deere; check level periodically. Some transmission systems are sealed and can't be serviced; see the dealer.
- fix: Hot hydraulic fluid can signal hydrostatic damage; let it cool; if mower runs fine cold but weak when hot, have the John Deere dealership check it.
- fix: Air in hydraulic system: must be bled after changing hydraulic fluid or the mower can't move at normal speed; follow John Deere procedure in owner's manual.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, loose-steering-linkage
- fix: Unequal tire pressure: check and fill to manufacturer spec; tires on each side need equal pressure.
- fix: Bad dampers on zero turn make the mower jerk to one side moving forward: replace the damper.
- fix: Zero-turn tracking: when one tire turns faster, mower pulls; adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the tire's rotation speed (see owner's manual).
- fix: Riding mower worn or loose steering components (bushings, washers, gears): inspect and replace failed parts.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "John Deere mower shaking / vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Even a pebble or acorn can cause excessive vibration. Debris, loose hardware, missing engine mounting bolts and a bad clutch all contribute. Always check the deck: damaged/unbalanced blades, deck components, bad bearings.
- fix: Loose/missing engine mounting bolts (they reduce vibration): secure, replace missing.
- fix: Bad clutch (bearing failure, bearing falls out; manual-engagement clutch bushing and linkages can fail): replace clutch.
- fix: Unbalanced/damaged blades: replace damaged, ensure balanced before installing.
- fix: Material (string, long weeds) wrapped around the blade shaft: remove, check for spindle damage.
- fix: Bad spindle housing bearing: replace bearing or whole spindle housing assembly.
- fix: Worn pulleys or deck belt (bearing failure in pulleys): replace worn parts.
- fix: Debris lodged in the deck or mower: check everywhere small items can lodge.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "John Deere is leaking gas"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Evaporating gas can make leaks hard to find; often no large wet spot.
- fix: Dirty/failed carburetor (old gas deposits cause blockages or failed parts): clean and replace damaged parts.
- fix: Old fuel filter plugged: replace. Bad fuel pump leaking at seams: replace.
- fix: Fuel tank: metal tank can rust through; poly tank can leak at seams; replace tank (a rust hole can sometimes be repaired if no replacement is available).
- fix: Shut-off valve (metal and plastic prone to leaking): replace. Cracked fuel lines (dry rubber): replace. Gas cap seal failed (gas splashes out): replace cap.

## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "John Deere blades won't engage or turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: Engage PTO and nothing happens: deck components worn, PTO or safety switch bad, battery weak, or clutch bad.
- fix: Worn deck belt doesn't grip pulleys: replace. Worn/missing tensioner spring (holds idler pulleys in line): replace; replace worn tensioner bracket/arm.
- fix: Bad pulley bearing (pulley seizes or wobbles, belt rolls off): replace bearing or pulley assembly.
- fix: Faulty PTO switch fails to pass battery voltage to the clutch: replace. Bad clutch no longer transfers power to blades: replace.
- fix: Worn clutch cable/lever/linkages (manual clutch): replace worn items.
- fix: Weak battery can't power clutch solenoid: charge; replace if it won't hold charge. Bad safety switch: test and replace.
- fix: Blown fuse: replace with same capacity; consult a mechanic for root cause if it keeps blowing.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "John Deere engine overheats"
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud (engine guard), clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- safety: When the engine smells hot, stop running it right away to avoid extensive engine damage.
- fix: Wrong oil (wrong viscosity for ambient temperature or not for air-cooled engines): drain and refill with fresh air-cooled engine oil of suitable viscosity.
- fix: Low oil: change and add fresh oil; low oil may have caused damage a small engine mechanic should diagnose.
- fix: Plugged/damaged cooling fins: clean; replace damaged fins. Engine guard missing or out of place (keeps air circulating): attach securely.
- fix: Very dirty air filter starves engine of air: replace. Deck full of debris makes engine work harder: scrape deck. Dull blades magnify a plugged deck: sharpen and balance.
- fix: Overworking (long, wet, thick grass): slow down, avoid wet grass, double or triple-cut an overgrown lawn.
