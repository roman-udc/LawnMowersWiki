## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "riding mower won't stay running", "quits running", "runs sluggish then dies"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, engine-overheats (see overheats)
- fact: Summary: old fuel restrictions in lines, filter, carburetor; also bad fuel pump, dirty plug, faulty ignition coil, plugged air filter.
- pro: dealer/repair shop for complicated repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Consume gas within 30 days (also fuel in containers); add stabilizer otherwise (author: Sea Foam Motor Treatment: stabilizes, cleans, reduces moisture).
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture, corrosive to the fuel system; the ethanol-water mix leaves gummy deposits restricting flow and separates, sinks to the tank bottom, runs hot through the engine and can damage it.
- fact: Gas spec: unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; avoid E15, E30, E85 (up to 15%, 30%, 85% ethanol). Ethanol-free is best but costlier: REC-90/recreational fuel at some pumps or canisters at hardware stores. Store fuel cool and dry away from combustibles and moisture.
- fix: remove old gas with a fuel siphon pump; fill with fresh gas plus additive like Sea Foam.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: stop fuel (shut-off valve or pinch pliers), remove filter from lines with a rag for fuel; install new with the arrow facing fuel flow (toward the carburetor, away from tank); turn fuel back on.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel; remove the end of a line section into a container; start fuel flow and confirm good flow.
- fix: if poor, stop fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow compressed air through, repeat; if it can't be cleared or line is dry/cracked replace with new line of the same length and diameter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Used when the carburetor sits higher than the tank. Vacuum pump uses crankcase vacuum to move a diaphragm and force gas to the carb.
- confirm: confirm fuel reaches the pump inlet; then remove the fuel line from the carburetor into a container, start fuel flow and start the mower; expect a steady or pulsating stream.
- fix: replace a damaged pump or one that doesn't pump a stream.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Bowl holds a little fuel; old fuel leaves varnish/sticky deposits clogging the jet and gumming small components; runs sluggish and dies.
- confirm: confirm fuel reaches the carb; remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner in intake, start; runs fine then dies = clean and inspect carb.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dirty filter causes running problems and can cause engine damage. Replace annually; clean several times per season.
- step 1: Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe out housing without letting dirt into the intake.
- step 3: Tap against a solid surface.
- step 4: Hold to light; reuse if light shines through, else new.
- step 5: Reinstall and attach the cover.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: After warm-up the choke must be adjusted to let air in or the mower sputters and stops.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Fouled plug fires intermittently; mower runs sluggish and dies. Check for oil, dirt, carbon. Clean with wire brush and reuse, or (author's preference) replace (inexpensive). Gap per engine manufacturer's specs and attach wire/boot securely.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Windings can separate and short out when the mower gets hot; plug gets no spark.
- confirm: check for a break in continuity with an ohmmeter.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cap vent plugged creates vacuum, fuel can't leave the tank; runs for a while then shuts down.
- confirm: after it stops, remove the cap and start; if it runs fine, suspect the vent; refit cap while running to see if it shuts off after a while.
- fix: cleaning may work only temporarily; author recommends a new riding mower fuel cap.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- fact: An overheating engine shuts down. Causes named: low oil level, wrong type of oil, clogged mower deck, plugged air filter. (causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, clogged-air-filter)

## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- fact: siphon old gas, refill fresh + additive.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: see old-fuel spec above (87 octane min, 10% ethanol max, avoid E15/E30/E85).
