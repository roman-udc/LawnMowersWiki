## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "mower clicks but won't start or turn over"
- alias: "click click click when turning the key"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-starter-solenoid, faulty-charging-system, bad-starter-motor
- safety: Follow operator's manual; gloves and eye protection. Always disconnect the negative (black) battery cable before electrical repairs.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: Check cables/wiring battery-to-solenoid and solenoid-to-starter; terminals free of corrosion and tight.
- fix: Remove corrosion; replace cables that keep coming loose or are broken (bad cables can contribute to bad battery). Disconnect battery, clean terminals with wire brush and baking soda mix (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda). Repair/replace corroded wiring. Add dielectric grease to protect terminals and wiring. Replace damaged terminals.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: Check black cable from battery to mower frame; check ground from solenoid. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: Replace damaged battery-to-frame cable; remove corrosion from battery and solenoid grounds.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: just clicks and doesn't turn over.
- confirm: Multimeter red/black to matching terminals. Common riding/zero-turn battery 12 V; reading between 11.5 and 12.7; 11.5 V almost dead, 12.7 V fully charged.
- fix: Charge; replace if it won't hold charge. A battery that dies but can be recharged may indicate a charging system problem.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Safety gear for eyes and skin (acid, electrical shock).
- step 2: Access battery/terminals (screwdriver to uncover body).
- step 3: Leave battery in casing with terminal cables attached.
- step 4: Connect red (positive) charger clamp first to positive terminal; black to negative.
- step 5: Only touch rubber coating of cables and clamps.
- step 6: Set charger to 12 V; amperage: start at 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps; more amps charges faster.
- step 7: If charger has a gauge, keep connected until fully charged.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: mower keeps clicking.
- fact: Electromagnetic switch engaging starter motor. Most mounted on the starter but needn't be; follow positive wire from battery to find it. Fails from weak internal spring or corroded copper plate; bad ground, weak starter or bad battery can also make it fail.
- confirm: Test with volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches. If mower starts when solenoid is bypassed, replace it.
- tools: volt-ohms meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches.
- fact: Bypassing the solenoid and starting the mower means solenoid must be replaced (details in a separate article not in this Source).

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: After battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid check good, remove starter and test.
- fix: Starter is pricey; have local dealership confirm, or a shop specializing in starter/alternator repair test and often rebuild it.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Bad charging system won't keep battery charged. Larger mowers may have external alternator like a car; most have internal alternator under the flywheel. Possible bad stator/alternator, regulator or other electrical problem.
- confirm: Check charging system with volt-ohms meter (steps in separate article).
- pro: Have a mechanic familiar with charging systems test and repair; guessing means buying parts that can't be returned (electrical).

## page: causes/bad-alternator
- fact: see charging system above (stator/alternator, internal under flywheel on most).
