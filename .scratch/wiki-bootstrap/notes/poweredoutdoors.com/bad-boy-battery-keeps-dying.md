## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "Bad Boy lawn mower battery keeps dying"
- alias: "I have to keep charging the battery"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, faulty-charging-system, parasitic-battery-drain, weak-or-dead-battery
- safety: wear gear protecting skin and eyes around the battery; follow operator's manual precautions.
- fact: Most batteries last at least 3 years with proper care; changing more often than every 3 years points to a connection or battery problem.
- fact: Most batteries fail because of vibration; the plates can break up inside. Make sure the battery sits securely in the tray and does not bounce.
- tip: Care tips: keep cables clean; cables tight and battery held down; do not let the battery freeze, keep fully charged in cold weather (a battery not fully charged can freeze and be damaged).

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: battery keeps dying (problems/battery-keeps-dying). Mower bounces/vibrates in normal use and can loosen cables.
- confirm: follow the red (positive) cable from battery to solenoid, then on to starter; make sure all cables are secure and make good connection.
- confirm: check connections for corrosion, a white or green substance; corrosion gives a weak charge.
- fix: remove and clean corroded components. Clean cables/connections with baking soda and water: mix 4 cups water with about six rounded tablespoons baking soda into a paste, spread on corrosion (it foams), rinse with water (messy, rinse outdoors). Wire brush speeds it up. Cola soda also removes corrosion.
- safety: disconnect the negative cable first to break the circuit; otherwise a wrench touching frame can arc, throw sparks and potentially blow up the battery. Always wear safety glasses and skin protection. Positive cable is last off and first on.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: battery dies repeatedly even with good cables (problems/battery-keeps-dying).
- confirm: with a volt-ohm meter set to read 12 V: engine off, red lead on positive, black on negative; reading should be 12.4-12.8 V (some batteries read higher depending on charge rate/condition). Write it down. Set parking brake, start engine, run at about 3/4 throttle, read again: should be 13.2-13.9 V or higher. Higher than the first reading means the alternator is charging.
- confirm: if the second reading does not change from the first, charging system issue; cause may be a bad regulator or bad stator/alternator.
- fact: some systems are 15 amp, others 20 amp, depending on manufacturer and engine.
- pro: author recommends a professional mechanic/Bad Boy dealer to find root cause; many expensive parts could be at fault, throwing parts at it gets costly.
- tip: test can be done any time on the battery if cables are not dirty; it may also reveal a bad connection.

## page: causes/parasitic-battery-drain
- symptom: dead battery after key left in ON position (problems/battery-keeps-dying).
- fact: leaving ignition on won't affect the battery much unless there is an oil light on the dash or a 12-volt accessory cord plugged into an accessory port (Bad Boy with accessory port).
- fix: charge the battery.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: battery on a charger for an hour or two that will not charge above 12 volts is bad and must be replaced.
- fix: buy 12-volt lawn and garden battery at Bad Boy dealer, automotive store or most home improvement stores; take old battery along. Stores usually charge a core fee of $15-$25 unless you provide the old battery.
- fact: new batteries usually fully charged; if long on shelf, charge or jump-start before use.
- brand: for Bad Boy with electric clutch deck, battery of at least 300 cold cranking amps (cca); without electric clutch can use about 250 cca, "bigger is better". Check measurements fit the battery holder.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: battery charger (author likes one with a trickle charge setting for very weak battery), volt meter
- step 1: disconnect battery cables (positive red is last off, first on). If battery has removable caps, check water level; if plates not covered add distilled water (well water not good; city water a little better; distilled best).
- step 2: hook charger to battery (red to positive +, black to negative -).
- step 3: check battery voltage. 11.8 V needs little charge time; 10.1 V needs a long time; the lower the voltage the longer.
- step 4: Method one (very low battery): "slow bake" on 2 or 4 amps (chargers may have 6, 4, 2 amp switches) for about an hour; check voltage is rising; if so charge another hour.
- step 5: Method two (not too low and you need the mower): charger up to 6 amps for about half hour to reach about 12 V, remove charger, start mower, keep throttle 3/4 to full so battery charges while running; check reading after mowing.

## page: procedures/jump-start-the-battery
- step 1: confirm both batteries are 12 V (car battery and mower battery).
- step 2: park car next to mower and shut engine off. Do not start the car engine.
- step 3: red positive cable to mower positive terminal, then to car battery positive.
- step 4: black negative cable to car battery negative; connect other black end to mower frame.
- step 5: set brake, start mower, let run a few minutes.
- step 6: remove cables in opposite order of installing. Alternator keeps battery charged; check with volt meter.
