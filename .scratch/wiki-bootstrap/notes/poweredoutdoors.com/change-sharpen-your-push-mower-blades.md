## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Change blades once a year (more often with more dirt and gravel); do it during routine annual service. If old blades are not damaged/excessively worn, sharpen and keep as spare set.
- fact: Sharpen every 25 hours of use; average homeowner = twice a year; more often in sandy/gravel conditions.
- fact: New blades do not need sharpening: manufacturer sharpens and coats in paint; paint wears off the edge when cutting.
- tools (remove/install): socket wrench, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, block of wood.
- step 1: safety: disconnect spark plug boot, remove key if mower has one, wear gloves.
- step 2: turn fuel valve to off to minimize gas leaking when tipping mower on its side; tip mower so the air filter is on the high side.
- step 3: lodge a block of wood between blade and deck housing to stop the blade turning; remove blade bolts and washers with socket wrench.
- step 4: remove the blade.
- step 5: clean the area under the deck where the blade mounts. If reusing sharpened blades, make sure balanced.
- step 6: install upper and lower blades with bolts and washers; concave side of the washer faces the blade.
- step 7: tighten bolts with torque wrench to manufacturer's spec in owner's manual (so blade doesn't come off); use block of wood to keep blade from rotating.
- tools (sharpening): 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc grinder; vice; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves.
- step S1: clean blade with rag, wire brush for stubborn buildup (gloves, glasses on).
- step S2: secure blade in vice; flip and re-secure to sharpen other side.
- step S3 (file): push file at an angle in one direction along the blade; do not use a sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges; flip and repeat.
- step S3 (drill sharpener): flat guide of sharpener along flat portion of blade with cutting edge slotted into the bevel; move up and down the blade edge; flip and repeat.
- step S3 (angle/bench grinder): hold grinder perpendicular to blade edge; run along length to level rough spots/nicks; keep it moving so it removes a little metal at a time for even result; flip and repeat.
- step balance: use a blade balancer; or place a nail in the wall with head 3/4" to 1" from the wall, hang the blade center on the nail; blade should sit level; if not, shave a little more metal off the side hanging lower.
- fact: Failing to balance causes vibration and crankshaft damage.
- safety: gloves and safety glasses; wrong torque can let blade come off, risking injury.
- pro: see a professional if not comfortable sharpening/balancing; unbalanced or still-worn blades can damage crankshaft or engine. Pro reasons: safer; sharpening is often cheaper than a new blade; can grind out large nicks and gouges; properly balanced (less vibration). Author personally has local dealer sharpen because they put a nicer, cleaner edge.
- tip: sometimes DIY sharpening is not worth it; savings depend on method.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: bad or uneven cut (problems/uneven-cut); check blade condition.
- fact: Chips and gouges compromise cut; small chips can be smoothed by sharpening; replace blade with larger nicks/gouges.
- fact: Bent blade from hitting tree root or rock. Confirm by matching old blade against a new one; or take it to local dealer.
- mistake: never straighten and reuse a bent blade; compromises structure, can crack.
- fact: Cracked blade must be replaced right away; can break off and fly out, risking people, pets, structures.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: The sail is the high side of the blade; dirt sucked under deck wears it thin. A worn sail cannot move the air needed to create suction in the deck that stands grass tall for a precise cut.
- confirm/fix: replace blade where sail is thinner than the center of the blade.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades: replace yearly; sharpen every 25 hours.
- fact: Sand, debris wear blades prematurely (only mitigation: thicker grass cover to reduce dirt sucked in). Lawn chemicals may be corrosive to blades and deck; corrosion can fuse blades to deck components making removal nearly impossible without damage; frequently removing/installing blades reduces this; regularly clean and remove blades.
- tip: walk lawn before mowing to remove toys, sticks, rocks.
