## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "belt keeps coming off my lawn mower"
- alias: "belt keeps breaking"
- causes-named: belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Drive belts and deck belts are necessary; if one falls off, the mower fails to move or fails to cut.
- fact: Summary: belt guides and brackets out of adjustment; debris collecting under pulleys and tensioners; damaged brackets, bearings or bushings; worn belt.
- safety: Remove the spark plug before working.
- fact: Belts can cost $20 to $200. Author recommends an OEM belt from the mower's manufacturer; aftermarket/auto-parts-matched belts rarely last as long or perform as well.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Belt guides (belt keepers) are small rod-shaped posts around the pulleys; some travel alongside the belt. They get bent or out of place when a belt snaps or has a lot of slack; slack belt hits the guide and bends it toward the pulleys, making the belt jump off.
- confirm: Guides should never touch the belt; they should sit 1/8 in to 1/4 in from the pulley/belt.
- fix: Adjust brackets back by tapping with a small hammer or using vice grips.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt looks loose; sign it's time to replace. Author replaces both mower belts at the same time (one worn/stretched, the other probably due).
- fact: Shredded belt: rubbing on a bracket or belt guide, or not tracking correctly. Shiny spot on a bracket/guide shows where it rubs. Adjust the bracket or guide so it no longer rubs; check bushings in brackets (some brackets have replaceable bushings; others the whole bracket must be replaced).

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Pulleys not running flat and straight suggest a bad bearing; most often in an idler pulley, which has a sealed bearing so the whole pulley is replaced. Drive pulleys or spindle housings may have replaceable bearings.
- confirm: Spindle housing bearing: grab the blades and rock back and forth; movement means bearings or whole cutter housing need replacing. Noisy, excessively vibrating spindles can make the belt come off the deck.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Rocks, wood chips, dried mud in pulley grooves; debris caught under pulleys keeps the belt from stretching around it, so it can break. New belts often snap because foreign material is caught, not because they're old.
- fact: Dry grass/debris under the mower or on the deck can stop the tension pulley from moving, so it can't keep proper tension. Keep the area under belt covers and mower body clean.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Idler bracket usually has a spring on one side and a pulley on the other. Worn spring-attachment hole or worn spring causes constant vibration. Idler bracket bushing wears if not greased, making the pulley run at an angle; it can cut into the deck or the underside of the mower (transmission belt), and the vibration plus angle throws the belt.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil leaks collect on the belt; oil (petroleum product) makes the belt swell if left; oil-covered belt slips and just builds heat. Rust dries out the belt and it cracks; cracked belts need replacement as soon as possible. Keep pulleys clean of oil and rust.
