## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Snapper starts then dies", "suddenly quits", "dies after running a while"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, faulty-charging-system
- safety: prevent running/moving: remove spark plug boots and ignition key (if it has one), set parking brake; see operator's manual.
- pro: unsure = dealer/repair shop.
- tip: Check oil each time you mow: low oil can reveal a leak or oil burning; catching engine problems early prevents damage.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Most gasoline has ethanol (corn-based); keep it at 10% or less. Ethanol attracts moisture; evaporating moisture leaves gummy substance; ethanol-fuel mixture separates and sinks to the tank bottom, can make the engine run hot and damage it. Use fresh fuel; stabilize if you can't use within 30 days. Author adds Sea Foam Motor Treatment to every tank (stabilizes, cleaning agents, reduces moisture).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Restricted airflow kills the engine; replace annually and clean regularly; neglect can overheat and badly damage the engine.
- step 1: Remove filter from housing without dropping dirt into the intake; wipe housing with dry cloth.
- step 2 (paper): tap on a solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light shows else replace; reinstall cap.
- step 3 (foam): inspect for brown spots, tears, dry brittle condition (replace if present); wash with mild dish detergent and water; rinse all soap; lay flat to dry; coat with foam filter oil without letting it drip; wring out excess; reinstall.
- fact: A new foam air filter needs filter oil added (helps prevent dirt passing through). Don't confuse a foam filter element with a foam pre-filter (used with an air filter as extra protection); don't oil a pre-filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Replace once a year; new filter arrow points in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: check for kinks/clogs; start/stop flow with shut-off valve or crimp, check each section; with flow stopped remove the end furthest from the tank into a container; start flow.
- fix: remove clogged section (supply off); spray carb cleaner in; blow compressed air; if that fails replace line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: confirm fuel reaches carb; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner into intake and start; dies after running = disassemble and clean.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Cooling fins (plugged with debris or broken) dissipate heat from block and cylinder head; plugged fins restrict air; engine runs hot, starts then dies. Also clean around the engine and engine shroud; make sure the heat shield is securely attached; engine can be hot.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil builds crankcase pressure; mower overheats and shuts down.
- fix: remove some oil via drain plug or oil filter, or with an oil evacuator or turkey baster through the fill; check dipstick; adjust until at the full line.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil (leak, or engine using/burning oil) causes friction and heat; overheating and dying; heat can melt internal parts. If shut down from lack of oil, significant damage likely.
- pro: take to an experienced small engine mechanic to tear down and test.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: May spark enough to start but not to keep running; improper gap or insecure wires also cause it. Clean the tip; replace if very dark, burnt electrode, or damaged; gap per engine manufacturer; attach wires securely.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Windings separate and short when hot; no voltage for spark; dies after running a while.
- confirm: ohmmeter check for a break in continuity; replace if broken.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Leaving the choke on after the engine starts and warms makes it shut down.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: run with and without the cap; removes cap and it runs, replace cap and it eventually shuts off = cap problem.
- fix: clean to remove clog or replace the cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Packed deck makes the engine work harder; overheats and shuts down. Scrape regularly; avoid wet grass; deck spray minimizes, doesn't prevent.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Run at high engine speed; don't idle for long periods (engine power charges the battery). If you keep having to charge the battery with good battery/wiring, suspect the charging system.
- pro: Have an experienced mechanic diagnose; electrical parts are expensive and likely non-returnable; replace battery if tests show bad.
