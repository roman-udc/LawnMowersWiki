## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- fact: Source recommends carburetor cleaner in place of starter fluid to start a hard-starting mower; emergencies only until repaired.
- fact: starter fluid is a very dry chemical with no lubrication value; too much or too often can damage internal cylinder components.
- safety: remove spark plug wire; carb cleaner is very flammable; check manual.
- step 1: remove air filter housing (screwdriver or Torx driver; some housings just clip off).
- step 2: lift air filter out (some housings open like a door); inspect housing for water, debris, anything unusual; clean chamber fully.
- step 3: spray carb cleaner either directly into intake port (directly under the air filter) or into spark plug port (location per manual); spray only 2 to 3 seconds; too much can damage the engine. If using starter fluid anyway, spray on a rag and hold it to the intake, not directly in.
- step 4: reassemble; set engine speed to midpoint if possible; choke full (closed); press primer bulb a few times if present.
- step 5: reattach spark plug wire; pull cord or turn key; if it starts, shut off, replace filter and housing. If not after a few pulls, repeat steps 1-4; still no start -> professional.
- mistake: needing carb cleaner every start -> see professional to avoid damage.
- fact: if mower starts for under five seconds then stalls, carburetor likely needs cleaning; if starts and stalls after about 30 seconds, replace fuel (bad or water-saturated).
- fact: carb cleaner suited to carbureted engines having trouble starting: harder than usual starting; part not sold anymore; to rule out carburetor when diagnosing; colder than usual temperatures. Do NOT use on diesel engines, 2-cycle gasoline engines, or when you cannot identify the problem.
- tip: WD40 mentioned as a fallback if no carb cleaner ("when in a bind").
- fact: other causes if still no start: bad spark plugs, bad carburetor, bad fuel.
