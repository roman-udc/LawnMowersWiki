## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Simplicity starts then dies"; "mower quits while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- safety: Follow the Simplicity operator's manual: remove the ignition key and spark plug wires; wait for the engine to cool and all parts to stop moving.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; consume within 30 days. Ethanol/water mixture leaves varnish that restricts flow or causes component failure.
- fact: Use gas with minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; avoid E15 and E85.
- tip: Sea Foam Motor Treatment must be added to fresh gas; it cannot reverse the effects of old gas.
- fix: Drain old fuel; fill with fresh fuel and a stabilizer that helps clean the fuel system and reduce moisture.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter between the fuel lines; replace annually. A lack of fuel makes the mower run sluggishly and possibly die.
- fix: Replace; install with the arrow on the plastic housing pointing in the fuel flow direction.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: When ethanol-moisture evaporates it leaves gummy substance narrowing or blocking the line.
- confirm: Use the fuel shut-off valve to start and stop flow while checking flow out of each section.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow with compressed air; repeat if necessary. Replace with a new section of the same diameter and length if it won't clear or the line is cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Needed when the carburetor sits higher than the tank. A vacuum pump uses engine vacuum to move fuel.
- confirm: Shut-off valve or clamps to start and stop flow; verify fuel gets to the pump; then remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start the mower; expect a steady or pulsating flow.
- fix: Replace a pump that is cracked or can't pump fuel to the carburetor.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Varnish clogs passageways and makes internal components stick or corrode; common with ethanol fuel.
- confirm: First make sure fuel reaches the carburetor (otherwise check filter, lines, pump). Then remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the intake; if it starts and runs fine while burning the cleaner and then dies, the carburetor is the likely cause.
- fix: Remove, clean and replace damaged parts; shop can clean or replace.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: The tank vents through the cap; a broken or plugged vent makes a vacuum so fuel can't flow.
- confirm: Loosen the cap to let air in, start and run the engine. If it runs fine, tighten the cap and continue; if it sputters and dies again and won't restart until air gets to the tank, bad cap.
- fix: Replace with a new Simplicity fuel cap.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Plugged with grass and dirt, engine overheats from air starvation; can cause extensive engine damage.
- fix: Remove without knocking dirt into the intake. Paper: tap on a solid surface, hold to light, reuse if light passes, replace if no light, damaged or very dark. Foam pre-filter: wash in mild detergent and water, air dry, install around the primary paper filter. Other filter types: see the Source's air filter article.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: Remove the engine cover and clean fins, replace damaged fins, ensure the heat shield is attached correctly.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Intermittent spark.
- fix: Replace if very dark, worn or damaged; if only a little dirty, clean with a small wire brush. Check gap and wire attachment.
- tip: Author replaces the plug when diagnosing to rule it out (inexpensive part).

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Quits when the coil gets hot; windings separate and short; insufficient voltage to the plug.
- confirm: Verify a good spark plug first; ohmmeter test for continuity break; replace if bad.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Smokes and dies while mowing; smoke clogs the air filter; possible internal damage/engine replacement.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Too little oil adds friction and overheating, then dies.
- fix: Change oil per Simplicity's recommendations; fill to the correct oil fill levels.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Packed deck plus dull blades makes the engine work harder, overheat and shut down.
- fix: Inspect the deck for damage, scrape debris, sharpen blades, run full throttle, avoid wet grass.
