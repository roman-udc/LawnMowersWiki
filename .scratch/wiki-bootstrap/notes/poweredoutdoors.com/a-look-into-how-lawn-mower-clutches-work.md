## page: guides/how-clutches-work
- fact: Source calls it an "electric lawn mower clutch", also called the power take-off (PTO) clutch; it sends power to the mower blades. The electromagnetic apparatus acts as a brake to slow the blades when the clutch is disengaged.
- fact: The clutch is where the blade connects to the engine and transmits power to cut grass. Can be one of the most expensive mower parts.
- fact: Parts: a powerful (electro)magnet that engages the blade, a brake pad that slows the blade when the mower is turned off, a flat pulley on top connected through a shaft through the middle, a rotor, a plate within the rotor, plates/bolts/springs/pulleys.
- fact: How it works: the engine spins a rotor inside the clutch; when disengaged the rotor spins freely. Engaging the clutch activates the magnet, which pulls against a plate within the rotor, which starts spinning the pulley connected to the blades.
- fact: Braking: on disengage the magnetic force is released and the brake pad on the clutch contacts the pulley to slow the deck.
- fact: Engaged by the switch on the dashboard; you should hear the clutch click on under the deck, otherwise there may be a problem.
- fact: Several clutch types exist with slightly different parts; all serve the same purpose.
- symptom: Signs the clutch may need replacement: mower won't start when you engage the blades; deck doesn't spin fast enough when engaged; blade vibrates too much and slows when it starts to spin; blades not spinning/not engaging when clutch is turned on; unusual sounds, not running properly, vibration.
- tip: Before replacing, be sure the problem is the clutch and not another part; a multimeter helps diagnose.
- confirm: Power line check: if you don't hear the clutch engage, use a multimeter (volt/ohm meter) on the power going to the clutch; it should receive about 12 volts. Not enough = problem is elsewhere in the mower, not the clutch; enough voltage = problem is the clutch.
- confirm: Magnet check: with power present, set multimeter to Ohms and attach to the electromagnet; reading should be between 1 and 3 ohms. High reading = higher resistance (needs more energy to complete circuit); low reading may indicate wiring problem / electromagnet too powerful.
- confirm: Brake pad check: remove and inspect the hub for buildup, corrosion or obstructions that prevent good contact; the pulley connected to the blade must make good contact with the brake pad. Replace the brake pad when worn (the fine grit wears down).
- confirm: Inspect plates, bolts, springs and pulleys for wear/damage; make sure all tight. Generally clutch should have 50-60 ft-lbs torque; each machine differs, find spec before disassembling. Some clutches can be disassembled for inspection.
- fix: Replace broken/damaged components or the whole clutch depending on the situation; the mower's dealer usually has the part.

## page: procedures/test-the-pto-clutch
- step 1: Check power line into the clutch with a multimeter when no engagement click is heard; expect about 12 volts.
- step 2: If powered, check the electromagnet resistance with multimeter on Ohms; expect 1-3 ohms.
- step 3: Remove and inspect the hub/brake pad for buildup, corrosion, obstructions, and wear.
- step 4: Inspect plates, bolts, springs, pulleys; torque about 50-60 ft-lbs (check machine spec).
- tools: multimeter (volt/ohm meter)

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- symptom: blades not engaging, deck too slow, vibrating and slowing when blade starts (problems/blades-wont-engage)
- confirm: see test steps above (12 V at clutch, 1-3 ohm coil, brake pad/hub condition).
