- fact: Source lists 11 reasons (counts wrong oil, low oil, high oil separately). Engine and charging-system problems may be better left to a mechanic.
- pro: after a shutdown from low oil, an oil change rarely fixes it; small engine mechanic to test for internal damage.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Starved of air, engine overheats and shuts down. Start each season clean; check/clean at least a couple of times a season, more in dry dusty conditions.
- step (paper): remove filter from housing; wipe housing, no dirt into intake; tap on solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light shines through; replace if not; reattach cover.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas degrades as quickly as 30 days. Ethanol attracts moisture: corrosion of fuel system and gummy deposits. Use unleaded min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; stabilizer.
- fact: Diesel tractors: similar moisture buildup, corrosion and sediment; additive stabilizes and reduces moisture.
- fix: Siphon old fuel into an approved container; refill fresh gasoline with additive. Author recommends Sea Foam Motor Treatment (cleaning agents; good for carburetor and valve train).

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old fuel clogs carburetor components including the fuel jet; gummy and crusty deposits can shut mower down after running a while.
- confirm: Verify fuel reaches the carburetor; remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner in intake, start; if it starts then shuts down, disassemble/clean or rebuild carburetor.
- fix: Clean/rebuild if mechanical; shop cleaning; or install new carburetor.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Use air-cooled engine oil with high zinc concentration (zinc as cooling agent) in gas riding mowers; differs from car oil (water-cooled).
- fact: Most small engine makers recommend SAE 30 or 10W-30; may need 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures. Use manufacturer's viscosity chart (a Kawasaki chart referenced).
- fix wrong oil: drain, refill with engine manufacturer's viscosity.
- fact low oil: oil heats and thickens, friction and heat rise; mower may smoke from burning oil and melting engine parts. Check oil before each mowing; low oil can mean burning oil or a leak.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Crankshaft and rod must push through excess oil; crankcase pressure rises, loads internal parts, engine quits when hot; excess oil pushed into cylinder through valve train burns, smokes, plugs air filter.
- fix: Remove spark plug wire; drain small amounts: drain plug (quickly remove/replace), oil filter (rag), fill hole (tip mower, carburetor and air filter high), oil extractor pump, turkey baster. Recheck level. After correcting, check and replace air filter if oily.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- see overfilled oil methods above.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins circulate air around block and cylinder; plugged or broken fins let engine overheat and quit.
- fix: Remove debris around fins, replace broken fins; remove debris under engine shrouds; confirm heat shield securely in place.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Fouled plug may spark enough to start but not keep running. Check for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain, burnt electrode. Clean with metal brush and reuse, or replace if damaged/very dirty/dark. Loose wire or wrong gap cause intermittent running.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: When hot, coil windings separate and short out; insufficient voltage to plug.
- confirm: Ohmmeter continuity (no break). Replace if faulty.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cap vent lets air in; blocked, vacuum prevents fuel leaving tank, mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: Runs with cap removed but quits after a short time with cap on.
- fix: Clean cap or just replace.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Clogged deck loads the engine; dull blades add to it; shutdown when hot.
- fix: Sharpen blades, scrape deck; avoid wet conditions.
