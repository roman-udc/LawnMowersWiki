## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Husqvarna belt keeps coming off"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Belt worn, stretched or shredded; excessive vibration from worn bearing, tensioner bracket or tensioner spring.
- tip (life extension): clean deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off the belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix/replace damaged brackets or belt keepers. Deck belts can be pricey.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- confirm: Inspect for wear, cracking or shiny glazed appearance; replace if any.
- fact: Shredded belts usually rub a belt keeper or bracket on the deck, or are installed/routed incorrectly. Check routing against the decal on the mower or the owner's manual diagram. Shiny smooth spot on metal shows rubbing; fix the bracket. Some brackets allow replacing only the bushing; others need the full bracket assembly.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Keeper is a rod sitting 1/8 in to 1/4 in from the pulley; belt doesn't touch it. Slack in the belt can hit and bend a keeper, then the belt slips off.
- fix: Bend rods back with vice grips and a hammer.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Pulleys must sit flat and parallel to the deck; a bad bearing can leave one side higher, vibrating.
- confirm: Spin each pulley by hand; good spins smoothly, bad has restriction and may make noise.
- fix: Idler pulleys have sealed bearings: replace the entire pulley assembly.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- safety: Remove key and unplug spark plug boot(s) before working under the deck; wear work gloves.
- confirm: Grab each end of the blade and rock up/down; movement or knocking suggests spindle housing problem. Remove housing, inspect bearing and spindle.
- fix: Some models allow replacing a bearing; others need the whole spindle assembly.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Idler tensioner bracket typically has a pulley on one side and spring on the other. Weak spring lets the belt come off; worn/enlarged spring hole adds vibration; replace the worn part.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Debris around pulleys and tensioner stops the tensioner moving, belt loosens and vibrates. Mud/small debris in pulley grooves stresses the belt as it stretches around it and it can snap.
- safety: Dry debris on top of the deck can catch fire from belt heat.
- fix: Clean pulley grooves and deck.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Oil swells the belt and makes it slip and come off; rust from old rusty pulleys dries and cracks the belt.
- fix: Repair the oil leak, clean oil and rust off deck and pulleys; replace badly rusty pulleys; then replace with a new Husqvarna belt.
