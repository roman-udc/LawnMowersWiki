## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Ferris zero turn blades won't engage or turn on"

- fact: Intro list: worn deck belt, worn idler tensioner arm, worn tensioner spring, bad pulley, faulty PTO switch, bad clutch, weak battery, bad safety switch, blown fuse.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch is a knob on the mower that allows battery voltage to power the clutch. When it fails, blades won't turn on.
- confirm: Check for continuity in the switch.
- fix: Replace a switch with a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: Ferris PTO clutch transfers engine power to blades by engaging the drive belt. Worn or faulty clutch that fails to power blades must be replaced. See clutch article.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Electric clutch requires battery power; weak battery may not supply the clutch solenoid so blades won't engage.
- confirm: Multimeter; fully charged 12-volt battery reads about 12.7 volts. Charge if lower.
- fix: If it won't hold a charge, replace. Buy a 12-volt battery with at least 300 CCA (cold cranking amps) to power an electric clutch. Bring old battery to avoid core fee (typically $15-$25).

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear protective gear (skin electric shock, eyes). Do not touch anything without rubber coating to prevent electrocution.
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver (to uncover battery)
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (under hood or under seat); may need screwdriver to uncover. Do not remove battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect charger positive cable first (red, plus sign) to positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach negative cable (black, negative sign) to negative terminal.
- step 4: Set voltage and amperage. Lawn mower batteries are usually 12 volts. More amps charge faster; start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Ferris zero-turn uses several operator-presence safety switches; one is the seat switch under the seat. System is designed to stop blades when operator isn't in seat; a seat switch not working correctly can prevent blades from turning.
- confirm: Test with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: Do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse blows protecting against power surge or short; clutch may then get no power. Replace with same capacity fuse. If it keeps blowing, take to Ferris service dealer or repair shop for root cause.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: worn deck belt doesn't grip pulleys, may slip. Cracks, stretching, fraying, glazed appearance; thinner belt that sits deep in pulley grooves.
- fact: Replace a worn belt even if it's not the main reason blades won't engage. Belt grip affects blade speed; fast blade speed creates suction under the deck to lift grass for an even cut.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Tensioner bracket and spring hold idler pulleys in line (pulley one side of bracket, spring other side). Hole in bracket where spring attaches can wear, or spring can wear/break; belt becomes loose and falls off pulleys and blades stop.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Bearing failure makes pulley wobble so it isn't flat/parallel to deck; one side shouldn't sit higher than the other; belt can roll off. Replace a pulley with a bad bearing.
