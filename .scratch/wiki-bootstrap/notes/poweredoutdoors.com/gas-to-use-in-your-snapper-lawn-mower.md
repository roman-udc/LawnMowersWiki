## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Snapper 4-cycle ("4-stage") engines use unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Most current Snappers are 4-cycle; older 2-cycle models need a gas/oil mix (check operator's manual for ratio).
- fact: regular gas can be used; most unleaded sold as regular is 87 octane (per US Energy Information Administration).
- fact: avoid high ethanol: E85 and E15 (85% and 15% ethanol) appear at pumps; never buy gas with more than 10% ethanol. Read pump labels for octane and ethanol. Less ethanol is better for small engines.
- fact: ethanol facts per author: corn-based; attracts moisture from air causing separation; separated ethanol/water sink to tank bottom and get fed through the engine causing ignition problems; leaves gummy deposits; corrosive and burns hot, can overheat the engine.
- fact: wrong or old gas can damage fuel system and engine and shorten mower life.
- fact: ethanol-free options: recreation fuel / REC-90 at some stations (find at pure-gas.org, US and Canada); up to $3.00 more per gallon at the pump; 4-cycle canned fuel at hardware stores/online (most expensive, convenient for small-tank mowers).
- opinion: author recommends higher-quality fuel if affordable; not always better to buy more expensive.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gas can break down as soon as 30 days; consume within that time.
- tip: store fuel in an approved container in a dry location; rotate so oldest fuel is used first.
- fact: if not used within 30 days, stabilize with an additive.
- fact: ethanol-free recreation or canned 4-cycle fuel needs no stabilizer, though it doesn't hurt.
- tip: author adds Sea Foam Motor Treatment to every tank (stabilizes, reduces clogging).

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: gasoline can damage or corrode fuel parts causing leaks; signs: smell, wet spots on the mower, burn marks in the lawn; leak may be hard to find. (Detail in a separate Snapper leaking-gas article.)
