## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil can cause engine damage from overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets or a hydrolocked engine. If lucky, correcting the level fixes it; otherwise needs a small engine mechanic.
- symptom: Overheating - crankcase pressure rises and loads parts since the crankshaft and rod push through excess oil; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil can't flow properly; aluminum engines add heat. Can pop out a valve guide or valve seat from the block (engine then needs to be scrapped out by a mechanic's assessment).
- symptom: Seal damage - pressure can blow out seals, oil drains out; if not noticed, engine can run out of oil, spin a rod or weld itself to the crankshaft, break the rod out the side of the block, piston can weld to the cylinder and seize.
- symptom: Lock up - oil gets into the cylinder; if oil gets past worn rings it can hydrolock: combustion chamber should hold only air and a little fuel; piston can't compress oil; likely bends the piston (rod) when starting.
- symptom: Runs poorly/smokes - oil can foul the spark plug (inhibits spark, power loss, runs rough); oil in the valve train burns in the cylinder giving thick whitish-blue stinky smoke; if the air filter clogs the engine may pull air and oil out of the crankcase.
- symptom: Blown gaskets - excess heat blows gaskets. Twin-cylinder gasket replacement costs more (engine must be removed from the mower, more labor).
- fact: Too much oil can prevent starting: small crankcases leave little margin; hydrolock when oil is past the piston; can foul the plug; oil via the valve train into the carburetor and on into the air filter also stops the engine running.
- mistake: Assuming going over the required oil is OK. Check twice; only add what the manufacturer requires. Don't keep running with engine problems.
- pro: Technician tests whether the engine is repairable or should be replaced.
- brand: Craftsman push, riding and zero-turn engine problems are mostly the same.

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- fact: Oil in the combustion chamber that can't be compressed; pulling the rope or starter won't let the piston move to the top. Likely bends the piston/rod.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- safety: Remove the spark plug wire first.
- step 1: Drain plug or valve port: may be on the engine side by the dipstick or bottom of the oil pan under the mower; drain pan ready; remove the plug for a quick second and replace; check level.
- step 2: Oil filter: loosen or remove the filter to drain a little; rag ready.
- step 3: Fill hole: many push-mower engines have no drain plug; tip the mower to drain a little from the fill hole.
- step 4: Oil extractor pump (evacuator) through a tube in the fill hole.
- step 5: Turkey baster for small amounts; don't reuse it for cooking.
- tip: Avoid overfilling: find capacity in the owner's manual (or search online). Most push-mower small engines take about 3/4 quart; a V-twin about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" engines (about 34 hp or larger) 3 quarts. Best: know capacity and add 1/2 quart at a time, then small amounts near full, checking the dipstick.
