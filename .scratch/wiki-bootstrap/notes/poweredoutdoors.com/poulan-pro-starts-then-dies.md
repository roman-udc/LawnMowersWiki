## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Poulan Pro starts then dies"
- alias: "mower dies after running a while"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-choke-setting, bad-ignition-coil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-deck-chute
- fact: Starts then dies when it no longer gets air, fuel or spark, or the engine overheats or is under extreme load.
- safety: Remove the ignition key (if any) and the spark plug wire before repairs; let the engine cool; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Gas begins to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol (corn-based) attracts moisture, leaving sticky residue that clogs the fuel system; fuel can separate.
- fix: Drain and flush the tank, refill with fresh fuel plus stabilizer and cleaner (author prefers Sea Foam Motor Treatment).
- tip: Ethanol-free fuel (recreation fuel, REC-90) at some stations or in hardware store canisters avoids ethanol effects but is pricey.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fix: Replace; replace annually.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fix: Use the shut-off valve or crimp to check sections; spray carburetor cleaner in the clogged section, blow with compressed air; replace if unclearable.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Needed when the tank sits lower than the carburetor; not all Poulan Pro mowers have one.
- confirm: Remove the fuel line from the carburetor into a container, start the mower; a good steady or pulsating flow means the pump works.
- fix: No good flow: install a new fuel pump.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Varnish from old fuel clogs the fuel jet and stops small parts working.
- confirm: Remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake, start; if it starts but does not keep running, clean the carburetor.
- tip: Take photos while removing it. Alternatives: repair shop cleaning, or a new carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fix: Paper element step 1: remove filter from housing; wipe out remaining dirt; do not let dirt fall into the intake.
- fix: step 2: tap on a solid surface. Do not use compressed air to blow out the filter (can damage it).
- fix: step 3: hold to a light; reuse if you see light; replace if no light, oil-covered or damaged.
- tip: Check and clean several times during the season; consult the manual for other filter types.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies (intermittent spark; may start but not stay running)
- fact: Carbon buildup fouls a plug; incorrect gap or a loose plug wire also cause intermittent spark.
- fix: Clean, but replace if excessively dark or damaged (dark tip, broken porcelain, burnt electrode). Verify gap and wire security. Replace annually during maintenance.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies (engine overheats and shuts down)
- fact: Fins dissipate heat from the engine block or cylinder head; plugged or broken fins let the engine overheat.
- fix: Clean around fins, replace broken fins, remove debris around the engine; careful of a hot engine.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Overfilling raises crankcase pressure (crankshaft and rod pushing through extra oil), may overheat and shut down; oil can enter the cylinder via the valve train and burn, making smoke that clogs the air filter.
- fix: Drain until the level is between the full lines on the dipstick: drain plug, oil filter, oil evacuator, or a turkey baster (do not reuse it for cooking). Replace the air filter if smoke-clogged. If problems persist, have a small engine mechanic check for internal damage.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Choke restricts airflow to give more fuel for cold starts; left on after the engine heats up the engine receives more fuel and less air and shuts down.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies (dies once hot)
- fact: Windings separate and short out so the plug does not get the voltage it needs.
- confirm: Check for a break in continuity with an ohm meter (after verifying the plug is good); replace if broken.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- confirm: Loosen or remove the cap and start; if it runs, let it run a short time; if it shuts down again with the cap on, replace the cap.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Deck packed with grass and dirt makes the engine work harder; dull blades magnify the load.
- fix: Scrape the deck and sharpen blades frequently; avoid wet conditions (wet grass sticks to the deck and clumps).
