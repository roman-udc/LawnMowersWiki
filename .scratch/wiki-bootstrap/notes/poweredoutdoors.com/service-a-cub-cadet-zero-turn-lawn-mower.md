## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: Annual service: change engine oil and filter; replace air filter and fuel filter; clean around engine and transmission; change transmission oil and filter if required; scrape deck; sharpen blades; replace worn/missing parts. Do before the mowing season.
- fact: Cub Cadet zero turn service interval: every 50 hours or annually. Keep engine, transmission and deck clean, lubricated.
- time: Dealer annual maintenance: about 2 hours of labor plus parts.
- tools: Engine oil and filter; transmission oil and filter if needed; air filter; fuel filter; spark plugs; socket/spanner wrenches; drain pan; funnel; filter wrench; rags, work gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover; deck scraper, wire brush or metal putty knife.
- step 1: Check the safety interlock per the Cub Cadet manual: engine shouldn't start if the parking brake isn't engaged; engine should shut down if PTO engaged and operator leaves the seat. Inspect ROPS and seat belt (work together).
- step 2: Battery (12-volt lawn and garden battery): voltmeter; 12.6 V or more = 100% charged. Cables tight; clean/replace corroded terminals; dielectric grease.
- step 3: Engine oil and filter every 50 hours or annually; new engine: after the first 5 hours.
- step 4: Spark plug: inspect; author replaces annually. If reusing, wire brush off buildup, check gap to the engine manufacturer spec; don't attach plug wires until done.
- step 5: Fuel filter replace and inspect lines for leaks; gas breaks down to gummy deposits; replace lines when dry and brittle.
- step 6: Tires: lug nuts tight, drive tire pressure equal both sides at Cub Cadet's recommended pressure; uneven pressure gives uneven cut and pulls to one side.
- step 7: Air filter: replace annually (starved engine runs sluggish and may overheat); remove carefully without dropping dirt in the intake, wipe housing, install new Cub Cadet filter, reattach cover; clean regularly in season.
- step 8: Remove engine shroud and clean dirt from around the engine, exhaust, cooling fins; also hydro fan cooling fins, guards and fan.
- step 9: Belts and pulleys: replace worn, cracked or glazed belts. Turn each pulley by hand slowly: resistance or noise means replace. Spindle: gloved, rock the blade up and down; movement means replace bearing or spindle housing assembly.
- step 10: Scrape the deck (jack stands for access) with a deck scraper or metal putty knife; stiff wire brush also works. Deck buildup compromises air movement/suction that stands the grass up for a precise cut.
- step 11: Blades: dull blades strain the mower and cut poorly (brown grass tips a couple of days after mowing); inspect each service; replace damaged or bent blades.
- step 12: Lubricate: high-performance multi-purpose grease on idler arm pivots on the deck and pumps and all pivot points and grease zerks; check the manual for all points; some caster wheels need grease.
- fact: Winterizing adds fuel system prep and battery care so it doesn't freeze.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Disconnect cables; positive (red +) is last off and first on. Removable caps: check water; if plates not covered add distilled water (well water not good; city water slightly better; distilled best).
- step 2: Hook up the charger.
- step 3: Check voltage: 11.8 V needs little charge time; 10.1 V needs longer; lower = longer.
- tip: "Slow bake" very low battery: 2 or 4 amps (some chargers have 6/4/2 amp switches) for about an hour, check that voltage is rising; if so charge another hour; if not rising buy a new battery (dealer, hardware store or automotive parts store).

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- step 1: Cover the work surface (cardboard, newspaper).
- step 2: Run 2-3 minutes to warm the oil.
- step 3: Remove key and spark plug boot(s).
- step 4: Clean around the oil plug or oil drain hose (depends on model), drain pan underneath, remove plug and drain.
- step 5: Clean around the filter; remove with a filter wrench; film of fresh oil on the new gasket; install; refit drain plug after draining.
- step 6: Fill to the manufacturer's quantity and type; neither too low nor too high.
- step 7: Refit spark plug boots, run a few minutes, stop, let oil settle/cool, recheck, top up.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Cub Cadet calls it transmission oil. Low or old fluid makes the mower seem weak and can damage the transmission. Most Cub Cadet zero turns: change transmission oil every 200 hours (check manual). If a full change isn't due, check level and top off.
- fact: Level to the "full cold" line on the expansion reservoirs on top behind the seat: one for the left transmission, one for the right.
- fact: Some entry-level zero turns have non-serviceable transmissions where oil can't be changed. Procedure varies by model; air must be removed after a change.
- step 1: Park flat, remove key, engage parking brake, wait for moving parts to stop; engine/transmission may be hot.
- step 2: A filter is near each rear wheel, maybe under a guard or skid plate; remove it and clean around the filters first.
- step 3: Pan under a filter, unscrew, drain; repeat for the other; recycle old oil.
- step 4: Thin layer of fresh oil on new filter rubber seals; install clockwise; after contact turn about one more full turn.
- step 5: Fill with good quality fresh 20W-50 oil through the expansion reservoirs (clean around caps first) to the "full cold" line; two tanks, one each side.
- step 6: Transaxles with a vent plug on each: loosen, fill until oil comes out of the vent plug, tighten, continue to the "cold full" line.
- brand: Cub Cadet transmission oil spec in this Source: 20W-50.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Jack stands under the rear so wheels no longer touch the ground.
- step 2: Open the bypass valve, start the mower, about 1/2 throttle, release the parking brake.
- step 3: Move the speed levers forward and reverse slowly about 6 times in total.
- step 4: Repeat with the bypass valve closed until wheels rotate at a normal rate and sound.
- step 5: Let the system cool, recheck oil level, add if needed.

## page: guides/zero-turn-safety
- fact: Interlock: no start without the parking brake engaged; engine shuts down if PTO engaged and operator leaves the seat. Check ROPS and seat belt. Zero turns are dangerous.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Cub Cadet zero turn: every 50 hours or annually (vs 100 hours in the generic zero-turn article).
