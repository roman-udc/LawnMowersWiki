## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Ferris zero turn shakes and vibrates"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade (wrong blade; see unmapped), dull-or-bent-blade (unbalanced), debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring (worn idler bracket/spring), debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Some vibration is normal when the engine runs; it feels worse on an uneven lawn.
- safety: Before looking for the vibration remove the ignition key and spark plug wires and wait for all parts to stop moving; wear safety gear; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- symptom: Extreme vibration when engine mounting bolts (motor mounts, installed under the engine to absorb vibration) are broken, loose or missing.
- fix: Inspect, tighten loose bolts, replace worn or missing ones.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: The PTO clutch transfers power from engine to blades. The ball bearing may fail on an electric clutch causing shaking.
- fix: Replace the PTO clutch.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Unbalanced blade (heavier on one end) wobbles and vibrates at high speed. Causes: normal use (dirt under the deck wears blades unevenly) and sharpening (more metal removed from one end).
- fix: Balance with a blade balancer before installing.

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- symptom: Wrong-size or non-fitting blades cause vibration and can damage the deck shell and spindle housings.
- fact: Ferris sells blades designed for its decks; aftermarket blades may not fit even with right length and center hole size. Author prefers OEM blades.
- tip: To check an aftermarket blade, lay a new OEM blade on it and match center hole size, length and width. Author has received aftermarket blades that the maker said fit but did not.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- symptom: Wire, string and long weeds wrap around the spindle; hard to see while mowing.
- fix: Remove what's wrapped around it; then check spindle and housing for damage.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: Bent spindle or worn bearings in the spindle housing make blades wobble when engaged.
- confirm: Work gloves on; spark plug wires disconnected and key out; grab each end of the blade and rock it up and down; movement or a knocking sound = problem. Then remove blade and spindle housing, inspect bearing/spindle, repair or replace.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: Stretched deck belt leaves slack and doesn't seat securely on the pulleys, so the deck vibrates.
- fix: Replace if stretched, cracked or glazed. If the belt looks good, check routing around the pulleys and adjust tension to remove slack.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: Failed pulley bearings let the pulley sit not flat/parallel to the deck, so the mower shakes as the belt runs.
- confirm: Check each pulley is securely attached with no wobble (one side higher off the deck than the other = likely bad bearing). Slowly rotate each pulley by hand, feel for restriction, listen for bearing noise.
- fix: Replace the bearing; some pulleys use sealed bearings only, so replace the pulley and bearing assembly.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- symptom: Loose or worn idler bracket or spring; the bracket hole where the spring attaches wears larger or the spring stretches.
- fix: Replace worn bracket or spring; check bearing and bushing in the engagement linkage and replace if worn.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Small debris (even a wood chip) in crevices causes severe vibration; debris under pulleys keeps them from sitting flat; buildup stops the tensioner keeping belt tension, causing slack, shaking, even the belt coming off.
- fix: Remove spindle covers and clean the deck regularly; clean the zero-turn after each mowing (also prevents corrosion from debris holding moisture).
