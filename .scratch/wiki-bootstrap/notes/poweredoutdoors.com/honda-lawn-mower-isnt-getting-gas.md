## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Honda mower barely chugs along, acting like it's not getting gas"
- alias: "mower is ready to stall out / died from lack of fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Source frames this as a fuel restriction, usually from old gasoline clogging filter, lines and carburetor, or a bad gas cap.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- brand: Honda requires unleaded gasoline, minimum octane 87, maximum ethanol content 10 percent.
- fact: Gasoline can begin breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol (described as corn-based) and the moisture it attracts gum the fuel system and degrade components.
- fix: Drain old fuel with a siphon; fill with fresh gasoline treated with Sea Foam Motor Treatment.
- tip: Use fuel within 30 days or add a stabilizer; author treats every tank and any stored extra with Sea Foam (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fix: Replace. Arrow on the filter housing must point in the direction of fuel flow: toward the carburetor and away from the tank.
- tip: Replace the fuel filter once a year, typically with the annual Honda service; relatively inexpensive.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: Arrow on side of filter housing points toward the carburetor, away from the fuel tank. Replace annually.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Old fuel leaves gummy deposits that clog lines.
- confirm: step 1: start and stop flow with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank, or crimp the line.
- confirm: step 2: remove the end of a hose section farthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel will not run uphill).
- confirm: step 3: turn fuel on; you should see a stream. If not, shut off and remove that hose section.
- fix: Spray carburetor cleaner into the removed section to loosen the blockage, blow compressed air through; repeat until clear; reinstall. If you cannot clear it, replace the line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- confirm: After confirming fuel reaches the carburetor, remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake port, start the mower. If it starts, runs and then dies, the carburetor is restricting fuel and needs cleaning.
- fix: Disassemble and clean if mechanically inclined (Source links step-by-step article), have a small engine mechanic do it, or replace the carburetor assembly.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Gas cap is vented; a plugged vent forms a vacuum in the tank and restricts fuel flow.
- fix: Try cleaning the cap or replace with a new Honda gas cap.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Honda: unleaded, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol. Lower ethanol is better; ethanol-free recreation fuel (REC-90) is sold at some stations and in 4-cycle fuel cans at hardware stores.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Treat fresh fuel with stabilizer; use within 30 days; keep ethanol at or below 10 percent.
