## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets, or hydro-locked engine
- fact: pressure builds in the crankcase and can blow out seals so oil leaks; unnoticed leak can run the engine out of oil - heat can spin a rod or weld it to the crankshaft, break the rod and throw it out of the block, weld piston to cylinder (seize)
- fact: overheating - crankshaft and rod push through more oil, crankcase pressure rises, internal parts under load; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil may not flow; aluminum engines get very hot; can pop a valve guide or valve seat out of the block (needs an experienced small engine mechanic)
- symptom: runs poorly and smokes - oil reaches the spark plug and fouls it (inhibits spark); oil into the valve train burns in the cylinder giving thick whitish-blue stinky smoke; with a clogged air filter engine may pull air and oil out of the crankcase; oil can pass through carburetor into air filter and stop engine running
- fact: blown gaskets from excess heat; gasket replacement on a twin-cylinder engine costs more than a push mower engine because the engine must be removed (extra labor)
- safety: remove spark plug boot(s) and let the engine cool before troubleshooting; don't keep running when you notice engine problems
- pro: see a small engine technician if a simple oil change or repair doesn't fix; technician tests whether repairable or should be replaced
- tip: check twice and add only what the manufacturer requires

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- symptom: engine turns over very hard or locks up
- fact: oil gets past the rings (due to wear) into the cylinder; piston can't compress oil (designed to compress air); possible bent piston when starting; "hydrolock" - oil fills combustion chamber so piston can't reach the top; pulling the rope or using the starter doesn't move it
- fact: the small oil pan on lawn mower engines leaves little room for error

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- causes-named: overfilled-oil, clogged-air-filter
- symptom: whitish-blue stinky smoke when excess oil reaches the valve train and burns in the cylinder

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- causes-named: hydrolocked-engine, overfilled-oil
- symptom: too much oil can stop the engine from running; hydrolock

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: too much oil can foul the plug and stop the engine running

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- safety: remove the spark plug wire first
- step 1 (drain plug or valve port): find it on the side of the engine by the dipstick or bottom of the oil pan; drain pan ready; remove the plug for a quick second and replace; check level
- step 2 (oil filter): if the engine has a filter, loosen or remove it to drain a little oil; rag ready
- step 3 (fill hole): many push mower engines have no drain plug; tip the mower and drain a little from the fill hole
- step 4 (oil extractor pump / evacuator): removes oil through a tube inserted in the fill hole
- step 5 (turkey baster): good for small amounts; don't reuse it for cooking
- fact: methods listed are alternatives

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: know crankcase capacity from owner's manual (or search engine); most push mower small engines take about 3/4 quart; v-twin gas engine about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" (about 34 horsepower or larger) takes 3 quarts
- tip: add only 1/2 quart at a time and keep checking until the mark; near full add a little at a time and check the dipstick or gauge
