## page: guides/mower-got-wet
- fact: won't start after rain because water may have gotten into engine oil, fuel system, and electrical components (ignition switch, safety switches, spark plug boot).
- fact: mowers aren't waterproof but can get wet; some parts tolerate a little water; don't leave out in heavy or consistent rain; light rain mowing possible but wet grass clumps, builds up in the deck, gives a poor cut, and makes tire tracks more pronounced.
- step 1: dry the mower: leaf blower or air compressor with air gun; or outdoors on a warm day (check forecast), breeze helps.
- step 2: check engine oil for water; milky white oil = water in oil; do a regular oil change. Not changing it can cause major internal damage.
- step 3: air filter: if water got in oil, probably got into the crankcase via the air filter. Replace a paper filter (sun-drying doesn't give 100% protection). Foam filter: wring out, dry in sun, apply air filter oil, wring, reinstall (foam needs oil to keep dust out).
- step 4: fuel tank: cap has a vent so rain can get in; water doesn't burn in the combustion chamber and can dilute fuel so it won't run. If water found: drain into a safe container, take to recycling center, refill fresh, change fuel filter if fitted. If you can't drain all, try STA-BIL or Sea Foam. Fuel has about a 30-day shelf life.
- step 5: electrical: spray WD-40 inside the ignition switch; remove spark plug wire and spray boot and wire to displace water; spray safety switches (water collects at switches, takes path of least resistance).
- step 6: start it; may take a pull or two or a couple key turns; may smoke initially; dirty-looking liquid may drip from the engine area (rainwater, oil, dirt), should stop at operating temperature. Then lubricate choke and throttle cables and any wet wires with WD-40; get it inside the cable conduit or cables can rust or freeze in cool weather.
- tip: author runs a fuel additive every other fill to keep condensation out in humid areas.
- tip: store in a garage, pole barn or shed; if outdoors, a waterproof cover (author bought one for his zero-turn).

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- symptom: water in ignition switch, safety switches, spark plug boot after rain. Fix: spray WD-40 as above.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- symptom: water via the vented cap; fuel diluted, won't run. Fix: drain, refill, change filter.

## page: causes/oil-in-air-filter-or-tipped-mower
- fact: (water, not oil): water in the oil likely entered the crankcase through the air filter; replace paper filter or dry/oil foam.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: change oil when it looks milky white; article links separate push and riding mower oil change articles (no steps here).
