## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "Ferris lawn mower leaking gas"
- alias: "dead grass trail / smell of gas"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: gas dripping on lawn leaves dead-grass trail; or only a gas smell.
- fact: leak may come from carburetor (bad bowl gasket, stuck float, stuck float needle) or fuel filter, shutoff valve, line, pump, gas cap or tank seam.
- safety: gas fumes harmful; work in well-ventilated area away from combustibles; wear safety glasses; follow other safety procedures.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: leak between the carburetor and the bowl at its bottom (gasket); or leak near top by the air intake port (stuck float).
- fact: bowl gasket looks like a rubber band; dries and goes brittle with age because of heat/cool cycles near the engine.
- fact: float regulates fuel entering the bowl; stuck float lets bowl overflow. Float needle works with the float; a stuck needle requires carburetor rebuild.
- fact: old gas left in carburetor leaves varnish/gummy deposits that stick small parts, so gas runs out.
- fix: bowl leak -> replace gasket; need the engine model and spec number (different from mower model/serial).
- fix: stuck float -> disassemble and free it (clean carburetor); may need rebuild kit or replacement carburetor.
- tip: tapping carburetor with a rubber mallet (or a tool's rubber handle) can temporarily free a stuck needle, once or twice; eventually replace the float needle.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: stuck float needle fix is a carburetor rebuild; stuck float may need a rebuild kit or replacement carburetor depending on condition.

## page: procedures/replace-the-carburetor
- fact: replacing is an option if too worn for rebuild (stuck float case).

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: old gas leaves varnish/gummy deposits causing small parts to stick and gas to run out; clean carburetor to free the float.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step 1: (replace bowl gasket procedure from article) Wipe around the outside of carburetor to remove dirt and debris.
- step 2: Remove the screw at the bottom of the carburetor bowl; have a rag ready to catch remaining gas.
- step 3: Lower and remove the bowl.
- step 4: Remove old gasket and install new gasket.
- step 5: Keep lubricants off the new gasket.
- step 6: Reinstall bowl and tighten screw.
- fact: note these steps are the Ferris carburetor gasket replacement, not a full carb clean.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: replace fuel filter at least annually or when plugged; old fuel softens plastic housing so it leaks at seams; filter can also crack.
- fix: replace leaking filter. Plastic filter ends can soften and break off in the fuel line; be careful. Replace regularly; filter keeps dirt out that wears carburetor and engine.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: annually or when plugged; careful: plastic filter ends soften and can break off in the line.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump degrades from old gas like the filter; check seams for leaks; replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- brand: Ferris zero-turn/walk-behind tanks are high-density polyethylene; can fail at the seams. Replace tank when it leaks at a seam.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- brand: Ferris has a fuel shutoff valve; a zero turn most likely has two, by left and right fuel tanks. Valves leak over time; replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: lines dry, crack and leak with age. Follow lines from tanks; check hoses, connectors and clamps.
- fix: replace leaking lines; match line diameter. Good practice to replace dry/cracked lines before they leak.
- tip: replace pinch-style clamps with worm gear clamps (less likely to puncture the line).

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-line
- tip: note diameter of line; swap pinch clamps for worm gear clamps.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: seal in the cap dries out; gas sloshes and leaks past it. If mower sat, evaporation may leave no wet spot.
- confirm: carefully rock mower back and forth (not aggressively; don't tip it) to splash fuel up around the cap; a wet area outside the tank around the cap means replace the Ferris gas cap.
