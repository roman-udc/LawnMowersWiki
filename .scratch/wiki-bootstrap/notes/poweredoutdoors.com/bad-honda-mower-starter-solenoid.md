## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "mower won't turn over, all I hear is clicking"
- alias: "click or hum when I turn the key and mower doesn't start"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, bad-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: Clicking with no turnover often is a bad starter solenoid.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Solenoid is an on/off electromagnet switch that engages the starter motor. Usually mounted on the starter motor but need not be; some are mounted closer to the battery. Found on every Honda electric-start engine.
- fact: Inside is a spring and a copper plate. Can go bad if the spring gets weak or the copper plate corrodes. Can also fail as a result of a weak starter, bad battery, or bad ground.
- symptom: On a riding or zero-turn Honda, a click or hum when the key is turned and the mower doesn't start. Also a wire getting hot and beginning to smoke or melt.
- fact: Appearance varies: round or square, 3 or 4 posts. Some attach at the top of the starter. Positive (+) battery wire attaches to one side; follow the positive cable from the battery to find it. Wiring differs by mower; schematics may include lights and 12-volt ports.
- confirm: Solenoid is likely bad when it is bypassed and the starter motor turns the engine over.
- mistake: Before replacing the solenoid, check for loose or corroded wires or a bad ground; these keep the solenoid from getting needed power.
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches (check loose wires), needle-nose pliers (if screwdriver doesn't work), battery charger (optional)

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- step 1: Check battery is fully charged: voltmeter reads at least 12.3 volts.
- step 2: Ready the mower: set parking brake, make sure in neutral, key to on.
- step 3: Bypass the solenoid: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the two cables (cable from the battery and cable to the starter). A spark may occur; this is common. If the engine turns over while bypassed, the solenoid is probably bad. Needle-nose pliers can be used to jump it if the screwdriver doesn't work well.
- step 4 (bench test, starter-mounted solenoid): Remove the starter from the engine. Attach battery charger negative (-) clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid. Quick bench test to see whether the starter works with the solenoid.
- safety: Follow Honda operator's manual precautions; always wear safety gear; bypass spark is normal but be careful.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fact: Weak battery can contribute to solenoid failure; check battery at least 12.3 V before testing (links to battery-draining article).

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: Bad ground, loose or corroded wires can prevent the solenoid from getting power.
