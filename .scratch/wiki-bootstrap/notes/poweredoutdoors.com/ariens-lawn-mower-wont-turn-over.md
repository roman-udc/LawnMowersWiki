## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Ariens lawn mower won't turn over"
- alias: "Ariens mower won't crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- symptom: starter motor is not getting the power it needs to turn the engine over
- safety: follow the Ariens operator's manual; disconnect the black negative battery cable before electrical repairs
- pro: if fuses keep blowing, take it to a dealer/repair shop to find the root cause; if unsure how to diagnose safely, use a professional

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: weak or dead battery will not supply the power to turn the engine over (problems/engine-wont-crank)
- fact: keep battery fully charged to extend life, especially in cold weather; a battery stored in cold weather can freeze when not fully charged
- confirm: test battery per the author's "5 Things That Are Draining the Life of Your Lawn Mower Battery" article
- fix: if battery fails to hold a charge, replace it (dealer, hardware or automotive store); bring the old battery to avoid a core fee

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- tools: battery charger (12 V), screwdriver to uncover battery, eye/skin protection
- safety: wear protective gear for eyes and skin; do not touch anything that doesn't have a rubber coating
- step 1: access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover); do not remove battery from its casing
- step 2: connect charger cables, positive first (red, plus sign) to positive terminal
- step 3: attach negative cable (black) to negative terminal
- step 4: set charger voltage and amperage; average mower battery is 12 V; more amps charges faster; start at 2 amps and go up to no more than 10 amps; a slow charge is best

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: no crank (problems/engine-wont-crank)
- fact: vibration and bouncing while mowing can loosen cables, wiring and connections; moisture on connections causes corrosion
- confirm: check cables and connections after the battery; make sure secure with no corrosion that breaks continuity
- fix: clean corrosion with small wire brush and baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); disconnect battery and remove components before cleaning; replace terminals/components if corrosion is severe

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: fuse protects the electrical system from power surge or short
- confirm: place multimeter probes on each prong of the fuse, measure resistance; near 0 = good, infinity = bad
- fix: replace with a fuse of the same capacity
- pro: if fuses keep blowing, take to dealer/shop to find root cause

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: set multimeter to resistance; touch a probe to each prong of the fuse
- step 2: reading near 0 = good; infinity = bad
- step 3: replace blown fuse with same capacity fuse
- pro: repeated blowing means root cause needed at dealer/shop

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: insert key and turn, nothing happens; mower fails to turn over (problems/engine-wont-crank)
- confirm: multimeter continuity; find prongs marked B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid); key in start position, multimeter on resistance, probes on B and S; good switch is near 0 ohms, bad switch reads infinite and needs replacing

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: Ariens has an operator presence control system; a defective switch prevents cranking; example: Ariens riding mower will not start when brake isn't engaged, and a faulty brake switch can block starting even with brake on
- confirm: test with multimeter; may temporarily bypass the switch for troubleshooting only
- safety: never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run a mower with a safety switch bypassed

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: starter solenoid is an electromagnetic switch that triggers the starter motor
- fact: can fail when its spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also cause failure
- confirm: need a fully charged battery before testing; author links a separate solenoid diagnosis article

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid, remove and test the starter
- tip: author recommends having a starter/alternator repair shop test and rebuild the starter if possible rather than buying a pricey new one
