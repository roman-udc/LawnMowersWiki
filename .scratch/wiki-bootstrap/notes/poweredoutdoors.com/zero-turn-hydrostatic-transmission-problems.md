## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "zero turn won't move, nothing happens when moving steering levers"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, low-hydraulic-oil, air-in-hydraulic-system, worn-drive-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, bad-hydrostatic-transmission, hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: Ensure the transmission bypass valve is not open (open lets you push the mower). It must be in the operating position. There is a separate bypass valve for each side.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "zero turn pulling to one side"
- alias: "weak on one side", "one side moves faster than the other"
- causes-named: low-hydraulic-oil, air-in-hydraulic-system, worn-drive-belt, bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: Hydrostatic symptoms list: pulling to one side; one side faster/weak; excessive wheel noise; moving slowly; lack of power; hot hydraulic oil; hydraulic oil leak; nothing when moving steering levers.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Low level may not lubricate enough and makes the mower feel weak and move slowly on the side with low oil.
- confirm: Check the overflow tanks just behind or under the seat. Oil must be at the FULL COLD line when cold.
- fix: Add fresh oil; check for leaks if low.
- brand: Some entry-level zero-turns have non-serviceable (sealed) transmissions: no filter/oil change; some allow adding oil.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: Air trapped when the system isn't bled after a fluid change causes cavitation: excessive noise when the wheel turns and slow movement.
- fix: Bleed the system (procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system).

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Park on a flat level surface, engage brake, confirm PTO switch is off.
- step 2: Confirm hydraulic oil level is full in each transmission.
- step 3: Raise rear wheels just off the ground on jack stands (best method; alternatively run the mower in a large open area).
- step 4: Start the engine.
- step 5: Release the parking brake.
- step 6: Engage (open) the transmission bypass valves so wheels can spin by hand; one per side.
- step 7: Move the motion control levers slowly forward and reverse about 6 times.
- step 8: Close the bypass valve.
- step 9: Move the levers forward and reverse about 6 times.
- step 10: Repeat with bypass open and closed as needed until drive wheels move smoothly at normal speed forward and reverse with normal noise.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Always wipe loose dirt from around oil fill caps, transmission filters and drain plug before opening. Change at intervals in the operator's manual; don't change early unless oil is getting hot (lubrication problem) or contamination is suspected. Use fresh oil; don't leave bottle caps off. Interval and fluid type depend on the transmission manufacturer. Use only the oil recommended by the zero-turn or transmission manufacturer.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: Pump drive belt transfers engine power to transaxles. Wears, slips or comes off the pulley.
- confirm: Look for worn edges, cracking or glazed appearance; replace if any.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Tensioner pulley (mostly hard plastic) or its bearing can wear/break; replace. Keep the tensioner arm greased so it doesn't freeze. Idler spring tensions the pump belt; replace if broken or fallen out.

## page: causes/bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: Zero-turn uses an independent hydraulic wheel motor and pump on each side; each wheel moves independently. Fluid flow increases as the steering lever moves away from neutral, raising motor pressure and wheel speed.
- fact: Most hydraulic failures are due to oil contamination.
- fact: Pump leak: best to replace the pump (expensive). A rebuild kit is only a temporary fix; opening the transmission introduces contaminants leading to failure; author has seen mechanics repair then buy a new transmission.
- pro: Take to the dealer (Hustler dealership named) for hydraulic pump leaks or if the mower runs fine cold but stops when hot.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: Bad or low fluid can't lubricate efficiently: more friction, overheating. Hot fluid can indicate more extensive damage.
- pro: See above (runs fine cold, stops when hot).

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: See problems/mower-wont-move.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- tip: Check hydro fluid level regularly; change oil and filters per manufacturer's interval; use correct oil; keep dirt out (wipe around overfill caps, drain plugs, filter area before opening); clean the transmission cooling system (hydro fan cooling fins, guards, fan); inspect pump belt, pulleys and spring for tension.
