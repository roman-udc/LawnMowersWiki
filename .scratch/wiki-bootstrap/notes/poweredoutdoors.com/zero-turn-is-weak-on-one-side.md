## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "zero turn is weak on one side"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, brake-hung-up, low-hydraulic-oil, air-in-hydraulic-system, bad-hydrostatic-transmission, unbalanced-load
- fact: best case: low tire, bad steering dampers, hung-up brake, trapped air, unbalanced load; worst: hydraulic oil/filter change needed, or transmission pump / wheel motor failure.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: a low tire makes the mower pull to one side and seem weak on that side; pressures must be equal; also causes uneven cut.

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fact: steering dampers are small gas shocks on each steering lever for smooth control; failure -> jerky movement; the mower feels weak/unresponsive on the bad-damper side when the lever is pushed forward.
- fix: make sure damper is secure, tighten hardware, replace if bad.

## page: causes/brake-hung-up
- confirm: raise the rear with jack stands (securely), disengage transmission with the bypass levers so wheels move freely, spin each wheel and check for resistance.
- fix: repair/replace what causes resistance.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- fact: when one tire moves faster than the other, adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change that tire's rotation speed so both match; varies by model; consult operator's manual.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: low or old oil gives poor lubrication and weak hydraulics on one side.
- fix: level must be at FULL COLD on the overflow tanks; add fresh oil to that level; check for leaks if low. Change hydraulic oil and filter if past the manufacturer's service interval.
- brand: some entry-level zero-turns have non-serviceable (sealed) transmissions whose fluid can't be changed; contact the dealer.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: change oil and filter at manufacturer's interval; sealed transmissions on entry-level zero-turns are not serviceable.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: after changing hydraulic fluid, air must be bled; trapped air can stop one side working.
- fix: on most zero-turns: flat surface; chock the front wheels to stop it rolling; raise the rear drive tires off the ground and let them spin forward and backward until no excessive noise and wheels move at normal speed. See operator's manual for model steps.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: place mower on a flat surface.
- step 2: chock front wheels.
- step 3: raise rear drive tires off the ground.
- step 4: let them spin forward and backward until excessive noise stops and wheels move at normal speed.
- step 5: refer to manual for detailed steps for the model.

## page: causes/bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: zero-turn hydro is two separate systems (pump plus wheel motor each side) so each wheel works independently; control lever movement raises hydraulic flow to the motor.
- fix: a leaking pump or failed wheel motor should be replaced; expensive. A pump rebuild kit may work briefly but in the author's experience the pump will most likely need replacement.

## page: causes/unbalanced-load
- fact: grass catcher, trailer or other attachment can pull the mower to one side and seem weaker; center the load weight.
