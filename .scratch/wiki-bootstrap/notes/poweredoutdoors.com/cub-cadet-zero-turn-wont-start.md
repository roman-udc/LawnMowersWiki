- safety: follow operator's manual; wait for the engine to cool and remove the spark plug wire(s) before repairs
- brand: Cub Cadet zero-turn

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: no fuel; forgot to refill, fuel gauge may be faulty, or a fuel leak developed
- fix: check the fuel system for leaks; repair or replace leaking part; add fresh fuel

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- brand: Cub Cadet zero turns require unleaded gas, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol
- fact: ethanol attracts moisture from air; ethanol/water mix leaves varnish and gummy deposits causing restrictions or component failure; use low-ethanol or ethanol-free fuel; high-ethanol gas can cause significant engine damage

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: old gas is often the main culprit when the mower runs sluggishly, won't start, or dies after running
- fact: gasoline begins breaking down and losing effectiveness as soon as 30 days; buy only what you can use in 30 days
- tip: if more than 30 days' worth, stabilize with a fuel additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (author adds it to all small-engine fuel)
- fix: drain the tank (a manual siphon pump is an inexpensive tool for this), add fresh gas plus fuel additive to clean the system, reduce moisture and stabilize

## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- step 1: drain the tank with a manual siphon pump
- step 2: refill with fresh gas and a fuel additive

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Cub Cadet filter strains fuel from the tank; replace once a year; more often if a tank of fuel was very dirty (but don't use dirty fuel - drain the tank)
- fix: replace with a new filter; install with the arrow on the filter pointing in the direction of fuel flow

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: remove clogged filter
- step 2: install new filter with the arrow pointing in the fuel flow direction
- fact: replace yearly

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: sticky deposits from old fuel narrow the line
- fix: remove the line from the mower; spray carburetor cleaner into it; follow with compressed air; if the clog won't clear, buy a new line with the right diameter and length
- tip: replace lines that are dry and cracking even if not clogged

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump works against gravity to lift fuel to the carburetor; most Cub Cadet mowers use a vacuum-style pump that uses engine vacuum; fuel can degrade the pump over time
- fix: replace if leaking, or if no constant/pulsating flow
- confirm (test): step 1 shut off fuel supply with the shut-off valve under the tank or pinch the line with clamps; step 2 remove the hose from the pump inlet port and put it in a container lower than the tank; step 3 open fuel flow - flow into container confirms fuel reaches the pump, no flow = a restriction before the pump; step 4 shut off fuel, replace hose on inlet; step 5 remove the hose from the carburetor into a container; step 6 open fuel flow and start the engine, watch for steady or pulsating flow; step 7 shut off mower and reattach hose to carburetor
- fact: article also notes a carburetor can fail over time

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: carburetor can't regulate air and fuel so engine won't start; old fuel clogs passages and leaves varnish making internal parts stick
- fix: clean the carburetor and replace stuck or damaged parts; or take to a small-engine shop, or replace the whole carburetor assembly
- fact: article links its own Cub Cadet carburetor cleaning steps (not in this source)

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cub Cadet zero-turn tank vents through the fuel cap; no vent = vacuum in tank keeps fuel from reaching the carburetor
- confirm: run with and without the cap; if it won't start with the cap on, loosen it (you may hear vacuum release); if it then starts, suspect cap venting; tighten and run to see if it dies and won't restart until the cap is loosened
- fix: replace the fuel cap

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: loose wires or wrong gap cause intermittent running/starting; fouled plugs cause intermittent spark
- confirm: remove plug and inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, or very dark colour
- fix: replace if damaged, worn or very dark; otherwise clean the tip with a small wire brush; gap correctly; secure wires

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: reduced air; may not start, may overheat or smoke
- tip: author recommends replacing the filter annually and checking condition through the season
- fix: clean if in good condition; replace if very dirty or damaged

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove the air filter from its housing
- step 2: don't let dirt fall into the intake; wipe the housing and cap with a dry cloth
- step 3: tap the filter against a solid surface to knock loose dirt
- step 4: hold up to a light and check for light through the paper element
- step 5: reuse if you can see light; replace if no light, extremely dirty or damaged
- step 6: install the filter
- fact: this is the PAPER filter method

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: disconnect battery and remove corroded components; clean with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) or cola plus a small metal brush; make sure all wiring and components are securely installed

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: test with a multimeter; reading less than 12.7 volts - put the battery on a charger
- fix: replace if it can't hold a charge

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step 1: measure battery voltage with a multimeter
- step 2: below 12.7 volts = charge it

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence control system; defective switch can stop starting
- confirm: test switches with a multimeter; temporary bypass only for troubleshooting
- safety: never operate without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: test with a multimeter; replace if bad

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum when turning the key; a wire attached to the solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts
- fact: electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts airflow to start a cold engine; wrong lever position can prevent starting

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- brand: Cub Cadet zero-turn cold start, combined throttle/choke lever: step 1 full choke; step 2 turn key to start and release when engine starts; step 3 move lever to the choke detent position; step 4 run a few minutes before loading
- brand: separate throttle and choke levers: step 1 choke lever full choke; step 2 throttle halfway between slow and fast; step 3 key to start, release when engine starts; step 4 choke lever to off; step 5 idle at half throttle a few minutes before full throttle
- fact: safety interlock requirements to start: sit in the operator's seat; steering control levers in the outward position to engage the brake (or engage the separate brake lever); PTO off

## page: procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower
- step 1: sit in the operator's seat
- step 2: put steering levers outward to engage the brake (or engage brake lever)
- step 3: make sure PTO is off
- step 4: set choke per model (see incorrect-starting-procedure notes), turn key to start, release when engine starts
- step 5: warm up a few minutes before mowing

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: contributes to a weak battery that won't start the mower next time
- fact: bad stator or alternator, among several other electrical parts; article links a test using an ohmmeter
- pro: bring to the Cub Cadet zero-turn dealer; many components, otherwise you throw parts at it; electrical parts are expensive and usually non-returnable

## page: causes/bad-alternator
- fact: bad stator or alternator can cause the charging system to fail
