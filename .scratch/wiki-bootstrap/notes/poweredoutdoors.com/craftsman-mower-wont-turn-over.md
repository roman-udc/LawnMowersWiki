## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Craftsman mower won't turn over or crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- safety: take caution with the electrical system to avoid injury/electrocution; consult an experienced mechanic if unsure.
- tip: rule out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid before the starter.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: engine will not crank (problems/engine-wont-crank)
- confirm: a fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 volts; lower means charge it.
- fix: charge, and replace if it fails to hold a charge. Buy new at a Craftsman dealer, hardware or automotive store; bring the old battery or you will probably be charged a core fee.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for eyes and skin. Do not touch anything without a rubber coating while connected.
- step 1: access the battery and terminals (may need a screwdriver to uncover the battery). Do not remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: connect the positive cable first (red, or the one with the plus sign) to the positive terminal.
- step 3: attach the negative cable (black or minus sign) to the negative terminal.
- step 4: set the charger's voltage and amperage; average mower battery voltage is 12 V. More amperage charges faster; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best.
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- symptom: no crank (problems/engine-wont-crank); break in continuity
- fact: wires and connections loosen from vibration and shaking during operation; moisture corrodes connections and terminals.
- fix: check connections are secure; remove corrosion with a baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); secure loose wires; replace damaged or severely corroded wires, connections, terminals.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: a bad ground can make a solenoid fail; see bad-starter-solenoid.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: fuse protects the electrical system; found on all Craftsman mowers except some manual pull-start models.
- confirm: place a multimeter probe on each prong of the fuse and measure resistance; near 0 = good, infinity = bad.
- fix: replace with the same amperage fuse. If fuses keep blowing, take it to a dealer or repair shop to find the root cause.
- procedure: see procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: with a multimeter on resistance, probe each prong of the fuse.
- step 2: near 0 resistance = good; infinity = blown.
- step 3: replace a blown fuse with one of identical amperage.
- pro: repeat blowing means have the root cause found at a dealer/shop.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned and nothing happens (problems/engine-wont-crank); some models have a toggle switch instead of a key switch.
- confirm: multimeter on resistance (continuity); find the prongs marked "B" (Battery) and "S" (Starter Solenoid). Insert key and turn to start (or flip the toggle to start). Touch one probe to B and the other to S. Good switch: near 0 ohms. Bad: infinite resistance, replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: the mower has an operator presence control system with safety switches that prevent starting if certain conditions are met, such as engaging the brake. A defective switch can stop the engine cranking.
- confirm: test with a multimeter; may temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one, for troubleshooting only.
- safety: never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run a mower with a switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic switch that initiates the starter motor. Goes bad when the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes; a weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also make it fail.
- confirm: battery must be fully charged before testing; follow the steps in the Source's linked solenoid article (see how-to-tell-your-lawn-mower-solenoid-is-bad).

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid, remove the starter and test it.
- pro: author recommends a shop that specialises in starter/alternator repair test and rebuild it if possible before buying a pricey new starter.
