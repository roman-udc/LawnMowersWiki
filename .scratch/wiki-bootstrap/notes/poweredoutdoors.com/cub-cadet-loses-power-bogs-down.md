## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Cub Cadet loses power and bogs down"
- alias: "engine doesn't feel like giving the power it once did"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: mower loses power when the engine doesn't get good airflow and fuel flow, or runs hot (low oil, clogged air passageways, plugged air filter, dirty carb, restricted fuel components); or when under load from a plugged deck or too fast ground speed. Title says 13 reasons, headings say 14.
- tip: engine repairs get expensive if not addressed right away.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dirt and clippings kicked up are drawn into the air filtration system.
- tip: replace the air filter annually and clean several times through the mowing season; more often in very dusty conditions.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: instructions are for a paper-style filter.
- step 1: remove the cover from the housing (often clips, knobs or wing nuts).
- step 2: carefully remove the filter without letting dirt fall into the air intake.
- step 3: clean any dirt remaining in the housing with a dry clean rag.
- step 4: tap the filter against a hard surface to knock dirt loose.
- step 5: hold it up to a light; if light shines through the paper, reuse it; if not, or if damaged or oil-covered, replace.
- step 6: install the filter and reattach the cover.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas begins to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Ethanol draws moisture into the fuel system leaving gummy deposits that restrict fuel flow; engine loses power when starved of fuel.
- fix: drain the tank and fill with fresh gasoline.
- brand: Cub Cadet gas mowers need unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Current Cub Cadets use 4-cycle engines (straight gasoline); older ones may be 2-cycle needing a gas and oil mixture.
- tip: add Sea Foam Motor Treatment to stabilize gas, reduce moisture and clean the fuel system; also when you have more gas than you can use within 30 days; author adds Sea Foam to every tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: strains fuel leaving the tank; unchanged filter plugs.
- tip: replace annually during routine maintenance; inexpensive part.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: shut off the fuel flow with the shut-off valve, or fuel pinch-off pliers if no valve.
- step 2: pull the filter out of the fuel lines and install a new one.
- tip: many inline filters have an arrow on the side; it must point in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: gummy deposits from old gas can clog lines.
- confirm: stop fuel flow, remove the end of a section of fuel hose and put it in a container, restore fuel flow and check for good flow.
- fix: stop fuel flow, remove the section; spray carburetor cleaner into it to loosen clogs; blow compressed air through to free the obstruction. If it can't be cleared or the line is dry and cracked, replace with new line of the same length and diameter.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carb regulates fuel and air to the cylinder for combustion; residue from old fuel collects and starves it.
- confirm: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into the air intake and start; if it runs but won't stay running, clean the carburetor.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: intermittent spark, loss of power; carbon, dirt, oil buildup on the tip.
- fix: replace with a new plug (author recommends); if only dirty and not very dark, can try cleaning with a wire brush and reuse.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low oil causes power loss; causes: not enough added at last change, oil leak, or burning oil from wrong viscosity.
- fact: lack of lubrication builds friction and extreme heat; can overheat and smoke from burning oil and melting parts; significant engine damage possible, even engine replacement.
- tip: check oil before each use.
- confirm: remove dipstick, wipe with clean rag, reinsert, remove and read; oil must be at full level.
- fix: add fresh oil until full; do not overfill.
- pro: if problems continue after refill, have an experienced small-engine mechanic diagnose; low oil may have caused internal damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: overfilled crankcase makes the engine smoke; pressure builds and oil is pushed into the cylinder through the valve train; bluish-white smoke when it burns.
- fact: thick smoke can plug the air filter causing running issues; check air filter and spark plug, clean or replace.
- fact: continued running with too much oil can cause seal damage, hydrolock and a bent piston rod.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: loosen and quickly tighten the drain plug to let only a little oil out; or remove oil from the oil filter or the oil fill area with a turkey baster or oil evacuator.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: engine uses air around block and cylinder head; keep fins clear of clippings and mud; replace damaged fins; clean around the engine shroud and air intake areas too.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- fact: only says remove clippings and mud; also remove debris under the engine shroud and make sure the heat shield is securely in place.

## page: causes/clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet
- fact: "blocked air passageways": remove clippings, dirt, debris around engine and under the shroud; make sure heat shield is secure.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: fast ground speed adds load in thick, wet or tall grass. fix: slow down for conditions, including on an incline.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: wet grass makes engine work harder, clumps and sticks to deck underside; mow dry. Very tall grass can bog the mower.
- tip: for tall grass, double or triple cut: first cut at highest deck setting, then lower the cutting height for the next cut.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: a plugged deck makes the engine work harder to turn blades through debris. fix: regularly scrape the deck clean; also helps quality of cut.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify a plugged deck problem, requiring more engine power. fix: sharpen or replace.
