## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Belt keeps coming off my Gravely mower"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Source lists 9 reasons: loose/worn belt, damaged belt keeper, shredded belt, bad pulley bearings, bad spindle housing bearing, worn idler tensioner bracket or spring, debris on deck interfering with tension, debris in pulley grooves, oil or rust on belt.
- safety: Follow Gravely owner's manual; remove spark plug wires and ignition key before working.
- tip: Extend belt life: clean deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off the belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix or replace damaged brackets or belt keepers.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: Stretching, cracking, shiny glazed look; belt sitting deep in the pulley grooves.
- fix: Replace the deck belt.
- symptom: Shredded/torn edges: most likely a bracket or belt keeper is out of place and the belt rubs it, or the belt is routed incorrectly.
- confirm: Check routing against the operator's manual or parts diagram. Find what the belt catches on with the deck engaged; look for a shiny flat spot on a keeper or bracket.
- fix: Adjust the part so it no longer rubs; if a bracket with a bushing, replace the bushing; sometimes the whole bracket assembly must be replaced when sold only that way.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Belt keeper is a rod sitting 1/8" to 1/4" away from the pulley; the belt moves around the pulleys and doesn't touch the keepers.
- symptom: With slack in the belt, it hits a keeper and bends it out of place so the belt slips off.
- fix: Bend the keeper back with a hammer or vise grips; if it can't be adjusted correctly, replace it.
- tools: hammer or vise grips

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: A bad pulley bearing lets the pulley sit not flat/parallel to the deck, one side higher; belt more likely to come off.
- confirm: Slowly turn each pulley by hand; it should rotate smoothly without restriction. Restriction or bearing noise means replace the pulley.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: Bad spindle bearing allows vertical blade movement, deck vibration, belt comes off.
- confirm: Key removed and plug wires disconnected; wear gloves, grab both blade ends and rock up and down. Movement or knocking sound: remove spindle housing and check bearing.
- fix: Replace failed spindle bearing.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- symptom: Idler tensioner bracket (holds idler pulleys in line) and spring wear over time; spring weakens or breaks; the hole where the spring attaches can wear and add vibration.
- fix: Replace worn bracket or spring.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Debris on top of deck interferes with tensioner pulley movement, losing belt tension, belt vibrates and comes off. Debris in pulley grooves (bark, stone, packed mud) makes belt stretch around it, roll off or snap.
- safety: Dry debris on top of the deck can catch fire from belt heat.
- fix: Keep belt area of deck clean; check pulley grooves for objects.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- symptom: Oil on belt makes it slip off the pulley; rust dries the belt prematurely so it breaks more easily. Source: rust from rusty deck components or oil leak.
- fix: Fix the oil leak/rust cause before fitting a new belt. Check engine oil before each use to spot leaks. Clean rust off pulleys and deck; replace badly rusted pulleys.
- mistake: Replacing the (expensive) belt without fixing the cause.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: Check routing in the operator's manual or parts diagram before installing.
