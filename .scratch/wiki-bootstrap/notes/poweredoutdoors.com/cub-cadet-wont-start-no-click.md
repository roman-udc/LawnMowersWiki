## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Cub Cadet won't even click when I turn the key"
- alias: "nothing happens when I turn the key"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, incorrect-starting-procedure, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: No click: look at electrical components. Most common causes: dead battery, loose wiring, bad ignition switch; also operator-presence safety devices.
- fact: Related Source articles referenced: clicking/humming but won't start; battery keeps going dead; starter solenoid symptoms.
- brand: Cub Cadet riding mower / lawn tractor and zero turn.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Check the battery with a multimeter. A fully charged 12-volt battery reads 12.7 volts.
- fix: Best: charge slowly with a battery charger. Alternative: jump-start from a car battery with jumper cables; once started, keep operating the mower so it keeps charging the battery.
- fix: If the battery won't hold a charge, replace it (hardware or automotive store). Bring the old battery or you will be charged a core fee at most places.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear protective gear for skin (electric shock) and eyes.
- step 1: Access the battery and terminals (battery is under the hood or under the seat); a screwdriver may be needed to uncover it. Do not remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect the charger positive cable first (red or plus sign) to the positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach the negative cable (black or minus sign) to the negative terminal.
- step 4: Do not touch anything without a rubber coating, to prevent electrocution.
- step 5: Set charger voltage and amperage. Average mower battery is 12 volts; more amperage charges faster.
- step 6: Start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 7-10 amps. A slow 2 amp charge is best but takes longer (may postpone mowing by a day).
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Check cables and terminals for corrosion; remove corrosion with a baking soda and water mixture.
- confirm: Follow cables from the battery and check wiring to the ignition switch, safety switches and control module for damage or corrosion; must make good connections.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Cub Cadet requires conditions met before it starts (safety). All must be in the right position or most likely nothing happens when you turn the key.
- fact: Brake must be engaged. Riding mower/lawn tractor: depress the foot pedal brake; with no pedal the brake may be a lever below the steering wheel. Zero turn: depress the foot pedal brake; with no pedal the brake may be engaged when the driving arms are in the outward position.
- fact: Must be seated: weight centered on the seat, not too far forward, to engage the seat switch.
- fact: PTO must be off. Electric PTO: knob depressed = deck off, must be off to start; pull the knob up once started to engage the deck. Manual PTO: lever pushed forward engages the deck; must be pulled back to off to start.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Each safety item has an on/off switch showing whether the condition is satisfied. A defective one prevents starting.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter. May temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one, for troubleshooting only. If it tests well, make sure the plate makes good connection to engage it.
- safety: Never operate a mower without the safety switch; never run with a safety switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: Nothing happens after inserting and turning the key.
- confirm: (Common key switch; varies by model) Check continuity with a multimeter set to resistance. Find prongs marked "B" (Battery) and "S" (Starter Solenoid). Insert key, turn to start, touch one probe to B and the other to S. Good switch reads near 0 ohms; bad switch reads infinite resistance and must be replaced.

## page: procedures/replace-the-ignition-switch
- fact: Only the test is described, see causes/bad-ignition-switch.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: Fuse protects the electrical system.
- confirm: Place a multimeter probe on each prong of the fuse to measure resistance; near 0 is good, infinity is bad.
- fix: Replace with a fuse of the same amperage; do not use a different amperage.
- pro: If the fuse keeps blowing, take it to a Cub Cadet dealer or repair shop to find the root cause.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- step 1: Probe each fuse prong with a multimeter measuring resistance (near 0 good, infinity blown).
- step 2: Replace with same amperage fuse.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- fact: Multimeter test of a charged 12-volt battery: 12.7 volts.
