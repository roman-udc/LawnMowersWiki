- safety: Before repairs remove the ignition key, remove the spark plug wires, wait for all parts to stop moving; follow the operator's manual.
- safety: Follow operator's manual; consult a professional if not skilled.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: tank may be low because of a fuel leak, a non-working gauge, or forgetting the last fill.
- fix: Fill with fresh fuel; inspect fuel system and repair leaks; make sure the fuel gauge works.
- brand: Big Dog: gas with minimum 87 octane and no more than 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas deteriorates and becomes less effective after about 30 days. Ethanol attracts moisture to the fuel system, leaving varnish and gummy residue that clogs fuel lines, filter and carburetor.
- tip: Buy fresh gas at 87 octane minimum (article text says "a minimum of 10% ethanol content" in this tip list, but elsewhere says maximum 10%; conflicting wording). Consume fuel within 30 days. Use a fuel additive for stability. Store fuel in a dry location away from moisture.
- fix: Remove old fuel and refill with fresh fuel plus additive. Steps: wipe around fuel cap and remove it; drain fuel with a siphon pump or other method into an approved container; add fuel additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment to the fresh gas (cleans fuel system, removes moisture, keeps gas stable longer); if mower starts, let it run so treated fuel works through the system; if not, keep troubleshooting.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: The tank must vent so air enters as fuel is used. On a Big Dog the tank vents through the gas cap. A plugged vent makes vacuum in the tank that prevents fuel flowing to the carburetor.
- confirm: (pressure gauge, or without one) Wipe around the cap; loosen the cap to let air in; try to start. If it doesn't start, tighten cap and keep troubleshooting. If it starts, tighten the cap and let it run; if it dies after a while with the cap tight, the cap is probably not venting.
- fix: Replace a non-venting cap with a new fuel cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Bad plug connection or damaged plug; carbon buildup or oil on the tip can foul the plug, causing misfire and no start. Some engines have two plugs.
- fix steps: remove spark plug wire (boot); wipe around the plug; remove with spark plug socket wrench (3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on engine); inspect for damage, wear, dark tip from carbon; replace if so; can clean lightly dirty good plug with a wire brush; check gap against engine manufacturer spec; install; reattach wire (remove wires if doing more repairs).
- tip: Spark plugs should be replaced annually; author replaces when dirty. Cheap maintenance item.
- fact (chart): replace plug if very dirty, broken porcelain, or damaged electrode.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- step 1: Remove spark plug wire (boot).
- step 2: Wipe around plug to remove loose dirt.
- step 3: Remove with spark plug socket wrench (3/4" or 5/8" depending on engine model).
- step 4: Inspect for damage, wear, dark carbon tip; replace; may wire-brush clean if lightly dirty.
- step 5: Check gap to engine manufacturer's spec.
- step 6: Install plug, attach wire.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dirty filter starves engine of air; engine may overheat and draw air from the crankcase, which can cause extensive engine damage. Replace annually, clean several times per season.
- fix (paper): remove filter from housing; wipe out housing and cover, keep dirt out of intake; tap filter to loosen dirt, you should see light through it; if very dirty, damaged, or no longer seals the intake, replace; install; reattach cover. Chart: replace if very dirty, dark in color, wet or damaged.
- tip: Check operator's manual for filter type and cleaning instructions.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove filter from housing.
- step 2: Wipe out housing and cover without letting dirt into intake.
- step 3: Tap paper filter; light should show through; if very dirty/damaged/not sealing, replace.
- step 4: Install clean or new filter; reattach cover.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- confirm: Check that fuel running into the filter also runs out; if not, it's plugged.
- fix steps (replace): shut off fuel via shut-off valve or clamp fuel line; use needle nose pliers to remove clamps and slide each end of filter out of the lines; filter may have a directional arrow that must point in the direction of fuel flow; install new filter and clamp lines securely.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: Shut off fuel supply with shut-off valve or clamp line.
- step 2: Use needle nose pliers to remove clamps and slide filter ends out of the fuel lines.
- step 3: Note any direction arrow; it must point in direction of fuel flow.
- step 4: Install new filter and clamp lines securely.
- tools: needle nose pliers

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Old fuel and dirt leave deposits that clog the line.
- fix: Remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner into it, blow compressed air through until free of dirt and gummy residue; or replace with a new line. Chart: shut off fuel, remove line, carb cleaner to loosen, compressed air to remove; replace if restriction can't be removed.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Pump moves fuel to the carburetor, needed when the tank sits below the carburetor. Can fail over time; likely cause is old fuel deteriorating pump components.
- confirm (vacuum-style pump): inspect for cracks; fuel outside the pump or cracks mean it can no longer use crankcase pressure to move fuel. Make sure fuel reaches the pump inlet first (else check line/filter restriction). Shut off fuel; remove fuel line from the carburetor into a container; start the mower and watch flow; should be a steady or pulsating flow; replace pump if no good flow.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor regulates fuel mixed with air; crusty buildup and gummy deposits from old ethanol fuel make the engine run rough or not start.
- fix: Clean yourself if somewhat mechanical or take to repair shop; may replace if very bad. Chart: remove and clean with carb cleaner; rebuild or replace if needed.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: Follow the positive cable to the solenoid and the cable from solenoid to starter; loose or corroded connections can stop starting. Check all wiring and components.
- fix: Tighten cables; secure all wiring; remove corrosion with water and baking soda mixture.
- safety: Remove the negative battery cable first, then the positive.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Test with a multimeter; need about 12.7 volts; charge if lower; replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Several safety switches in the operator presence control system kill the engine when the operator leaves the seat. A faulty switch may not detect the operator in the seat and prevent starting.
- confirm: Temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one.
- safety: Don't operate a mower without safety switches installed; always keep them installed and working. Replace a bad switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: Insert key, turn, nothing happens. 
- confirm: Test with a multimeter. fix: replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Coil supplies voltage so the plug fires.
- confirm: After verifying the plug is good, check coil continuity with an ohm meter; replace if there is a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: mower clicks and won't start; click or hum on key turn; a wire attached to the solenoid gets hot and smokes or melts.
- fact: A solenoid is an electromagnetic switch that actuates the starter motor.
- fix: Test then replace if bad (article links to a separate solenoid test).

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Not the main reason for no-start but can contribute to a weak battery. Bad stator or alternator among other electrical parts. Test charging system with an ohmmeter (linked article).
- pro: Let a small engine mechanic identify which charging component is at fault; guessing and throwing parts is expensive.
- confirm (chart): battery is good and holds a charge but mower keeps dying with dead battery.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Safety features prevent starting unless the procedure is followed. On a Big Dog riding mower or zero-turn, the brake must be engaged and the PTO switch must be off for the engine to start.
- fix: Refer to operating manual.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Gas begins to break down after about 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture and leaves varnish; use stabilizer such as Sea Foam; store fuel in a dry location.

- brand: Big Dog: minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol.
