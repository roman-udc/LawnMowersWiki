## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "John Deere zero turn won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "won't go forward"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: 7 reasons in the order: bypass lever not in operating position; drive/pump belt worn, loose, or broken; bad tensioner pulley; worn or missing idler arm spring; low or old hydraulic fluid; hot hydraulic fluid; air in hydraulic system.
- mistake: towing or pulling the mower with other equipment can damage the transmission (large repair bill). Use bypass lever to push.
- safety: follow operator's manual; take caution around a hot transmission.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: zero-turn pumps transmission fluid to wheel motors; pressure so high you can't move the mower by hand without engaging the bypass levers. Must disengage after pushing.
- symptom: after transporting or servicing you may have forgotten to return the levers to operating position.
- brand: John Deere zero-turn has a couple of bypass levers, one per transmission (left and right).
- safety: engage/disengage levers on a level surface with parking brake engaged.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: hydraulic pump is driven by a v-belt off the engine; loose or off the pulley = no move.
- confirm: check belt positioned correctly on pulleys; replace if worn, cracked, stretched or broken.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: belt keeps falling off but isn't worn or stretched -> check tensioner pulley (often hard plastic with a bearing in the center); pulley can break or bearing fail so belt falls off or stops rotating.
- fix: replace pulley; keep tensioner arm greased so it moves and doesn't seize.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: idler spring tensions the drive belt (see missing-idler-arm-spring).

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fix: replace spring if broken, missing or stretched.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Some transmissions in entry-level John Deere zero-turns are sealed and unserviceable (can't change oil) but must still be topped off; check operator's manual whether regular service is required.
- fact: Serviceable transmission: change hydraulic oil at JD-recommended intervals; first change comes sooner than later ones. Old or low oil can make the mower not move or seem very weak.
- fix: top up with hydro oil to the full level on transmission overflow tanks when oil is cool. Check for leaks.
- fact: Service includes hydraulic fluid and filters.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: low/old/bad fluid doesn't lubricate; friction overheats fluid; hot fluid may indicate extensive transmission damage.
- pro: if mower runs fine cold but stops when hot, take to JD dealership; experienced mechanic checks the transmission to assess damage.

## page: causes/bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: see hot-hydraulic-fluid: heat can indicate extensive transmission damage.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- symptom: after changing fluid air must be bled; air makes mower move slowly or not at all.
- fix (most JD zero-turns): level surface; use wheel chocks to prevent rolling; raise rear drive tires off ground; let them spin forward and backward until no excessive noise and wheels turn at normal speed. See operator's manual for detailed steps.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: park on level surface, chock wheels. step 2: raise rear drive tires off the ground. step 3: let them spin forward and backward until no excessive noise and normal speed.
