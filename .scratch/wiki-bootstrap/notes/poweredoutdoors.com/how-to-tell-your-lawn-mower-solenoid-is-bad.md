## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "all I hear is a clicking sound when I go to start"
- alias: "starter clicks but no crank"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: starter may click but not crank when the solenoid isn't providing power; due to weak battery, loose or corroded wiring, or faulty solenoid.
- tip: narrow the problem down rather than throwing expensive parts at it; author's favourite tools: volt-ohm meter, battery charger, continuity light.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on turning the key with no start; wire getting hot, smoking or melting; starter engaging and not disengaging after releasing the key.
- fact: solenoid is an electromagnet on/off switch that engages the starter motor by sending more current; when it fails it does not provide the current to engage the starter. Mounted on top of the starter or on the frame (sometimes nearer the battery). On every electric-start engine (also trucks, cars, tractors). Round or square, 3 or 4 posts. Find by following the positive (+) battery cable to it.
- fact: inside a spring and copper plate; fails if the spring weakens or the plate corrodes; also from weak starter, bad battery or bad ground.
- mistake: replacing before checking for loose wires and bad ground.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches for loose wires, needle-nose pliers (if screwdriver fails), battery charger (optional)
- step 1: confirm battery has at least 12.3 volts.
- step 2: set parking brake, neutral, key to on.
- step 3: bypass: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the cable from the battery and the cable to the starter. A spark is common. If the engine turns over while bypassed, solenoid is probably bad. Needle-nose pliers can jump it if the screwdriver doesn't work well.
- step 4: bench test (starter-mounted solenoid): remove the starter, attach the negative (-) charger clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and exciter wire on the solenoid.
- safety: spark on bypass is normal but be careful.

## page: procedures/replace-the-starter-solenoid
- fact: only before replacing, rule out loose wires and bad ground (no replacement steps in Source).
