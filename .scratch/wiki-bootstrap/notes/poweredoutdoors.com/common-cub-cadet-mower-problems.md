Hub article: cause/solution tables for 14 problems. Many fixes are simple at home; some need an experienced small engine mechanic.

## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Cub Cadet won't start"
- fact: fuel or air restrictions, bad spark plug, or failed electrical component.
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-recoil-starter
- tip: clogged cap vent: try a thin piece of wire to clear the vent; replace cap if needed.
- fix: bad plug: replace, secure connections, gap to manufacturer spec. Fuel pump: replace. Battery: charge, replace if it won't hold charge, tighten cables, clean corrosion.
- fact: pull-start mower may have a broken recoil: check if it can be restrung or broken parts (pulley) replaced; else replace recoil assembly.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "clicks and won't turn over"
- fact: riding mower or zero turn clicking and not turning over: bad battery, ground, starter solenoid, or starter motor. Ensure cables/wiring are secure, have good continuity, not corroded.
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- confirm: ground: the battery ground cable and the starter solenoid ground cable must make good contact free of corrosion. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: battery: test, trickle charger if needed, replace if it won't hold. Solenoid: test and replace if faulty (links to a solenoid test). Starter motor: replace.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "loses power and dies"
- fact: loss of power when fuel or air systems compromised: bad fuel, bad air filter, dirty carburetor, clogged fuel lines.
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, bad-gas-cap-seal, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bad-ignition-coil, fouled-spark-plug
- fact: wrong oil level: too little causes friction and heat in the crankcase; too much builds crankcase pressure. Correct the level; see a mechanic if that doesn't solve it (possible engine damage).
- fact: plugged deck adds load and may kill the engine; scrape the deck regularly, avoid cutting wet grass.
- fact: ignition coil can separate and short out when hot, keeping voltage from the plug; replace a coil with a break in continuity.
- fact: dirty plug causes intermittent spark; clean or replace if extremely dirty/worn.
- fact: cooling fins packed with dirt, oil, grass cannot cool the block; replace broken fins and clean.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: mower smokes when oil gets into the cylinder and burns off; components burn from increased friction with low oil; internal failures: piston ring, valve train, gaskets.
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket
- fix: too much oil: drain a little until at manufacturer's recommended level (excess oil pressurizes the engine, pushing oil into the cylinder through the valve train).
- fix: low oil: take to a small engine shop to inspect for damage; adding oil is often too late.
- pro: internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket): bring to a small engine repair shop.

## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- fact: stops when hot: fuel-related issues, wrong oil, wrong oil amount, plugged cooling fins, bad ignition coil, plugged deck.
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-ignition-coil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fix: old fuel: drain, fill with fresh gas plus fuel system cleaner and stabilizer (Sea Foam or STA-BIL).
- fix: carburetor: remove and clean with carb cleaner; rebuild or replace if necessary.
- brand: wrong oil: use air-cooled engine oil like the oils provided by Kawasaki (article links a viscosity chart in its overheating article).
- fix: cooling fins: clean fins, replace broken ones, clean around the block and under the engine shroud.
- confirm: ignition coil: test with ohmmeter; windings separate when hot; replace on break in continuity.
- fact: clogged deck keeps blades from turning easily and puts the engine under load.

## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "runs rough or sputters", "bogs down"
- fact: lack of air, fuel or spark; or overworking engine: fast ground speed, low engine speed, plugged deck, dull blades.
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, mowing-too-fast, wrong-engine-speed, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fix: choke: must be adjusted to open once the engine is warm; check choke lever placement, cable, plate.
- fix: fast ground speed (thick, tall, wet grass): slow down. Low engine speed: blade engagement needs lots of power; set throttle to highest position.
- fix: dull blades: remove, sharpen, balance before reinstalling. Plugged deck: scrape it.
- fix: dirty plug: replace, gap correctly, secure wire.

## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: deck belt worn or off, PTO switch bad, clutch failed; manual-engagement clutch linkages/cable worn; or battery too weak to power an electric clutch.
- causes-named: worn-drive-belt, belt-keeper-missing/worn idler, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fix: worn belt: replace if worn, cracked or shiny.
- fact: belt coming off: worn idler arm and spring fail to hold tension; stretched belt; pulleys not parallel to the deck; ensure belt routed correctly.
- fix: bad PTO switch fails to let battery energize clutch: replace. Bad/worn clutch can't transfer power: replace. Manual clutch cable, lever, spring, linkages worn: replace.
- confirm: weak battery: test; charge if 12-volt battery reads less than 12.7 volts.
- fact: seat switch not sensing the operator won't allow blade engagement (article's solution text says "Replace a bad battery", apparently a typo; fix is replace the bad switch).
- fix: blown fuse: replace with the same amperage; have a mechanic find root cause if it keeps blowing.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "won't move forward or reverse"
- fact: bad or missing drive belt, worn tensioner pulley, missing idler spring; transmission bypass rod or drive release lever in the wrong position on hydrostatic mowers.
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: drive release lever lets hydrostatic zero-turns roll freely; must be in operating position on a zero-turn. A bypass rod is found on a riding mower; must be disengaged.
- fix: belt: replace worn belt; ensure it is securely around pulleys. Tensioner pulley bearing can fail: replace. Broken/missing idler arm spring can't tension drive belt: replace.
- fact: low or old hydraulic oil: change at intervals recommended by Cub Cadet; check periodically that it's full.
- fact: hot hydraulic fluid can signal damage to the hydrostatic system; let it cool. If it runs fine cold but weak when hot, have it checked at a dealer.
- fact: air must be bled from the hydraulic system after changing fluid or the mower can't reach normal speed; follow owner's manual procedure.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper (bad dampers), tracking adjustment, loose-steering-linkage
- fact: unequal tire pressure pulls to one side; keep to manufacturer's spec, equal on each side.
- fact: bad dampers on zero turn make the mower jerk to one side moving forward; replace damper.
- fact: zero-turn tracking: when one tire turns faster than the other the mower pulls; adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change tire rotation speed (owner's manual).
- fact: worn bushings, washers, gears in a riding mower steering system: inspect and replace failed parts.

## page: problems/loose-steering
- fact: worn or loose steering components on a riding mower (bushings, washers, gears) can keep it from steering one way or the other.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- fact: loose hardware, failing bearings in clutch/pulleys/spindle housings, damaged or unbalanced blades, debris lodged under pulleys.
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fix: engine bolts reduce vibration; secure and replace missing bolts.
- fact: failing clutch: bearing failure and the bearing falls out; a manual-engagement clutch can have bushing/linkage failures; replace the clutch.
- fix: blades must be balanced before installing; replace damaged ones.
- fix: string or long weeds wrapped on the blade shaft: remove all, check for spindle damage.
- fix: bad spindle housing bearing: replace the bearing or the whole spindle housing assembly.
- fix: check deck belt and pulleys for wear and bearing failure.
- fact: stones, acorns, woodchips lodged under pulleys can shake the mower; check deck and remove.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- tip: leaks are hard to find when residue evaporates; follow the fuel line from the tank and check every component along it.
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, bad-gas-cap-seal, cracked-primer-bulb
- fact: carburetor bowl gasket dries and fails to seal: replace. Float or float needle stuck from old gas lets fuel overflow: clean carburetor, replace damaged parts.
- fact: old fuel lines crack; poly tanks leak at seams; metal tanks can rust through (replace tank; patch if a new one is unavailable). Shut-off valve can leak. Fuel can degrade plastic in a filter or the housing can crack. Pump can weaken at seams. Gas cap seal can dry out. Primer bulb can crack or the line behind it can be loose.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- safety: when the engine smells hot, stop running it immediately to avoid extensive damage.
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fix: wrong oil (wrong for ambient temp or not designed for air-cooled engines): drain and fill with fresh air-cooled engine oil of a viscosity suited to outdoor temperature.
- fix: low oil: change and add fresh oil; engine damage should be diagnosed by a mechanic.
- fact: engine guard missing or out of place stops it guiding air around the engine; securely attach it.
- fact: very dirty air filter starves engine; replace.
- fix: overworking (long, wet, thick grass): slow down, avoid wet grass, double or triple-cut an overgrown lawn. Sharpen and balance blades.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- symptom: low engine speed with blades engaged: runs sluggish and bogs down (problems/engine-runs-rough). Fix: throttle to highest position.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- symptom: fast ground speed in thick/tall/wet grass works the engine hard. Fix: slow down.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- symptom: mower won't move; broken/missing spring can't tension drive belt. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: see problems/engine-wont-crank notes above: ground cables from battery and from solenoid must be clean and secure; 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
