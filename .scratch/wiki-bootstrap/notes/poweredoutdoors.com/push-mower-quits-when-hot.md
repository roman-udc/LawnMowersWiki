## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- alias: "push mower quits when hot"
- alias: "mower stops working after mowing a while"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, dirty-cooling-fins, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- safety: wait for engine to cool and parts to stop; remove spark plug wire before repairs; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: starves the engine of air; can also overheat and damage the engine. Author: start each season with a new filter; check regularly and clean several times a year; more often in dust.
- step (paper): remove (don't knock dirt into intake), wipe housing with dry clean cloth; tap on solid surface; NO compressed air (damages); hold to light; reuse if light shows; replace if no light, wet or damaged; reinstall with cover.
- step (primary foam; not for foam pre-filter): replace if dry, brittle, torn or brown spots; else wash with mild dish soap and water to remove dirt and oil; rinse until clear; lay flat to air dry (sun speeds); coat with foam filter oil and squeeze out excess so it isn't dripping; NEVER apply oil to a foam pre-filter; reinstall.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: steps above.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: varnish and sticky deposits cause component failures and restrictions; engine runs sluggish then dies after running a while.
- fact: mowers need unleaded gasoline with under 10% ethanol (ethanol-free best, never more than 10%). 4-cycle push mower engines take straight gas; 2-cycle engines take a gas and 2-cycle oil mix.
- fix: drain the tank into an approved container with a siphon pump (or remove the fuel line from the carb and drain); add fresh fuel with Sea Foam Motor Treatment (cleans, reduces moisture, stabilizes); author uses it in every tank.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: make sure fuel reaches the carb; remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into intake and start; if it runs a bit then shuts down, disassemble and clean.
- fix: DIY if mechanical or have a shop do it.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: best oil is air-cooled engine oil with high zinc (cooling agent); most small engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures. Wrong oil can overheat and shut down the engine. Source points to the Kawasaki oil viscosity chart plus owner's manual.
- fix: drain and refill with the correct air-cooled oil.
- fact (low oil): check before every use; friction and heat; oil thickens and parts can melt; severe damage; may need to replace the mower. Adding oil usually too late; have a small engine mechanic test. Author: usually most cost-effective to buy a new mower instead of replacing the engine.
- pro: mechanic test for damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: excess oil raises crankcase pressure; crankshaft and rod must push through oil, loading the engine so it gets hot and quits; oil can be pushed into the cylinder via the valve train, making thick smoke that can plug the air filter and starve the engine.
- fix: disconnect spark plug wire(s); drain a little via the drain plug or oil fill port (author uses a turkey baster); check with the dipstick; adjust; check the air filter if it smoked; reconnect plug wire(s). If still trouble, have a mechanic check.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: disconnect spark plug wire(s). step 2: remove a little oil via drain plug, fill port, or turkey baster. step 3: check dipstick; repeat. step 4: check air filter if it smoked; reattach wires.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins dissipate heat to keep block and cylinder cool; clean annually. Fix: remove debris, replace broken fins, clean dirt around the shroud.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: carbon buildup, broken porcelain or burnt electrode can mean no spark; may start but not keep running. Clean if buildup, replace if tip extremely dark or damaged; check wires secure.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: when hot the windings can separate and short; the coil supplies voltage to the plug.
- confirm: ohmmeter for a break in continuity; replace if bad.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap equalizes tank air pressure; failing vent forms vacuum starving the carb; stops after mowing a while.
- confirm: run with cap tightened vs loosened; shuts off when tight but fine loose = replace cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck strains engine; scrape periodically; avoid mowing wet.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify the plugged-deck load; sharpen.
