## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "John Deere mower runs rough and sputters"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, mowing-too-fast, wrong-engine-speed, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: look first at items limiting air, fuel, spark; then items putting extra strain on the engine
- safety: wait for engine to cool and moving parts to stop; remove spark plug boot(s); follow John Deere operator's manual

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: protects the engine; plugged filter makes engine run rough, possibly overheat or smoke
- tip: replace once a year, clean several times a season; more often in dusty conditions or heavy use
- fix: clean if a little dirty; replace if extremely dirty, damaged or not sealing

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove filter from housing
- step 2: wipe dirt out of housing without letting it fall into the intake
- step 3: tap filter on a solid surface
- step 4: hold to light; if light shows through the paper element and it isn't damaged, reuse; otherwise replace
- step 5: reinstall and attach cover
- step 6: foam pre-filter - wash with mild detergent and water, rinse, air dry; DO NOT APPLY OIL

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke closed to start cold (rich mix), open once warm
- symptom: wrong or stuck choke - rough running and bogging
- fix: choke closed for cold start, open for warm; check choke plate moves with the lever; carburetor cleaner to free a stuck choke; replace worn choke cable

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas stays good only about 30 days; ethanol (made from high-starch plants) attracts water; ethanol/water mix is corrosive and separates leaving varnish causing restrictions and component failure
- fix: replace old fuel; fresh gas plus cleaner/stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment; use 87 octane minimum, 10% ethanol maximum, ethanol-free best

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: use fuel shut-off valve or hose crimp pliers to stop/start flow while checking flow; collect fuel in a container lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump)
- fix: shut off fuel, remove the line, carburetor cleaner then compressed air, repeat; replace with same length and diameter if cracked or can't clear

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: inline filter; change annually, more often if plugged or damaged
- fix: install new inline filter with arrow pointing the direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: first confirm fuel reaches the carburetor (else check fuel line, filter, fuel pump); remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake and let it run; runs well then sluggish again = clean the carburetor

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: John Deere cap vents the tank; failed vent causes vacuum as fuel is used
- confirm: use a pressure gauge to test tank vacuum, or loosen cap while running rough; if it immediately runs better suspect cap; retighten, wait for sluggish, loosen again to confirm
- fix: new John Deere gas cap

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: fouled plug fires intermittently; also gap and loose wires
- fix: replace if very dark, burnt electrode or damaged; if only a little dirty clean buildup with a wire brush; gap to manufacturer spec; attach wire securely

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: slow down when bogging; tall grass - multiple cuts, highest height first then lower

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: run highest engine speed when engaging blades; rabbit icon on many mowers

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fix: scrape deck with scraper or wire brush; avoid wet/damp grass; deck spray helps but not a miracle

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: dull blades tear grass, brown tips a couple of days later; magnify plugged-deck problem
- fix: remove, sharpen and balance
