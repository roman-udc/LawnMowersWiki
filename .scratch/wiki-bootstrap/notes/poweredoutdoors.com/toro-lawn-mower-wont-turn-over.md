## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Toro lawn mower won't turn over or crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- safety: caution with electrical system; consult an experienced mechanic if unsure

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; lower means charge it; test per the author's battery-draining article
- fix: replace if no hold charge (Toro dealer, hardware or automotive store); bring old battery to avoid core fee

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: protective gear for eyes and skin
- step 1: access battery and terminals; screwdriver may be needed; don't remove battery from casing
- step 2: connect positive (red, plus) cable first to positive terminal
- step 3: attach negative (black) cable to negative terminal
- step 4: touch only rubber-coated parts
- step 5: set charger to 12 V typical; start with 2 amps up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge best

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: vibration loosens; moisture corrodes; break in continuity
- fix: secure; remove corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons); replace damaged/severely corroded wires, connections, terminals

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: fuse on all Toro mowers except some manual pull-start models
- confirm: multimeter probe on each prong, measure resistance: near 0 = good, infinity = bad
- fix: replace with same amperage; repeated blowing: dealer/shop for root cause

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: model may have a toggle switch instead of keyed switch
- confirm: multimeter continuity; prongs B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid); key (or toggle) in start; probes on B and S; good ~0 ohms; bad = infinite, replace

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: operator presence control system with switches preventing start if certain conditions are met, e.g. brake engaged
- confirm: multimeter; temporary bypass for troubleshooting only
- safety: never operate without or with bypassed safety switch

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: electromagnetic switch that initiates starter motor; fails when spring weakens or copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground also cause failure
- confirm: need fully charged battery before testing

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- confirm: after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground, solenoid: remove and test starter
- tip: have a starter/alternator repair shop test and rebuild before buying new
