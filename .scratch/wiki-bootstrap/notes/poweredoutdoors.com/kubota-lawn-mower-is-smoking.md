## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "smoke rolling out of my Kubota mower"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, bad-head-gasket, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train
- fact: Source order: plugged air filter, low engine oil, too much engine oil, bad engine gasket, piston ring problem, valve train problem.
- fact: White or blue smoke: excess oil burning off. Check for oil leaking onto the muffler, a plugged air filter, the oil level; then decide whether it is an internal engine problem.
- fact: Black smoke: often the mower running rich (higher fuel concentration than air). Check for a plugged air filter; then other airflow restrictions including a stuck choke.
- fact: Smoke colour supplements, but does not replace, the diagnostic steps.
- tip: Check engine oil level and air filter before every mowing.
- pro: Internal engine problems (rings, valve train) need a small engine mechanic to tear down and test; smoke can indicate significant engine damage needing repair or replacement.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Plugged filter makes engine run rich with black smoke; if so plugged that engine cannot pull air through the intake it draws air from the crankcase, pulling oil into the cylinder; burning oil smokes. Can also overheat the engine and cause internal damage.
- tip: Replace the filter annually; check and clean several times a season, more often in dry dusty conditions. Never run without an air filter.
- fix: step 1: remove the paper element from the housing.
- fix: step 2: wipe out remaining dirt with a dry clean rag; keep dirt out of the intake.
- fix: step 3: tap against a hard surface until no more dirt falls.
- fix: step 4: hold up to a light; if light shines through, reuse; if not, replace.
- fix: step 5: reinstall and secure the cover.
- fact: Replace a filter that shows oil or is plugged (oil pushed through the intake can coat it).

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Low oil: friction builds heat, components can melt and oil burns off. Correcting level afterwards most likely will not help; likely significant engine damage.
- fact: Low oil level can point to a leak or an engine burning/consuming oil.
- fix: Check for oil leaks and repair; if the cause is unfound have an experienced small engine mechanic test before running further.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Excess oil builds crankcase pressure, pushing oil into the cylinder and up into the air intake through the valve train; burns off as smoke.
- fix: Bring oil to the manufacturer's full level (lines on the dipstick) by draining a little from the drain plug or oil filter, or sucking oil out of the fill area with an evacuator or turkey baster. Recheck and adjust: no higher, no lower.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- tip: Drain plug, oil filter, evacuator or turkey baster; recheck dipstick and repeat until at full.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Bad engine gasket leaks oil onto the muffler where it burns and smokes. Inspect for leaks and replace the leaking gasket; easy or hard depending on gasket location.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- confirm: Remove the spark plug (3/4" or 5/8" socket, varies by engine) and look for oil on the tip; oil indicates piston ring or valve train problem.
- fact: A mechanic may find cylinder wall scoring letting oil into the combustion chamber, or oil leaking past a failed piston ring. A compression test can help identify it.

## page: procedures/check-compression
- fact: A compression test can help identify the piston ring problem (mechanic-performed per Source).

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- symptom: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Burnt valve = overheated valve. Red-hot muffler can indicate burnt valve or timing problem.
- confirm: Only by removing the cylinder head and doing a leak-down test, by an experienced mechanic.
- fix: Cut the valve and seat at specific angles to seat correctly.
