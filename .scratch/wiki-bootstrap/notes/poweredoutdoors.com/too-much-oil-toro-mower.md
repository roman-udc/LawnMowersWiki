## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: engine overheating, seal damage/oil leaking out, blown gaskets, hydrolock, fouled plug, rough running, loss of power, whitish-blue stinky smoke.
- safety: Remove spark plug boot(s) and let the engine cool before troubleshooting.
- fact (overheat): Crank and rod push through excess oil, raising crankcase pressure and loading internal parts; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil may not flow properly; aluminum engines magnify heat. Can pop a valve guide or valve seat out of the block; engine then needs scraping out by a mechanic.
- fact (seals): Pressure can blow seals, oil drains out; if not noticed, engine runs out of oil: can spin a rod or weld to the crankshaft, break the rod out the side of the block, weld piston to cylinder (seize).
- fact (hydrolock): Excess oil gets into the cylinder; if oil passes worn rings it can lock the engine. Piston can't compress oil; piston may bend on start attempt.
- fact (running/smoke): Oil fouls the plug and inhibits spark -> power loss, rough run. Oil gets into valve train and burns in the cylinder = whitish-blue smoke. With a clogged air filter the engine may pull air and oil out of the crankcase. Oil can also pass via valve train into carburetor and air filter, preventing running.
- fact (gaskets): Excess heat can blow gaskets; gasket replacement on a twin-cylinder engine costs more than push mower repairs since the engine must come out.
- pro: Don't keep running with engine problems; consult an experienced technician if not fixed by an oil level correction; they can test whether it's repairable or should be replaced.
- tip: Check twice; only add what the manufacturer requires.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- safety: Remove spark plug wire first.
- method 1 drain plug or valve port: on side of engine by the dipstick or bottom of oil pan; drain pan ready; remove plug for a quick second, replace, check level.
- method 2 oil filter: loosen or remove filter to drain a little; rag ready.
- method 3 fill hole: many push mower engines have no drain plug; tip mower to drain a little from the fill hole.
- method 4 oil extractor/evacuator pump: tube through fill hole.
- method 5 turkey baster: for small amounts; don't reuse for cooking.

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: Typical capacities: push mower small engine about 3/4 quart; v-twin about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" (about 34 horsepower or larger) 3 quarts. Find capacity in owner's manual (or search).
- tip: Know crankcase capacity and add only 1/2 quart at a time, checking dipstick; add smaller amounts when near full.

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- fact: Hydrolocking is oil in the cylinder past the piston filling the combustion chamber so the piston can't reach the top; pulling the rope or starter won't turn it because oil won't compress. The small-engine oil pan has little room for error. Too much oil can prevent engine from running/starting.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- fact: Overfilled oil gives whitish-blue stinky smoke (burning in cylinder via valve train).

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- fact: Hydrolock can keep the engine from turning over.
