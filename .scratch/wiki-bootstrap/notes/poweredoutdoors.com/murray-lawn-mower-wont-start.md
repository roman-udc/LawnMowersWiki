## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: add fresh fuel. If using more gas than normal, check for fuel leaks (tank, lines, filter, carburetor, fuel pump if present) and repair.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: ethanol (corn-based) attracts moisture; the moisture/ethanol mix evaporates leaving gummy sticky deposits that restrict fuel, corrode the fuel system and degrade components. Gas breaks down as soon as 30 days after purchase: use within 30 days or stabilize. Use gas with no more than 10% ethanol.
- fix: drain tank with a fuel siphon; add fresh fuel with a stabilizer/cleaner like Sea Foam Motor Treatment.
- tools: fuel siphon.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap lets air through to equalize pressure; plugged vent forms vacuum in tank so fuel won't flow.
- confirm: run the mower with and without cap; starts and runs without cap but dies with cap on = cap problem. Don't let debris into the tank while cap is off.
- fix: attempt to clean; author prefers replacing the gas cap; replace if clog can't be removed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: carbon and oil buildup cause misfire; loose spark plug wires or wrong gap also cause no start.
- confirm: remove; look for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, burnt electrode -> replace. Gap per engine manufacturer spec.
- tip: can clean tips with a wire brush but replacing is recommended; plugs are the least expensive maintenance item and should be replaced annually.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- mistake: never run the mower without an air filter, even briefly.
- tip: start each mowing season with a new air filter; check and clean several times a season.
- step (paper): remove filter; wipe housing without dirt falling into intake; tap on solid surface; do NOT use compressed air (damages paper element); hold to light - light visible = reuse, else replace.
- step (foam): remove; wipe housing; dark spots or dry/brittle = replace; else wash in mild dish soap and water to remove dirt and oil; rinse until water runs clear; lay flat to dry (sun speeds it); when fully dry (or new) lightly saturate with clean engine oil: completely covered but not dripping (too much oil causes smoking when pulled into engine and burned off); reinstall with cover.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: fuel pump needed when carburetor sits above fuel tank; push mowers most likely have none. Pump builds pressure using crankcase vacuum; replace when it can't. Visible signs: small crack in housing or fuel leaking at seams.
- confirm step 1: verify fuel reaches the pump by checking fuel out of the line to the pump inlet port.
- confirm step 2: use fuel shut-off valve, fuel clamps or hose pinch pliers to start/stop flow. With flow stopped, remove fuel line from carburetor, put in container.
- confirm step 3: start fuel flow and start mower; watch for a steady or pulsating flow into the container; insufficient flow = pump damaged, replace.
- tools: hose pinch pliers, container.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: plugged or cracked filter must be replaced.
- fix: install new filter with the arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow: toward carburetor, away from tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel with shut-off valve (bottom of tank) or pinch-off pliers; per section: shut off flow; pick section; remove the end furthest from tank and put in container; turn on flow; good flow = reattach; no flow = clogged.
- fix: remove line, spray carb cleaner through the tube, blow compressed air through until free of debris and gummy residue; replace line if can't clear.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fix: if mechanical, take apart and clean with carb cleaner including float bowl and needle; replace if too dirty.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: clean corroded terminals with baking soda solution of 2 cups water + 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda; scrub with wire brush. Check terminals tight on battery. Push mower may have no battery.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step: multimeter reading about 12.7 volts; less -> charge on charger; if it won't hold charge replace.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- see test above.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: switches prevent deck running without operator and prevent start when parking brake isn't engaged.
- confirm: test with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify bad switch.
- safety: never operate without the safety switch; never run with a switch bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: test with multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: push mowers without electric start use a recoil; bad pulley, loose/missing spring, or broken clips cause failure.
- fix: try replacing spring and restringing; if clips or pulley damaged, replace recoil assembly.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: coil supplies voltage to spark plug. After confirming plug good, check coil continuity with multimeter; break in continuity = replace.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: not the main reason for no start but contributes to a weak battery; bad stator or alternator among electrical parts; link to testing charging system with an ohmmeter.
- pro: take to local repair shop; without experience you'll throw parts at it; most mower centers don't allow electrical part returns.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Murray safety precautions require certain starting/operating steps; follow operating manual so you don't trip safety features that shut off the mower.
