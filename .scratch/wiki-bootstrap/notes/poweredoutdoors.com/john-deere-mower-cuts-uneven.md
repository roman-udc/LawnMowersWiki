- fact: check tire pressure FIRST, before bent blades and unlevel deck, so you don't alter a deck that was fine.
- fact: summary table: worn blade - sharpen/replace; low tire pressure - correct; bent blade - replace; blade installed incorrectly - sail up; clogged deck - scrape; deck not level - level side-to-side and front-to-back; spindle/housing damage - replace; bad belt/pulleys - replace; engine speed too low - full throttle; ground speed too fast - slow for conditions; incorrect paths - overlap; damaged deck shell - repair/replace; uneven yard / high spots - smaller deck / roll the lawn.
- brand: John Deere deck leveling procedure varies by model; see owner's manual.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: worn blade leaves strips of uncut grass; dirt sucked into the deck wears the metal; unavoidable wear item. Sharpen when dull, replace when worn, including when the sail is thinner than the center.
- fact: bending comes from tree roots, rocks, dog toys; walk the yard first. You may feel a jolt or hear grinding, or not notice.
- safety: before checking, park on a flat surface, set the parking brake, remove the key, disconnect the spark plug wires; wear work gloves.
- confirm (blade removed): reach the underside of the deck (remove the deck if needed), lay the removed blade on a new John Deere blade; gaps mean replace.
- confirm (blade installed): on a flat surface measure ground to blade tip on one side, rotate 180 degrees and measure the other; difference greater than 1/8" means replace.
- mistake: straightening a bent blade and reusing it; it compromises the metal, may crack and fly out at high speed, injuring people or pets or damaging structures.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: replace when the sail becomes thinner than the center of the blade.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: uneven pressure makes the deck sit unlevel; a low tire makes the deck sit lower on that side. Equalise both sides and bring to the pressure on the tire sidewall.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: author reports customers' new mowers cutting terribly because blades were installed upside down. Sail down tears and bruises grass instead of cutting cleanly; the sail can hit the ground and damage spindle housings.
- fix: the high side (sail or fin) points up toward the deck. Some blades are stamped "BOTTOM" or "THIS SIDE DOWN"; otherwise remember "Sail Up".
- fact: use the correct hardware in the right order (bolt and washer, nut and washer, etc.) per owner's manual or John Deere parts diagram; with a concave washer the cupped side faces the blade.
- fact: blades must be balanced before installing; new blades are fine, reused sharpened blades must be balanced. Balanced means equal weight both sides; shave metal off the heavy side. Use a blade balancer, or hang the blade on a wall nail (head sticking out 3/4" to 1") and remove metal from the end hanging lower until level. Unbalanced blades vibrate and damage deck and engine.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: the deck uses the blades' airflow and the area under the deck to create suction that stands grass up for cutting; a clogged deck reduces airflow, gives a bad cut, makes the engine work harder and possibly shut down.
- fix: scrape the deck periodically.
- tip: don't cut wet or damp grass (clumps and collects under the deck; tire tracks show more); coat the underside of the deck with silicone or Teflon spray to reduce sticking.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- fix: level side-to-side and front-to-back per owner's manual; procedures vary by John Deere model.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: with gloves, grab each end of the blade and rock up and down; listen for knocking and feel for movement; those mean a most likely bad bearing - take the spindle housing apart.
- fix: replace the bearing if possible; sometimes only the whole spindle housing assembly is sold; varies by deck model.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- confirm: look for cracks or a shiny appearance; a belt sitting deep in the pulleys or stretched is worn. Replace.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: rotate each pulley slowly; resistance or bearing noise = bad; a bad pulley may not sit flat and parallel to the deck. Replace with a pulley-and-bearing assembly.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: blade tip speed affects cut quality; run full throttle with the deck engaged. If power seems down, have a small engine mechanic at a John Deere dealer or repair shop check it.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: engine loads heavier in thick, tall or wet grass; slow down; too fast pushes grass over rather than cutting.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: author rushed with widely spaced passes and left strips; overlap passes.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: shell damaged by hitting a fence post, tree or building; repair or replace. If it can't be repaired get a price from the John Deere dealer for a replacement shell and for a complete deck assembly, plus labor to swap components to a new shell for comparison.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: see mowing-too-fast and plugged deck notes.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- fact: follow the owner's manual to level side-to-side and front-to-back; specs vary by model.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: balancing and hardware notes above (concave washer cupped side to blade).

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: 1/8" ground-to-tip difference after 180-degree rotation = replace; or stack on a new blade and look for gaps.
