## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "John Deere lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-starter-solenoid, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Won't start when lacking air, fuel or spark for combustion; most likely dirty carburetor, clogged air filter, fouled plug, weak battery, or old gas. Also wrong choke setting listed in intro.
- fact: Some of these 16 items (battery, wiring, switches, solenoid) are really no-crank causes.
- pro: If charging system is the suspect, have a small engine mechanic identify it; guessing and throwing parts is expensive.
- tip: Dealer repair in busy season can take a week or two, so check these items first.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "when I turn the key on my riding mower nothing happens"
- alias: "John Deere won't crank after sitting"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-pto-switch, bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Nothing on key turn: charge battery, clean and tighten all wiring and cable connections at battery and electrical components; check safety switches, ignition switch, PTO switch, starter solenoid.
- fact: Won't crank after sitting: most likely weak/bad battery; loose or corroded wiring and terminals; or wire damage from rodents.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: Author found this the reason for several customers' mowers; may also be a fuel leak.
- fix: Fill with fresh gasoline (diesel mowers: diesel fuel).
- brand: 4-cycle John Deere engines require minimum 87 octane, max 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas has a 30-day shelf life before it breaks down. Ethanol draws moisture; evaporation leaves residue that clogs the fuel system.
- fix: Remove old fuel with a fuel siphon pump; add fresh fuel with an additive that cleans the system and reduces moisture. Use a stabilizer if not consumed in 30 days.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cap is vented by design; broken/clogged cap makes a vacuum in the tank so fuel can't leave.
- confirm: Remove the cap and try to start. If it runs fine, reinstall the cap and keep running, waiting for it to go sluggish and die; if it does, remove the cap and it restarts. That confirms the cap. Don't let dirt get into the tank with the cap off.
- fix: Replace with a new fuel cap (online or dealer).

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Bad connection or damaged plug; excessively dirty plug fouls and misfires. A loose connection or incorrect gap also causes starting issues.
- confirm: Remove and inspect for dirt, carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator.
- fix: Replace if very dirty or damaged, gap per manufacturer's spec. If good with a dirty tip, wire brush it and reuse. (Table: replace if very dirty, broken porcelain or damaged electrode.)

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Engine essentially suffocates; can cause extensive engine damage if not addressed. Dusty conditions require more frequent checking, cleaning, replacing.
- tip: Replace annually and clean several times per season.
- step 1: Remove paper filter from housing without letting dirt into intake.
- step 2: Tap against a solid surface.
- step 3: Hold to light; no light means replace; if light shows and it's not dark, oil-covered or damaged, reuse.
- step 4: Insert into housing and reattach cover.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most John Deere mowers use a vacuum fuel pump when the tank is lower than the carburetor; 3 ports: inlet, outlet, and a third fitting that uses crankcase pressure.
- confirm: 1) Inspect for cracks or external fuel; replace if seen. 2) Verify fuel reaches the pump: shut off the valve or pinch the line, disconnect the line from the inlet and put in a container lower than the tank, restore flow, check it flows. If not, check for blocked lines or plugged filter. 3) Reconnect inlet line, remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start the mower, expect steady or pulsating flow.
- fix: Replace if no flow from the outlet, or if damaged or leaking.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Plugs from dirty/old fuel.
- fix: Replace; install with the arrow on the side pointing in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Old fuel leaves gummy deposits.
- confirm: Use the fuel valve or crimp the line to stop/start flow, checking each section.
- fix: Stop flow, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air, repeat as needed. New line if you can't clear it or lines are dry, brittle and cracking.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fix: Take apart and clean with carburetor cleaner including float bowl and needle; replace or rebuild if in very bad condition or cleaning doesn't help. Article links to a John Deere carburetor cleaning article.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Multimeter reading about 12.7 volts; lower, charge it. If it won't hold a charge, replace.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Make sure cables are secure; clean corrosion with baking soda mix (2 cups water and 3 heaping tbsp baking soda) and a wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Operator's presence control system uses several switches, e.g. one that kills the engine if the operator leaves the seat with the deck engaged. See manual for all devices.
- confirm: Temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch, or use a multimeter.
- safety: Never operate without a good safety switch or with it bypassed; many people are injured annually by rolling mowers or falling off.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned, nothing happens. Check with a multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Supplies voltage to the plug.
- confirm: After verifying the plug is good, check coil continuity with an ohmmeter; a break means replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor.
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; a wire attached to the solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts.
- fix: Test (links to John Deere solenoid test article); replace if bad.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Can contribute to a weak battery that can't start the mower next time. Bad stator or alternator among other parts; test with an ohmmeter.
- symptom: Battery is good and holds a charge, but the mower repeatedly dies from a dead battery and won't start.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: John Deere safety procedures shut the mower off, e.g. getting off a zero-turn with the engine running without setting the parking brake first.
- fix: Follow the operating manual.

- fact: Listed in the FAQ as something to check when nothing happens on key turn.
