## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "strong fuel odor in garage from my zero turn"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Fuel evaporates, so leaks are hard to find after the mower has sat.
- pro: If unsure or uncomfortable, have an experienced mechanic at a brand dealership or repair shop check it.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Bowl gasket (rubber-band-like) dries and goes brittle from heat/cold cycling near the engine.
- step (replace gasket): 1 shut off fuel (shut-off valve or crimp line). 2 wipe dirt off carb. 3 remove screw at bottom and the bowl, rag for remaining fuel. 4 remove old gasket. 5 install new; keep carburetor cleaner or other material off the gasket. 6 reinstall bowl, tighten screw. 7 wipe down carb.
- fact (stuck float): leak near intake port; float regulates start/stop of fuel into the bowl; stuck lets fuel overflow; disassemble to find why; clean or replace.
- fact (stuck needle): works with float; if it sticks, replace. Tapping with rubber mallet works a time or two. Usually a rebuild kit exists. Have the engine model and spec number (on the side of the engine housing; different from mower model/serial number) when ordering a kit or replacement carb.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Old fuel softens filter, leaks at seams. If plastic is soft, careful removing so plastic doesn't break off in the lines. Install with arrow facing fuel flow: toward carburetor, away from tank.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Old fuel degrades plastic housing, leaks at seams; replace a leaking vacuum pump.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Newer zero-turns may have HDPE tanks (seams fail -> replace tank). Older metal tanks rust through; replacement may be discontinued; may patch with a material like JB Weld.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: At the bottom of the tank, plastic or metal; both prone to leaking; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Lines dry and crack; clamps can loosen or puncture the hose. Replace dry/cracked lines.
- tip: Convert pinch-style clamps to worm gear clamps (don't pinch the line).

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Seal dries and fuel seeps out while sloshing during mowing; may smell it in storage area without seeing a wet spot.
- confirm: Rock mower back and forth to splash gas to the cap; wet area forming on the outside of the tank around the cap = replace cap.
