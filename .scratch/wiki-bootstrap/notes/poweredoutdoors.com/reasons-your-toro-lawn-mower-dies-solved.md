## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Toro lawn mower starts then dies"
- alias: "Toro dies while mowing"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, dirty-carburetor, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys (clogged deck and dull blades)
- fact: dies when engine doesn't get sufficient air or fuel flow; also low oil, clogged deck, dull blades

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with fresh gas

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas begins to degrade about 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture, evaporation gums and clogs; ethanol corrosive to fuel system; water and gas separate and sink
- tip: use fuel within 30 days; buy from a busy gas station; don't leave fuel outdoors where it attracts moisture; store in dry place; if can't use in 30 days add stabilizer (Sea Foam Motor Treatment: petroleum-based, stabilizes, removes moisture, cleans fuel system)
- fix: drain tank, refill fresh with additive; don't dump old gas on the ground, recycle it

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: easy to pick wrong fuel at pump; handles in yellow, blue, green may signal E85, Ethanol-Free, Diesel; read the label
- fact: Toro needs octane 87 or higher and ethanol no more than 10% (sold as E10 or Regular); lower ethanol better; over 10% can badly damage small engine
- fix: drain tank, flush it, refill fresh fuel treated with Sea Foam

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: gummed carburetor may not allow fuel to flow; usually cleanable, sometimes deposits too bad, may be easier to replace; mechanically inclined can clean it, else repair shop

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: pump used when carburetor sits higher than the tank; fails over time; may see damage or leaks at seams; otherwise test using shut-off valve or hose clamps to stop/start flow; check inlet port gets fuel, then check outlet
- fix: replace if no steady or pulsating flow from outlet

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace if gas restricted; clamps help tell filter blockage from line blockage; Toro filters relatively inexpensive; author changes his yearly

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air until clear; reinstall; or replace if can't clear or line dry and cracked (online or hardware store)

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vacuum in tank stops fuel
- confirm: remove cap and run; if it no longer dies suspect cap; confirm by replacing cap and letting it run longer; take care that no dirt enters fuel system during test
- fix: clean vent; if can't, replace cap

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: kicked-up clippings and dirt plug filter; engine starved of air shuts down and may overheat causing internal damage
- tip: author changes filters annually and cleans about once a month; sooner in dusty conditions
- fix (paper): remove, tap on solid surface, hold to light; no light through = replace

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: remove engine cover, clean fins, replace damaged fins

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil can make the engine smoke and die while mowing; smoke clogs air filter; can cause internal engine damage and engine replacement

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: too little oil raises friction, overheating and death
- fix: change oil per Toro recommendation; fill to correct levels

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: packed deck or dull blades make engine work harder (blades hit debris each turn); engine overheats and shuts down; also uneven cut
- fix: inspect deck for damage; scrape deck; sharpen blades; always run at full throttle; avoid wet or very tall grass
