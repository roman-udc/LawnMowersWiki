## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Skipping winterizing can cause rust, run-ability problems, increased corrosion and possible rodent damage to the electrical system.
- tools: wire brush; deck scraper or putty knife; rubber gloves; rags/paper towel; fuel stabilizer; gas container (if emptying tank); cardboard/newspaper under oil work; oil change supplies (plastic funnel, socket wrench or spanner set for drain plug, filter and filter wrench, 2-3 quarts oil per owner's manual, drain plug).
- step 1: Wash and remove debris; mower must be completely dry before storage (do it on a sunny day; handheld leaf blower helps). Moisture must be gone from electrical components and elsewhere. Most current Husqvarna zero turns have sealed spindle bearings; still do not use high-pressure water on the deck, use a garden hose.
- step 2: Disconnect spark plug boots (two plugs most likely) before working under the deck. Do not skip.
- step 3: Change engine oil (see procedures/change-the-engine-oil section).
- step 4: Lift front of deck with jack stands or similar; inspect/remove blades for replacement or sharpening (work gloves); scrape deck with scraper or putty knife (wire brush also); reconnect spark plug boots when done.
- step 5: Add fuel stabilizer or drain tank (see fuel section).
- step 6: Battery: remove and store, or fully charge (see below).
- step 7: Check/replace maintenance items: outer and inner air filters (may have foam pre-cleaner), fuel filter, spark plugs; author changes all at least once a year.
- step 8: Lubricate cables and grease all grease zerks.
- step 9: Store in cool dry place; tarp or drop cloth for dust indoors; waterproof cover outdoors.
- step 10: Rodent prevention.
- fact: Rodent damage commonly seen to filtration systems and wiring harnesses; expensive to replace. Use a deterrent safe for pets: mothballs around the mower (listed option); author (opinion) uses Grandpa Gus's Mouse Repellent (all-natural; extra strength packets plus spray on wires; packets around mower).
- fact: Indoor: garage/pole barn - tarp for dust. Basement or in the house: do not store with fuel; not next to combustibles.
- fact: Outdoor (under deck/overhang): rain can fall between deck boards; snow blows in; author uses heavy-duty marine-grade fabric mower cover. Indoor storage (garage, pole barn, shed) is best.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: Change every 50 hours or per engine manufacturer; author changes at least once per season even under 50 hours. New mower or new engine: first change much sooner, around 2-10 hours; check manual.
- step 1: Warm oil by running engine a couple of minutes, shut off; disconnect spark plug leads.
- step 2: Drain oil, remove old filter, install new filter, fill with oil per engine manufacturer's directions.
- tools: plastic funnel, socket wrench/spanner wrenches for drain plug, filter and filter wrench, 2-3 quarts oil, drain plug, cardboard under work area

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Drain option: siphon as much fuel as possible, then run the mower until it shuts off and won't restart.
- fact: Author does not drain; uses stabilizer. If storing with fuel you must use stabilizer, else spring starting/running problems; old fuel causes gumming and varnish buildup in the fuel system.
- fact: Author prefers Sea Foam (stabilizes fuel and reduces moisture in the fuel system; stabilizes fuel up to 2 years); STA-BIL is a good alternative.
- step: Add stabilizer to fresh fuel per bottle directions, add to tank, run the mower a few minutes to work it through the fuel system. Make sure you're running the right fuel.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: A 100% fully charged battery will not freeze. Option: remove and store in cool dry place, or ensure fully charged before storing.
- step: Wear gloves and eye protection (battery acid); mower off; remove hold-down brackets; adjustable wrench turn black negative terminal counterclockwise and remove cable, then red positive; wire-brush corrosion off terminals and cables; store in a cool dry place away from combustibles.
