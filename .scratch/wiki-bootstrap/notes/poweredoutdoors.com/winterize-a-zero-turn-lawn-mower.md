## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Winterize by emptying or stabilizing fuel (removes moisture, prevents breakdown); remove battery or charge it so it doesn't freeze; remove debris and grease to prevent corrosion. Skipping causes spring starting and running problems.
- tools: engine oil and filter; hydraulic oil and filter if needed; air filter; fuel filter; spark plugs; socket or spanner wrenches; drain pan, funnel, filter wrench; fuel stabilizer (Sea Foam); rags, work gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover; deck scraper, wire brush or metal putty knife.
- step 2 safety systems: safety switches prevent starting when parking brake not engaged and levers not in open/outward position; engine should shut down if PTO is engaged and operator leaves seat; check all interlocks per manual; inspect ROPS and seat belt (designed to work together).
- step 3: remove engine shroud; clear dirt/debris from engine and exhaust (air must circulate or engine overheats); check cooling fins, clean, repair broken fins; heat shield securely in place; clean around hydro fan cooling fins, guards and fan to keep transmission cool.
- step 5: replace air filter annually, clean several times a season (paper steps: remove cover, remove filter carefully, wipe housing with dry clean cloth, tap on hard surface, check with light/flashlight, don't reuse if no light/damaged/oil-covered, reinstall).
- step 7: replace spark plug(s) annually; gap per engine manufacturer; don't attach wire until service complete.
- step 8: replace fuel filter and inspect lines (replace dry, brittle lines); arrow on filter points in the fuel flow direction.
- step 9: lug nuts tight; drive tire pressures equal per manufacturer; uneven pressure causes uneven cut and pulling.
- step 10: belts worn, cracked, or glazed -> replace; turn each pulley slowly, replace if noise, resistance, or wobble; spindle bearing: gloved, rock blade up/down, movement = replace bearing or spindle housing assembly (some manufacturers only allow rebuild, some only sell the assembly, others both).
- step 11: dull blades strain engine and give poor cut (brown ends days later); inspect every service; bent or damaged = replace.
- step 12: scrape deck with scraper, metal putty knife, or stiff wire brush at every service; access with jack stands or a lift; buildup compromises airflow/suction.
- step 13: wash with garden hose; if using a pressure washer do not use high pressure (sealed components can be damaged); dry completely before storage (leaf blower or sun); moisture corrodes and causes electrical problems.
- step 14: gas breaks down as soon as 30 days after purchase; either drain (siphon pump, then run engine until it dies from no gas) or add stabilizer. Sea Foam (author's choice): 16-oz can treats up to 16 gallons; fill 1-gallon can with 1 gallon gas, add 1 oz per gallon for regular maintenance or 2 oz per gallon for cleaning the tank (more than 2 oz okay); add to tank; run the mower to work it through.
- step 15: battery: remove or fully charge to avoid freezing; store removed battery dry, not damp/humid, where it won't freeze. 12-volt; 12.6 V or greater = 100% charged; lower, charge. Tighten cables; clean or replace corroded terminals. Charging: disconnect cables (positive/red last off and first on); if cells have caps check water, add distilled water if plates not covered (not well water; city water slightly better); connect charger; check voltage (11.8 V charges quickly; 10.1 V needs longer); "slow bake": very low battery on 2 or 4 amp setting about an hour, then check voltage is rising, then another hour if so.
- step 16: clean terminals with baking soda mix (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) and wire brush; thin layer of dielectric grease on terminals/electrical components.
- step 17: lubricate idler arm pivots on the deck and pumps and all pivot points/grease zerks with high-performance high-temperature grease (check manual); some casters need grease; wipe excess; lubricate cables with WD-40 or Sea Foam Deep Creep to stop freezing and corrosion.
- step 18: rodent deterrent: mice nest under the hood and chew wire harness; author recommends Grandpa Gus's Mouse Repellent (packets and wiring spray).

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: Change annually or sooner with heavy use; hourly interval per manual (varies by engine). New engine: change after initial 5-10 hours.
- step 1: Cover work surface (cardboard/newspaper).
- step 2: Run engine 2 to 3 minutes to warm oil (warm flows better), park on cover; caution hot engine.
- step 3: Remove ignition key and spark plug boot(s).
- step 4: Clean around oil plug; drain pan under; remove plug and drain. Some zero-turn engines use an oil hose port instead of a drain plug.
- step 5: Remove oil filter with filter wrench; light film of fresh oil on new filter gasket; install; reinstall drain plug after draining.
- step 6: Fill crankcase with manufacturer quantity and type; level not too low or too high.
- step 7: Reinstall plug boots, run a few minutes, shut off, let cool, recheck level, add if needed. Keep boot disconnected while continuing work.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Low/old hydraulic fluid makes mower seem weak and can damage the transmission; change fluid and filters at manufacturer's interval; break-in change sooner, some as soon as 75 hours. Some entry-level zero-turns have unserviceable hydraulic systems. Procedures vary by model; air must be removed after change; for most mowers raise rear tires off ground.
- step 1: Park on flat surface, key out, parking brake on, wait for moving parts to stop; engine and transmission can be hot.
- step 2: Filters are near each rear wheel, maybe under a guard/skid plate; remove guard and clean around filters first (keep dirt out).
- step 3: Pan under a hydraulic filter; unscrew and drain; repeat for the other; recycle the old oil.
- step 4: Thin layer of fresh hydro oil on the new filters' rubber seal; install turning clockwise; once seated turn about one more full turn.
- step 5: Fill with recommended hydraulic oil through the expansion reservoir (small plastic tank on top behind the seat; clean around it first); fill to the "full cold" line; may have two tanks (one each side). If transaxles have a vent plug, loosen each, fill until oil comes out, tighten, then continue to the "cold full" line.
- step 6: Purge air (see below).
- step 7: Let hydraulic system cool; recheck oil; add if needed.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Jack stands raise the rear just enough that wheels don't touch ground so drive wheels turn freely.
- step 2: Start the mower, throttle to about 1/2.
- step 3: Release the parking brake.
- step 4: Move speed levers forward and reverse several times for a few minutes total until wheels rotate at normal rate and sound.
- step 5: Cool and recheck oil level.
