## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil can cause engine damage from overheating, seal damage, blown gaskets, or hydrolocked engine; you may get lucky and just correct the level, otherwise major work or engine replacement.
- symptom: seals: pressure may blow out seals; unnoticed, engine can run out of oil and blow; extreme heat can spin a rod, weld it to the crankshaft, break it and throw it out the block side, weld piston to cylinder (seize).
- symptom: overheating: crankshaft and rod push through excess oil, raising crankcase pressure and loading internal parts; valve spring and rocker arm get hot; oil may not flow properly; aluminum gets very hot; can pop out a valve guide or valve seat from the block (engine then needs scraping out by a mechanic).
- symptom: lock-up: oil gets into the cylinder, engine turns over very hard; if oil gets past worn rings it can hydrolock; piston can't compress oil; piston may bend on starting.
- symptom: runs poorly/smokes: oil fouls the spark plug (inhibits spark); oil into the valve train is burned in the cylinder giving thick whitish-blue stinky smoke; with a clogged air filter the engine may pull air and oil out of the crankcase.
- symptom: gaskets: excess pressure may blow gaskets; twin-cylinder gasket replacement costs more than push mower since the engine must be removed.
- tip: when changing oil or adding, check twice; add only what the manufacturer requires.
- safety: don't keep running a Cub Cadet with engine problems; see an experienced small engine technician for non-simple oil-level fixes; technician can test whether repairable.
- safety: before troubleshooting remove spark plug wires and let engine cool.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- symptom: thick whitish-blue stinky smoke from oil in the valve train burning in the cylinder.
- causes-named: overfilled-oil, clogged-air-filter, bad-head-gasket

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- causes-named: overfilled-oil (crankcase pressure and load).

## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- fact: too much oil can prevent starting/running: hydrolock (oil past piston fills combustion chamber; piston designed to compress air, not oil or water; pulling the rope or starting won't move the piston); fouled plug; oil into the carburetor via valve train and on into the air filter.
- causes-named: overfilled-oil, hydrolocked-engine, fouled-spark-plug

## page: causes/hydrolocked-engine
- symptom: engine very hard to turn / won't turn; oil in the combustion chamber (oil pan on small engines is very small, little room for error).
- fact: piston may bend when you try to start.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: excess oil reaches the plug and fouls it, blocking spark.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- symptom: overfill pressure may blow gaskets (not specifically head gasket).

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- safety: remove spark plug wire first.
- step 1 (drain plug or valve port): if present, on the side of the engine by the dipstick or at the bottom of the oil pan; drain pan ready; remove plug for a quick second and replace; check level.
- step 2 (oil filter): if fitted, loosen or remove it to drain a little; have a rag.
- step 3 (fill hole): many push mower engines lack a drain plug; tip the mower to drain a little out of the fill hole.
- step 4 (oil extractor pump/evacuator): removes oil through a tube inserted into the fill hole.
- step 5 (turkey baster): good for small amounts; don't reuse for cooking.

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: find capacity in the owner's manual (or online by model). Most push mower small engines take about 3/4 quart; a v-twin about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" engines (about 34 hp or larger) about 3 quarts.
- tip: to avoid overfilling, know crankcase capacity and add only 1/2 quart at a time; near full add a little at a time, checking dipstick/gauge.
