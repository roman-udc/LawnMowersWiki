## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor regulates fuel-to-air mixture; old fuel leaves gummy and crusty buildup that clogs components and restricts fuel flow.
- symptom: bad carburetor shows as: won't start, backfires from running lean, sputters and runs rough, surges, consumes too much fuel.
- confirm: Do not disassemble unnecessarily. First confirm good fuel flow to the carburetor. Then remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake; if the mower starts, runs well, then shuts down, the carburetor likely needs cleaning, rebuilding or replacing.
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers
- tip: Take several photos before and during tear-down for reassembly reference.
- step 1: Shut off fuel: use the fuel shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank, or hose pinch-off pliers on the fuel line.
- step 2: Detach the throttle and choke cables from the carburetor.
- step 3: Remove the hardware attaching the carburetor to the air filter housing.
- step 4: Remove the springs from the carburetor slowly so they do not stretch (stretched springs must be replaced); may need to twist the carburetor a bit to release them.
- step 5: Remove the carburetor from the engine block carefully; a gasket sits between them, replace it if torn.
- step 6: Remove the bowl at the bottom (holds a little fuel; have a rag ready). Remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl.
- step 7: Do not get carburetor cleaner or other solution on the gasket between bowl and carburetor (looks like a rubber band) or it must be replaced.
- step 8: Find the holes in the stem hanging from the center of the carburetor with a flashlight; clear them by sliding a thick piece of wire through.
- step 9: Clean gummy and crusty buildup from all components with carburetor cleaner and a clean rag. Make sure float and needle move freely. You will not get all crusty material out; remove as much as possible.
- step 10: Reassemble in reverse order using the photos.
- fact: If cleaning fails and you are sure the carburetor is the problem, rebuild or replace it.
- mistake: Throwing parts at the mower hoping to fix it gets expensive; a repair shop diagnosis may be better.
- pro: Take to a local repair shop for diagnosis if unsure the carburetor is at fault.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Always use fresh gas with a fuel stabilizer. Author says Sea Foam Motor Treatment is safe for the mower: stabilizes fuel, reduces moisture, cleans the fuel system.
