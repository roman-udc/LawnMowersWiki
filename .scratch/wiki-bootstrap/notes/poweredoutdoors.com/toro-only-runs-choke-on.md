## page: problems/engine-only-runs-with-choke-on
- alias: "Toro only runs with the choke on", "sputters and dies when choke opened"
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Look for too little fuel or too much air. Lack of fuel: old gas, dirty carburetor, clogged fuel line, bad fuel pump, plugged fuel filter, bad fuel cap. Too much air: bad carburetor gasket, puncture in fuel line. The choke compensates for the fuel/air ratio.
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug boot before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Begins to break down about a month after purchase; gums the system and corrodes. Ethanol attracts moisture; ethanol and water separate and sink to tank bottom; sticky deposits on evaporation; water corrosive.
- brand: Toro mowers need unleaded gas, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol; never more than 10%.
- fix: drain old gas; refill fresh with Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old gas coats the inside, clogging passageways and making internal parts stick; the restriction requires choke to correct the ratio.
- fix: Clean it (a mechanical homeowner can); if it still doesn't work after cleaning, rebuild or replace; or use a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: A puncture or crack lets air in so the engine gets too much air.
- confirm: follow the line from tank to carburetor looking for punctures/cracks.
- fix: replace with a new section of the same diameter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: shut off flow with the shut-off valve; remove the end of a line section into a container; turn on flow and watch for good flow.
- fix: if poor, shut off, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner, then compressed air; repeat; replace if you can't clear it.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline filter between fuel lines; replace annually; new inline filter with arrow in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Moves fuel uphill to the carburetor; most are vacuum pumps using engine vacuum.
- confirm: confirm fuel reaches the inlet port; remove the line from the carburetor into a container and test flow.
- fix: replace if flow is insufficient or cracks/leaks.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Gasket behind the carburetor deteriorates; air sucked in; engine runs lean (more air, less fuel); choke compensates.
- confirm: inspect carb bolts tight and gasket good.
- step 1: Access carburetor; carefully remove linkages and bolts; remove carburetor and gasket.
- step 2: Install new gasket; reattach carburetor, bolts, linkages. Good time to clean the carb.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: On a Toro the tank vent is in the gas cap; plugged vent creates a vacuum blocking fuel to the carb. Pressure gauge can check vacuum.
- confirm: loosen the cap; choke off; if engine continues with choke off, cap may be at fault. Confirm: tighten cap with choke off and keep running; if it sputters after a while and won't run well until the cap is loosened, bad cap.
- fix: replace.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: arrow toward flow; annual.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- fact: referenced; rebuild/replace if still bad.
