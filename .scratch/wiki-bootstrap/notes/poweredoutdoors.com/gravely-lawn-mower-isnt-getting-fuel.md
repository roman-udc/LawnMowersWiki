## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Gravely isn't getting fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Mower runs sluggish or doesn't run at all when fuel supply is restricted.
- safety: Follow the Gravely owner's manual; take caution working near a hot engine.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase. Ethanol attracts moisture and leaves gummy deposits that block the fuel system.
- tip: Use unleaded gasoline with 10% ethanol or less; buy only what you can use in 30 days; stabilize excess (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment: stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans fuel passages).
- fix: Siphon old fuel into an approved container for recycling; refill with fresh fuel with a stabilizer/cleaning additive.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace; the arrow on the filter points in the flow direction, away from the tank and toward the carburetor. Wrong direction causes a flow problem.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Use the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank or fuel hose pinch-off pliers to stop flow. Pick a section, stop flow, remove the end furthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without the pump), start flow and watch.
- fix: Remove the clogged section; spray carburetor cleaner to loosen, then compressed air. Replace the line if you can't clear it or if it's dry and cracked.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Fuel sitting in the pump can degrade its components, or it wears from use; a failed pump can't draw pressure.
- confirm: First confirm fuel reaches the pump inlet port. Then remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start the engine and watch for a steady or pulsating flow. None = replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Quick test: remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, start. If it starts, runs briefly then shuts down, clean the carburetor.
- fix: Clean it (a little mechanical, comfortable with small parts), replace it, or have a shop clean it. When buying one, have the engine model and spec number (not mower model/serial).

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- tip: Quick air-intake carb-cleaner test first (see causes/dirty-carburetor).

## page: procedures/replace-the-carburetor
- tip: Order by engine model and spec number, not mower model/serial number.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Vented cap plugged, tank acts like a vacuum and fuel won't flow.
- confirm: Run the mower for a while with and without the cap. Runs fine without but shuts off after a while with it = plugged cap.
- fix: Try cleaning the vent; otherwise replace the cap.
