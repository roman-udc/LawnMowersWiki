## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "John Deere is leaking gas"; "strong gas smell in the garage"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter (cracked/deteriorating filter, see cracked-fuel-filter), bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- tip: Leaks are hard to find: gas evaporates, so there are often no wet spots; the first clue may be fumes filling the storage area.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom: Fuel leaking around the carburetor bowl. A rubber-band-like gasket sits between the bowl and carburetor; the carburetor sits next to the engine so it heats and cools constantly, drying and embrittling the gasket until it loses sealing.
- fix: Clean outside of the carburetor; remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl (rag ready for remaining gas); remove the bowl; swap the old gasket for a new one; reinstall the bowl and tighten the screw.
- symptom: Leak near the air intake port = stuck float (float regulates fuel entering the bowl; stuck float can't stop flow, fuel overflows). Fix: disassemble the carburetor to find the cause; may clean to unstick, rebuild or replace.
- symptom: Stuck float needle (float fine): needle works with the float to keep gas flowing into the bowl. Must rebuild the carburetor; rebuild kit from a John Deere dealer. Temporary loosening: tap the carburetor with a rubber mallet or the rubber end of a hammer; may work once or twice; replace the float needle to fix properly.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Gas sitting in the mower leaves deposits that clog and stick the small carburetor parts (bowl gasket, float, float needle) so it leaks.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: Old gas degrades the filter plastic, making it weak and soft; leaks at the seams.
- fix: Replace; careful, soft plastic filter ends can break off in the hose. Replace annually at service.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Gas can degrade the pump like the filter; check seams for leaks; replace a leaking pump.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: Current John Deere tanks are high-density polyethylene, which can fail and leak at the seams.
- fix: Replace the tank when a seam leaks or it's damaged.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Shut-off valve on the bottom of the gas tank; leaks are common over time; replace the valve.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: Lines dry and crack with age; clamps can puncture the hose.
- fix: Replace cracked lines; replace dry/cracking ones preventively. Replace pinch-style clamps; author prefers worm gear clamps because they're less likely to pinch the line.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: Fuel leaks around the cap when sloshing during operation; may not be noticed because gas evaporates.
- confirm: Rock the mower back and forth to splash fuel around the cap; wet spots outside the cap = bad seal.
- fix: Replace the cap.
