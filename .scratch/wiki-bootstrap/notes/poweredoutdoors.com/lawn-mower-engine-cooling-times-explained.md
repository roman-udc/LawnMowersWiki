## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- fact: cooling time varies with air vs liquid cooling, engine size, oil age, and external variables (outside temperature, grass thickness). Smaller, cleaner, air-cooled engines cool faster than larger, dirtier ones. No exact times are given.
- fact: overheating causes listed: metal expansion (components heat and expand; engine temperature can rise faster than cooling can keep up), low or old oil, dirty engine, damp and thick grass, old components; clogged air pathways (debris lodged in or outside the block blocking vents) are a common cause.
- symptom: overheating shows as smoke or steam from the engine cap, engine noises change to chugging, or the mower shuts down.
- tip: idle after use: lets oil spread heat evenly (temperature stabilization) and lets fans of air-cooled systems push air across the engine; typically 10 to 30 seconds of idling is enough; idling too long heats oil and engine more and slows cooldown. Idling may mean sitting stationary before shutoff or reducing power/speed for the last few minutes; many manuals advise idling after each use.
- tip: check oil only after the engine has been off a substantial time (avoid burns); use dipstick or oil pump; if oil is dark and grainy it is too old, sticks to parts and burns, adding heat; change it, then see whether cooldown speeds up.
- tip: keep the outside of engine and mower clean; dirt, grime and grass on top block air filtration and cooling; dried grass is flammable next to a hot engine.
- fact: follow the user manual; DIY cooling "rigs" risk voiding the warranty and a new engine.
- fact: air-cooled systems (most push and riding mowers) use a fan pushing air across cooling fins; keep oil and engine clean; debris clogs fins. Liquid cooling is less common, found on large mowers with heavier engines; more effective but costlier and heavier; use the proper coolant and keep levels correct.
- fact: external: an engine is more likely to overheat at 100 degrees than 75; longer, thicker or wet grass raises power demand and heat.
- fact: engine cleaning steps: turn off the engine and unplug the spark plug wire; remove engine screen/cover per manual; use a brush and/or mild washing soap on cooling fins; remove large visible debris.
- mistake: avoid compressed air or long brushes; they push debris further into the engine.
- fact: don't clean too often; engines need some grease and grime. Most manuals advise basic maintenance and oil changes around every 50 hours (about every two to three months for the average person); a new engine needs its first oil change within 7-10 hours.
- fact: "cleaning" means simple maintenance: change oil, change air filters, new spark plugs.
- fact: winterizing: drain gas or add fuel preservative; wash off debris; sharpen blades; remove battery and spark plug; store in a dry place. Keep rodents and insects out with traps or repellent (rodents chew wires, nest and clog air paths); author uses Grandpa Gus's packets and wire spray.
