## page: problems/not-bagging-grass
- alias: "mower is not bagging grass"; "grass catcher leaves clippings in the yard"
- causes-named: clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, clogged-deck-chute, worn-blade-sail, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet (inlet tube and inlet screen), worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-blower-fan-or-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, bad-bagger-or-full-bag
- fact: Root issue is loss of airflow: debris under the deck, clogged tubes, worn blades, slow engine, bad belts, worn fan or damaged blower housing in a belt-driven collection system.
- tip: Summary: don't mow wet grass; full throttle and slower ground speed; silicone spray on the underside of the deck, blower tubes and inlet screen; scrape the deck regularly and run sharp blades; seal any bagger areas where air can escape.

## page: causes/clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet
- confirm: Remove the blower tubes from the bagger (grass catcher), inspect each for grass, sticks, dirt; scrape out.
- tip: Coat blower tubes with silicone spray so debris doesn't stick; it reduces but doesn't eliminate cleaning; author suggests every other mow.
- fact: Inlet tube: remove bags/catchers from the hopper and check the inlet tube; common buildup spot. Clean it and replace bags/catchers.
- fact: Inlet screen: lets air leave the hopper so air carries clippings up the tubes; clogged screen loses the draft. It sits on the unexposed side of the grass hopper cover, so it's often overlooked. Brush off by hand; wire brush if bad; silicone spray allowed but let it dry before bagging.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Deck packed with debris blocks the air movement that pushes clippings through the tubes.
- fix: Lift the deck securely with jack stands or similar, mower off, key removed, spark plug wires disconnected. Scrape with deck scraper or wire brush. Keep the deck raised to check other items.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fix: Clear old or wet grass buildup from the chute; silicone spray inside helps flow.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- symptom: Thinning sails move less air under the deck, so air isn't pushed into the tubes.
- fix: Replace the blades with a new set.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Worn or damaged blades reduce air movement; see sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: Blade types: mulching, gator, low-lift, high-lift. For bagging use high-lift (higher sail gives more air movement). Sails must point upward toward the deck so suction forms under the deck.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: In a belt-driven grass collection system (adds a fan to blow clippings), the deck belt drives the blower belt for the fan. Replace belts that are glazed/shiny, cracked or frayed.

## page: causes/worn-blower-fan-or-bearing
- symptom: Fan (impeller) blades or blower housing worn or damaged lose the airflow that carries clippings.
- confirm: Check for gaps between the fan and blower housing; airflow is lost if too wide.
- fix: Replace a worn fan. Bearings on the fan shaft can wear; string or long grass wrapping the shaft can ruin them. Most blower housings must be disassembled to replace bearings.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: Run at full throttle when bagging; most engines need about 3200 to 3650 RPM for maximum power. If the engine doesn't sound like it's giving its usual power, see a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Too fast a ground speed loads the engine and gives the catcher no chance to pick up grass; slow down.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Wet grass is heavier and stickier; collects under the deck, in the chute, blower tubes and inlet tube; restricts airflow. Wait until dew is gone or the lawn has dried after rain; saves cleaning and scraping.

## page: causes/bad-bagger-or-full-bag
- fact: Bags must be the correct ones for the system. Older catchers use cloth bags (can sometimes be repaired). Today's bags are loosely knit mesh of nylon or polyester so air can escape; without that air movement bags would expand and burst at the seams.
- fact: Check bagger assembly: every component securely in place, no areas where air escapes; seal anything that reduces airflow.
