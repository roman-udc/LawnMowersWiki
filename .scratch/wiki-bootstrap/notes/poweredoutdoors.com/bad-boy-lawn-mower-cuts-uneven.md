## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Bad Boy mower cuts uneven"
- alias: "bad cut", "stair-step cut", "strips of uncut grass between passes"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, worn-blade-sail, dull-or-bent-blade, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, unlevel-deck, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: 13 reasons in the headline. Summary: deck doesn't sit level (low tire pressure, unleveled deck, damaged deck shell); worn/dull/bent blades; spindle housing damage; worn deck belt; incorrect operating procedures.
- safety: Before working on it: remove ignition key and disconnect spark plug wires; never reach under the deck while the engine runs, wait for all moving parts to stop; wear heavy work gloves; see owner's manual for model-specific safety.
- tip: Check tire pressure FIRST; skipping it can lead to unnecessary adjustments you later have to reverse.
- pro: If unsure how to diagnose/repair safely, have a professional (Bad Boy dealer or repair shop) do it.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- symptom: low tire means the deck does not sit level (problems/uneven-cut)
- confirm: use a tire pressure gauge; pressure should be the same on each side; correct as needed per the manufacturer's recommended pressure on the tire sidewall.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: uneven cut; blade ends rounded leaving uncut grass between blades.
- fact: Blades can usually be sharpened several times before replacement. Replace when tips have become round or the sail is thinner than the center of the blade. Wear is normal (sand swirling under the deck).
- tip: Author starts each season with a new set of blades, then checks and sharpens regularly.
- fact: A blade spinning at high speed can bend on a solid object (tree root, rock); can also damage the spindle housing. Walk the lawn and remove objects beforehand.
- confirm: Bent blade, blade on mower: park on a flat level surface, measure from ground to blade end, record; rotate blade 180 degrees, measure the same spot; more than 1/8" difference = bent, do not reuse.
- confirm: Bent blade, blade off mower: lay used blade on a known-good blade, look for gaps/bend; don't reuse a blade that doesn't sit flat against the good one.
- safety: NEVER attempt to straighten and reuse a bent blade; bending it back weakens the metal, more likely to crack; a blade breaking off at speed endangers people nearby.
- fact: Unbalanced blade (one side heavier) comes from uneven wear or from shaving more metal from one side when sharpening; it wobbles, can damage spindle bearings, causes significant vibration.
- confirm: Check balance with a blade balancer (inexpensive tool), or put a nail in the wall with head about 1 inch from the wall and hang the blade's center on it; a balanced blade sits level.
- fix: if one side sits lower, shave a little metal off that end until it sits level on the nail.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: The sail is the high side of the blade that creates air movement under the deck. When worn it can't create the suction that stands grass up for an even cut. Worn by the dirt/sand drawn in by that suction. Replace the blade when the sail is thinner than the center of the blade.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: blade wobbles, significant vibration at high speed, uneven cut.
- confirm: with blade still attached, grab each end of the blade and rock it up and down; check for movement; a knocking sound when it moves.
- fix: remove the blade, take apart the spindle housing, replace damaged parts.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: plugged deck (packed with clippings/debris) reduces space for airflow and suction; uneven cut plus strain on the engine.
- fix: keep deck clean; scrape regularly; avoid mowing wet (wet grass clumps and sticks to deck).
- tip: Sprays can reduce buildup but no spray prevents all of it.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- symptom: one side lower than the other gives a stair-step cut.
- confirm: check level side-to-side and front-to-rear. On some deck models the front sits slightly lower than the rear; see owner's manual for the exact spec.
- fix: leveling procedure varies by Bad Boy model; refer to the operator's manual.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: worn deck belt fails to grip pulleys and turn blades at high speed; reduced blade tip speed hurts cut quality.
- confirm: inspect for glazed shiny appearance, cracks; a stretched belt that isn't tight on the pulleys or sits deep in the pulleys.
- fix: replace with a new belt.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: pulley doesn't sit flat/secure on the deck; vertical movement, one side higher than the other.
- confirm: slowly spin each pulley by hand; feel for restriction and listen for bearing noise.
- fix: replace the pulley.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: Engage the deck at full throttle; engine speed must be at its highest to power the blades. If the engine no longer gives the needed power, have a small engine mechanic test it.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Engine overloads at fast ground speed giving a bad cut. Slow down in tall, wet or thick grass and on hills; too fast and grass isn't cut or is pushed over.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: Overlap mowing paths so no strips are left between passes; takes practice on a new mower; avoid mowing the same section twice.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- symptom: hitting a fence, tree, pole etc. damages the deck shell and causes uneven cut.
- fix: repair, or replace the shell.
- tip: Get quotes from the Bad Boy dealer for a deck shell replacement (including labor to swap all components over) and for a complete deck replacement; the price difference may be small.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- step 1: Park on a flat level surface; measure ground to blade end; record.
- step 2: Rotate blade 180 degrees; measure the same spot; more than 1/8" difference = bent.
- fact: Off-mower check by laying over a known-good blade; balance check via nail-on-wall method.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Replace when tips are round or sail thinner than center; sharpening several times is possible; check balance after sharpening (nail method or blade balancer).
