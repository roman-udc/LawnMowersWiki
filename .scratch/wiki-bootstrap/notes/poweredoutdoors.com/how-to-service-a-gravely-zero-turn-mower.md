## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: Full annual service in 21 steps (below). Routine maintenance finds small problems before they become large ones.
- tools: socket or spanner wrenches, filter wrench, deck scraper and/or wire brush, plastic funnel, oil drain pan
- parts: engine oil, engine oil filter, hydraulic oil and filters (if required), spark plugs, air filter, fuel filter, grease, rubber and heavy work gloves, rags/paper towel, mower blades (if not resharpening), fuel stabilizer, cardboard or newspaper
- step 1: Check safety devices and interlock system (deck won't run without operator in seat, plus other features per manual); never operate with any not working; inspect ROPS bar and seatbelt.
- step 2: Prepare: park on a flat level surface; run a few minutes to warm the oil (warm flows better); cardboard/newspaper under mower; set parking brake; shut off and remove key; disconnect spark plug wires; beware hot engine/transmission and sharp blades.
- step 3: Change engine oil (see procedure).
- step 4: Replace spark plug(s) (see procedure).
- step 5: Replace air filter.
- step 6: Clean debris from cooling fins and engine; hydro fans and transmission area (fans keep the transmission cool; transmissions may be hot).
- step 7: Change hydraulic oil and filters if necessary.
- step 8: Check battery and clean terminals; test battery and charging system.
- step 9: Remove debris around electrical components and wiring (moisture collects on debris, corrodes electrical parts).
- step 10: Replace fuel filter; check the fuel system for leaks (follow the line from tank to carburetor, tight connections).
- step 11: Check tire pressures with a gauge to the psi Gravely recommends, lug nuts secure (a low tire can cause steering issues and an unleveled deck).
- step 12: Clean top of deck: lowest deck position, remove belt covers, remove debris under pulleys and brackets and on the deck top (debris restricts the tensioner bracket and belt; dry debris is a fire risk); remove the deck per manual if you can't get access.
- step 13: Check pulleys and belts; scrape deck and check blades; check spindle housings.
- step 14: Replace damaged, worn or missing parts; check all hardware.
- step 15: Grease: pivot points, castor fork and castor wheel bearings, any zerks, with high-performance grease.
- step 16: Wash and dry completely.
- step 17: If storing, do the storage steps.
- safety: Follow the Gravely operator's manual.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- tools: oil drain pan, filter wrench, rag
- step 1: Place the drain pan under the drain plug or hose (under the hood, side of the engine near the bottom). Clean around the oil fill and drain plug so debris doesn't enter the crankcase.
- step 2: Remove the drain plug and let the oil drain; remove the dipstick from the fill to speed flow.
- step 3: When fully drained, reinstall the drain plug.
- step 4: Remove the oil filter with a filter wrench; catch the oil from the used filter.
- step 5: Put a light film of fresh oil on the new filter's seal and install.
- step 6: Add oil to the manufacturer's level; verify on the dipstick at "full"; low or high can cause engine damage and running problems.
- step 7: Wipe up drips; put used oil in a container marked "For Recycling"; take to a recycling center (some auto supply stores accept oil).

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- step 1: Remove plug(s) with a 3/4" or 5/8" socket depending on engine model.
- step 2: Install new plug(s) gapped to the engine manufacturer's recommendation.
- step 3: Don't reattach plug wires until service is complete.
- fact: Replace annually; if not replacing, inspect and wire-brush clean; look for broken porcelain or burnt electrode (replace).

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Replace at least annually, more in dry dusty conditions; check and clean during the season. Remove from housing; wipe housing with a dry cloth without dirt into the intake; install new filter and cover.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: Shut off fuel with the shut-off valve or crimp the line.
- step 2: Remove old filter, install new with the arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow. Change annually.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Low or old transmission fluid makes the mower seem weak and can damage the transmission. Most Gravely zero turns: change transmission oil every 400 hours with an initial break-in change at 75 hours; follow the operator's manual. If not changing, check level and top off to the "full cold" line on the expansion reservoirs (top of mower behind the seat, one per transmission, left and right).
- fact: Gravely says 20W-50 or 15W-50 works with the Hydro-Gear transmissions used in their zero turns.
- step 1: Park on a flat surface, remove key, engage parking brake, wait for moving parts to stop; engine and transmission can be hot.
- step 2: Locate filters (one near each rear wheel; may have a guard or skid plate); remove guard; clean around filters so no dirt enters the system.
- step 3: Pan under one transmission filter; unscrew and drain; repeat for the other; take old oil to recycling.
- step 4: Apply thin layer of fresh transmission oil to the new filters' rubber seals; install by turning clockwise; once it contacts the filter adapter, turn about another 3/4 to a full turn.
- step 5: Clean around the expansion reservoirs before removing caps; fill with good quality fresh oil through the reservoirs to the "full cold" line (two tanks). Some transaxles have a vent plug on each; loosen them, fill through the reservoir until oil comes out the vent plug, tighten, and continue filling to "cold full."
- step 6: Purge air (see procedure).
- step 7: Let the system cool, recheck oil level, add if needed.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Raise the rear of the mower with jack stands just high enough for the wheels to turn freely.
- step 2: Open the bypass valves, start the mower, throttle to about 1/2.
- step 3: Release the parking brake; move the speed levers forward and reverse slowly about 6 times in total.
- step 4: Repeat with the bypass valves closed until the wheels rotate at a normal rate and sound; repeat as necessary to remove all air.
- step 5: Let it cool and recheck the oil level.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Disconnect battery cables; positive (red +) is the last one off and first one on. If caps are removable, check water level; if plates aren't covered, add distilled water (well water is not good; city water slightly better; distilled best).
- step 2: Hook the charger on; check voltage with a meter. 11.8 V needs little charging; 10.1 V needs a long time; the lower the reading the longer.
- step 3: Author charges a very low battery "slow bake": 2 or 4 amps (chargers may have 6, 4, 2 A settings) for about an hour; recheck; if voltage is rising continue another hour; if not rising, buy a new battery (dealer, hardware or auto parts store).
- fact: Clean terminals with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) and wire brush; add dielectric grease.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- step: Remove the engine cover and clean debris around the engine, the cover and the cooling fins (needed so air circulates around block and cylinder).

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- confirm: With heavy work gloves grab each end of a blade and rock up and down; extra play or knocking means disassemble the spindle housing (likely failed bearing).
- fact: Remove and sharpen blades still in good condition (sharp edge, smooth small nicks). Replace blades that are bent, have large gouges, or are extremely worn (ends rounded, or sail thinner than the center).
- fact: Scrape the deck with a deck scraper or wire brush; plugged deck gives uneven cut and extra engine load.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: signs: cracking, shiny glaze, shredding; stretched belt not sitting tight on pulleys; replace.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: Pulley must sit flat and parallel to the deck; vertical movement means replace. Spin by hand; restriction or noise means bad bearing. Clean rust off pulleys (rust dries the belt); replace extremely rusty pulleys.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: Low tire can cause steering issues and unlevel deck; set to Gravely's recommended psi (no number given).

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: See tune-up steps; hydraulic oil 75 h break-in, 400 h interval; spark plug, air filter and fuel filter annually.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Stabilize fuel (ethanol and moisture separate, causing running problems and gumming); author uses Sea Foam. Without an additive, drain all fuel, start the mower and run until it shuts off.
- fact: Fully charge the battery (a fully charged battery will not freeze) or remove and store in a cool dry place away from freezing and combustibles.
- tip: Use a rodent deterrent; author has seen extensive wire harness damage from chewing. Uses Grandpa Gus's Rodent Repellent (pouch and spray); pouches tucked in the mower; spray on wiring harness.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: Wash with a garden hose; if pressure washing don't use high pressure (can damage sealed bearings). Dry completely (stored wet corrodes and fails electricals): leaf blower, sun, or a breezy day.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: see charge-the-battery; test the battery and charging system annually.

## page: guides/zero-turn-safety
- fact: Interlock system won't run the deck without the operator in the seat; never operate with safety elements not working; inspect ROPS bar and seatbelt.
