## page: problems/not-bagging-grass
- alias: "mower won't pick up leaves"
- alias: "bagger keeps clogging"
- causes-named: clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet, clogged-deck-chute, worn-blade-sail, blade-installed-upside-down, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-blower-fan-or-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, bad-bagger-or-full-bag
- fact: Bagger clogs when air movement is lacking: leaf buildup under deck, worn blades, plugged inlet screen. Belt-style systems: bad belts, impellers, housing. Common theme: everything restricts air movement the system needs.
- fact: Leaf collection = same systems as grass bagging; existing grass bagger works for leaves. Check dealer for OEM bagger that fits your model.
- fact: Three types for riding/zero-turn: flow (blade airflow moves chopped leaves through tube into bag; simplest; on push mower leaves go through discharge chute into catcher), belt-driven (blower housing on discharge side of deck protects impeller fan; added belt drives impeller for more airflow through blower tube to bags), pull-behind (extra engine on trailer creates vacuum pulling leaves through tube into trailer bags/compartment).
- tip: Make passes over leaves; may need more than one pass.

## page: causes/clogged-blower-tubes-or-inlet
- fix (tubes): Remove blower tubes, check for grass, sticks, leaves; scrape inside clean; spray silicone inside to reduce sticking; reuse silicone frequently.
- fix (inlet tube): Remove bags/catchers from hopper; inspect and clean inlet tube.
- fix (inlet screen): Screen lets air leave hopper to carry leaves up tubes; often forgotten, located on side of hopper cover facing the hopper (top, unseen). Clean by hand, wire brush for tough buildup, spray silicone and let dry. Clogged screen = no air draft, bags won't fill.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: Leaf chute can plug with old or wet leaves; clean, silicone spray inside helps flow.
- fact: Deck full of grass/leaf debris won't create airflow; raise deck (jack stands), scrape with deck scraper or wire brush; remove key and spark plug boots first.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: Worn sails (high side of blade) can't move grass and leaves under deck to create air movement. Need sharp good blades.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: High-lift blade (taller sail) works best with leaf collection. Sail must point up toward the deck to create vacuum.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: Belt-driven system: deck belt drives blower belt; remove belt covers and floor pan to inspect; worn or shiny glazed belt = replace. Same for blower belt.

## page: causes/worn-blower-fan-or-bearing
- fact: Fan (impeller) in blower housing: worn blades or housing, or large gap between fan and housing, loses airflow.
- fact: Larger blower housings have bearings on fan shaft; debris wrapped on shaft ruins bearing; housing usually must be disassembled to check.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: Bagger needs maximum horsepower; most engines should run about 3200 to 3650 rpm. If engine sounds like it isn't giving all it has, check engine; engine may be hurt or too small for the collection system.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Wet leaves are heavy, build up under deck and stick inside tubes, reducing airflow. Don't mow very wet leaves; systems work best with dry or lightly moist leaves. May need a couple of passes.

## page: causes/bad-bagger-or-full-bag
- fact: Bags were cloth (sometimes repairable); today mostly mesh/loosely knit polyester or nylon, not easily repaired. Bag life about two to three years depending on debris. Mesh vents allow airflow; without airflow-passing bags, bags expand with leaves and air and can be damaged.
- fact: Bagger not assembled/secured properly lets air escape, reducing air movement.
