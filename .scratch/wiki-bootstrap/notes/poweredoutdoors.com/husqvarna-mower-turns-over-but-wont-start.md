## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Husqvarna turns over but won't start", "engine acts like it wants to start but won't fire"
- causes-named: wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Starting needs air, fuel, spark. Article title says 9 reasons.
- safety: Follow operator's manual; wait for engine to cool; remove ignition key and spark plug wires before repairs.
- pro: If not comfortable, contact a Husqvarna dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts air for cold starts; cold engine needs rich mixture (more gas, less air). After warm-up the choke lever must go to off. Warm engine: choke must be off.
- fix: choke on/closed for cold, off/open for warm. If correct but airflow issue, check choke plate isn't stuck and cable moves freely; carburetor cleaner can free a stuck plate/linkage; replace a stretched/worn choke cable with a new Husqvarna choke cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Filter stops dirt entering intake. A plugged filter restricts airflow. Author starts each season with a new filter and cleans several times per season.
- fix: Paper filter clean: remove filter without letting dirt fall into the intake; wipe housing with clean dry rag; tap filter on a solid surface; hold up to light: reuse if light shows through; replace if no light, extremely dirty or damaged; reinstall cover.
- fix: Foam pre-filter (extra filter used with paper filter; don't confuse with a primary foam filter): replace if torn or brittle; wash with water and mild dish soap; rinse until water runs clear; lay flat to dry (sun speeds it); install with paper filter. NEVER add oil to a pre-filter or you will damage the paper filter.
- fix: Foam primary filter: remove, wipe housing, wash with water and mild dish soap, rinse till clear, squeeze out water, dry completely, saturate in clean engine oil, squeeze out excess, install.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: damaged or very dirty plug causes intermittent/no spark. Look for dark tip, burnt electrode, broken porcelain: replace.
- fix: Remove with 3/4" or 5/8" socket (depends on engine). Clean a good but slightly dirty plug with a small wire brush for carbon; confirm electrode gap before reinstalling; attach wire securely. Author installs new plugs each season.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas begins to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; leaves varnish and sticky deposits causing restrictions. Use fresh gasoline within 30 days, min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol. 4-cycle Husqvarna mowers use gasoline; older push mowers may be 2-cycle using a gas/oil mix.
- fix: Drain the tank with a fuel siphon pump; refill with fresh fuel plus stabilizer like Sea Foam (cleans system, reduces moisture); if it starts, let it run so fuel/additive circulates.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Inline fuel filter between fuel lines strains dirt; plugged if not replaced regularly; mower turns over and won't start. Replace with a new Husqvarna fuel filter.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Follow the line from tank to carburetor for kinks. Then shut off fuel (shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers), remove the end furthest from the tank into a container, turn on fuel and check flow section by section.
- fix: Remove line; spray carburetor cleaner in to loosen the clog; blow compressed air through; repeat until clear; reinstall; replace if clog remains or line is aged/cracking.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Pump used when the carburetor sits higher than the tank. Most mowers use a vacuum pump (crankcase vacuum).
- confirm: First ensure fuel reaches pump inlet (check line/filter). Then remove fuel line from carburetor into a container, start fuel flow and try starting: expect steady or pulsating flow; none = replace pump.
- brand: Husqvarna with electronic fuel injection pump: measure pressure with a gauge; see operator's manual for spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old gas varnish plugs the fuel jet or sticks internals.
- confirm: Confirm fuel flow to the carb; remove air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into the intake and start. If it starts then runs sluggish/dies when cleaner burns off, carb is dirty. Don't use starter fluid.
- fix: clean, repair parts or replace carburetor.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Husqvarna tank vent is in the gas cap; plugged vent creates a vacuum, so fuel can't reach the carb; mower turns over but won't start.
- confirm: loosen the cap and try to start; if it starts, suspect the cap. Confirm: with it running, tighten the cap; if it sputters/dies and won't restart until the cap is loosened.
- fix: replace the cap.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: see steps under causes/clogged-air-filter (paper, foam pre-filter, foam primary).

## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- fact: drain old gas with siphon pump, refill with fresh + stabilizer.
