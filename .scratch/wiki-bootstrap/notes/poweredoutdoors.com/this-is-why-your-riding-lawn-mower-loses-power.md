## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "riding mower not giving the power it once did, keeps bogging down"
- alias: "riding mower running sluggish"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: riding mower loses power when engine lacks airflow or fuel flow or runs hot (low oil, clogged air passages, plugged filters, dirty carburetor, restricted fuel components), or is under load (plugged deck, too-fast ground speed).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: replace annually, clean several times a season, more often in very dusty conditions.
- step 1: remove cover from housing (often clips, knobs or wing nuts).
- step 2: carefully remove filter without dropping dirt into the intake.
- step 3: clean remaining dirt from housing with dry clean rag.
- step 4: tap filter against a hard surface.
- step 5: hold to light; if light passes through, reuse; if not, or damaged or oil-covered, replace.
- step 6: reinstall filter and cover.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down and becomes less effective after 30 days; ethanol and moisture leave gummy deposits restricting flow.
- fix: drain tank, fill with fresh gas; most gas riding mowers need unleaded, min octane 87, max ethanol 10%. Add Sea Foam Motor Treatment to stabilize, reduce moisture, clean the fuel system. Use fuel within 30 days; otherwise add additive (author adds Sea Foam to every tank).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: replace annually with routine maintenance; inexpensive part prevents supply issues and engine damage.
- tools: shutoff valve or fuel pinch pliers (if no valve)
- step 1: shut off fuel flow with shutoff valve or pinch pliers.
- step 2: pull the filter out of the lines, install a new one.
- step 3: arrow on filter side points in direction of fuel flow: toward carburetor and away from tank.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- see causes/clogged-fuel-filter steps above.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop flow, remove end of a hose section into a container, restart flow, check for good flow.
- fix: remove blocked section, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air; if can't clear or dry/cracked, replace with line of same length and diameter.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: before disassembly, remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into air intake and start; if it runs but won't stay running, clean the carburetor.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: fouled plug -> intermittent spark -> power loss. Look for carbon, dirt, oil buildup on the tip; replace dirty/damaged plug; if only dirty and not very dark, wire brush and reuse.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low oil causes power loss. Causes: not enough added at last change; oil leak; burning oil from wrong viscosity.
- fact: no oil -> friction -> extreme heat -> overheating, smoke from burning oil and melting parts; significant engine damage, big repair bill or engine replacement.
- confirm: check oil before each use: remove dipstick, wipe with clean rag, reinsert, remove again and read; must be at full mark. Add fresh oil to full; do not overfill.
- pro: if problems continue after refilling, have an experienced small engine mechanic diagnose; low oil may have caused internal damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: bluish-white smoke when oil burns in the cylinder; pressure builds and oil is pushed into the cylinder through the valve train; the smoke cloud can plug the air filter.
- fact: check air filter and spark plug and clean/replace if needed.
- safety/risk: continued running with too much oil can cause seal damage, engine hydro lock, bent piston rod (as written in source).
- fix: remove a little oil: loosen and quickly tighten the drain plug to release a little; or use oil evacuator or turkey baster at the oil filter or fill area.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: option A: loosen then quickly tighten the drain plug so only a little oil comes out.
- step 2: option B: remove oil from the oil fill area or oil filter with a turkey baster or oil evacuator.
- tools: oil evacuator, drain plug, turkey baster

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins dissipate heat; plug with mud and grass clippings, limiting airflow around the engine block. Clean debris and replace broken fins.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: remove debris collected under the engine shroud to keep air passages clear (listed as "blocked air passageways").

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: fast ground speed loads engine in thick, wet or tall grass; slow down; also slow down on inclines at fast speeds.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: wet grass makes engine work harder and clumps and sticks to deck; mow when dry. Tall grass bogs the mower.
- tip: double or triple cut tall grass: first cut with deck at highest setting, then lower for next cut.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: packed deck makes engine work harder to turn blades through debris; regularly scrape; also helps cut quality.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades worsen the load of a packed deck; check and sharpen or replace.
