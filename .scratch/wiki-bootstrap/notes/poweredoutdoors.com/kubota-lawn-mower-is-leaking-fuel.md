## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "Kubota mower leaking fuel"
- alias: "smell gas but no wet spot"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Often the only sign is a gas odor; gas evaporates leaving no wet spot.
- fact: Carburetor can leak from a bad bowl gasket, stuck float, or stuck float needle; also filter, lines, tank, pump and shut-off valve.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact (bowl gasket): sits between bowl and carburetor, looks like a rubber band. Near the engine, heat/cool cycles dry it and it loses its seal, leaking fuel from the bowl.
- fix (replace gasket): stop fuel flow; wipe the carburetor exterior; remove the screw under the bowl and the bowl (rag for fuel); replace the gasket; keep carburetor cleaner or other substances off the gasket or buy a new one; reinstall bowl and tighten the screw; restore fuel flow and check for leaks; if still leaking check float and needle.
- fact (stuck float): leak near the air intake port suggests it; float regulates fuel in the bowl; stuck float lets fuel overflow. Disassemble to find the cause; may clean or rebuild.
- fact (stuck float needle): needle works with the float; stuck open fuel runs out. Tapping the carburetor lightly with a rubber mallet or hammer handle end may free it temporarily; eventually replace the needle.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Fuel through the carburetor leaves gummy and crusty deposits; the carburetor holds a little fuel in the bowl.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Ethanol in gasoline degrades plastic; filter softens and leaks at the seams. Replace; take care removing it, since soft plastic can break off in the line. Install with the arrow pointing in the direction of flow, away from the tank toward the carburetor.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Fuel degrades the pump too; leaks at seams; a leaking pump can't build pressure. Pump should be close to the carburetor, mounted on the engine, with a line from tank to pump and pump to carburetor. Replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Kubota fuel tanks are high-density polyethylene; seams can fail; best to replace the tank when it leaks.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Metal or plastic valve at the bottom of the fuel tank; both types prone to leaking; replace a leaking one.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Lines dry, crack and leak with age. Check ends under clamps; clamps can puncture lines. If the mower uses pinch-style clamps, replace them with worm gear clamps (less likely to leak). Replace old lines.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: gas fumes but no wet spot; fuel may leak around the cap and evaporate.
- confirm: Check after everything else; rock the mower back and forth to splash fuel up to the cap area and watch for a wet spot outside the tank around the cap. Replace the cap if it leaks.
