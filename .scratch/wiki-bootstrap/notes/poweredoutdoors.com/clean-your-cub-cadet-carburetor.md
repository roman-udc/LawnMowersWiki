## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor regulates the air/fuel ratio into the cylinder. Dirty carburetor restricting air or fuel: engine may use more fuel, run poorly, or not get fuel.
- symptom: Rich = too much fuel; lean = too much air. Bad carburetor symptoms: won't start; starts then dies; backfires (from running lean); sputtering/running rough; surging; black smoke from muffler (rich); consuming too much fuel.
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers; fuel hose pinch-off pliers (optional); rag; flashlight.
- confirm: Remove air filter from housing, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, start the engine. If it fires but shuts down, disassemble and clean the carburetor.
- tip: Take many photos during disassembly to help reassembly.
- step 1: Shut off the fuel supply (fuel shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers on fuel line). Remove the fuel line from the carburetor.
- step 2: Remove throttle cable and choke cable if the mower has them.
- step 3: Detach the filter housing: remove screws and nuts attaching the carburetor to the filter housing.
- step 4: Remove the springs slowly without stretching them; may need to twist the carburetor a bit. Do not tear the gasket between engine block and carburetor.
- step 5: Remove the float bowl (bottom of carburetor, where gasoline is stored). Clean dirt from the outside of the bowl first, then remove the screw from the bottom of the bowl; keep a rag to catch gas.
- warning: Do not damage the o-ring between bowl and carburetor (looks like a rubber band). Do not get carburetor cleaner or other substances on the o-ring; it stretches and can't be reused.
- step 6: Check the stem (hangs down from carburetor center) for clogged holes; clogged holes won't draw fuel up to the jet. Use a flashlight to find the holes and a thick wire to unplug them.
- step 7: Check for white crusty buildup (from fuel additives including ethanol); remove as much as possible with carburetor cleaner. Nearly impossible to remove all.
- step 8: Reassemble in reverse order using the photos; reattach springs, filter housing, throttle and choke cable (if used), and fuel line.
- step 9: Restore fuel flow; fill with fresh gasoline mixed with a fuel additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment. Let fuel fill the bowl and start. A pull-cord engine may need several pulls to start.
- safety: Follow operator's manual safety instructions; consult a professional if unsure.
- pro: If corroded or damaged parts, replace the carburetor.

## page: procedures/replace-the-carburetor
- fact: If cleaning does not work because carburetor is corroded or has damaged parts, replacement may be best. Have engine model and spec number available when ordering from an engine dealer or online.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: A rebuild kit may be available; compare its cost with a new carburetor, as there may be no significant price difference.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor

- fact: A dirty or bad carburetor can cause Cub Cadet not starting, quitting after starting, and leaking fuel.
- confirm: Air filter off, spray carb cleaner into intake and start: runs then dies means dirty carburetor.
