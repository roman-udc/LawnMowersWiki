## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- alias: "Cub Cadet quits when it gets hot"
- alias: "mower shuts off after running a while"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Source notes the shutdown might not be overheating at all; other reasons exist (see common Cub Cadet problems).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: too little air can overheat and quit; replace annually, clean several times a season; more often with frequent mowing or dusty dry conditions. Neglect can contribute to internal engine damage.
- fix (paper): remove carefully so dirt doesn't enter the intake; wipe the housing with a dry cloth; tap on a solid surface; hold to light (Source text says "replace if you can see light" which is reversed vs. other Sources; likely typo - flag); use new filter if no light.
- fix (foam): wash with mild dish soap and water; rinse until all soap is gone; lay flat to dry (sun speeds); lightly coat with filter oil and squeeze out excess (not dripping); don't reuse if dry, brittle or torn.
- note: the paper-filter light-test wording conflicts with the other Sources (reuse if light shines through).

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas goes bad after 30 days; ethanol and moisture separate and cause gummy deposits that may make the mower shut down after running a while.
- fix: siphon old fuel into a gas container; refill with fresh gasoline containing a stabilizer/cleaner; author recommends Sea Foam Motor Treatment (cleaning agents for the engine and fuel system, good for the carburetor and valve train).

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake, start; if it starts then shuts down, take the carburetor apart to clean or rebuild.
- fix: clean it (if mechanical), shop clean, or replace.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: use air-cooled engine oil with a high concentration of zinc (zinc is described as a cooling additive); differs from car oil (cars are liquid-cooled). Most small engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; 20W-50 may be needed at higher ambient temperatures. Kawasaki viscosity/temperature chart referenced.
- fix: drain and refill with the viscosity recommended by the engine maker.
- fact (low oil): below the recommended level, oil heats and thickens, increasing friction; an oil change rarely fixes it once the mower has shut down; likely internal damage; small engine mechanic needs to test. Low oil may mean burning or using oil, or a leak. Check oil before each mowing.
- brand: Cub Cadet (Kawasaki-chart reference).

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: crankshaft and rod push through excess oil, crankcase pressure rises and loads internal parts, so the engine quits when hot; oil smoke can clog the air filter and the engine shuts off.
- fix: remove spark plug wire first, then drain a little: drain plug (remove and replace quickly), oil filter (rag ready), oil fill hole (push mowers with no drain plug or filter: tip the mower, keep carburetor and air filter on the high side), oil extractor pump / oil evacuator, turkey baster. Recheck the level, adjust; replace the air filter if it has oil on it.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: remove spark plug wire.
- step 2: drain a little via one of: drain plug quickly removed/replaced; oil filter removed with rag; fill hole by tipping mower with carburetor and air filter on high side; oil evacuator; turkey baster.
- step 3: recheck oil level; repeat if needed.
- step 4: replace air filter if oily.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: clean annually; debris plugs them so they can't dissipate heat. Remove debris around fins, replace broken fins, clear debris under engine shrouds, confirm the heat shield is securely in place.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: may start the mower but not keep it running. Look for carbon buildup or cracked porcelain; clean and reuse if just dirty; replace if damaged or excessively dark. Correct gap and securely attached wires (loose wire or wrong gap gives intermittent running/starting).

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: when hot it can stop working; windings separate and short; insufficient voltage to plug.
- confirm: ohmmeter, check continuity; replace a faulty coil.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vacuum in the fuel system after running a while; mower runs with cap removed and quits after a short time with it installed - replace the Cub Cadet gas cap.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: engine strain turning blades through debris; quits when hot; sharpen blades and scrape deck; avoid wet conditions (wet grass collects under deck and clumps).

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades add to the load; sharpen.
