## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Bad Boy zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- fact: a fuel-powered mower needs air, fuel, spark and compression to start and run.
- fact: table rows - no gas: fill; bad/old fuel: drain, refill, use additive like Sea Foam; clogged cap vent: clean or replace cap; bad plug (dirty, broken, bad connection, wrong gap): replace, secure, gap to spec; plugged air filter (dirt and grass): clean or replace; plugged fuel filter: replace; bad/leaking pump: replace; blocked lines: carb cleaner + compressed air, replace if needed; dirty carb: clean, replace if needed; dead battery/loose cables/corroded terminals: charge, replace if it won't hold a charge, tighten, clean terminals; bad safety switch: test and replace; bad ignition switch or coil: test and replace; broken recoil (pull-start): restring or replace parts/assembly.
- tip: neglecting regular oil changes and filter replacement may void remaining warranty.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "zero turn loses power and dies"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: lose power/shut off from fuel or air restriction; may also shut down from overheating (lack of air, plugged cooling fins, low oil). Also check battery, charging system, plug and coil.
- fact: too much engine oil increases crankcase pressure, causing overheat and dying; fix: remove a little oil.
- fact: coil windings can separate and short; check continuity with an ohm meter, replace if bad.
- fact: choke is for cold starts; if left on after warm-up it keeps restricting airflow and can shut the engine off.
- fact: clogged deck strains the engine and can shut it down; scrape the deck periodically.
- fact: Bad Boy vents tank through gas cap or rollover vents; plugged vents restrict fuel.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Bad Boy engine is smoking"
- causes-named: water-in-fuel, spilled-oil-on-engine, clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, bad-head-gasket, bad-valve-train, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: smoke may come from water in the fuel system or oil burning off a hot component; an engine oil leak or spill burns off a hot engine or muffler.
- fact: too much oil: pressure builds, oil gets into the cylinder through the valve train; drain a little until at manufacturer's recommended level.
- fact: low oil: more friction and extreme heat; take to a small engine repair shop to inspect for damage; adding oil is often too late.
- pro: internal engine issues (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) go to a small engine repair shop.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "zero turn won't drive straight"
- alias: "hydro feels weak on one side"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, worn-drive-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: weak hydrostatic transmission can come from a bad hydraulic belt or pulley, old hydraulic oil, low hydraulic tank level, or a hydraulic leak; when weak on one side the mower pulls to one side.
- fact: unequal tire pressure pulls to one side; tires on each side need equal pressure at manufacturer spec.
- fact: bad dampers make the mower jerk to one side moving forward; fix with a new damper.
- fact: tracking: when one tire turns faster than the other, adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change tire rotation speed; see owner's manual.
- fact: bad drive belt (frayed, cracked, worn) makes the pump inefficient; failed tensioner pulley no longer holds belt tension: replace pulley and grease the tensioner arm.
- fact: low or old hydraulic oil: change at intervals recommended by Bad Boy; check level periodically.
- tip: some transmissions are non-serviceable (sealed), oil cannot be changed.
- pro: sealed hydraulic system or bigger transaxle problems go to a repair shop or Bad Boy dealer.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "zero turn won't go forward or reverse"
- causes-named: brake-hung-up, worn-drive-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: first check the bypass drive release levers at the rear (left and right); pulled out = neutral/hydros disengaged so mower rolls manually. Must be in operating position.
- fact: drive pump belt fallen off or worn: replace worn/cracked belt, make sure it is around pulleys. Tensioner pulley bearing can fail: replace. Idler arm spring broken/missing: replace.
- fact: hot hydraulic fluid can signal hydrostatic damage; let it cool; if mower runs fine cold but weakens hot, have the dealer check.
- fact: air must be bled from the hydraulic system after changing fluid or mower won't move at normal speed; follow Bad Boy's change/bleed procedure in owner's manual.
- pro: sealed hydraulic systems go to a dealer.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: some vibration is normal; intensifies with loose or broken parts, worn linkages, bad clutch, debris. Deck causes: unbalanced blades, worn spindle housing/pulley bearings, damaged spindles, worn belt, bent brackets.
- fact: engine mounting bolts reduce vibration; secure and replace missing bolts.
- fact: clutch: bearing failure and bearing falling out causes vibration; a manual engagement clutch may have a failing bushing and linkages; replace the clutch.
- fact: blades must be balanced before installing; replace damaged ones.
- fact: string or long weeds wrapped around the blade shaft cause vibration; remove, check spindle for damage.
- fact: bad spindle housing bearing: replace bearing or whole spindle housing assembly.
- tip: even the smallest debris can cause significant vibration; inspect closely.

## page: problems/uneven-cut

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "burnt grass trail", "gas smell in shed"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: leak can be hard to locate; fuel may evaporate leaving no clues. Signs: trail of burnt grass, strong odor in storage shed. Old gas degrades components, weakens plastic housings.
- fact: poly tank can leak at seams; metal and plastic shutoff valves prone to leak; rubber fuel lines dry and crack; gas cap seal failure lets gas splash out around cap; pump leaks at the seams.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "zero turn clicks or hums and won't turn over"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-motor
- fact: click or hum suggests a bad starter solenoid; also check power reaching the solenoid (battery, cables, terminals, ground), then starter motor.
- confirm: test battery; place on a charger if reading is less than 12.7 volts on a 12 V battery; replace if it won't hold a charge.
- fact: ground: remove corrosion; confirm battery ground cable and solenoid ground cable make good contact. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.

## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: blades won't engage when PTO switch faulty, battery weak, safety switch failed or clutch bad; won't turn when belt worn/fell off from stretched belt, bad pulley bearing or worn tensioner parts.
- fact: replace a belt that is worn, cracked or shiny/glazed. Failed PTO switch can't pass battery voltage to the clutch. Clutch engages the drive belt; on a manual engagement clutch replace worn clutch cable, lever, springs, linkages. Clutch is powered by the battery (test at 12.7 V rule above). Bad safety switch: seat switch may not recognize operator in the seat. Blown fuse: replace with same amperage; if it keeps blowing have a mechanic find the root cause.

## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "runs rough and sputters", "bogs down"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, mowing-too-fast, wrong-engine-speed, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: look at fuel flow, airflow, intermittent spark, and engine load (fast ground speed, plugged deck, dull blades). Throttle not at fastest speed makes engine sluggish with blades engaged: put throttle at highest when engaging blades.
- fact: carbon on plug can cause intermittent starting; clean with wire brush, replace if very dark, worn or damaged.
- fact: slow ground speed for tall, thick or wet grass.

## page: problems/engine-overheats
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: engine oil helps cool the engine; Bad Boy requires air-cooled engine oil high in zinc; use 10W-30 in most conditions, different viscosity possibly needed in very warm conditions; keep level full.
- fact: low oil reduces lubrication causing friction and heat.
- fact: cooling fins push air around cylinder heads/block; clear dirt and debris from fins, around engine and air intakes.
- fact: missing/loose engine guard lets air escape instead of circulating around engine; replace or secure.
- fact: avoid cutting wet grass; double or triple-cut an overgrown lawn.
- fact: plugged air filter makes engine work harder searching for clean air.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: drain tank, refill with fresh fuel plus stabilizer like Sea Foam; Source also links "use the right fuel".

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: throttle lever in highest position when engaging mower blades.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: slow ground speed to match mowing conditions; slow for tall, thick or wet grass.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fix: scrape the deck periodically to keep it clean.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify the load of a plugged deck; sharpen; replace worn blades.
