## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "I have to charge the battery every time I mow"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, faulty-charging-system, parasitic-battery-drain, bad-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for skin and eyes; follow operator's manual

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: follow the red (positive) cable from battery to solenoid then to starter; all secure and making good connections
- symptom: corrosion appears as a white or green substance; weak charge and battery keeps dying
- safety: before cleaning, disconnect the negative cable first; otherwise a wrench touching frame can arc and spark and could blow up the battery; always wear safety glasses and skin protection; positive cable is the last one off and the first one on
- fix: with cables off, clean with baking soda paste (4 cups water with about six rounded tablespoons baking soda), spread on corrosion, it foams; rinse with water (outdoors, messy); wire brush speeds up; cola also removes corrosion

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- confirm (test): step 1 volt-ohm meter set to 12 volts; step 2 engine off, red lead to positive, black to negative: should read 12.4 to 12.8 volts (some higher); write it down; step 3 set parking brake and start engine; step 4 leads on battery again; step 5 run at about 3/4 throttle: should read 13.2 to 13.9 volts or higher; higher than first reading = alternator charging
- symptom: no change between readings = charging system problem; possibly bad regulator or bad stator/alternator
- fact: some systems 15 amp, others 20 amp depending on manufacturer and engine
- pro: have a mechanic or Husqvarna dealer identify the root cause; expensive parts, don't throw parts at it
- tip: if cables are clean you can run this voltage test any time; it may also reveal a bad connection

## page: causes/bad-alternator
- fact: bad regulator or bad stator/alternator suspected when voltage doesn't rise with engine running

## page: causes/parasitic-battery-drain
- symptom: key left in ON after mowing; little effect unless an oil light on the dash or a 12-volt accessory cord plugged into an accessory port
- fix: recharge the battery

## page: causes/bad-battery
- confirm: after an hour or two on the charger it won't charge above 12 volts
- fix: replace; 12-volt lawn and garden batteries from Husqvarna dealer, auto store, home improvement store; bring old battery; core fee $15 to $25
- fact: new batteries usually fully charged but may need charging if shelf-sitting long; charge with the charger or jump-start
- fact: most batteries last at least 3 years with proper care; changing more often than 3 years suggests a connection or battery problem
- tip: battery care: keep cables clean; cables tight and battery held down securely; don't let it freeze, keep fully charged in cold
- fact: most batteries fail from vibration; plates break up inside; an uncharged battery can freeze and be damaged
- fact: size: at least 300 cold cranking amps if deck uses an electric clutch; if no electric clutch, a smaller one like 250 cca works; bigger is better; make sure it fits the holder

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1 (method one): disconnect cables (positive last off, first on); if battery has removable caps check water level; if plates not covered add distilled water (well water is not good; city water a little better)
- step 2: hook up the charger (red to positive, black to negative)
- step 3: check voltage; 11.8 volts charges briefly; 10.1 volts needs a long time; lower voltage = longer
- tip: author puts a very low battery on a "slow bake" at 2 or 4 amps for about an hour, checks voltage is rising, then another hour if so
- step method two: if reading isn't too low and you need to mow, charge at up to 6 amps for about half an hour to reach about 12 volts, remove charger, start mower, keep throttle 3/4 to full so it charges while running; check reading after mowing
- fact: link to its own battery charging times article

## page: procedures/jump-start-the-battery
- fact: both batteries must be 12 volts
- step 1: park the car next to the mower and shut off the car engine
- step 2: red positive clamp to the mower positive terminal, then to the car battery positive
- step 3: black negative clamp to car battery negative, then the other black clamp to the mower frame
- step 4: set the mower brake and start the mower; do not start the car
- step 5: let mower run a few minutes; remove cables in the opposite order of installation; alternator keeps the battery charged
- tip: check battery with a volt meter afterward
