## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: service a riding mower every 100 hours or annually, whichever comes first.
- fact: checklist: safety systems; battery; engine oil and filter; replace spark plug; replace fuel filter and inspect fuel lines; tire pressures and lug nuts; clean/replace air filter if necessary; remove dirt/debris from exhaust and engine areas; debris around transmissions; sharpen or change blades; scrape the deck; belts and pulleys condition; lubricate mower and deck; clean and wash mower.
- tools: engine oil and filter, air filter, fuel filter, spark plugs, socket or spanner wrenches, drain pan, funnel, filter wrench, rags, work gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover, deck scraper / wire brush / metal putty knife.
- safety: remove spark plug wires and key so the mower can't start while servicing/working on the deck.
- safety: always use jack stands or comparable lock-in-place supports to raise the mower for underside access.
- step 1: Check the safety systems. Engine should not start if the parking brake isn't engaged; engine should cut out if you leave the seat with the PTO engaged. Verify per operator's manual.
- step 2: Check the battery (12 V; 12.6 V or higher = 100% charged; charge if lower; cables tight; clean/replace corroded terminals).
- step 3: Change engine oil and filter (see change-the-engine-oil).
- step 4: Replace spark plug(s) annually; or inspect for excessive buildup, damage, cracked porcelain insulator.
- step 5: Replace fuel filter; check fuel lines for dry/brittle and replace old ones.
- step 6: Check lug nuts secure; tire pressures at sidewall level and equal.
- step 7: Inspect/clean or replace air filter.
- step 8: Remove engine shroud; clear dirt/debris around engine, exhaust, cooling fins, heat shields; also around the transmission.
- step 9: Check belts, pulleys, spindle housings.
- step 10: Sharpen or replace blades; balance blades after sharpening.
- step 11: Scrape the deck.
- step 12: Lubricate.
- step 13: Secure all hardware, clean and wash; dry completely before storage.
- pro: none stated.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: riding mower service interval: every 100 hours or annually, whichever first. New engine: change oil after the first 5 hours.
- fact: spark plug annually; fuel filter replaced at service; ethanol is corrosive, creates gummy deposits that clog filter/lines and breaks down plastic and rubber in the filter and lines.
- fact: tires at sidewall pressure, same on all; uneven pressure makes the deck sit unlevel and cut unevenly.
- fact: lube points with high-performance multi-purpose grease: idler arm pivots, grease zerks, pedal pivot points, deck spindles if serviceable, front wheels; check manual for all points.
- fact: deck clean: buildup under the deck compromises air movement for a good cut; blade and deck suction make grass stand tall; scrape with deck scraper, metal putty knife or stiff wire brush; deck wash port per manufacturer instructions (may still need scraping).
- fact: dry mower in the sun or with a handheld blower; helps evaporate moisture that harms electrical components.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: change oil and filter every 100 hours or annually; new engine after the initial 5 hours.
- step 1: Lay covering (cardboard/newspaper) on work surface.
- step 2: Warm the oil: run engine 2 to 3 minutes parked on the cover; careful of hot engine (warm oil flows better).
- step 3: Remove ignition key and unplug spark plug boot(s).
- step 4: Open hood, find oil drain plug on lower side of engine; clean around it; place drain pan, remove plug, remove oil fill cap so it flows better. Some mowers use a drain port with a hose attached; open port, attach hose, other end in pan, remove fill cap.
- step 5: Using a filter wrench, remove the oil filter and replace with a new one. After oil has drained, reinstall drain plug.
- step 6: Fill crankcase with the manufacturer's recommended quantity and type of oil; must not be too low or too high (both damage).
- step 7: Reinstall spark plug boots, start engine for a few minutes, shut off, let oil cool, recheck level, add if needed.
- tools: drain pan, filter wrench, funnel

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Disconnect battery cables; positive (red +) is last off and first on.
- step 2: If caps are removable, check water level; if plates aren't covered add distilled water (well water is not good; city water slightly better; distilled best).
- step 3: Hook charger to battery.
- step 4: Check voltage with the meter; 11.8 V charges quickly; 10.1 V needs a long time; lower reading = longer charge.
- tip: for a very low battery use a "slow bake": charger switch at 2 or 4 amps (some have 6/4/2 amps); charge about an hour, recheck that voltage rises; if so charge another hour.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- fact: use a voltmeter on a 12 V battery: 12.6 V or higher = 100% charged; lower, charge it.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: engine starved of clean air overheats.
- step 1: Remove filter carefully without knocking dirt into intake; wipe housing with dry clean cloth.
- step 2: Tap filter on a solid surface; do not blow with an air compressor.
- step 3: Hold to light; if light passes through the paper, keep using; if blocked or damaged, replace.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: replace at service; check lines; tip: use worm gear clamps instead of pinch-style clamps to avoid damaging the line.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- fact: replace annually, or inspect for buildup, damage, cracked porcelain insulator.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- fact: check pulley bearings: grab each pulley and slowly turn it; resistance or noise -> replace the pulley.
- fact: check spindle bearings: with work gloves grab both sides of the blade and rock up and down; any movement -> replace the bearing or spindle housing assembly.
- brand: some manufacturers only allow rebuilding a spindle housing, some only sell the whole assembly, some both.
- step 1: Remove belt off the bad pulley: put something narrow between belt and groove, carefully turn belt by hand, let the object follow to the pulley and pry the belt off.
- step 2: Remove the pulley: socket on the nut atop the pulley; usually normal thread so turn left/counterclockwise. Some mowers have allen or hex set screws on the shaft; may not come easily; may need a puller.
- step 3: Remove the spindle: 4 to 8 bolts hold it; remove them and the assembly drops out the bottom of the deck.
- step 4: Replace bearing: place housing on flat hard surface; sometimes a snap ring holds it, other times replace the whole housing with bearings. Replace bearing if assembly allows; grease it; reassemble in reverse.
- step 5: Bolt housing to deck, install pulley, make sure all is tight with no movement; reinstall belt; turn belt by hand to confirm it is on all pulleys.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: dirt and debris wear blades; dull blades strain the mower and give a poor cut; replace bent/damaged blades. Balance blades after sharpening; unbalanced = deck vibration and uneven cut.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: worn/cracked/glazed belts should be replaced; belts and pulleys affect hydraulic pump and blade function; pulley bearings and spindle bearings wear.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: scrape deck, clean engine area, wash mower and dry completely.
