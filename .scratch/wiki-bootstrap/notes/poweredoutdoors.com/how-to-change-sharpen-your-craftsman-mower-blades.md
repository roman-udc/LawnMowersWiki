## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Change Craftsman blades at least once a year, more often with sand, gravel or rocks in the yard, large yard, frequent mowing, or damage. Replace with new blades or sharpened ones if still in good condition.
- fact: Sharpen after every 25 hours of mowing (about twice a year for the average homeowner).
- tools (removal): ratchet set, work gloves, safety glasses, block of wood, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional)
- safety: Remove the spark plug boot (and ignition key) before removing blades.
- step 1 (remove): Place a block of wood between deck and blade so the blade won't turn; remove the hex flange nut at the blade center. If stubborn, put a block of wood on the bolt head and hit it with a hammer.
- step 2 (reinstall): Sail (high side/fin) points up; some blades are marked "Bottom". Install and secure with blade bolt; tighten to 75-90 ft. lbs. with a torque wrench; do not over-tighten (can damage the spindle).
- step 3 (inspect): Sail thinner than the center = worn, replace. Small chips/dings sharpen out; large gouges replace. Bent blade must be replaced: compare to a new blade, or on a level surface measure ground to blade end, rotate 180 degrees, measure the other end; more than 1/8 inch difference = bent. Cracked blade: dangerous, can break off and throw metal (injury or siding damage).
- mistake: Do not straighten and reuse a bent blade; compromises the metal.
- tools (sharpening): 10 inch flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; eye protection and work gloves
- step 4 (sharpen): Clean debris with rag/wire brush. Hold blade in vise; sharpen one cutting edge, rotate 180 degrees, sharpen the other. Remove equal metal from each edge so it stays balanced.
- step 4a (file): push file at 25 to 30 degree angle in one direction along the blade, no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges, then flip and repeat.
- step 4b (drill sharpener): flat guide along flat of the blade with cutting edge slotted into the bevel; move up and down the edge; repeat other side.
- step 4c (angle/bench grinder): hold perpendicular to the blade edge; run along its length to level nicks; keep it moving, removing a little metal at a time; flip and repeat.
- step 5 (balance): use a blade balancer, or hang the blade by its center hole on a nail on the wall; if one end hangs lower, sharpen that end until level (less accurate than a balancer).
- pro: Shops sharpen blades; benefits: safer, often cheaper than buying a blade, can grind out large nicks and gouges, proper balance (reduces vibration, damage, injury). Author keeps a spare set so he is not without the mower; leaving blades takes a couple days.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: (problems/uneven-cut) Grass tips turn brown a day or two after mowing = dull blades bruising grass instead of a clean cut.
- fact: Bent blade (from toys, rocks, other objects) causes uneven cut, spindle damage, or vibration.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: Deck and blade create suction that stands grass up; sail thinner than center = worn; replace.
