## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Change zero-turn blades once a year; inspect regularly, change more often if damaged/worn.
- fact: Sharpen after every 25 hours of use (about twice a year for average homeowner); dry dusty conditions or heavy use means more often.
- fact: New blades need no sharpening (sharpened in manufacturing; paint coating wears off edge with use).
- tools: remove: ratchet/socket, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional). Sharpen: 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; gloves.
- safety: Remove spark plug boots and ignition key, set parking brake; safety glasses and gloves.
- step 1: Remove spark plug wires/boots and key, set parking brake.
- step 2: Remove bolt with socket and ratchet; use impact gun if stubborn, or put a block of wood over bolt head and hit with hammer to loosen.
- step 3: Remove bolt, blade and any washers; reinstall in opposite order. Reused blades must be sharpened and balanced first.
- step 4: Clean blade with rag, wire brush for buildup; secure in vise.
- step 5 (file): push file at an angle in one direction along blade; no sawing motion; remove rough spots; flip, re-secure, do other side.
- step 5 (drill sharpener): flat guide on flat part of blade with cutting edge in bevel slot; move up and down length of edge; flip and repeat.
- step 5 (grinder): hold grinder perpendicular to blade edge, run along length to level nicks; keep moving to remove a little metal at a time; flip and repeat.
- step 6: Balance blade (see below).
- mistake: Straightening a bent blade; weakens metal, piece may break off and fly.
- tip: Author keeps a spare set of blades, has dealer sharpen (takes a day or two). Reasons: safer, cheaper than new blade, pro can grind out large nicks/gouges, blade balanced properly (less vibration and deck damage). Opinion.
- tip: Lawn chemicals can corrode deck components and fuse blades to deck; remove blades periodically so they don't form a tight seal.
- fact: Balance: blades with unequal weight make mower shake. Use blade balancer, or a nail on wall (head ~1 inch from wall); hang blade by center hole; remove metal from the side that hangs lower until level. Shave metal off the heavy side.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- step 1 (off deck): lay used blade on top of a new blade; if it doesn't lie flat, it's bent, replace.
- step 2 (on deck): park on hard level surface; measure ground to blade tip, rotate blade 180 degrees, measure again; more than 1/8" difference = replace blade.
- fact: Blade sail is the raised part that creates air movement under deck; with deck shell and baffles creates suction to lift grass. Sail worn when thinner than the blade's center. Worn sail or rounded/thin blade tips (leave a strip of grass between tips) = replace.
- fact: Small chips removed by sharpening; large chips/gouges = replace. Cracked blade = replace (can break off, injure people, damage house siding).

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: uneven, torn, bruised cut; grass with brown tips a day or two after mowing = dull blade; engine works harder.
- confirm: see measure-a-mower-blade above (1/8" test, lay against new blade).
- fix: sharpen dull blade in good condition; replace bent, cracked, badly chipped blades. Never straighten.
- fact: Sticks, rocks, toys bend/gouge blade; sand/debris (sparse lawns pull sand into deck) dulls blades faster.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: see sail definition above; worn sail or blade tips require new blade.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: After significant impact, grab both blade ends (gloved) and rock up/down; movement or knocking = remove spindle housings and inspect.
- fix: May be able to replace spindle bearing; some manufacturers only allow full spindle assembly replacement.
- symptom: vibration after blade impact.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- fact: Worn or damaged blades give bad, uneven cut. Source links "13 Reasons Your Zero Turn Mower is Cutting Uneven".
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, worn-blade-sail

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Annual service of zero-turn: change fluids and filters, check components for wear/damage. Also regularly check engine oil level, lubricate, clean air filter, remove debris.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades: replace yearly; sharpen every 25 hours.
