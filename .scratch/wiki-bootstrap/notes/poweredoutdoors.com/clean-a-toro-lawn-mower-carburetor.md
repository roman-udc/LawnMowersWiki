## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- symptom: dirty carburetor symptoms: engine won't start; backfires from running lean; sputters and runs rough; surges; consumes too much fuel.
- confirm: Once fuel is verified reaching the carburetor, remove the air filter from its housing, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, and start the mower. If it starts, runs, then shuts down, disassemble and clean the carburetor.
- tools: pliers, screwdriver, socket/ratchet set, carburetor cleaner, thick wire, needle nose pliers
- tip: Take photos before and during teardown so you know where parts go.
- step 1: Take photos.
- step 2: Shut off fuel: use the fuel shut-off valve on the bottom of the fuel tank; if none, crimp the fuel line.
- step 3: Detach throttle and choke cables from the carburetor.
- step 4: Remove hardware attaching the carburetor to the air filter housing.
- step 5: Slowly remove the springs; don't stretch them or they must be replaced. May need to twist the carburetor a bit. Don't rip the gasket between engine block and carburetor or it must be replaced.
- step 6: Remove the bowl (holds a little fuel after the tank). Keep a rag for leftover fuel. Remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and lower the bowl. A gasket (looks like a rubber band) sits between bowl and carburetor.
- safety: Don't get carburetor cleaner or other substances on the bowl gasket; it will be ruined and must be replaced before reattaching the bowl.
- step 7: Check holes in the stem that hangs down in the center of the carburetor; old fuel gum can clog them. Use a flashlight and a thick wire to unclog.
- step 8: Check other components and body for gumming and white crusty buildup; remove gummy deposits and as much crust as possible with carburetor cleaner and a clean cloth. Almost impossible to remove all the crust.
- step 9: Confirm components (float needle and float included) are in good order; reassemble in reverse order using the photos.
- fix: If cleaning fails, rebuild or replace the carburetor.
- brand: Toro uses other manufacturers' small engines; have engine model and spec on hand to order the right part(s). Replacement carb or rebuild kit from online, a Toro dealer, or an authorized engine dealer.
- tip: Refill with good fresh gasoline plus a fuel additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (author's choice) to stabilize fuel, reduce moisture and clean the fuel system.
- safety: Follow operator's manual; consult a professional if not skilled.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old fuel sitting in the carburetor gums its components, preventing sufficient fuel reaching the engine; mower runs sluggish or doesn't start.
- symptom: engine-runs-rough, engine-cranks-but-wont-start. Also backfire (lean), surging, high fuel consumption.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: Cleaning sometimes doesn't restore function; small parts may need replacing or old fuel damage may require replacement. Rebuild kit or replacement available (see above).

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Add a stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (author uses it) to stabilize fuel, reduce moisture and clean the fuel system.
