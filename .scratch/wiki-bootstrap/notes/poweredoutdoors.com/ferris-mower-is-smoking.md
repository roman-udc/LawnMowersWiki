## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Ferris mower smoking"
- alias: "black / blue / white smoke from my Ferris"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, spilled-oil-on-engine, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train
- safety: stop operating immediately when smoke appears; continuing can worsen damage. Before diagnosing, wait for the engine to cool and remove the spark plug wire; follow the Ferris operator's manual.
- fact: smoke colour: blue or white = burning excess oil (damaged piston rings, valve train, engine gasket leak, or oil pushed into the cylinder by a plugged air filter). Black = excess fuel burning, from a plugged filter giving a fuel-rich mixture; if the filter is not the cause look for another air restriction.
- confirm: internal damage cannot be pinpointed without teardown, leak-down and compression checks; one simple check: remove the spark plug(s) and look for oil, which indicates a piston ring or valve train problem but not which.
- pro: internal engine problems (piston ring, valve train, burnt valve) need a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dusty, dry conditions with sparse lawn plug filters if not cleaned/replaced. Plugged filter restricts air, engine runs rich and emits black smoke. If so plugged it restricts all air, the engine draws air from the crankcase and pulls oil that burns in the cylinder.
- mistake: never run the mower without an air filter, even briefly; dirt reaches the cylinder and engine seals through the intake.
- tip: author starts each mowing season with a new filter and cleans it several times during the season; checking takes a couple of minutes before mowing.
- fix: replace when extremely dirty, oil-covered or damaged.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove the filter from the air filter housing.
- step 2: wipe remaining dirt out of the housing with a dry clean cloth; keep dirt from falling into the air intake.
- step 3: tap the paper element against a solid surface to knock out loose dirt.
- step 4: hold it to a light source; if light shows through the paper, reuse it; if not, install a new filter.
- step 5: reinstall the filter and cover.
- fact: this is for a paper element.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: crankcase holds a specific amount; fill only to the "full line" on the dipstick.
- fact: low oil gives not enough lubrication, friction, heat; oil can burn and engine parts melt, producing smoke. Adding oil can be attempted, but damage is probably already done; use an experienced small engine mechanic.
- tip: check oil level before each mowing.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil raises crankcase pressure and pushes oil into the cylinder and into the air intake through the valve train; oil in the cylinder burns off and smokes.
- fix: remove a little oil through the drain plug or oil filter, or use an oil evacuator or turkey baster to suck oil out of the fill area. Recheck with the dipstick, add/remove until at "full". Check the air filter is not oil-covered or plugged from smoke; install a new one if so.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: remove a little oil via the drain plug or oil filter, or evacuate through the fill area with an oil evacuator or turkey baster.
- step 2: recheck with the dipstick; repeat until at "full".
- step 3: check the air filter for oil/plugging; replace if so.
- tools: oil evacuator or turkey baster

## page: causes/spilled-oil-on-engine
- fact: a bad gasket leaks oil onto a hot surface like the muffler where it burns and smokes.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: needs engine teardown; a piston ring problem or scoring in the cylinder wall lets oil into the combustion chamber and burn.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- fact: an overheated Ferris can develop a burnt valve; only determined by removing the cylinder head and doing a leak-down test; repair by a mechanic who must cut the seat and valve correctly to complete the combustion chamber.
