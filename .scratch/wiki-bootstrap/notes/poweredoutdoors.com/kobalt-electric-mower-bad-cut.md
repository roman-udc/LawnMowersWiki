## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Kobalt electric mower bad or uneven cut"
- alias: "lawn turns brown a day or two after mowing"
- causes-named: bad-tire, dull-or-bent-blade, worn-blade-sail, blade-installed-upside-down, bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade, wrong-cutting-height, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, mowing-too-fast
- safety: shut off the mower, remove the battery, wait for all parts to stop moving before troubleshooting; park on a flat level surface and remove battery pack(s) before working under the deck.
- fact: item 9 is an uneven lawn (ruts, bumps): the mower won't fix it; author rolls his lawn each spring after snow melts because rodents tunnel; otherwise bad cut and mower bounces. (Lawn-care note, minor.)

## page: causes/bad-tire
- symptom: damaged or loose wheel makes the mower sit unlevel, deck closer to the ground on one side, uneven cut.
- fix: inspect wheels; tighten loose hardware; replace damaged wheels. Check this first.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom (dull/worn): blade tears the grass; lawn tips turn brown a day or two after mowing; grab a handful and look for little tears in the tips.
- fix: sharpen with a file if still in good condition; replace if extremely worn, gouged, rounded ends, or blade sail thinner than the center of the blade.
- symptom (bent): hit rock, stump, toy; uneven cut and vibration; sometimes not obvious.
- confirm: remove the blade and lay it on top of a new Kobalt blade; even a very slight bend causes a bad cut.
- mistake: NEVER try to straighten a bent blade to reuse it; metal is weakened and the weak area can break off and fly out of the deck at high speed.
- tip: walk the lawn first to clear objects.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: the sail is the high side of the blade that creates airflow under the deck for a nice cut; replace the blade if the sail is thinner than the center.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- tools: file, blade balancer (inexpensive), or nail on a wall.
- step 1: remove battery pack(s); wait for moving parts to stop.
- step 2: remove the blade; sharpen with a file if still in good condition.
- step 3: balance: equal weight on both ends. Blades become unbalanced from uneven wear by sand pulled under the deck, or from removing more metal from one end when sharpening.
- step 4: check with a balancer, or hang the blade on a nail sticking out 3/4 in to 1 in from the wall; remove metal from the end hanging lower until the blade sits level.
- fact: an unbalanced blade may cause significant damage to the mower.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- fact: compare a suspect blade laid on a new blade of the same model to detect bend.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: blade must have the sail up (sails are the high sides that move air and toss grass); sail facing down damages the blade motor, beats and tears grass, can hit high spots, impacting blade and potentially damaging the motor.
- tip: Kobalt blades may be stamped to show which side faces up and which faces the ground.

## page: causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade
- fact: if the blade is not secure it will fail to rotate and mower won't cut. Refer to operator's manual for blade bolt torque spec and tighten.

## page: causes/wrong-cutting-height
- fact: deck too low can't handle the grass volume; raise deck for thick tall grass to avoid overworking the motor.
- tip: double or triple cut: highest setting first, lower for each additional cut.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: plugged deck leaves insufficient area for blade airflow/suction (deck and blade work together to lift grass); also wears the motor.
- fix: regularly clean with a deck scraper or wire brush; avoid wet grass (clumps).

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- symptom: mower pushes grass over instead of cutting; overloaded motor can shut off.
- fix: slow down in thick, tall or wet grass.
