## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Winterizing a riding mower: change engine oil, remove dirt and debris, stabilize fuel, fully charge the battery, store in a cool dry place. Reduce moisture in the fuel system to prevent gumming/blockages; remove debris to prevent corrosion.
- tools: socket/spanner wrenches, filter wrench; engine oil filter; engine oil; spark plugs; inner and outer air filter; fuel filter; rubber gloves, work gloves, safety glasses; plastic funnel; rags/paper towels; deck scraper or wire brush; mower blades; fuel stabilizer.
- step 1: Park on a flat level surface; cardboard/newspaper under to catch drips; set the parking brake. Engine can be hot, blades sharp.
- step 2: Oil change - run a few minutes to warm oil, shut off, remove key and spark plug wire. Clean around fill area, drain plug and filter; drain pan under plug or hose; remove drain plug (remove dipstick to drain faster); reinstall plug when drained; remove filter with a filter wrench, coat the new filter seal lightly with fresh oil, spin on; fill to the full mark on the dipstick without overfilling (remove a little if overfilled); wipe spills; recycle used oil at a recycling center or automotive store.
- step 3: Replace spark plug(s) and check the gap; don't connect plug wires until service is done.
- step 4: Clean dirt/debris from cooling fins and engine, and around the transmission and fans.
- step 5: Clean battery terminals if corroded: baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda) and a wire brush; make sure connections are good; thin coat of dielectric grease on terminals and electrical components reduces corrosion.
- step 6: Fully charge the battery or remove it. Author stores with a fully charged battery (a fully charged battery will not freeze). If removed, store in a cool dry place away from combustibles (avoids freezing when not charged properly).
- step 7: Remove debris around wiring and electrical components to prevent corrosion/damage.
- step 8: Air filter: author replaces yearly; more often in extremely dusty conditions or heavy use; clean in between: remove carefully, wipe housing, tap, hold to light (reuse if light shows), refit cover.
- step 9: Replace the fuel filter; plastic inline filter arrow points in the direction of fuel flow.
- step 10: Check the fuel system for leaks (carburetor, fuel pump, fuel lines, fuel valve) so fumes don't fill the storage area.
- step 11: Tire pressure: fill to sidewall pressure; may go a couple of psi higher since tires lose pressure in cold. Underinflated tires in long storage develop flat spots that cause vibration; often work out after use, sometimes the tire must be replaced.
- step 12: Remove the deck for access (see owner's manual). Remove belt covers; clean the top of the deck, including debris under pulleys and brackets.
- step 13: Belt/pulleys: replace a worn, stretched or shredded belt; if shredded, check brackets and belt keepers for what it touched. Clean rust off pulleys or replace if excessively rusty. Turn each pulley slowly by hand; resistance or bearing noise means replace.
- step 14: Underside: before removing blades check spindle bearings: grab each end of the blade and rock up and down; movement or noise means remove the spindle housing and repair. Author replaces blades with a new set each season; if in good condition with a dull edge, sharpen, balance and reinstall. Never reuse a bent, sail-worn or gouged blade. Scrape the deck.
- step 15: Reinstall the deck in reverse; check deck level side-to-side and rake front-to-rear per owner's manual.
- step 16: Grease all zerks on deck and mower with high-performance grease (rims, front axle, deck wheels, tensioner brackets may have zerks); lubricate cables and linkages with spray lubricant.
- step 17: Fuel: either drain (remove fuel; run the mower until it stops) or stabilize (add stabilizer; author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment; run a few minutes to work it through). Author chooses to stabilize.
- step 18: Wash with a garden hose; if using a pressure washer, not high pressure (can damage sealed bearings). Dry completely (leaf blower speeds up; author leaves it in the sun) since moisture causes rust and corrosion.
- step 19: Store in a dry garage or shed, out of rain and snow, away from flammable sources.
- step 20: Rodent prevention: mice nest under the hood and chew through the wire harness; author uses Grandpa Gus's Mouse Repellent.
- fact: Storing outside (if no indoor option): keep it elevated off the ground, use a marine-grade cover; outdoor mowers attract rodents (dealer customers brought mowers with wire harness and filter housing damage); use a rodent deterrent (author uses Grandpa Gus Rodent Deterrent packets around the mower and spray on wires).
- safety: Not safe to store in the house or basement: harmful flammable fumes. Keep away from wood stoves, water heaters, ranges, clothes dryers.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Two options for storage: add fuel stabilizer (author: Sea Foam; reduces moisture buildup, run a few minutes) or drain the tank and run until the engine stops.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: For storage either remove battery or fully charge it; a fully charged battery will not freeze; store removed battery cool, dry, away from combustibles. Clean corrosion with baking soda solution; dielectric grease.

## page: causes/bad-tire
- fact: Flat spots from long storage with underinflated tires cause vibration; sometimes uncorrectable so tire must be replaced.
