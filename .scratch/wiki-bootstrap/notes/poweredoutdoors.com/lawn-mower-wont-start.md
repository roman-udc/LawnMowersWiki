## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "lawn mower won't start"
- alias: "push mower won't start" / "self-propelled mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, fuel-valve-off, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, wrong-choke-setting, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter, bad-safety-switch, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: causes: bad fuel, dirty plug, plugged air filter, clogged fuel system, dirty carb, clogged fuel cap, bad safety switch; electric start adds bad battery, loose/corroded wires, bad solenoid.
- confirm: engine needs fuel, air, spark. Before diagnosing verify: (1) fuel shut-off valve is open; (2) enough fresh fuel; (3) parking brake engaged or bail lever engaged; (4) PTO switch off (riding, tractor, zero turn); (5) choke closed/on to start a cold engine.
- confirm (fuel test): remove the air filter cover and filter; spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake (author uses carb cleaner rather than starter fluid); try to start (recoil or key). If it attempts to start or starts, the fuel system is at fault (most common: dirty carb, clogged fuel filter, clogged lines; pump if fitted). If it does not attempt to start, most likely spark plug problem.
- confirm (air test): remove air filter and inspect; clean/replace if very dirty or damaged. With the filter out inspect the choke plate: manual choke - lever ON = plate closed, OFF = plate open (on some models off means fast throttle); auto choke (newer push mowers, thermostat) - closed when cold, opens as it warms. If it isn't opening/closing check choke linkage and cable; free a stuck choke by lubricating linkages and choke shaft with carb cleaner; replace a bad choke cable.
- confirm (spark test): remove the boot from the plug; attach a spark plug tester to the ignition wire; other end to the plug (still installed); pull cord or electric start; glow in the transparent section = spark. No glow: bad plug or bad ignition coil (armature); on electric start also battery, wiring, starter solenoid.
- tools: spark plug tester, carburetor cleaner, compressed air, multimeter.
- pro: if unsure of mechanical skills take to a professional.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- fact: valve is often closed for storage or transport; make sure it is open.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with gasoline of octane 87 or higher and ethanol no greater than 10%.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol and moisture leave sticky substance that clogs lines, filter and carb. Add a stabilizer if you can't use fuel within 30 days (to tank and to stored gas).
- fix: remove old fuel, flush tank, add fresh fuel with cleaner/stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: run the mower for a while with the cap off and then on.
- fix: use a thin piece of wire to clear the clogged vent (table); replace the cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: carbon buildup (clean or replace if tip very dark), broken porcelain insulator or burnt electrode (replace). Start the season with a new plug. Some engines use 2 plugs. Check gap and wire connection.
- fix: inspect, replace, gap to manufacturer spec, secure wires.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- mistake: never run without an air filter even briefly.
- step (paper): tap out dirt; hold to light; reuse if light passes else replace; reinstall with cover.
- step (foam): if dark spots or dry/brittle replace; else wash in mild dish soap and water, rinse, lay flat to dry (sun speeds it); then lightly saturate with foam filter oil (not dripping); install.
- tip: remove carefully so dirt doesn't fall in the intake; wipe the housing with a clean cloth.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: paper and foam steps as above.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: may be inline between the fuel lines, or inserted in the bottom of the fuel tank. Replace if plugged.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: remove line, carb cleaner, compressed air, repeat; replace if clog stays.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: ethanol and additives form gummy substances that clog small carb parts.
- fix: DIY if mechanical, or shop; replace if in very bad condition; a small engine shop can clean or rebuild.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fix: tighten cables and terminals; clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda); test with a multimeter; replace a bad battery.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: key turned and nothing happens or feels wrong; test with a multimeter; replace.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: manual-start push mowers use a recoil; rope may come off the pulley (restring) or a pulley, spring or clips may break.
- tip: price the individual parts vs the whole recoil assembly; may be cheaper to replace the assembly.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on turning key; a wire on the solenoid hot, smoking or melting (problems/engine-wont-crank).
- fact: electromagnetic switch actuating the starter motor.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: kills engine when operator absent (e.g. letting go of bail lever); faulty switch may not recognize the bail lever. Other switch types per manual.
- confirm: temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: never operate without the safety switch installed and working.
- fix: replace bad switch.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: safety features prevent starting unless the procedure is followed (bail lever, choke etc.); varies by mower; refer to manual.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: see choke plate checks above; lubricate/replace choke cable.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: no spark at the tester with a good plug points to bad plug or coil (armature).
