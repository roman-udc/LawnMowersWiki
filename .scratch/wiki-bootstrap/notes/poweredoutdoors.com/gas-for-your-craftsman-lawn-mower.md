## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: most Craftsman mowers have four-stroke (4-cycle) engines using unleaded gasoline, octane 87 or higher, ethanol no greater than 10%.
- brand: many Craftsman push mowers with 2-cycle engines are still in use and need a gas and oil mix (separate article linked).
- fact: do not use fuel with ethanol over 10%; look for the sticker on the pump (typically 10 to 15 percent); choose unleaded marketed at 10% or less (E10).
- fact: never use E85 (85% ethanol); it damages a small engine.
- fact: why ethanol is bad: attracts moisture from air so gas separates from ethanol; gas sinks to the bottom of the tank; separated ethanol can cause ignition problems; ethanol is corrosive and very high levels burn very hot (can overheat the engine and damage it); evaporating moisture leaves deposits that clog the fuel system.
- fact: small engine manufacturers recommend ethanol no greater than 10%; sufficient if gas is always fresh (within 30 days) and fuel system and carb are kept clean.
- fact: gas stays fresh about 30 days before it breaks down; buy only what can be used within 30 days.
- tip: buy gas at a busy station (high turnover) rather than a quiet one.
- opinion: author likes premium unleaded or recreation fuel (REC-90); less ethanol means fewer carburetor deposits. Premium is at most stations; REC fuel harder to find, offered at many larger stations.
- tip: don't store fuel outside; it collects moisture.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: use a plastic gas can designed for gas (many plastics break down once gas is added).
- tip: add a fuel stabilizer to the can per package directions; author likes Sea Foam: stabilizes, cleans the fuel system, reduces moisture buildup.
- tip: rotate gas, using the old fuel first.
- fact: only buy quantities usable within 30 days.
- tip: refer to the Craftsman owner's manual.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: using ethanol above 10%, E85, or gas/oil mix mismatches (4-cycle vs 2-cycle) damages the engine; see guide above.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down after about 30 days and becomes less effective.
