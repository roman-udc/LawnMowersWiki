## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with fresh gasoline, octane 87 or higher, ethanol no greater than 10%. An older push mower with a 2-cycle engine needs a fuel and oil mixture.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas breaks down after about 30 days; ethanol attracts moisture, and when moisture evaporates it leaves gummy residue that clogs lines, filters and carburetor.
- fix: Remove old fuel (siphon pump); mix a fuel additive such as Sea Foam into fresh gas; start the mower and let the mix run through. Sea Foam stabilizes, cleans the fuel system and reduces moisture.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Cold engine will not start unless choke on/closed; warm engine will not start unless choke off/open.
- fix: Engage choke for cold start, then move to off once the engine warms. If correct and still airflow problems, check for plugged air filter, stuck choke or worn choke cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Blocked air makes the engine run hot and search for air including from the crankcase; can also cause overheating, smoking, engine damage.
- fix: Paper: remove filter without letting dirt into the intake; wipe housing; tap against your hand or a solid surface; hold to light and check light shows through every area; replace if no light, damaged/torn or no longer seals; install.
- fix: Foam pre-filter (used with a paper filter): replace if dark spots, brittle or torn; wash in water and mild detergent; rinse out detergent; squeeze dry (do not wring, avoid tearing); lay flat until completely dry; install.
- mistake: Never add oil to a pre-filter; it damages the paper air filter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Mower has a pump when the tank is lower than the carburetor. Three ports: inlet, outlet, and a port connected to a crankcase line that pressurizes the pump.
- confirm: Visual check for small cracks or leaking fuel; replace if seen.
- confirm: Fuel to pump: turn off the fuel valve or clamp (not every mower has a valve); disconnect the line at the pump inlet into a container below the tank; restore flow; confirm fuel flows; if not, check lines and filter for blockage.
- confirm: Pump output: reconnect the inlet line; remove the line from the carburetor into a container; start the mower; expect steady or pulsating flow.
- fix: Replace the pump if it cannot consistently pump fuel from the outlet.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace a plugged filter (old fuel gummy deposits plus dirty fuel plug it).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner (loosens) and blow compressed air (dislodges); repeat; replace if you cannot clear it.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Without the right gas/air mix the mower may run rough and not start.
- fix: Most often cleaning works; damaged parts can be replaced with a carburetor rebuild kit; replace the carburetor if cleaning does not work.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Battery must read 12.7 volts or greater; loose cables and corroded terminals add to starting trouble.
- fix: Test with a multimeter; charge if under 12.7 V; replace if it will not hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Clean corroded terminals with a baking soda solution of 2 cups water and 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda, scrub with a wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: White mowers use several safety switches; faulty ones prevent starting.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: Do not operate a mower without safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: Inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, burnt electrode.
- fix: Replace if any; gap per manufacturer; attach wire securely. A loose wire or wrong gap causes starting or intermittent running problems.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: Key turned and mower does not start or turn over.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Push mowers pull-start by recoil; bad pulley, broken spring or broken clips are likely.
- fix: Try replacing the spring and restringing; if clips or pulley are damaged, replace the recoil assembly.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- confirm: After verifying the plug is good, check coil continuity with a multimeter; replace if broken.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: Click or hum when turning the key; or a wire on the solenoid gets hot and smokes or melts.
- fact: Electromagnetic on-off switch actuating the starter motor. Replace if found bad (Source links test steps).

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Not the main cause of no-start, but fails to charge the battery so it is too weak next time. Bad stator or alternator and other electrical parts possible (Source links test using an ohm meter).
- pro: Have a small engine mechanic identify the actual cause; otherwise guessing gets expensive.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Push mowers use a safety bar to recognize the operator. Riding mowers use a seat switch to confirm the operator is on, and a brake switch that can fail so the mower will not start unless the brake is engaged.
- fix: Follow the White Outdoors operating manual for the model.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Plugged vent forms a vacuum; the mower acts fuel-starved.
- fix: Replace the cap.
