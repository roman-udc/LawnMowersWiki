## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Cub Cadet uses unleaded gasoline, octane 87 or higher, no more than 10% ethanol. Use within 30 days or add a fuel stabilizer.
- fact: Regular gas can be used if ethanol is not above 10%; regular generally has an octane rating of 87 (cites US Energy Information Administration).
- fact: Gas shelf life about 30 days before it degrades. Buy from a busy station; store unused fuel in a dry location to avoid moisture or water in the can.
- fact: Check decal on the fuel pump for ethanol content; typically 10-15% (E10 or E15). Avoid more than 10%.
- fact: Ethanol: corn-based; fine for most vehicles but damaging to small gas engines; attracts moisture from air, which makes the gas separate from ethanol; separated ethanol and water sink to the bottom of the tank and are fed through the engine causing ignition problems; leaves gummy deposits damaging fuel components; corrosive and burns very hot, can overheat engine.
- tip: Higher-quality/Top-Tier fuel if affordable. Ethanol-free fuel is best but pricey; look for "recreation fuel" at some stations or 4-cycle fuel cans at hardware stores. Stabilizer not needed in those but harmless.
- opinion: Expensive fuel is not always better.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Use a quality plastic gas can designed for unleaded gas (some plastic containers break down).
- tip: Add fuel stabilizer to the can with the extra gas; author likes Sea Foam Engine Treatment.
- tip: Avoid buying more than a 30-day supply.
- tip: Empty the can you're using before adding new gas; use stored gas first; don't add fresh gas to old.
- tip: At season end discard leftover gas appropriately (can go into car or other machines); never leave a can sitting for months.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Wrong fuel (octane under 87, ethanol over 10%, old gas) can hurt the mower; old gas may cause stopping or hard starting.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas degrades after about 30 days.
