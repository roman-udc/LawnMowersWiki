Note: Source does not name specific Toro mower types; covers both drive belts and deck belts (mower fails to move or fail to cut when a belt comes off). Mentions transmission belt.

## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "Toro belt keeps coming off"
- alias: "belt keeps breaking / snapping"
- causes-named: belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-or-stretched-belt, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: Fix summary: adjust belt guides and brackets; remove debris from pulleys and tensioners; replace damaged brackets, bearings, bushings; replace loose, oil-covered or cracked belts. 8 reasons.
- fact: if a belt comes off, mower fails to move (drive belt) or cut (deck belt).
- safety: remove the spark plug before working.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: belt guides (keepers) are small rod-shaped posts around the pulleys; on some mowers travel alongside the belt.
- symptom: guides get bent or moved when a belt snaps or has a lot of slack; slack belt hits guide and bends it back toward pulleys, sometimes making the belt jump off.
- fact: guides should never touch the belt; stay 1/8" to 1/4" from the pulley.
- fix: adjust brackets by tapping with a small hammer or using vice grips to move back into place.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: loose belt = time to replace.
- tip: replace mower belts at the same time (if one is worn, the other is probably due).
- fact: shredded belt (deck or drive): most likely rubbing on a bracket or belt guide, or not tracking correctly. A shiny spot on a bracket or guide shows rubbing. Adjust so it no longer rubs; check bracket bushings (some replaceable, others mean replacing the whole bracket).
- fact: belt cost $20 to $200 (author's range). Author recommends OEM belt from the mower manufacturer; cheaper or auto-parts-store matched belts rarely last as long or perform as well. Belts: drive belts and deck belts are the most common. Link to v-belts and belt life articles.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: pulley not running flat/straight = bad bearing; most often in an idler pulley, which has a sealed bearing so replace the whole pulley. Not every pulley is sealed; drive pulleys or spindle housings may have replaceable bearings.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: grab the blades and rock back and forth; movement = bearings in cutter housing (or whole cutter housing) need replacement. Noisy, excessively vibrating Toro spindles can throw the belt off the deck.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: rocks, wood chips, dried mud caked in pulley grooves; belt can't stretch around the extra debris; the stress breaks it.
- fact: belts don't always break from age; many new belts snap because foreign material is in the pulleys.
- fact: dry grass and debris on deck/under the mower keeps the tension pulley from moving, so belt can't stay tight. Keep area under belt covers and under mower body clean; cleaner mower makes failing parts easier to find.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: idler bracket normally has a spring on one side, pulley on the other. Worn spring-attachment hole or worn spring = constant vibration. Bushing on idler bracket wears if not greased -> pulley runs on an angle; can cut into deck or (for transmission belt) the underside of the mower; vibration and angle throw the belt.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: oil leak collects on belt; petroleum swells belt left in oil; oily belt slips on pulleys, can't grab, builds heat. Rust dries the belt and cracks it; cracked belts should be replaced ASAP. Pulleys need to be clean of oil and rust.

## page: guides/belt-types
- fact: drive belts and deck belts are the two most common mower belts; cost $20-$200; OEM recommended.
