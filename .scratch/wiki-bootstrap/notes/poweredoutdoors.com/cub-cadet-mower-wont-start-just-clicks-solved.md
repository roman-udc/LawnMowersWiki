## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Cub Cadet mower clicks but won't start or turn over"
- alias: "all I hear is click, click, click when I turn the key"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, faulty-charging-system
- fact: clicks but won't turn over when battery weak; cables/wiring loose or corroded; ground bad; solenoid faulty; or starter motor failed. (Article links a separate "no click" article for the silent case.)
- safety: always disconnect the negative (black) cable from the battery before electrical repairs; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: cables/wiring battery->solenoid and solenoid->starter securely attached, in good condition, uncorroded; terminals free of corrosion with good contact (excellent conductivity needed).
- fix: remove corrosion; replace cables/wiring that keep coming loose or are broken. Disconnect battery and clean terminals with wire brush and baking soda mix: 2 cups water + 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda. Repair/replace corroded wiring. Replace damaged terminals.
- tip: apply dielectric grease to protect terminals and wiring from corrosion.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: weak battery doesn't give enough power to turn over. Check voltage; trickle charge if needed; replace if it won't hold a charge.
- confirm: multimeter red/black to matching battery terminals. Common riding/zero-turn battery is 12 V. Reading between 11.5 and 12.7 V: 11.5 V = almost dead; 12.7 V = fully charged.
- fact: if battery charges but keeps dying, check the charging system.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step 1: Touch multimeter red and black probes to matching colored battery terminals.
- step 2: Read: 11.5 V almost dead; 12.7 V fully charged (12 V battery).

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear safety gear for eyes and skin (acid, electrical shock); skin should touch only the rubber coating on charger cables/clamps.
- step 1: Get access to battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover body).
- step 2: Leave battery in its casing with terminal cables attached.
- step 3: Connect charger cables red (positive) first: red clamp to positive terminal, black clamp to negative terminal.
- step 4: Set charger voltage (usually 12 V) and amps; more amps charges faster; start at 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps.
- step 5: If the charger has a charge gauge, leave connected until fully charged.
- fix: replace the battery if it no longer holds a charge; a battery that charges but is repeatedly dead points to the charging system.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: check the black ground cable from battery to the Cub Cadet frame for good contact and no corrosion; also the ground from the solenoid. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: replace a damaged ground cable; remove corrosion from battery and starter solenoid grounds.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: mower keeps clicking (problems/engine-wont-crank).
- fact: solenoid is an electromagnetic on-off switch that engages the starter motor. Most are mounted on the starter but don't have to be; follow the positive wire from the battery to find it.
- fact: failure causes: weak internal spring, corroded copper plate; bad ground, weak starter, or bad battery can also make it fail.
- confirm: test with volt-ohms meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches (article links a solenoid test article). If the mower starts when the solenoid is bypassed, replace it.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches
- fact: if bypassing the solenoid starts the mower, replace the solenoid.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: suspect when battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid are all good. Starter can be removed and tested.
- tip: starter is pricey; have local dealer confirm before replacing, or take it to a repair shop specializing in starter/alternator work that can test and often rebuild it.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: bad charging system won't keep battery charged -> weak battery won't start the mower. Larger mowers may have an external alternator like a car; most have an internal alternator under the flywheel.
- confirm: use volt-ohm meter charging system steps (linked battery article).
- pro: have a mechanic familiar with the charging system test and repair; troubleshooting is difficult and otherwise you "throw parts"; electrical parts can't be returned. Could be bad stator/alternator, regulator, or other electrical fault.
