## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "smell gas but no puddle under the mower"
- alias: "Spartan lawn mower leaking gas"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Hard to find because the wet spot may have evaporated.
- safety: Work in a well-ventilated area; gas fumes are harmful; follow the Spartan operator's manual.
- tip: Troubleshooting checklist: carburetor around the bowl or near the air intake; fuel lines and clamps; fuel filter; fuel pump; shut-off valve; tank seams; fuel cap seal.
- brand: Spartan (zero turn). Fuel tank is high-density polyethylene.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Gasket between carburetor and bowl at the bottom looks like a rubber band; seals them. Dries out from extreme temperature changes; heats while running near the engine and cools when off.
- fix: Replace the bowl gasket (see procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor).
- confirm: Gasket not the leak or leak continues: check float. Leak near the air intake suggests a stuck float (gatekeeper; sticks from old-fuel deposits; fuel overflows). Need to disassemble to find why; clean carb and float or replace the float.
- confirm: Stuck float needle: replace if it sticks. Tapping the carb with a rubber mallet is temporary.
- tip: To order carb parts, use the engine model and spec from the engine housing, not the mower model/serial.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: Bowl gasket replacement steps:
- step 1: Shut off fuel with the shut-off valve or crimp the fuel line.
- step 2: Wipe dirt around the carb.
- step 3: Remove the bottom screw and the bowl; rag for remaining fuel.
- step 4: Remove the old gasket.
- step 5: Fit the new gasket; keep carb cleaner/other material off it.
- step 6: Reinstall the bowl and tighten the screw.
- step 7: Wipe down the carb.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Fuel degrades/softens a filter that hasn't been changed regularly; plastic weakens and leaks at seams. Replace regularly. Don't break off soft filter ends when removing from lines. Install with arrow in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Old fuel degrades the plastic housing; softens, seam leaks. Replace a leaking vacuum pump.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: HDPE tank seams can fail over time; replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Shut-off valves are prone to leaking; replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Lines dry with age and crack; clamps can loosen or puncture lines. Replace dry/cracked lines; convert pinch-style clamps to worm gear clamps (don't pinch).

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- confirm: Gently rock the mower back and forth with the cap installed to splash fuel to the cap and watch for a wet spot; replace the cap if one forms. Wet spot may be missed as fuel evaporates. Check after everything else is ruled out.
