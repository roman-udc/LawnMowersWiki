## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "Snapper blades won't turn on"
- alias: "can't get the blades to spin"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: Look at electrical problems or worn clutch/deck parts. 8 reasons: worn belt; belt off pulleys (stretched belt, bad pulley, worn tensioner arm, missing spring); faulty PTO switch; bad clutch; worn clutch cable (manual); weak battery; bad safety switch; blown fuse.
- safety: never reach under deck until per manual: remove ignition key, remove spark plug wires, wait for all parts to stop.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt slips, then won't turn pulleys; blades don't spin.
- confirm: cracks, fraying, shiny glazed appearance, sits deep in pulley groove.
- fix: replace; replace on first signs of wear even if not the main cause. Belt grip affects blade speed; fast blade speed creates suction under deck to lift grass for even cut.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: tensioner arm and spring hold idler pulleys in line; bracket hole where spring attaches can wear or spring wears -> belt loose, vibrates off pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: each pulley has a bearing; wear lets pulley wobble, no longer parallel to deck, one side higher; belt rolls off. Replace pulley with bad bearing.
- tip: link to Snapper belt-keeps-coming-off article.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch usually a knob; passes battery voltage to power the clutch; failed = blades don't turn.
- confirm: check continuity; replace if break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers engine power to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace worn/faulty clutch that fails to power blades.

## page: causes/bad-clutch-cable-or-linkage
- fact: manual-engagement Snapper has clutch lever and cable. Check lever, cable, spring, bushings, linkages for wear; replace worn parts.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: electric clutch uses battery power; weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12-volt battery about 12.7 volts; less -> charge.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: wear protective gear for skin (electrical shock) and eyes.
- tools: battery charger, screwdriver (to uncover battery), cables.
- step 1: access battery and terminals (under hood or seat); may need screwdriver; do not remove battery from casing.
- step 2: connect charger positive cable first (red / plus sign) to positive terminal.
- step 3: connect negative cable (black / minus sign) to negative terminal.
- step 4: do not touch anything not rubber coated.
- step 5: set charger voltage and amperage; mower batteries usually 12 volts; more amps charges faster; start with 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge is best.
- fact: if battery won't hold charge, replace; 12-volt batteries at hardware/automotive store or mower dealer; bring old battery - core fee averages $20 otherwise.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: see charge steps; link to "5 things draining your battery".

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- brand: Snapper has a seat switch under the seat as part of operator presence system; blades won't turn on without operator in seat; failed seat switch fails to sense operator and blocks blades.
- confirm: test seat switch with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify.
- safety: do not operate without safety switch installed and working.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: fuse protects electrical system; no battery power to clutch may mean blown fuse.
- fix: replace with same-capacity fuse. Repeated blowing -> Snapper service dealer or repair shop to find root cause.
