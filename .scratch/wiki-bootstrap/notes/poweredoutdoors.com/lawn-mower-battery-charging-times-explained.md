## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: a fully charged mower battery typically lasts about one hour (Source's claim); full charge, especially trickle, takes longer but reduces how often you must charge and improves battery health
- fact: charging time depends on battery voltage, amps used, frequency of use and run time
- fact: voltage depends on mower size and age; 1980s mowers most likely use 6 V batteries (shorter charge and use time)
- fact: charge slowly to avoid overheating and overcharging; depending on use between charges a 6 V battery can take up to 6 hours to charge and a 12 V up to 12 hours
- fact: 12 V is the standard for most mowers: good balance of run time and charge time; use the battery recommended by the manufacturer
- fact: average mower charger delivers up to 10 amps; some chargers deliver 20 amps, which shortens charging but can damage the battery, especially if used regularly; 7 amps or less recommended; at 6 or 7 amps battery usable within an hour (Source's claim); 7 amps called best balance
- fact: batteries drain in storage; causes of excess drain: leaving the mower on, key left in ignition (standby draw), age-related current draw; older batteries lose charge faster; a good battery holds most of its charge with minimal leakage; if it drains too quickly replace
- fact: ongoing drain causes: bad/corroded terminals, loose cables, key left in on position, faulty charging system
- fact: corrosion: battery gases plus high temperature corrode the battery; remove with wire brush
- tip: keep blades sharp (dull blades increase draw); sharpen with metal file, chisel or sharpener; store protected from rain and snow; don't mow wet grass; keep battery from getting wet
- tip: trickle charging: delivers 1-3 amps to 6 or 12 V battery; prevents overcharging, draining, temperature and sulfate damage; battery sitting unused can suffer sulfation; cold in a shed/garage over winter can crack the battery; trickle charger keeps it charged and warm
- step (trickle charger): unplug the charger from outlet; connect positive charger side to positive battery; negative to negative; check voltage setting; plug back in
- fact: most trickle chargers are automatic (detect voltage and charge level); full charge takes up to 24 hours on a trickle charger; put the charger on well before mowing season; keep on in cold months so it doesn't freeze
- fact: some regular chargers auto-shut-off; for those that don't, remember to remove the charger to avoid overcharge

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- fact: charge at 7 amps or lower; trickle charger 1-3 amps up to 24 hours; see guide above
