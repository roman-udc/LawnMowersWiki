## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Bad Boy zero turn won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "wheels move very slowly or won't move when moving the drive arms"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: could be a simple repair or a more significant transmission problem. Source lists 7 reasons (bypass levers, pump drive belt, tensioner pulley, idler spring, low fluid, hot fluid, air).
- safety: be careful with the transmission system; wait for it to cool before repairs or an oil change; follow operator's manual precautions.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- symptom: Bad Boy won't move with drive arms (problems/mower-wont-move).
- brand: two neutral bypass (drive release) levers at the rear of the mower; used to bypass the transmission so the mower can be pushed/free-rolled with the engine off (e.g. quit mid-yard, to move to garage or trailer).
- fix: levers must be pushed in toward the mower for the "operating" position for the drive arms to move the mower.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: pump drive belt worn, loose, broken or off pulleys: zero turn moves poorly (problems/mower-wont-move).
- confirm: check that the pump drive belt has not fallen off and sits correctly on the pulleys.
- fix: replace the belt if it shows any wear. If belt looks good, check tensioner pulley and spring.
- fact: Bad Boy belt tension should be 60-65 lbs; adjust with the jam nuts on the tensioner.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: tensioner pulley/bearing bad (problems/mower-wont-move).
- fact: many pump tensioner pulleys are hard plastic and can break or wear; the bearing can also fail.
- fix: replace a worn pulley or one with a bad bearing.
- tip: keep the tensioner arm greased so it does not seize.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: the idler spring tensions the pump drive belt.
- fix: replace the spring if broken or fallen out; adjust spring tension if needed.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- symptom: mower does not move or seems weak (problems/mower-wont-move).
- confirm: check level while oil is cool; wipe around caps before removing; dirt is often a factor in failed hydraulic systems so avoid contamination. Two reservoirs are located under the seat.
- fix: add hydraulic fluid until the level reaches the FULL COLD line on the reservoirs.
- brand: use Bad Boy hydrostatic oil or 20W-50 oil. Change oil and filters after the initial 50 hours; then every 250 hours.
- pro: if the level is low, check for leaks; contact the Bad Boy dealership for hydraulic fluid leaks.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: old or low fluid cannot lubricate efficiently, causing increased friction and overheating of the fluid. Hot fluid can also indicate more extensive damage.
- pro: take to the dealership if the hydraulic pump leaks or if the mower runs fine cold but stops when hot.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- symptom: slow or no movement, esp. after a fluid change (problems/mower-wont-move).
- fact: must bleed air after changing hydraulic fluid.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: raise the rear drive tires off the ground; if you can't, use a large open area free of obstructions and do it on the ground.
- step 2: open the bypass valves, release the parking brake, start the engine.
- step 3: move drive levers slowly forward and then reverse about 6 times.
- step 4: close the bypass valves. With throttle high, move the drive levers forward and reverse about 6 times.
- step 5: repeat until there is no excessive noise and the tires move at normal speeds.
- step 6: shut off engine, engage parking brake; check the expansion tanks are at the FULL COLD line; add oil if not.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Bad Boy: change oil and filters after first 50 hours then every 250 hours; bleed air after (see purge procedure); fill to FULL COLD.
