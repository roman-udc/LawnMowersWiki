## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "push mower engine smells hot"
- alias: "engine may even begin smoking"
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Continuing to run without fixing the root cause can permanently damage the engine.
- safety: Stop the mower, remove ignition key, wait for the engine to cool, remove spark plug boots before repairs.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Oil lubricates and also helps cool the engine. Air-cooled small engines use air-cooled engine oil with a high concentration of zinc, an additive that acts as a cooling agent. Regular car motor oil has little zinc because car engines are liquid-cooled.
- fact: Most small-engine manufacturers for push mowers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; in higher ambient temperatures you may need 20W-50. Check the manual and a temperature/viscosity chart (a Kawasaki 4-cycle chart is referenced).
- fix: Drain wrong oil and add correct air-cooled engine oil.
- fact: Low oil: friction builds heat; running low makes oil thicker and can cause significant damage. Check oil before each use; add a little if low.
- fact: If consistently low: oil leak (find cause, replace gaskets leaking; check oil filter seals), burning oil (plugged air filter makes oil burn and engine work harder), or using oil (from overheating, or valve/ring problem; needs repair-center diagnosis).
- fix: If it smells hot, do a full oil change since heated oil may have thickened.
- pro: If problems continue after an oil change, running low may have caused extensive damage; take to small engine dealer/mechanic for further tests.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins push air to the outside of the cylinder head and engine block. Top of engine has a fan, or a flywheel with fins that act as a fan, pulling in cool air and blowing it down over hot parts and out the bottom. Dirt-plugged or damaged fins can't dissipate heat.
- fix: Remove engine cover and clean fins; replace damaged fins; clean dirt from the cover and around the engine; make it annual maintenance.
- mistake: Washing the engine can push dirt and water into the engine area; don't spray water into the engine when washing.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Starved engine runs hot because it works harder to breathe.
- tip: Replace annually, clean several times per season; more often in dusty conditions or heavier-than-average use.
- tip: Don't try to stretch air filter life; cheaper than engine repair.
- step: Paper: remove; wipe housing without letting dirt into the intake; tap on a solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light shows and undamaged; reinstall with cover. If a foam pre-filter is used, wash with mild detergent and water, rinse, air dry; DO NOT oil the pre-filter.
- step: Foam: remove; wipe housing; wash with mild detergent and water; rinse until soap gone; squeeze out water and lay flat to dry; lubricate with filter oil or engine oil until lightly saturated but not dripping; squeeze to remove excess; replace if excessively dirty with dark spots or tears; reinstall.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Paper and foam steps as above (foam gets filter oil or engine oil, lightly saturated; foam pre-filter over paper never oiled).

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Buildup reduces under-deck airflow and makes the engine work harder to turn the blade.
- fix: Scrape with a deck scraper; wire brush or putty knife also work. Check often.
- tip: Under-deck coating products reduce but don't prevent buildup. Avoid cutting wet grass and run at full throttle to reduce clumping.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull blades magnify the plugged deck problem. For the average homeowner sharpen blades twice a season or about every 25 hours.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: Operate at a slower ground speed in long, thick or wet grass.
- tip: Double-cut or triple-cut long grass: highest deck setting for first cut, then lower a little for the second. Run at full throttle and fast engine speed.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Long, thick or wet grass loads the engine and may cause overheating. Avoid cutting wet grass; double/triple cut long grass.

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: Air-cooled engine oil with high zinc for push mower engines; SAE30 or 10W-30 typical; 20W-50 for higher ambient temperatures; check manual.

## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- fact: Wait for the engine to cool before repairs (no time given).
