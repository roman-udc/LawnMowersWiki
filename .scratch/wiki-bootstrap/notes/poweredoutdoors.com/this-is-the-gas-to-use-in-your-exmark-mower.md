## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Most Exmark mowers have four-stroke engines using unleaded gasoline: fresh (consume within 30 days), octane 87 or higher, ethanol no more than 10%. Exmark also has diesel and propane mowers; check the operator's manual.
- fact: Tips: octane 87+; no more than 10% ethanol; don't store fuel outside where it collects moisture; buy what you'll consume in 30 days.
- fact: Gas breaks down in a short time; best used within 30 days. Buy from a busy gas station (turnover) rather than a rarely used one.
- fact: Ethanol is corn-based; stations average 10-15%. Always avoid E85 (85% ethanol). Ethanol fuel attracts moisture, which separates the mix (gas sinks to the bottom per the Source wording; the separated ethanol is fed through the engine causing ignition problems); corrosive; high ethanol can burn very hot causing overheating and irreversible engine damage.
- fix: If you can't find ethanol-free or under-10% gas, add an extra fuel treatment to help stop separation.
- opinion: Higher quality, lower-ethanol fuel reduces deposit buildup in carb and fuel system. Author uses recreational fuel or premium unleaded plus stabilizer (Sea Foam).

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Use a plastic gas can designed for unleaded gas (some plastics break down).
- fact: Add stabilizer to gas not used right away; Sea Foam stabilizes up to two years and reduces moisture buildup (author).
- fact: Avoid buying more than a 30-day supply; rotate gas (oldest first); at season end discard leftover gas (recycle or use in an unleaded vehicle); see winterizing.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: discard leftover gas at end of the mowing season.
