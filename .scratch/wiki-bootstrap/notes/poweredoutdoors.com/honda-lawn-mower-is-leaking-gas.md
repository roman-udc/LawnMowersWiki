## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "Honda mower leaking gas", "strong gas odor in the garage"
- causes-named: cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-filter, cracked-fuel-line, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Fix before continuing to use the mower: excessive fuel use, and dripping fuel can kill the lawn. Old fuel degrades and gums fuel components. Six places: tank, shut-off valve, filter, lines, carburetor, gas cap. Inspect starting at the tank, then follow the fuel line out.
- safety: Take safety precautions whenever working on the mower; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Honda tanks may be metal or high-density polyethylene. Metal tanks corrode, rust spots become holes. Replace if available; if the tank is no longer made, patch the hole with a material like JB Weld. Polyethylene seams eventually fail and leak; best option is replacing the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Metal or plastic fuel shut-off valves are both prone to leaking; replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Fuel sitting in the filter degrades and softens the plastic housing; it leaks at the seams.
- fix: replace; install with the housing arrow in the direction of fuel flow, i.e. facing the carburetor, away from the tank.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: Rubber lines dry and crack with age; leaks at cracks and where a clamp attaches (clamp can puncture the line or loosen).
- fix: Use the shut-off valve to stop fuel while replacing the line. If pinch-style hose clamps, replace with worm gear clamps (less likely to leak).

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Old fuel forms gummy/crusty deposits making parts stick; fuel overflows. A leak near the air intake is most likely a stuck float or float needle (float regulates fuel entering the bowl). Must disassemble the carb to find the cause; may clean it or replace float/needle. Tapping the carb to free a stuck needle may work once or twice but is not a permanent fix.
- fact: Leak between bowl and carburetor: the bowl gasket (looks like a rubber band) dries and becomes brittle from heat/cold cycling next to the engine; easy part to replace.
- step 1: Wipe the carb down so dirt doesn't get in when removing the bowl.
- step 2: Remove the screw at the bottom of the carburetor and remove the bowl.
- step 3: Remove the old gasket, install the new one; keep carb cleaner or any material off the gasket or it must be replaced.
- step 4: Reinstall bowl and screw; tighten to seal.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: stuck float/needle from old-fuel deposits causes an overflow leak (see above).

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: wet spot around the cap area on the tank; fuel sloshing up to the cap leaks while running; may evaporate leaving no visible leak.
- confirm: if fuel smell but no leak found elsewhere, rock the mower back and forth to splash fuel to the cap and watch for a wet spot.
- fix: replace with a new Honda cap.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Run fresh fuel; a fuel stabilizer prevents breakdown and reduces moisture buildup.
