## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fact: check fuel level; may have forgotten fueling or have a leak emptying the tank sooner.
- fix: add fresh fuel; check for and repair fuel leaks.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Grasshopper gas mower uses unleaded gasoline with ethanol 10 percent or less and minimum 87 octane. Higher ethanol can cause significant engine damage. Lower ethanol is better.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol moisture leaves gummy deposits that clog fuel components.
- fix: drain and flush the tank using a fuel siphon; add fresh fuel with a stabilizer/cleaner such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: carbon buildup or oil on plug causes misfire/no start; damaged, mis-gapped or loose connection too.
- fix: remove and inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, burnt electrodes; replace; gap per engine manufacturer.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: plugged filter can stop starting and cause overheating/engine damage. Author: start each season with a new filter; homeowners check/clean several times per season, more in dust; commercial use check and clean daily.
- mistake: never run without an air filter (dirt wears seals and scores the cylinder).
- fact: if it starts but runs sluggish, overheats or loses power, check the filter.
- step 1: remove paper filter; step 2: wipe dirt from housing without letting it fall in the intake; step 3: tap on a solid surface, no compressed air (damages paper); step 4: hold to light; reuse if light shows through, replace if no light, oil-covered or very dark.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: see steps above (paper filter).

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: needed when carb is higher than tank; vacuum from crankcase builds pressure. Visible damage: small housing crack or leaking seams.
- confirm: verify fuel out of the line at the inlet port; then stop flow (shut-off valve, fuel clamps or hose pinch pliers), remove the line from the carburetor into a container, start flow and start mower; steady or pulsating flow expected, otherwise replace pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- tip: replace annually; install with arrow pointing in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel, remove the line section farthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump), restart flow.
- fix: remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air until clear; replace if the clog won't clear.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: varnish clogs the fuel jet or gums internal parts; may need rebuild if parts broken or replace if it won't function after cleaning. Can be DIY for the mechanical, or a small engine shop.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vent is in the gas cap on a Grasshopper; blocked vent creates vacuum.
- confirm: remove cap and try to start; if it starts, suspect cap; reinstall cap with the mower running to reproduce dying.
- fix: author prefers replacing a cap that no longer vents (cleaning doesn't always work).

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fix: test with a multimeter; want about 12.7 volts; charge if lower; replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: clean terminals with baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) and wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: several switches: stop the deck running without operator present; prevent starting when parking brake isn't engaged. Failure may stop starting or kill the engine.
- confirm: test with a multimeter or temporarily bypass.
- safety: never operate with a safety switch missing/bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: key turned and mower doesn't start or turn over; test with multimeter, replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: supplies voltage to the plug; after confirming plug is good, check coil continuity with a multimeter; replace if broken continuity.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: contributes to a weak battery; components include stator or alternator.
- pro: take to a small engine mechanic experienced with zero-turn charging systems; guessing parts is expensive and most retail stores don't accept returns of electrical parts.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- step 1: sit in operator's seat; step 2: parking brake on; step 3: steering levers apart in outward position; step 4: throttle about 1/3; step 5: choke on if needed for cold engine; step 6: key in and start; step 7: choke off when warm; step 8: to move, release parking brake and move steering levers together.
- fix: refer to the manual so safety features don't shut off or prevent starting.

## page: procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower
- step 1-8: as above (Grasshopper).
