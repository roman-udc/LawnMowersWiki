## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: Service a zero turn every 100 hours or annually, whichever comes first. Between services keep engine, hydraulics and deck clean and free of debris; check oil levels, tire pressures, safety system, belts, filters and blades.
- fact: Checklist: safety systems; starting system and battery; change engine oil and filter; check hydraulic oil (change oil and filter if necessary); replace spark plug; replace fuel filter and inspect fuel lines; check tire pressures and lug nuts; clean/replace air filter if necessary; remove dirt and debris from exhaust, engine, hydraulic systems; sharpen or change blades; scrape the deck; check belts and pulleys; lubricate zero-turn and deck.
- tools: Engine oil and filter; hydraulic oil and filter if needed; air filter; fuel filter; spark plugs; socket or spanner wrenches; drain pan, funnel, filter wrench; rags, work gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover; deck scraper, wire brush or metal putty knife.
- safety: Remove the key and the spark plug wires so the mower can't start while working.
- tip: Spark plug: inspect for damage or excessive buildup; author prefers annual replacement (inexpensive).
- tip: Fuel filter and lines: ethanol breaks down, is corrosive, clogs filter and lines. Replace the filter and inspect lines; replace lines when dry and brittle.
- tip: Tires: lug nuts tight, drive tire pressure equal on both sides, at the pressure on the sidewall. Uneven pressure gives uneven cutting and pulls to one side so the mower doesn't track straight.
- tip: Dirt and debris: remove the engine shroud and clean around engine and exhaust and the engine cooling fins; buildup stops cooling air and overheats. Also clean hydro fan cooling fins, guards and fan.
- tip: Lubrication: high-performance multi-purpose grease on the deck idler arm pivots and pumps and all other pivot points and grease zerks; check the operator's manual for all grease points; some caster wheels need grease.
- fact: Winterizing adds fuel system prep and battery care so it doesn't freeze; see Source's separate winterizing article.
- pro: If you can't find the problem or aren't comfortable, take it to a dealer or experienced small engine mechanic.

## page: guides/zero-turn-safety
- fact: Check the safety interlock system per the manufacturer manual. Engine should not start if the parking brake isn't engaged and the operator isn't in the seat. Engine should shut down if the PTO is engaged and the operator leaves the seat. Go through all interlock features.
- fact: Inspect the rollover protection system (ROPS) and seat belt for compromise; they work together. Zero turns are dangerous; keep the safety system in good order.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- fact: Most zero-turn batteries are 12-volt. With a voltmeter, 12.6 volts or greater is 100% charged; less needs charging. Make sure cables are tight; clean or replace corroded terminals.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Disconnect the battery cables. Positive (red +) is the last one off and the first one on. If the battery has removable caps check water level; if plates aren't covered add distilled water (not well water; city water slightly better; distilled best).
- step 2: Hook the charger to the battery.
- step 3: Check the voltage with a meter. About 11.8 volts needs little charge time; 10.1 volts needs a long time; lower voltage means longer.
- tip: For a very low battery use a "slow bake": charger on 2 or 4 amps (some have 6, 4, 2 amp switches) for about an hour, then check the voltage is rising; if so charge another hour (author practice).

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: Change oil and filter every 100 hours or annually. New engine: change after the initial 5 hours. Not changing regularly can damage the engine.
- step 1: Cover the work surface (cardboard, newspaper) to protect the ground.
- step 2: Warm the oil: run the mower on the cover for 2 to 3 minutes (warm oil flows better). Beware hot engine.
- step 3: Remove the key and unplug spark plug boot(s).
- step 4: Clean around the oil plug, put a drain pan under it, remove the plug and drain. Some zero-turn engines have an oil hose port instead of a drain plug.
- step 5: Remove the oil filter with a filter wrench; put a light film of fresh oil on the new filter gasket and install; refit the drain plug once drained.
- step 6: Fill the crankcase with the quantity and type of oil the manufacturer recommends; neither too low nor too high.
- step 7: Reinstall spark plug boots, run the engine a few minutes, stop, let the oil settle/cool, recheck level and top up.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Low or old hydraulic fluid makes the mower seem weak when moving and can damage the transmission. Change oil and filters at the manufacturer's interval; most have a break-in period where the first change comes sooner, some as soon as 75 hours.
- fact: Some entry-level zero turns have unserviceable hydraulic systems where you can't change the oil. Procedures vary by model; check the manual. Air must be removed after a change; for most mowers raise the rear tires off the ground.
- step 1: Park on a flat surface, remove key, engage parking brake, wait for moving parts to stop. Engine and transmission may be hot.
- step 2: Locate filters, one near each rear wheel; there may be a guard or skid plate; remove it and clean around the filters first so no dirt enters the hydraulic system.
- step 3: Put an oil pan under one filter, unscrew and drain; repeat with the other. Take old oil to a recycling center.
- step 4: Apply a thin layer of fresh hydro oil on the new filters' rubber seal; install by turning clockwise; once it contacts the filter adapter turn about one more full turn.
- step 5: Fill with recommended hydraulic oil through the expansion reservoir (small plastic tank on top of the mower behind the seat; may be two, one each side). Clean around it before removing the cap. Fill to the "full cold" line.
- step 6: Some transaxles have a vent plug on each transaxle: loosen each, fill through the reservoir until oil comes out of the vent plug, tighten the vent plugs, and keep filling to the "cold full" line.
- step 7: Purge air (see procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system).
- tools: oil pan, hydraulic oil, new filters.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Raise the rear of the mower with jack stands just high enough that the drive wheels no longer touch the ground.
- step 2: Start the mower, increase throttle to about half throttle, release the parking brake.
- step 3: Move the speed levers forward and reverse several times for a few minutes total until the wheels turn at a normal rate and sound.
- step 4: Let the hydraulic system cool and recheck oil level; add if needed.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Engine without clean air is starved and overheats.
- step 1: Remove the filter without knocking dirt into the intake; wipe the housing with a dry clean cloth.
- step 2: Tap the filter on a solid surface. Do not blow it clean with an air compressor.
- step 3: Hold to the light; light through the paper means reusable; blocked light, damage or oil means replace.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Wearing work gloves, grab each side of the blade and rock it up and down; movement means replace the bearing or spindle housing assembly.
- fact: Some manufacturers only allow rebuilding the housing, some only sell the assembly, some both.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- step 1: Remove the belt from the bad pulley: put something narrow between belt and groove, turn the belt by hand and pry it off the pulley.
- step 2: Remove the pulley nut with a socket; normally regular thread, counterclockwise. Some mowers have set screws (allen or hex) holding the pulley to the shaft that may be stiff and need a puller.
- step 3: Remove the 4 to 8 bolts holding the spindle; the assembly drops out of the deck bottom.
- step 4: Place on a flat hard surface. Sometimes a snap ring holds it, sometimes you replace the entire housing with bearings. Replace the bearing if possible, grease it, reassemble in reverse.
- step 5: Bolt the housing to the deck, fit the pulley, make sure everything is tight with no movement, refit the belt and turn by hand to make sure it is on all the pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: Slowly turn each pulley; it should move freely without resistance and silently. Noise or resistance: replace the pulley.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- confirm: Belts worn, cracked or glazed should be replaced; worn belts and pulleys can stop the hydraulic pump or blades working properly.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Dull blades strain the mower and cut poorly; the lawn can look brown at the ends a couple of days after mowing; dull blades beat up grass instead of cutting. Inspect at each service; replace blades that are damaged or bent.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Scrape the deck each service with a deck scraper or metal putty knife; a stiff wire brush also works. Access with jack stands or a lift. Buildup under the deck compromises air movement; blade and deck suction stand the grass up so the blade cuts it precisely.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Zero turn needs service every 100 hours or annually. Everything listed under procedures/tune-up.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Winterizing needs fuel system prep and battery care to avoid freezing; see Source's separate article.
