## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Troy-Bilt mower clicks but won't start or turn over"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, faulty-charging-system
- safety: wear protective gear; always disconnect the negative (black) battery cable before electrical repairs; consult a mechanic if uncomfortable with electrical work.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: check cables battery-to-solenoid and solenoid-to-starter motor are tight; look for corrosion on terminals/components.
- fix: replace cables/wiring that keep coming loose or are broken; disconnect battery, remove corroded components to clean.
- step 1: baking soda mix: 2 cups water with 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda.
- step 2: spread paste on corrosion (foams); rinse with water (outdoors; messy); wire brush speeds up.
- step 3: apply dielectric grease to protect terminals and wiring; replace damaged terminals.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: weak battery may not provide enough power to turn over the engine; if it charges but keeps dying look at the charging system.
- confirm: multimeter red/black prongs on same-colored terminals; most Troy-Bilt batteries are 12 V; reads 11.5 to 12.7 V; near 11.5 V almost dead; 12.7 V fully charged.
- fix: charge; replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: put on safety gear (acid, shock).
- step 2: access battery (screwdriver may be needed to uncover body/casing); leave it in its casing with cables attached.
- step 3: connect red charger clamp first to positive terminal; black to negative.
- step 4: only touch the rubber-coated parts of cables/clamps.
- step 5: set charger to 12 V; amps: start at 2 and work up to no more than 10; more amps charge faster.
- step 6: if charger has a gauge keep connected until fully charged.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step: multimeter on terminals; 11.5 V nearly dead, 12.7 V fully charged (12 V battery).

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: black ground cable from battery to frame must make good contact, free of corrosion; also check the solenoid ground; a 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: replace damaged ground cable; remove corrosion at battery and solenoid grounds.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: solenoid is an electromagnet switch that actuates the starter motor; most are mounted on the starter but needn't be; follow the positive wire from battery to find it.
- fact: causes: internal spring weak, copper plate corroding; also bad ground, weak starter or bad battery can make it fail.
- confirm: test with volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light and wrenches; if you can start the mower by bypassing the solenoid, replace it.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches; bypass test: engine starts when solenoid bypassed = replace (details in the author's linked article, not in this Source).

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: if battery, cables, wiring, ground, solenoid are good, starter may be the problem; can be removed and tested.
- pro: starters are pricey; have the dealer confirm before replacing; or a starter/alternator repair shop can test and maybe rebuild.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: won't keep battery charged; possible bad stator/alternator, regulator or other electrical problem.
- pro: recommend a mechanic familiar with the charging system; electrical parts usually can't be returned.
