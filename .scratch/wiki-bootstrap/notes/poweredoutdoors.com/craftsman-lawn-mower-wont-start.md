## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Craftsman lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-recoil-starter, bad-ignition-coil, bad-starter-solenoid, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Engine needs fuel, air and spark. Won't start when not getting air, fuel, or spark due to plugged air filter, bad fuel, faulty switches, bad battery, bad spark plug, fuel blockages or dirty carburetor.
- fact: Before testing fuel, if mower has a fuel shut-off valve, make sure it is open (often closed for storage/transport). (cause: fuel-valve-off)
- confirm: Fuel test: remove air filter cover and filter, spray carburetor cleaner into air intake (not starter fluid), try to start with recoil or key. If it tries to start or starts, the fuel system is the problem (dirty carburetor, clogged fuel filter, clogged fuel lines, possibly fuel pump). If it does not try to start, most likely a spark plug problem.
- confirm: Air test: inspect air filter (clean or replace if very dirty/damaged). With the filter off, inspect choke plate. Manual choke: lever to ON, plate closed; lever to OFF (on some models fast throttle position), plate open. Auto choke: plate closed when cold, opens as engine warms. If choke does not open/close properly check choke linkage and cable; loosen a stuck choke by lubricating linkages and choke shaft with carburetor cleaner; replace a bad choke cable.
- fact: Cold engine must run rich (more fuel, less air). Choke lever opens choke plate for more air and closes to restrict air. Newer push mowers use thermostat-controlled automatic choke.
- confirm: Spark test: remove plug boot, attach a spark plug tester to the ignition wire, connect its other end to the plug (plug still installed), crank by pull cord or electric start. Glow in transparent section of tester means spark. No glow: bad plug or bad ignition coil; on electric start models also battery, wiring, starter solenoid.
- pro: If charging system problem found, have a small engine mechanic identify the actual failure.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with fresh gasoline, 87 octane or higher, ethanol no more than 10%.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas begins to break down after about 30 days; buy from a busy station and only what you use in 30 days. Ethanol attracts moisture; when it evaporates, gummy deposits clog fuel lines, filters and carburetor.
- fix: Remove old fuel, flush the tank, add fresh fuel. Adding a fuel system cleaner like Sea Foam is a good idea, especially after running old gas.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Gas cap has a vent; when plugged the tank forms a vacuum preventing gas from moving through lines; mower acts starved of fuel.
- fix: Clean cap to open the vent; replace if broken or won't vent.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Bad connection or damaged plug; an excessively dirty plug fouls and engine misfires.
- fix: Remove and inspect for carbon buildup or cracked porcelain insulator; replace and gap per manufacturer's spec. Check for loose plug wire.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Blocked airflow makes engine run hot and seek air wherever it can, including air remaining in the crankcase.
- fix: Remove paper filter from housing without dropping dirt into intake; tap on solid surface; hold to the light; if light isn't visible through every area, replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Needed when the tank is lower than the carburetor. Pump has three ports: inlet, outlet, and a port connected to a line off the crankcase that pressurizes the pump.
- confirm: Look for small cracks or fuel leaking outside the pump; replace if seen.
- step 1: Verify fuel reaches the pump: turn off fuel valve or clamp the line (not every mower has a valve). Disconnect line from the pump inlet, place in container lower than the tank, unclamp/open valve, check fuel flows. If not, check lines and fuel filter for blockage.
- step 2: Verify the pump delivers: reconnect line to pump inlet, remove the line from the carburetor, put in a container, start the mower; expect steady or pulsating flow from the line.
- fix: Replace the pump if it can't consistently pump fuel out of the outlet.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Filter strains fuel; old fuel leaves gummy deposits that plug it.
- confirm: Remove the fuel line after the filter into a container (clamp to stop flow while moving the line, then remove clamp) to check flow.
- fix: Replace a cracked filter or one that doesn't pass fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove line, spray carburetor cleaner in and blow with compressed air until clear, or replace with new fuel line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Without correct gas/air ratio the mower may run rough and not start.
- fix: Clean or replace; most often cleaning does the job.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Battery must read 12.7 volts or greater; test with a multimeter. Under 12.7 V, charge it. If it won't hold a charge, replace.
- tip: Lawn and garden batteries sold at hardware and auto parts stores; stores often charge a core fee if you don't bring the old battery.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Clean corroded terminals with baking soda solution: 2 cups water and 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda; scrub with wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Mowers use several safety switches that keep you safe; faulty ones can prevent starting.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter, or temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one.
- safety: Do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed. Always have safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned, mower doesn't start or even turn over.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Push mowers pull-start with a recoil; bad pulley, broken spring or broken clips are likely culprits.
- fix: Try replacing the spring and restringing; if clips or pulley are damaged, replace the whole recoil assembly.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Provides voltage to the plug so it can fire.
- confirm: After verifying plug is good, check ignition coil continuity with a multimeter; a break in continuity means replace.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor.
- symptom: click or hum when turning the key; a wire on the solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts.
- fix: Test (article links to its solenoid test article); replace if bad.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Not the main reason a mower won't start but can cause a weak battery. Bad stator or alternator, among other electrical parts; test with an ohmmeter (see article on battery drain).
- pro: Have a small engine mechanic identify the actual failure, otherwise guessing gets expensive.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Starting procedures are for safety. Push mowers use a safety bar to recognize the operator. Riding mowers use a seat switch to confirm operator presence, and a brake switch that can prevent starting if the brake isn't engaged.
- fix: Refer to the operator's manual to avoid triggering safety shutoffs.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- fact: Valve often moved to closed for storage or transport; check it is open before diagnosing fuel.
