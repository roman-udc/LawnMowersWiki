- alias: "zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil
- fact: fuel system problems (bad fuel, dirty carb, bad fuel pump) plus bad spark plug, battery, safety switch, ignition switch can all stop a start.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch
- fact: Source lists the battery/cables/safety/ignition-switch rows under "Won't Start" (no separate cranking distinction).

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: fuel breaks down over time, less efficient, prone to clogging the fuel system (and engine damage, per starts-then-dies table).
- fix: drain the tank and fill with fresh fuel; use an additive like Sea Foam to stabilize the fuel and help clean the fuel system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: vent in the cap can clog causing a vacuum in the tank that restricts fuel flow; also a cause for dying after mowing a while (plugged cap makes a vacuum, fuel can't flow out).
- fix: clear the vent with a thin piece of wire; replace the cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: excessively dirty or broken plug; bad connection; not gapped correctly.
- fix: replace with new plug, secure connections, gap to manufacturer specification.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: dirt and grass plug the filter and prevent airflow (causes no-start, dying, and smoking).
- fix: remove and clean the filter; replace if in bad condition.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: dirty filter restricts fuel flow. fix: replace the fuel filter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: a failed or leaking pump no longer creates pressure to pump fuel; also may leak at the seams (leak list) as it wears and deteriorates. fix: replace the pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: bad fuel deposits and dirt build up and clog the lines.
- fix: carb cleaner and compressed air to clear the clog; replace line if necessary.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: clogged by bad fuel and ethanol deposits preventing fuel flow.
- fix: clean carburetor components; replace if needed.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: dead battery causes starting problems; also the clutch (PTO) is powered by the battery.
- fix: charge it, replace if it won't hold a charge.
- confirm: for blades not engaging, test the battery and charge if reading is less than 12.7 volts on a 12-volt battery; replace when it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: make sure cables are tight; clean corrosion from terminals.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: safety switches prevent starting in certain situations; when one fails, mower may not start. fix: test and replace faulty switch.
- fact: for blades, a bad switch may not allow blade engagement when the seat switch fails to recognize the operator in the seat. fix: replace the safety switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: will not start when the ignition switch is bad. fix: test and replace.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: will not start when the coil is bad. fix: test and replace.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "zero turn starts then dies"
- alias: "dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: same items that cause no-start can kill the engine while mowing; when it can't get enough air and fuel it shuts down; a clog could have moved through the fuel system or the air filter blocks air.
- fact: bad fuel cap that doesn't pass air through its vent shows up after mowing a while.
- fact: overheating can shut the mower down; inspect cooling fins for breaks or dirt plugging (fins keep air moving around engine block and cylinder head).

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: fins get packed with dirt, oil and grass so air can't cool the engine block.
- fix: replace broken fins and clean the fins.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "zero turn smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-head-gasket
- fact: smoke occurs when the engine doesn't get the right air-to-fuel ratio or oil becomes hot and burns off.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: excess oil builds pressure in the engine, letting oil get into the cylinder through the valve train.
- fix: drain a little engine oil until at the manufacturer's recommended level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: lack of oil increases friction and causes extreme heat.
- fix: take to a small-engine repair shop to inspect for engine damage; adding oil can be tried but is often too late.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) are detrimental if not caught soon. fix/pro: take to local dealer or small-engine repair shop.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- fact: "engine gasket" listed among internal problems causing smoke; refer to dealer/shop.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "zero turn won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "zero turn weak or slow, hydro seems weak"
- causes-named: bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system, bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: check drive belt, tensioner pulley and spring; if worn or missing, replace.
- fact: check the hydraulic release (drive release) lever; you may have recently moved the mower manually and released the hydros. Levers must be in the operating position or the mower will not move.

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: drive release lever lets hydrostatic mowers roll freely; must be in operating position.
- fix: put the drive release lever in the operating position.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- symptom: weak hydro; a bad drive belt makes the pump work inefficiently; mower won't move if belt has fallen off or is worn.
- fix: replace a frayed, cracked or worn belt; make sure belt is securely around the pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: tensioner pulley bearing can fail and no longer keeps tension on the drive belt.
- fix: replace the tensioner pulley and grease the tensioner arm (weak-hydro table); replace the pulley (won't-move table).

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: if the idler arm spring breaks or goes missing, it can't tension the drive belt. fix: replace broken or missing spring.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- fact: low or old hydraulic oil fails to lubricate, making the hydraulic system seem weak; running low or aged oil can develop transmission problems.
- fix: change hydraulic oil at manufacturer-recommended intervals; check level periodically to ensure full.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: hot fluid can signal damage to the hydrostatic transmission.
- fix: let the oil cool. If mower runs fine cold but weak when the hydraulic system is hot, have it checked at a dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fact: air must be bled after changing hydraulic fluid or the mower can't move at normal speeds; trapped air can make a wheel move slowly (pulling).
- fix: follow manufacturer procedures in the owner's manual for changing fluid and bleeding air; source also links a purge guide.

## page: causes/bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: transmission failure makes the wheel on one side move slower. pro: have the local dealership diagnose.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "zero turn won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, air-in-hydraulic-system, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, bad-hydrostatic-transmission, brake-hung-up, unbalanced-load
- tip: always start troubleshooting by checking tire pressures are equal on each side.
- fact: tracking adjustment row: when one tire moves faster than the other the mower pulls; adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the tire's rotation speed (zero-turn or stand-on); refer to operator's manual (procedures/adjust-the-tracking).

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: unequal pressure pulls to one side (also lists uneven tire pressure under bad cut). fix: keep filled to manufacturer spec; tires on each side need equal pressure.

## page: causes/bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper
- fact: bad dampers make the mower jerk to one side when moving forward. fix: replace the damper(s).

## page: causes/brake-hung-up
- fact: brake can hang up and not let the wheel move freely. fix: inspect braking system, repair worn or seized parts.

## page: causes/unbalanced-load
- fact: towing attachments can throw off balance and pull to one side. fix: balance the towed load or remove the attachment.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- step 1: adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change rotation speed of the tire that is moving faster/slower; see operator's manual.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- fact: Source only points to a separate guide; says bleed air after hydraulic fluid change per owner's manual.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: change at manufacturer's recommended intervals; follow owner's manual procedures for change and bleeding.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "zero turn shaking and vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: some vibration is normal; severe unusual vibration should be addressed to minimize damage.

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- fact: engine bolts reduce vibration when running. fix: secure bolts and replace any missing.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: vibration when bearing fails and falls out of the clutch; a manual-engagement clutch can have bushing and linkages that fail. fix: replace the clutch.
- fact: for blades not engaging: clutch transfers power from engine to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace clutch; on manual-engagement clutch replace worn clutch cable, lever, springs, linkages.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: damaged or unbalanced blades send vibration through the mower; blades must be balanced before installing. fix: replace damaged blades; ensure balance.
- fact: worn blade and bent blade listed as uneven-cut causes.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- fact: string or long weeds wrap the blade shaft. fix: remove all debris wrapped around shaft; check for spindle damage.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- fact: bad bearing in spindle housing causes vibration; also uneven cut cause (bent blade spindle or bearing). fix: replace the bearing or the whole spindle housing assembly.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: worn deck belt does not grip pulleys to rotate blades; vibration from worn belt.
- fix: replace a belt that is worn, cracked or shiny glazed; replace a stretched belt.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: smallest things can cause excessive vibration; check deck and mower for debris stuck; remove foreign material.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "zero turn cutting uneven"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, wrong-tire-pressure, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: table lists: worn mower blade, bent mower blade, blade installed wrong, clogged mower deck, uneven tire pressures, unleveled deck, bent blade spindle or spindle bearing, worn deck pulleys or belt, engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, damaged deck shell, operator error (no detail in this Source).

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "smell strong gas / burnt grass spots"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, bad-gas-cap-seal
- tip: follow fuel lines out of the tank to find the leak; leaks hard to find because fuel evaporates leaving only fumes.
- fact: common leak area is the carburetor: gasket around the bowl dries out losing its seal, or float sticks. Old gas can degrade plastics causing leaks in fuel filter, fuel pump or tank seams.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: bowl gasket dries and loses sealing; float can stick. fix: clean carburetor, replace damaged parts.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: metal tank can develop rust hole; poly tank can leak at seams. fix: replace the tank; sometimes a rust hole can be repaired if no replacement tank can be found.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: metal and plastic shut-off valves are prone to leaking. fix: replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: rubber fuel lines dry out and crack. fix: replace the lines.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: seal fails letting gas splash out around the cap. fix: replace the gas cap.

## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "zero turn blades won't engage or turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: blades won't engage when the PTO switch is faulty, battery weak, safety switch failed, or clutch bad. Blades won't turn when belt is worn or fell off pulleys due to stretched belt, bad pulley bearing or worn tensioner parts.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: failed switch won't let battery voltage engage the clutch. fix: replace PTO switch.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: a fuse may blow to protect the electrical system. fix: replace with same amperage; if it keeps blowing, have a mechanic find the root cause.
- pro: repeated blowing.

## page: procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse
- fix: replace with a fuse of the same amperage; recurring blowing needs a mechanic.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: belt falling off pulleys: replace worn or damaged deck parts including worn idler arm, worn spring, bad pulleys; replace a stretched deck belt.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: check mower before each mowing and perform routine service to prevent issues or catch them before they become costly; links its zero-turn service article.
