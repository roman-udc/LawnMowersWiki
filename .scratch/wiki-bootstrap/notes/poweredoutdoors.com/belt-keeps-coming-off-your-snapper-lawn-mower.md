## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "belt keeps coming off my Snapper mower"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- brand: Source's 9 reasons for Snapper: loose/worn belt; belt keeper damage; shredded deck belt; bad spindle housing bearing; bad pulley bearing; worn tensioner bracket or spring; debris interfering with belt tension; debris in pulley groove; oil or rust on belt.
- safety: Before working on deck: remove ignition key, disconnect spark plug wires, wear gloves (sharp edges).
- tip: Prevention list: clean deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix/replace damaged brackets or keepers.
- pro: If unsure how to diagnose safely, use a Snapper dealer or repair shop.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt looks shiny, cracked, or stretched; belt comes off deck pulleys.
- fix: Replace belt when signs of wear appear.
- fact: Shredded or torn belt edges: find cause (usually out-of-place bracket or keeper rubbing, or wrong routing). Check routing against operator's manual or parts diagram; look for a shiny flat spot on a keeper or bracket where belt rubs; adjust the part. If bracket has a bushing it may be replaceable; sometimes the whole bracket assembly must be replaced when Snapper sells it only that way.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: Snapper deck belt keeper is a rod sitting 1/8" to 1/4" from the pulley. Belt normally doesn't touch it. With slack, belt can hit a keeper and bend it out of place, letting belt slip off.
- fix: Bend keeper back with hammer or vise grips; if it can't be adjusted correctly, replace the keeper.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- symptom: vertical blade movement, deck vibration, which can throw the belt.
- confirm: Key out, spark plug wires disconnected, gloved; grab both ends of blade and rock up/down. Movement or knocking sound = remove spindle housing and check bearing.
- fix: Replace failed spindle bearing.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: pulley not flat/parallel to deck, one side higher; belt more likely to come off.
- confirm: Turn each pulley slowly by hand; it must rotate smoothly. Restriction or bearing noise = replace pulley.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Idler tensioner bracket holds idler pulleys in line; bracket and spring wear over time; spring can weaken or break, causing belt to come off.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Debris on top of deck interferes with tensioner pulley movement; belt loses tension, vibrates and comes off. Dry debris on top of deck can catch fire from belt heat.
- fact: Debris in pulley grooves (bark, stone, packed mud) makes belt stretch around it; belt can roll off or snap.
- confirm: Inspect pulley grooves.
- fix: Clean deck after each mowing.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- symptom: belt slips and comes off (oil); rust dries belt prematurely so it breaks.
- fix: Fix the oil leak or rust source before installing new belt. Clean rust from pulleys and deck; replace pulleys if badly rusted. Check engine oil before each use to catch leaks early.
- mistake: Replacing the belt without fixing the cause; belts are expensive.

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: Check routing against operator's manual/parts diagram when installing.
