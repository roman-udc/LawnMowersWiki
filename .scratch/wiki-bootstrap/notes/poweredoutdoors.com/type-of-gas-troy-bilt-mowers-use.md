## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Troy-Bilt mowers use unleaded gasoline, minimum octane 87, ethanol 10 percent or less (E10). Higher ethanol can cause extensive engine and fuel system damage.
- fact: Article covers 4-cycle ("4-stage") engines. Many Troy-Bilt push mowers still have 2-cycle engines requiring a gas and oil mix (see linked push-mower gas article, not in this source).
- fact: Regular gas is fine: per US Energy Information Administration, most regular is 87 octane and 10% ethanol. Check octane and ethanol before buying; some stations offer E15 (15% ethanol). Avoid E15 and E85 (85%).
- tip: Read decals on the pump for octane and ethanol; pump handle colours are not consistent between stations.
- fact: Ethanol: corn-based; attracts moisture from the air which makes gas and ethanol separate; separated ethanol/water sinks to tank bottom and is fed through the engine causing ignition problems; leaves gummy deposits; corrosive and burns hot, can cause overheating and significant damage.
- fact: Gasoline has a short shelf life; begins to break down after days. Buy fresh from busy stations; buy only what you can use in 30 days.
- fact: Store unused fuel in an approved container (some plastics degrade) in a dry location out of rain; water in fuel makes it run rough.
- fact: Ethanol-free options: recreation fuel REC-90 at some stations (station locator pure-gas.org lists US and Canada); 4-cycle fuel canisters at hardware stores or online. Non-ethanol fuel can cost up to $2.00 more per gallon at the pump, substantially more in canisters. No stabilizer needed for REC-90 or canned 4-cycle fuel (no ethanol), but it does no harm.
- opinion: Author recommends higher-quality, lower-ethanol fuel if you can afford it; more expensive is not always better.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: Best way to store unused gas: use a quality plastic gas can designed for unleaded gas; add a fuel additive (author likes Sea Foam Engine Treatment) with the extra gas; avoid buying more than a 30-day supply; empty the can you're using before adding new gas and use stored gas first (don't add fresh to old); at season end discard leftover appropriately (can go into a car or other machine); never let a gas can sit for months after season.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Wrong or old gas can damage the fuel system and engine; E15/E85 to be avoided.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Gasoline can damage or corrode fuel parts causing them to fail/leak; signs: smell, wet spots on the mower, burn marks in the lawn; leak list is in a separate article (not in this source).
