- fact: 12 reasons table: worn blade (sharpen/replace); low tire pressure; bent/damaged blade (replace); blade installed wrong (sail up); clogged deck (scrape); unleveled deck (side-to-side and front-to-back); spindle damage/worn bearing; bad belt/pulleys; engine speed too low (full throttle); ground speed too fast; not overlapping path; damaged deck shell. Bonus: uneven lawn.
- tip: check tire pressures first, before judging blades or deck level, to avoid unnecessary replacement/adjustment.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: worn blades leave uncut strips; grass turns brown a day or two after mowing; tips brown with little cuts/tears.
- fact: extremely worn blades are thin on the sails -> replace (should have been replaced sooner). Sharpen if still good. Author has blades professionally sharpened for a better edge.
- confirm bent: bends can be 90 degrees or slight; even slight cause bad cut and vibration. Method 1: lay removed blade on a new blade. Method 2: flat-level spot, spark plug boots off; measure ground to blade end, record; spin 180 degrees, measure opposite; over 1/8" difference = replace; repeat for each blade.
- mistake: never straighten a bent blade; weakens metal; may break off and fly.
- fact: balance: equal weight each side; shave metal from heavier side; best with a blade balancer; nail-on-wall method works but is less accurate. Balanced blades give level cut, less vibration, protect spindle housings.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- see Method 2 above (1/8" tolerance).

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: when one tire is lower the deck sits unevenly; left and right tires must have same pressure, at manufacturer spec.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: sail must point upward toward the deck; many blades are stamped; remember "Sail Up". Upside down: sails hit the ground and beat the grass and can damage spindles and spindle housings. Fix: remove and reinstall sail up.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: plugged deck stops air movement/suction under deck; blades may hit debris each pass. Cut relies on blade speed creating airflow suction that stands grass tall.
- fix: scrape the deck with a deck scraper or wire brush.
- tip: don't cut wet grass; deck spray helps but doesn't prevent everything.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- confirm: flat-level surface; measure ground to deck at each corner; compare side-to-side and front-to-back; front should sit slightly lower than back.
- fact: specs and adjustment vary by deck type; see owner's manual.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: work gloves; grip each side of the blade; rock up and down; movement or knocking = likely bearing failure; take apart spindle housing.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: turn each deck pulley; should be smooth with no resistance or bearing noise; otherwise replace pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- confirm: glazed, cracking, fraying; worn belt may fail to turn blades at top speed; replace.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: run at full throttle for blade speed.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: thick, tall, wet grass requires slower ground speed to avoid load on engine; too fast pushes over grass instead of standing it tall.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: wide non-overlapping paths leave small strips; overlap passes.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: shell can be tweaked by hitting trees, rocks; shaped to create suction. Repair or replace; if replacing, move components from old deck to new shell.
