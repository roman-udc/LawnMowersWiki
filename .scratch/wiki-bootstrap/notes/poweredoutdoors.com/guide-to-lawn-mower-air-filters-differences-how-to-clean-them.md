## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: air filter is typically near the top of the engine above the carburetor, behind a plastic or metal shroud (cover) usually held by thumb screws or clips.
- safety: before removing the filter turn off the engine and remove the spark plug boot; engine can be hot.
- step 1: turn off engine, remove spark plug boot.
- step 2: remove engine shroud (push mower: usually one thumb screw; larger: a couple of thumb screws or clips; canister type: housing cap held by a couple of clips).
- step 3: remove filter without knocking dirt into the intake; wipe remaining dirt from housing with a dry cloth.
- step 4 (paper): tap filter to knock dirt out; hold up to light; if you cannot see light through the paper element in areas, or it is damaged or oil-covered, replace it.
- step 4 (foam): wash with dish soap and water, rinse until soap is gone, squeeze out water, lay flat in the sun to dry; once dry apply filter oil until lightly saturated but not dripping (paper towel can absorb excess); replace if excessively dirty, dark spots or tears.
- step 4 (foam pre-cleaner): wash with (warm) soap and water, lay flat in the sun to dry; replace if dry and brittle, torn, or excessively dirty.
- step 4 (canister, 18 HP+): pull the inner filter out of the outer filter (sometimes difficult but they separate); tap, check against light; replace if light doesn't shine through, or filter damaged or extremely dirty; put inner into outer and back in the housing.
- step 5: refit pre-cleaner around the main paper filter (12-17 HP) and put it in the housing; reinstall shroud.
- mistake: do NOT blow out a paper filter with an air compressor; it can do more harm than good; only tap it.
- fact: types and sizes by engine:
- fact: push mower engines up to 10 HP: thin, flat, small filter of paper or foam, roughly 5 in x 7 in; may have a small foam pre-cleaner/sponge in front; low airflow need. Change after 25 hours of use; check condition monthly; sooner in dusty conditions.
- fact: 12 to 17 HP (common on v-twins): oval/round filter 8 to 10 in, about 3 in deep, paper element with sponge pre-cleaner wrapped around. Change after 200 hours; clean every 100 hours; more often in dry dusty conditions; pre-cleaner can be cleaned/replaced more often to extend the main element.
- fact: 18 HP and larger/commercial: canister-style paper filter with inner filter, about 10 in long and 4 in diameter. Change after 250 hours; clean every 100 hours; more often if dusty. Some manufacturers fit a smaller filter (like the 12-17 HP type) on high-end residential and entry-level commercial mowers to keep price down, and offer an upgrade to a larger commercial system.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: engine needs air, fuel, spark, compression; clogged filter can stop engine, cause smoking, or major damage.
- symptom: restricted airflow makes the engine look for air elsewhere, including the crankcase; engine starves for air and overheats; if not caught internal components can melt and engine seize.
- fact: running without a filter pulls dirt into the engine, damaging piston, cylinder and carburetor, sometimes irreversibly.
- fact: a plugged/excessively dirty filter is one reason a mower smokes (other causes: bad gaskets, piston ring problems).
