## page: procedures/replace-the-idler-pulley
- brand: Exmark idler pulley assembly = carriage bolt, lock nut, pulley, spacer (spacer sometimes mounted to deck with aluminum teardrop-shaped piece; or a bushing with wider bottom, narrower top that goes through pulley).
- fact: Pulley with riveted construction: bearing cannot be replaced alone; replace pulley and bearing assembly (Exmark sells pulleys with bearing installed). A seized bearing can take out the bushing holding the pulley (bushing is not part of the bearing). Carriage bolt often gets damaged and falls out; it is grade 5, 3/8 in, about 3 1/2 in long.
- confirm: turn each pulley; resistance or bearing noise = replace; or pulley seized / bearing blown out of center.
- parts: pulley, carriage bolt, spacer, lock nut.
- tools: 3/8 wrench, socket, jack stands (if needed).
- step 1: engine off, remove spark plug boot; author keeps key in pocket.
- step 2: remove the deck belt (most current Exmark decks use a self-tensioning spring; older models use spring-loaded tensioners that must be removed first).
- step 3: raise deck to the highest position (jack stands if needed to reach).
- step 4: scrape deck and clean debris where the pulley bolt goes through so bolt sits square.
- step 5: from underside slide carriage bolt through the hole; on top slide spacer over bolt, pulley on spacer, loosely tighten nut with 3/8 wrench.
- step 6: carriage bolt square portion near head must fit the square hole in deck; hold bolt tight against deck from underneath and tighten lock nut from top with socket; torque per owner's manual. Lock nut typically flanged; flange contacts the bearing so pulley spins properly. High-speed pulley; ensure hardware in place before replacing belt.
- step 7: replace belt (see replace-the-deck-belt).

## page: procedures/replace-the-deck-belt
- fact: replace when: shiny/glazed; burnt marks; frayed, cracked, snapped; squealing when deck engaged; blades slip when mowing.
- step 1: remove belt covers and lift foot pan; no tools needed on Exmark zero-turn deck unless entry-level Exmark Quest or an older Exmark.
- step 2: find belt routing decal on deck (also in owner's manual); if missing/worn, photograph the belt before removal.
- step 3: remove belt. Most Exmark mowers have self-tensioning spring that needn't be removed; older units and Quest use a tension spring that must be removed.
- step 4a (decks without self-adjusting spring): route new belt per decal/photo and install the tension spring.
- step 4b (self-adjusting spring decks): slide belt around the pulley closest to the engine, pull belts forward to front of deck; spring-loaded tensioner is on the right-hand side: wrap belt around the tensioner pulley then the discharge-side blade pulley; continue around remaining pulleys in decal pattern to the trim-side blade pulley (opposite discharge chute).
- step 5: with belt on all pulleys except trim-side blade pulley, hold belt with two hands, pull tight; start on one side of the pulley, push belt into groove with palm slowly; walk belt around the pulley as you slowly turn it.
- step 6: rotate belt around pulleys a couple of times to check it rides on all; tug to check tensioner spring reacts.
- step 7: with deck raised, grease the deck, the idler arm underneath, and under the unit where the hydraulic belt tensioner is, so both tensioner arms have grease in the cavity.
- step 8: before refitting belt covers, start engine and engage deck two or three times to confirm belt stays; then install covers.
- tip: easiest to start at the pulley nearest the engine.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- confirm: bad rumble or vibration when PTO engaged = likely bad spindle bearing in cutter housing. Raise deck as high as it goes (jack stands), key and spark plug boot removed, work gloves, rock each blade by both ends; movement or knocking = bearing bad, replace cutter housing.
- parts: nut, washer, pulley, key, shaft, spindle housing (six parts). Not all will need replacing if caught early.
- tip: disassemble first to identify needed parts (shaft threads can be damaged on disassembly); bring model and serial number to dealer (housings and spindles vary by deck). Dealer staff can identify parts if you bring the assembly.
- step 1: remove top nut from center of pulley (glove on one hand; socket or impact gun).
- step 2: remove washer and spindle shaft; usually falls out of housing. If not: remove keyway (tiny bar on side of shaft) and shaft should fall through bearings; else put nut back on shaft thread and tap with soft mallet or wood block; if still stuck buy a new shaft with the housing.
- step 3: remove the spindle housing (cutter housing): remove 6 or 8 bolts/nuts (depending on model) holding it to deck.
- step 4 (install): scrape grass from around the hole so the new housing sits flat on deck shell.
- step 5: slide housing in from underside through the deck hole; tighten bolts/nuts a little at a time in a star/cross pattern so it pulls tight equally; torque per owner's manual.
- step 6: insert spindle shaft from bottom of housing toward top of deck; insert keyway into groove so shaft is held.
- step 7: install pulley on top of the shaft; washer with beveled side down; add nut and torque to factory spec.
- step 8: reinstall blades (sharp and balanced) and deck belt.
- fact: procedure works for any of the three spindle housings; difference only with a double-stacked pulley in the center of the deck (center shaft commonly longer than outer two).

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- tools: socket and ratchet, work gloves, safety glasses, wrench, optional cordless impact gun.
- step 1: park on flat level surface; blade (PTO) switch off; engine off; remove key; wait for moving parts to stop.
- step 2: raise deck to transport position or 5-inch height; jack stands under front of mower.
- step 3: remove spark plug boot (do not skip).
- step 4: remove blades with socket/ratchet (or impact gun). Most mower blades are reverse-threaded (called right-hand threaded by the author): remove nut/bolt by spinning counterclockwise [as stated in Source].
- tip: stuck bolt: put block of wood on bolt head and hit with hammer to shock it loose.
- step 5: install: older/standard decks use bolt and washer; newer higher-series Exmark decks use a splined bushing through the blade center. Install splined bushing (if used), blade bolt and washer into the spindle; finger tight; wrench on top spindle nut; torque blade bolts 50-60 ft-lb.
- brand/torque: Exmark blade bolt 50-60 ft-lb.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- step: underside: remove grass/debris from underside and discharge deflector using wire brush or metal scraper.
- step: top: with belt covers off and foot pan lifted/removed, clean top of deck. Spindle bearings are sealed but not against high pressure: do not pressure wash; use garden hose around spindle housings and pulleys.
