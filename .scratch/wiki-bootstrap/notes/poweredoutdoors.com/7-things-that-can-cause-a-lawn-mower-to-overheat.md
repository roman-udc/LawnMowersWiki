## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "my mower smells hot while mowing"
- alias: "lawn mower overheating"
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- symptom: mower smells hot while mowing; stop the engine and find the cause before greater engine damage
- fact: article lists 8 things: low oil, wrong oil type, plugged/damaged cooling fins, clogged air filter, damaged/missing engine guard, plugged mower deck, dull blades, overworking the engine
- safety: follow operator's manual safety instructions; consult a professional if lacking skills
- tip: do not spray water into the engine when washing the mower; it pushes dirt and water into the engine area and compounds fin clogging

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: engine overheats (problems/engine-overheats)
- fact: modern air-cooled mower engines use air-cooled engine oil with high zinc concentration; zinc is described as an additive used as a cooling agent. Regular motor oil has little zinc because it is for liquid-cooled engines. "Oil is oil" is called untrue.
- fact: most manufacturers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30; may need 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures. Consult owner's manual; article references a Kawasaki 4-cycle oil viscosity/temperature chart.
- fact: low oil causes extra friction which can overheat the mower; running too long on low oil makes the oil thicker and can cause significant internal engine damage
- fix: change oil regularly; check oil level before each use and top up per manufacturer spec
- confirm: if consistently low on oil, check whether it is leaking, burning or being consumed
- fact: oil leak - look over the engine for signs, replace the gaskets that leak; if mower has an oil filter, check it seals properly
- fact: burning oil - check for plugged air filter (said to cause oil burn and make engine work harder); clean or replace the filter
- fact: using oil - can be due to overheating in use, or a valve or ring problem; take mower to a repair center for diagnosis
- pro: engine consuming oil (possible valve or ring problem) needs repair-center diagnosis

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: engine overheats
- fact: broken fins or fins clogged with dirt, grass and engine oil stop air passing around cylinder heads and engine block, causing overheating
- fact: the fan on top of the engine pulls cool air in, blows it down over hot parts and out the bottom
- fix: every fall remove the engine covers and clean the cooling fins; otherwise have a repair shop do it as part of regular maintenance
- tip: a small-engine mechanic inspects the fins during annual maintenance; owners should look at them too
- mistake: washing the engine pushes dirt and water into the engine area

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- step 1: remove the engine covers (every fall)
- step 2: clean out the cooling fins of dirt, grass and oil
- tip: do not spray water into the engine

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- symptom: engine overheats; the "engine guard"/shield is not in place
- fact: a screw can fall out so the guard shifts out of position; cool air then exits where the shield is no longer mounted and the engine area stays hot
- fix: check the engine shield regularly; tighten or replace mounting screws

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: engine runs hot because it is starving for air and works harder to breathe
- confirm: shine a flashlight through the filter; a good amount of light = OK; dull or no light = replace
- fix: author recommends replacing the air filter a couple of times a year, more often in dusty conditions (opinion)
- fact: some engines have an inner filter (narrower, fits inside a canister-style filter) or a foam pre-cleaner placed around the outside of the filter
- fact: air filter is cheap compared with fixing or replacing an engine damaged by debris
- mistake: skipping the air filter is the first thing cheap owners skip

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: flashlight test (see clogged-air-filter); replace a couple of times a year; foam pre-cleaner and inner filter exist on some engines

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: engine overheats; dull and rounded blades put a bigger load on the engine so it makes more heat
- tip: dull blades give poor cut and can turn lawn brown by beating up the grass
- fix: check blades regularly for damage and wear; sharpen (article links its own blade-inspection article)

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: engine overworks and may overheat; grass and dirt built up under the deck keep blades from spinning freely so they jam or work harder
- fix: clean the deck regularly with a deck scraper; inspect for damage
- tip: no product keeps all debris from sticking, but you can reduce it: always run at full throttle; avoid cutting wet grass; mow regularly so grass doesn't get too long and clump; coat the underside of the deck with Teflon or silicone spray designed for mower decks

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- symptom: engine overworked, may overheat
- fix: let grass dry before cutting; if very long, double or triple cut: set the deck higher and mow, then lower it a little and cut again, so an overgrown yard isn't cut in one pass

## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- fact: after mowing with a riding mower, let the engine cool before putting it in storage or a garage
- fact: best to open the hood outdoors and let the mower run under no load; normal cooling then begins
- fact: article links its own "Lawn Mower Engine Cooling Times Explained"

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: high-zinc air-cooled oil; SAE30 or 10W-30; 20W-50 for hot ambient (see wrong-oil-or-low-oil)
