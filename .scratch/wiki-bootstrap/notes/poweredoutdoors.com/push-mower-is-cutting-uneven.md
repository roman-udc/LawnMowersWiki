## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "push mower uneven or bad cut"
- alias: "grass tips turn brown a couple of days after cutting"
- causes-named: bad-tire, wrong-cutting-height, dull-or-bent-blade, worn-blade-sail, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: push mower cuts unevenly when a wheel is damaged, cutting height set differently, blade worn/bent/unbalanced, deck plugged, engine speed too low, or ground speed too fast.
- safety: never work under the deck without making sure the mower can't start; disconnect the spark plug wire.

## page: causes/bad-tire
- fact: a damaged or loose push-mower wheel makes one side sit lower than the other. Wheels of unequal size, broken, or not securely attached must be repaired or replaced.

## page: causes/wrong-cutting-height
- fact: all wheel height settings must be the same or the mower won't sit level.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: hitting a stump or rock can bend the blade; a bent blade not parallel to the ground cuts unevenly. Severe bends visible; otherwise remove and match to a new blade, or measure.
- confirm: first confirm wheels equal size and undamaged and cutting height settings all the same. With mower on flat even surface, measure ground to blade tip; rotate blade 180 degrees; measure from the same ground spot to tip. Difference greater than 1/8" -> replace blade.
- fact: dull edge tears and beats up grass instead of clean cut; evidence is grass tips browning a couple of days later.
- fact: sharpen blades after every 25 hours of use, more often in sandy conditions.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: the sail (high side of the blade) creates air movement under the deck; with the deck shell it gives the even cut. Replace the blade when the metal on the sail is thinner than the center of the blade.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- step 1: confirm wheels equal and undamaged, cutting height settings equal.
- step 2: mower on flat even surface; measure ground to blade tip; record.
- step 3: rotate blade 180 degrees; measure from same ground spot to tip; record.
- step 4: difference greater than 1/8" -> replace blade.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: clogged deck strains engine and gives bad cut; deck shell must be clean so air moves under it to create suction that lifts grass tall so the blade can cut it evenly.
- fix: periodically scrape with a deck scraper or metal brush. Avoid mowing wet grass (sticks to deck and clumps in yard).

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: throttle should be set high; when deck lacks power, blade tip speed slows leaving a bad/uneven cut. Many mowers have no throttle lever; they run high speed as soon as the bail lever is engaged.
- pro: if engine lost power, take to an experienced small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: adjust speed to conditions; thick, wet or tall grass strains the engine. Too fast pushes grass over instead of cutting and bounces mower on uneven ground.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: overlap paths or strips of grass are left.
