## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: Keep the mower out of frigid winter elements and free of moisture.
- tools: wire brush; deck scraper or putty knife; rubber gloves; rags/paper towel; fuel stabilizer; gas container (if emptying tank); cardboard/newspaper for oil; for oil change: plastic funnel, socket wrench or spanner wrenches for drain plug, filter and filter wrench, 2-3 quarts oil per owner's manual.
- step 1: Spray off with a garden hose; careful around the deck so as not to damage bearings. Wash early on a sunny day so it dries completely; handheld leaf blower helps. Never store wet (moisture collects in electrical components, rusts parts).
- step 2: Change engine oil if dirty or 50 hours since last change.
- step 3: Disconnect spark plug boot(s) (do not skip).
- step 4: Access deck underside; inspect/remove blades with work gloves if they need sharpening or replacement; scrape deck with scraper/putty knife (wire brush also); reconnect spark plug boots after.
- step 5: Fuel stabilizer or drain tank.
- step 6: Battery.
- step 7: Check air filter, fuel filter, spark plugs; replace if needed; author changes yearly.
- step 8: Lubricate cables and grease all grease zerks.
- step 9: Store in cool dry place; tarp or drop cloth indoors; waterproof material outdoors.
- step 10: Put cardboard under pneumatic tires if the mower has them (source says these are the small caster wheels at the front of the zero-turn); keep them off cold concrete.
- step 11: Rodent prevention.
- fact: Wires on many mowers are coated with soy products, an edible material rodents chew. Use deterrent safe for pets: mothballs around the mower, or (author, opinion) Grandpa Gus's Mouse Repellent (all-natural; extra strength packets plus spray on wires; packets around equipment). Author says rodent control can save thousands in electrical repairs.
- fact: Storage: garage/pole barn - tarp for dust. Outdoors under deck/overhang: rain falls between deck boards, snow blows; author uses heavy-duty marine-grade fabric cover. Indoors (garage, pole barn, shed) is best.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: Change if dirty or 50 hours since last.
- step 1: Warm oil by running a couple of minutes and shut off; disconnect spark plug lead(s).
- step 2: Drain oil, remove old filter, install new filter, fill per engine manufacturer's directions.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Drain option: siphon as much as possible, run mower until it shuts off and won't restart. Author does not drain.
- fact: If storing with fuel, must use stabilizer; otherwise spring starting problems; old fuel causes gumming and buildup.
- fact: Author prefers Sea Foam (stabilizes and reduces moisture); add to a full tank per bottle directions, run a few minutes; stabilizes fuel up to 2 years.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: A 100% fully charged battery will not freeze. Remove and store cool and dry, or ensure fully charged.
- step: Gloves and eye protection; mower off; remove hold-down brackets; adjustable wrench turn black negative terminal counterclockwise and remove cable, then red positive; wire brush corrosion; store in cool dry place away from combustibles.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- tip: Replace air filter, fuel filter, spark plugs once a year.
