## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Cub Cadet mower is smoking"
- alias: "black smoke", "blue smoke", "white smoke"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-choke-setting, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, bad-head-gasket
- fact: Smoke color is a clue. Black smoke: engine running too rich (more fuel burned than air); check air filter first, clean or replace; if not, check other airflow restriction (stuck choke, plugged air filter). Blue or white smoke: burning excess oil; check oil level first, then piston rings, valve train, engine gasket.
- tip: Start with the easiest checks and rule them out first.
- confirm: Check order: air filter, oil level (low/high), oil on spark plug (piston rings/cylinder wall), valve train, gasket.
- pro: Piston ring/cylinder damage and valve train problems need engine teardown/head removal; small engine shop if not mechanical.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: black smoke; engine can't breathe, may overheat; engine pulls air and oil out of the crankcase, oil burns off and smokes (problems/mower-is-smoking).
- confirm: Remove air filter cover (above the carburetor; usually single screw or two knobs; some filters are along the side of the engine next to carburetor). Remove filter carefully so dirt doesn't drop into the intake; wipe out loose dirt with a clean dry cloth. Inspect filter. Hold paper filter up to light: if no light passes through or it is damaged, replace.
- fix: Replace if it looks bad; otherwise knock loose dirt out and reinstall.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove air filter cover (always above the carburetor; single screw or two knobs).
- step 2: Remove filter carefully, keep dirt out of air intake; wipe loose dirt with clean dry cloth.
- step 3: Inspect; hold up to light (paper filter); if light can't be seen through or filter damaged, replace; else knock out loose dirt and refit.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: smoking; low oil means increased friction and extreme heat; parts can melt and fail.
- fact: Low oil from an oil leak or more significant engine problem. Can try adding oil and see if it runs, but often extensive engine damage cannot be fixed by adding oil; take to small engine mechanic.
- fact: Engine oil must be at the manufacturer's recommended level.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: smoke when engine runs at full speed (oil in cylinder burns off).
- fact: Too much oil causes excess crankcase pressure that pushes oil into cylinder and air intake.
- fix: Drain some oil out of the engine (source links article on draining a little oil).

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- fact: Source only says drain some oil out of crankcase; detail in a linked article not provided.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- symptom: oil on spark plug (sign of bigger internal engine problem); scored cylinder wall lets oil into the combustion chamber, burns, smoke.
- confirm: Remove spark plug with a socket (may need 3/4" or 5/8" socket) and check for excessive oil.
- fix: Engine teardown required; local small engine repair shop. Often the engine must be replaced; depending on mower may be better to buy a new mower (opinion).

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- symptom: valve train problem; muffler glowing red while running can indicate burnt valve or timing problem.
- fact: Burnt valve: valve overheated, edges fall apart. Only detected by removing cylinder head or performing a leak-down test, done by a small engine mechanic. Fix: replace the valve and grind the seat (seat is where valve contacts engine block to complete the combustion chamber); both valve and seat cut at certain angles.

## page: causes/bad-head-gasket
- symptom: bad engine gasket may leak oil onto muffler, burns, smoke.
- fix: Find and replace the bad gasket; difficulty depends on the gasket's location.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: Change regularly; skipping oil changes shortens engine life and damages it (source links "Engine Damage is Likely if You Skip Mower Oil Changes").

## page: procedures/check-compression
- fact: Leak-down test mentioned (by a mechanic) to detect valve train problem.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Check engine oil level and air filter before each mowing to avoid large expensive repairs.
