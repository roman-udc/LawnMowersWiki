## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Craftsman mower loses power and bogs down"
- alias: "engine not giving the power it once did"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-air-filter, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Loses power when the engine doesn't get good airflow and fuel flow, or runs hot. Result of low oil, clogged air passageways, plugged air filters, dirty carb or restricted fuel components. Also from load: plugged deck or too fast ground speed.
- tip: Fix performance problems early; engine repairs get expensive.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Sluggish running; mowing is dusty so filter plugs.
- fact: Replace with a new filter annually, clean several times through the season; more often in very dusty conditions.
- fix: Clean paper filter - see procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: Instructions are for a paper-style filter.
- step 1: Remove the air filter housing cover (often clips, knobs or wing nuts).
- step 2: Carefully remove the filter so dirt doesn't fall into the air intake.
- step 3: Clean dirt remaining in the housing with a dry clean rag.
- step 4: Tap the filter against a hard surface to knock dirt loose.
- step 5: Hold the filter to a light. If light shines through the paper, reuse it. If not, or it is damaged or oily, replace it.
- step 6: Install the filter and reattach the cover.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas begins to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Ethanol and moisture drawn into the system may leave gummy deposits restricting fuel flow.
- fix: Drain tank, refill with fresh gasoline.
- brand: Craftsman gas mowers require unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Current Craftsman mowers have 4-cycle engines needing straight gasoline; older Craftsman mowers may have 2-cycle engines requiring a gas and oil mixture.
- tip: Add Sea Foam Motor Treatment to stabilize gas, reduce moisture, clean the fuel system; also when you have more gas than you can use in 30 days. The author adds Sea Foam to every tank (opinion).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Strains fuel leaving the tank. Replace annually in routine maintenance (inexpensive part).
- step 1: Shut off fuel with the mower's shut-off valve or fuel pinch-off pliers if none.
- step 2: Pull the filter out of the fuel lines and install a new one.
- fact: Arrow on the filter side must point in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Gummy deposits from old gas clog the lines.
- confirm: Stop fuel flow, remove the end of a section into a container, start fuel flow and check for good flow.
- fix: Stop flow, remove the section, spray carburetor cleaner to loosen clogs, blow compressed air through. If unclearable or the line is dry and cracked, replace with a new line of the same length and diameter.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carb regulates fuel and air to the cylinder; residue from old fuel collects in it.
- confirm: Remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake, start the engine. If it runs but won't stay running, clean the carb.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Intermittent spark and power loss. Inspect tip for carbon, dirt and oil buildup.
- fix: Replace a dirty/damaged plug. If only dirty and not very dark, may clean with a wire brush and reuse.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Low crankcase oil causes power loss. Causes: not enough added at last change, oil leak, or burning oil from wrong viscosity.
- fact: Lack of lubrication creates friction and extreme heat; engine may overheat and smoke from burning oil and melting parts; could need an engine replacement.
- confirm: Check oil before each use. Remove dipstick, wipe with clean rag, reinsert, remove again and read; oil must be at the full mark. Add fresh oil until full; don't overfill.
- pro: If problems persist after refilling, have an experienced small engine mechanic diagnose; low oil may have caused internal damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Engine smokes; pressure builds and oil pushed into cylinder via valve train; bluish-white smoke when it burns.
- fact: Thick smoke can plug the air filter, causing running problems; check/clean/replace air filter and spark plug.
- mistake: Continuing to run with too much oil can cause seal damage, hydro lock and a bent piston rod.
- fix: Remove a little oil by loosening and quickly tightening the drain plug to let a little out; or pull oil from the oil filter or fill area with a turkey baster or oil evacuator.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- step 1: Loosen and quickly tighten the drain plug to release only a little oil, or
- step 2: Remove oil from the filter or fill area with a turkey baster or oil evacuator.
- tools: turkey baster or oil evacuator.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Engine is air cooled; air circulates around block and cylinder head. Keep cooling fins free of grass clippings and mud; replace damaged fins. Also clean around the engine shroud and air intake areas.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: Remove debris collected under the engine shroud; make sure the heat shield is securely in place (blocked air passageways).

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Fast ground speed adds load in thick, wet or tall grass. Slow down for conditions, including on inclines.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Wet grass makes engine work harder and clumps under the deck; mow dry.
- tip: Tall grass: double or triple-cut. First cut with deck at highest setting, then lower for next cut.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Packed deck strains the engine turning blades through debris. Scrape deck regularly; also helps cut quality.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull blades magnify a plugged-deck problem; need more engine power. Sharpen or replace.
