## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Simplicity riding and zero-turn: unleaded gasoline min 87 octane, max 10% ethanol.
- fact: regular gas OK if ethanol not above 10%; regular is generally 87 octane (cites US EIA).
- fact: avoid E15, E30, E85 (up to 15%, 30%, 85% ethanol); max E10; lower ethanol better.
- fact: ethanol is corn-based (or other high-starch plant); fine for most vehicles, damaging to small gas engines; attracts moisture so ethanol/water separates and sinks to tank bottom; separated ethanol fed through engine causes ignition problems and gummy deposits; corrosive and burns hot -> overheating, significant damage.
- fact: ethanol-free fuel is best for small engine though costlier; sold as recreation fuel or REC-90 at some stations.
- fact: source inconsistency: tips list says "minimum 89-octane" while the rest says minimum 87. Flag.
- safety: refuel in well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors; wait a few minutes for engine to cool before removing gas cap; do not overfill.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; buy fresh and use within 30 days; old gas causes starting and running problems.
- tip: buy from a busy fuel station; add stabilizer to fresh gas (author adds Sea Foam Motor Treatment to every tank; keeps fuel stable, reduces moisture, keeps system clean; must be added to fresh fuel, cannot reverse old-fuel effects); STA-BIL is an alternative.
- tip: store in approved fuel container; keep away from elements and moisture; dry location away from combustibles.
