## page: procedures/clean-the-gas-tank
- tools: face mask, safety glasses, fuel container/canister, flashlight, fuel additive; (also carb cleaner, small pin, rags, acetone-based cleaner, vinegar, or small rocks/pebbles)
- safety: dangers are fumes, fire, hazardous chemicals; read operating manual; no smoking near tank, away from flames/heat; store drained gas safely; well-ventilated area; face mask; safety glasses for harsh chemicals; keep a water hose nearby to dilute spills
- step 1: work in a safe environment (above)
- step 2: check and remove the spark plug (so engine can't start; gas fumes can remain even with tank removed); also disconnect the mower battery
- step 3: drain the tank: canister directly below tank, clamp the fuel line to the carburetor, detach fuel line, let drain completely; use flashlight to inspect tank for cracks, holes, debris, rust; may be repairable, extreme cases need replacement
- step 4: clean the carburetor (see below)
- step 5: rinse the tank: do NOT rinse with water (causes problems for carburetor, spark plug, engine); use a small amount of FRESH gasoline (not the drained fuel); dispose of the rinse gas at a hazardous waste site
- step 6: clean the metal tank: acetone-containing cleaner (evaporates quickly so no condensation): add cleaner, shake, pour out into a disposable container; careful and ventilated. Alternative (author's method): remove empty tank, add a few handfuls of small rocks or pebbles and a little solvent (author uses kerosene or diesel; even water works), shake side to side/up and down, rinse; repeat a couple times until no rust rinses out
- step 7: examine fuel filter and lines: replace filter every year; check lines for cracks/dry rot; most lines are 1/4 inch or 5/16 inch rubber hose; author recommends worm gear clamps instead of pinch-style clamps to minimize leaks
- step 8: put back together, replacing needed parts: carburetor bowl, main gasket, needles, nut, fuel filter, gas tank, spark plug; reconnect spark plug and battery
- step 9: fill with new fuel; should start; if not, another issue
- fact: signs tank needs cleaning: sat a few months, engine not performing, gasoline more than three months old, debris in tank
- fact: only needed when mower has sat for a long time; neglect can cause engine misfire, poor performance, reduced fuel efficiency
- tip: dispose of old fuel properly; do not use it in other tools or vehicles

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step 1: remove outer castings (screws) and the air filter
- step 2: undo screws/bolts holding carburetor; fuel may leak (rag); if no fuel appears when removing, the fuel line may be blocked: replace the line and find the cause
- step 3: clean the bowl with carb cleaner; unscrew the nut to remove the bowl; check the hole in the nut for debris (a main cause of carb not working), clear with a small pin, clean with carb cleaner
- step 4: damage check: bowl too dirty to clean, clogged nut, cracks or holes in bowl; replace bowl and/or nut
- step 5: replace both needles (one in the bowl holding the float; other on a small gasket inside carburetor)
- step 6: clean carburetor and all holes with carb cleaner and pin
- step 7: replace the main gasket (wears out over time)
- step 8: reassemble; take phone photos during teardown
- fact: bad carb symptoms: reduced engine power or gurgling noise
- tip: carburetor repair kit available; replace internal parts once per year; cleaning carb periodically extends life

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- fact: replace spark plug every year
- confirm: inspect for broken electrode, carbon buildup, corrosion; if looks fine, do an ignition test with an ignition tester; no spark means replace
- fact: dirty, old or corroded plug can cause misfires, poor performance, increased fuel use

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: change fuel at least every three months even if you rarely mow
- fact: fuel stabilizer prevents fuel going bad quickly; author likes Sea Foam, which stabilizes fuel for two years, helps with residue and deposits, reduces moisture buildup
- tip: preventive: fresh fuel, stabilizer, clean/repair the carburetor

## page: causes/dirty-fuel-tank
- symptom: tank full of debris, rust or old gasoline may not start after long inactivity (problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start)
- fact: consequences of rusted metal tank: fuel line blockage, irregular fuel injection (sic), rust inside engine
- fix (rust): after acetone cleaner, fill with vinegar and let sit overnight to dissolve/soften rust; finish cleaning in the morning; allow plenty of time to dry; abrasives to knock rust loose: aquarium gravel, BBs, gravel, loose bolts; rust holes or cracks mean new tank
- tip (prevent rust): swirl baking soda and water mixture in tank, dispose, flush with soapy water, then put a small amount of diesel in to coat tank with light oil; doesn't harm gasoline

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: inspect with flashlight for cracks, holes; rust holes/cracks need new tank (repair sometimes possible)

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: check lines for cracks and dry rot; replace
