## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "mower deck is vibrating", "shaken out of my seat"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down (wrong/incorrectly installed blades), debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, bent-crankshaft
- safety: Remove key and unplug spark plug boots before working on deck; raise deck with jack stands or comparable.
- fact: Vibration from damaged/unbalanced blades, material wrapped on blade shaft, worn belt/pulleys, damaged spindle/housing, debris in deck or under pulleys; push mower: bent crankshaft.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: bent blades, worn blades, chunks missing from hitting solid objects; unbalanced by wear or damage -> vibration.
- fact: Sand can wear blades unevenly; incorrect sharpening also unbalances. Sharpen evenly on both sides. Balance: blade balancer or nail on wall; center hole on nail; grind more from the side that hangs lower until level.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: Sail (high side) must point up toward deck; upside down beats up the grass instead of cutting.
- fact: Wrong/incorrect blade after recent replacement: ensure blade fits the mower (correct length and center hole size). Author (sold blades for years): not all blades are the same; some aftermarket blades with matching length and center hole still don't fit; listed specs may differ from the blade received. Match old blade to new before installing. Movement occurs when hardware is missing/wrong order or center hole too big; check parts diagram. Examples: some use blade bolt and washer; others blade bolt, splined bushing and several washers.

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- fact: String, wire or long weed wraps the blade shaft. Remove it and inspect spindle and housing for further damage.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Gloves on, grab both ends of blade and rock up/down; movement = bad bearing.
- fact: Some manufacturers sell replacement bearing to rebuild housing and a complete spindle assembly with bearing; others only rebuild kit or only assembly.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- step 1: Remove belt from the pulley: put something narrow between belt and groove of the bad pulley, turn belt by hand so the object follows the belt to the pulley, pry belt up and off.
- step 2: Remove pulley: socket on the nut on top of pulley; usually normal thread, turn left/counterclockwise. Some mowers have set screws (allen or hex) holding the pulley on shaft; may be stubborn and need a puller.
- step 3: Remove spindle: 4 to 8 bolts hold it; remove bolts and assembly drops out the bottom of the deck.
- step 4: Replace bearing: housing on flat hard surface; sometimes a snap ring holds it together, other times replace entire housing including bearings. Grease the bearing, reassemble in reverse.
- step 5: Bolt housing to deck, install pulley, make sure everything tight with no movement; reinstall belt; turn belt by hand to confirm it is on all pulleys.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: Remove covers to reach belt/pulleys. Replace belt with cracks or glazed look.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: Grab each pulley and turn slowly for resistance; replace if it doesn't turn smoothly or makes noise (bearing failure). Also check bearing and bushings on the engagement linkage. Check tensioner pulley and tensioner arm working; grease tensioner arm so it doesn't seize; it must move.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Wood chips, acorns, pine cones, stones, sticks can lodge under idler pulleys; a pulley not sitting level sends vibration through the deck. Inspect for loose hardware or foreign materials.

## page: causes/bent-crankshaft
- symptom: push mower; changing a bent blade doesn't cure vibration. Hitting something hard with blade spinning can bend crankshaft.
- safety: Operating with a bent crankshaft can be extremely dangerous.
- pro: Small engine repair shop; engine must be torn down to replace crankshaft; often costs more than a new engine; get a quote.
