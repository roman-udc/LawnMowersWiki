## page: problems/engine-overheats
- alias: "Cub Cadet engine smells hot"
- causes-named: wrong-oil-or-low-oil, dirty-cooling-fins, bad-cooling-shroud, clogged-air-filter, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Overheats with wrong oil, low oil, plugged cooling fins, clogged air filter, or engine guard out of place; also when overworked by a plugged deck, dull blades, or too fast ground speed in long, thick or wet grass.
- safety: Stop the mower, remove ignition key, wait for the engine to cool, remove spark plug wires before troubleshooting; follow operator's manual. Don't keep running a hot-smelling engine; extensive damage.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact (wrong oil): Oil helps cool the engine. Air-cooled small engines need air-cooled engine oil with a high zinc concentration (zinc as cooling additive). Car oil has little zinc because it's for liquid-cooled engines.
- fact: Most small engine makers recommend SAE30 or 10W-30 in a Cub Cadet; may need 20W-50 in higher ambient temperatures. Check owner's manual (article references a Kawasaki oil viscosity/temperature chart).
- fix: Drain wrong oil, add correct air-cooled engine oil.
- fact (low oil): Friction builds heat; oil thickens if the engine keeps running low. Check oil before use; add a little if low.
- symptom: consistently low oil: check for (1) oil leak: find source, replace the gaskets leaking, check oil filter seals; (2) burning oil: plugged air filter makes oil burn and engine work harder, clean/replace; (3) using oil: engine overheating, or valve/ring problem; take to a repair center for diagnosis.
- fix: Correct a slightly low level; if that's the cause of the hot smell do a full oil change (heated oil may have thickened). If problems continue after an oil change, low oil may have caused extensive damage; take to a Cub Cadet servicing dealer or engine dealer for additional tests.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Fins push air to the outside of the cylinder head and engine block. A fan on top of the engine pulls cool air in and blows it down; the air passes the hot parts and exits the bottom. Fins plugged with dirt or damaged can't dissipate heat.
- fix: Remove engine cover and clean the fins; replace damaged fins; remove dirt from engine cover and around the engine; make it annual maintenance.
- mistake: Washing the engine can push dirt and water into the engine area; don't spray water into the engine when washing the mower.

## page: procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins
- step 1: Remove engine cover.
- step 2: Clean out cooling fins; replace damaged fins.
- step 3: Remove dirt from the engine cover and around the engine.
- tip: Do annually. Don't spray water into the engine.

## page: causes/bad-cooling-shroud
- fact: The engine guard/shield must be in place so cooling air circulates around the engine; without it, cool air exits the area.
- fix: Check guard is in correct position; tighten or replace mounting screws; replace a missing or damaged guard.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Plugged with dirt, the engine is starved of air, runs hot, works harder to breathe.
- confirm: Remove the filter, shine a flashlight through the paper element; if good light and good condition, clean and reinstall; if dull/no light, damaged, or very dirty and dark, replace.
- brand: Some Cub Cadet engines use an inner air filter (narrow filter inside a canister-style paper filter); some use a foam pre-cleaner wrapped around a paper filter.
- tip: Replace annually, clean several times a season; more often in dusty conditions or above-average use. Don't stretch filter life; it's cheaper than engine repair.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Deck buildup of clippings and dirt reduces open area under the deck used for airflow and makes the engine work harder turning the blades.
- fix: Scrape with a deck scraper; wire brush or putty knife also works. Check often.
- tip: Deck-underside coating products help a bit but aren't miracle products. To reduce clumping, avoid cutting wet grass and always run at full throttle.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: Dull blades magnify the plugged-deck problem and strain the engine.
- fix: Sharpen about twice a season or about every 25 hours (average homeowner). See Cub Cadet blade sharpen/balance article.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: In long, thick or wet grass use a slower ground speed.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: Long, thick or wet grass puts the engine under load and may overheat it.
- tip: Avoid cutting wet grass; double-cut or triple-cut long grass (highest deck setting first, then lower a little and cut again); run full throttle; slow ground speed.

## page: guides/engine-cooling-times
- tip: Let the engine cool before putting the mower in a storage unit or garage. Best to park outdoors; on a riding mower with engine under the hood, lift the hood to expose the engine. Let the engine run under no load to begin normal cooling.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: sharpen twice a season or every ~25 hours.
