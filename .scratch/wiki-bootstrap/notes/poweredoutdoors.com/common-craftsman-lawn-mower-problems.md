## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Craftsman fails to start"
- alias: "Craftsman mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Craftsman may fail to start from a bad battery or charging system, faulty electrical components, and air and fuel restrictions (plugged filters, dirty carb, clogged fuel lines, bad fuel pump).
- fact: Source frames its list of 8 common Craftsman problems as: fails to start; loses power/dies while mowing; smoking; weak hydrostatic transmission; won't drive straight/steer; won't move; vibrating; bad/uneven cut. Some items should be left to a professional mechanic.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Fuel breaks down over time, becoming less efficient and prone to clogging the fuel system (and engine damage).
- fix: Drain the tank and fill with fresh fuel; use an additive like Sea Foam to stabilize fuel and help clean the fuel system.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Vent in cap clogged so tank forms a vacuum restricting fuel flow.
- fix: Use a thin piece of wire to try to clear the clogged vent; replace the cap if needed.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Excessively dirty or broken plug; bad connection; not gapped correctly.
- fix: Replace with a new plug, secure connections, gap to manufacturer specification.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Dirt and grass plug the filter and block airflow (start failure, power loss, smoking).
- fix: Remove and clean the filter; replace if in bad condition.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Dirty filter restricts fuel flow. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Failed or leaking pump no longer creates the pressure to pump fuel. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Bad fuel deposits and dirt build up and clog lines. Fix: carb cleaner and compressed air; replace line if necessary.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Becomes dirty and clogged from bad fuel and ethanol deposits. Fix: clean all carburetor components; replace if needed.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Dead battery, loose cables or corroded terminals cause starting problems. Fix: charge the battery, replace if it won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Make sure cables are tight and clean corrosion from terminals.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Safety switches prevent the mower starting in certain situations; when one fails the mower may not start. Fix: test and replace the faulty switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- fact: Mower will not start when ignition switch is bad. Fix: test and replace a bad switch.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Mower will not start when the coil is bad (listed together with ignition switch: "test and replace").

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: Pull-start mower with damaged or broken recoil.
- fix: Check whether the recoil can be restrung or broken parts like the pulley replaced; replace the recoil assembly if needed.

## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "Craftsman loses power or dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor
- fact: Loses power/dies mid-mow because of an air or fuel restriction. Clogged fuel filter, plugged air filter and dirty carburetor are the most common.
- fact: Bad fuel; plugged air filter; dirty or broken cooling fins (packed with dirt, oil, grass so air cannot cool the engine block; replace broken fins and clean); clogged fuel lines (carb cleaner and compressed air); dirty carburetor (clean all components, replace if needed).

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- fact: Same list as "loses power or dies while mowing" (table titled "Reasons a Craftsman Mower Starts Then Dies"): bad fuel, plugged air filter, dirty/broken cooling fins, clogged fuel lines, dirty carburetor.
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: Replace broken fins and clean the cooling fins.

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Craftsman begins smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-head-gasket, water-in-fuel
- fact: Usually from engine oil burning: oil pushed into the combustion cylinder, or oil leaking onto the hot muffler. Water in the fuel system is another reason.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Excess oil builds pressure in the engine, letting oil get into the cylinder through the valve train.
- fix: Drain a little oil until it is at the manufacturer's recommended level.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil raises friction and causes extreme engine heat.
- fix: Take to a small engine repair shop to inspect for damage; adding oil may be too late.
- pro: Internal problems with piston ring, valve train or engine gasket: bring to local small engine repair shop.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- fact: Internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) have a detrimental effect if not caught soon; send to a small engine shop.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- fact: Named as another reason for smoking; no detail.

## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "Craftsman won't move or go forward"
- causes-named: bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, missing-axle-key, worn-drive-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, missing-idler-arm-spring, bad-hydrostatic-transmission, hot-hydraulic-fluid
- fact: May not move due to a bad or missing drive belt, worn tensioner pulley or missing idler spring. Riding mower axle key may be missing; zero-turn drive release lever may be in the wrong position.
- fact: Drive release lever allows hydrostatic mowers to roll freely; it must be in the operating position.
- fact: Missing key in the axle (riding mower): replace the key.
- fact: Drive pump belt worn/fallen off: replace worn or cracked belt, make sure it is securely around the pulleys.
- fact: Tensioner pulley bearing can fail: replace tensioner pulley.
- fact: Idler arm spring broken/missing can no longer tension the drive belt: replace.
- fact: Hot hydraulic fluid can signal hydrostatic damage. Let the hydraulic oil cool. If the mower runs fine cold but weakens when hot, have it checked at the Craftsman dealership.
- fact: Most Craftsman zero turns have sealed, unserviceable transmissions; take to a Craftsman service shop.

## page: causes/missing-axle-key
- fix: Replace the key in the axle (small key in the axle of a riding mower may have fallen out).

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fix: Replace the broken or missing spring.

## page: causes/bad-hydrostatic-transmission
- fact: Craftsman mowers with a hydrostatic transmission feel weak when the hydraulic oil is old or low, or when the drive belt/tensioner pulley is worn or broken.
- fact: Most Craftsman zero-turn use sealed transmissions the owner cannot service; bring to a Craftsman service center.
- pro: Any transmission problem on a sealed Craftsman hydro goes to a service center.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- fact: Bad drive belt (frayed, cracked, worn) stops the pump working efficiently: replace.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Bad tensioner pulley fails to keep tension on the drive belt: replace pulley and grease the tensioner arm.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- see problems/mower-wont-move entry above.

## page: problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side
- alias: "Craftsman won't drive straight or steer correctly"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, bad-drive-control-linkage-or-damper, loose-steering-linkage
- fact: Tracking may be off on Craftsman zero turns, causing veering right or left.
- fact: Incorrect tire pressure: unequal pressure pulls mower to one side; keep at manufacturer spec; tires on each side need equal pressure.
- fact: Bad dampers on zero turn make the mower jerk to one side when moving forward; replace the damper.
- fact: Tracking adjustment needed on zero turn: when one tire moves faster than the other the mower pulls to one side; adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change the tire rotation speed (refer to owner's manual).
- fact: Worn or loose steering components in riding mower (bushings, washers, gears) can stop it steering one way; inspect and replace failed parts.

## page: procedures/adjust-the-tracking
- fact: Adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change rotation speed of the tire (Craftsman zero turn); see owner's manual.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: See pulls-to-one-side entry.

## page: causes/loose-steering-linkage
- fact: Worn bushings, washers, gears in a riding mower steering system.

## page: problems/loose-steering
- fact: Worn or loose steering components (bushings, washers, gears) can make the mower not steer one way or the other.

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Craftsman mower and engine is vibrating"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: From loose, failing or damaged parts including bearings and blades; debris lodged under deck pulleys also vibrates.
- fact: Loose/missing engine mounting bolts: engine bolts reduce vibration; secure and replace missing bolts.
- fact: Failing clutch: bearing failure and falling out of the clutch causes vibration; a manual-engagement clutch can have bushing/linkage failures. Replace the clutch.
- fact: Unbalanced or damaged blades: balance before installing; replace damaged blades.
- fact: Foreign material (string, long weeds) wrapped around blade shaft: remove debris, check spindle damage.
- fact: Bad spindle housing bearings: replace the bearing or the whole spindle housing assembly.
- fact: Bad deck belt or pulleys: check wear and bearing failure; replace damaged belts/pulleys.
- fact: Stones, acorns, woodchips stuck under pulleys etc. can shake the mower; check deck and mower and remove.

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Craftsman bad or uneven cut"
- causes-named: dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-deck-chute, wrong-tire-pressure, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: Deck gives uneven cut when deck shell, blades and spindle shafts are damaged. Not running full throttle or moving too fast also affects cut.
- fact: Table lists: dull or worn blade, bent blade, blade installed wrong, deck plugged with dirt, uneven tire pressures, deck not level, bent blade spindle, bad deck belt or pulleys, engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, damaged deck, operator error.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: Perform periodic checks before each mowing and regular service maintenance once or twice a year.
