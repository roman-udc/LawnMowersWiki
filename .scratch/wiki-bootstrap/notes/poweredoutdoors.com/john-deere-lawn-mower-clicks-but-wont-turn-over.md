## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "John Deere clicks but won't start", "click click click", "humming sound when starting"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor, faulty-charging-system
- safety: Follow John Deere operator's manual; wear gloves and safety glasses; consult a professional if uncomfortable with electrical work. ALWAYS disconnect the negative (black) battery cable before electrical repairs.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: check cables/wiring from battery to solenoid and solenoid to starter are secure; inspect cables, wiring, terminals for corrosion.
- fix: replace cables/wiring that keep coming loose or are broken (bad cables contribute to a bad battery). Disconnect battery, remove corroded parts, clean with wire brush and baking soda mix: 2 cups water + 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda. Repair/replace corroded wiring; add dielectric grease to protect terminals and wiring; replace damaged terminals.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: weak charge cannot turn over the engine. Charge when voltage is low; replace if it won't hold a charge. If it charges but keeps dying, check the charging system.
- confirm: multimeter red/black to matching terminals; most riding/zero-turn batteries are 12 V; reads 11.5 to 12.7; 11.5 V = almost dead, 12.7 V = fully charged.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: Put on safety gear (acid, shock).
- step 2: Access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed to uncover).
- step 3: Leave battery in its casing with terminal cables attached (as stated).
- step 4: Connect charger red cable (positive) first, red clamp on positive terminal, black clamp on negative terminal.
- step 5: Touch only the rubber coating of cables/clamps.
- step 6: Set charger to 12 V; amps: start with 2, work up to no more than 10.
- step 7: If charger has a gauge, keep connected until fully charged.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- fact: multimeter 11.5-12.7 V range; 12.7 = full, 11.5 = nearly dead.

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- confirm: check the black ground cable from battery to frame for good contact and no corrosion; check ground from the solenoid. A 3-post solenoid is self-grounded.
- fix: replace damaged ground cable; remove corrosion from battery and starter-solenoid grounds.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: mower keeps clicking.
- fact: Solenoid is an electromagnetic on/off switch that engages the starter motor. Usually mounted on the starter but needn't be; follow the positive wire from the battery to find it. Failure causes: weak internal spring, corroded copper plate, bad ground, weak starter or bad battery.
- confirm: test with volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches; if the mower starts when bypassing the solenoid, replace it.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: after good battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid, suspect the starter; it can be removed and tested.
- pro: Starter is pricey; have the dealer confirm before replacing, or take to a starter/alternator repair shop that can test and often rebuild it.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Bad charging system won't keep the battery charged. Larger mowers may have a car-like external alternator; others internal alternator under the flywheel; most John Deere mowers have an internal alternator. Test with a volt-ohm meter.
- pro: Have a mechanic familiar with the charging system test it; guessing leads to buying parts (electrical parts not returnable); possible bad stator/alternator, regulator or other.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- fact: tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches; bypass = starts means replace.
