## page: problems/mower-wont-move
- alias: "riding mower won't move forward or reverse"
- alias: "riding mower not driving"
- causes-named: bypass-valve-open, missing-axle-key, worn-drive-belt, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, missing-idler-arm-spring, low-hydraulic-oil, hot-hydraulic-fluid, air-in-hydraulic-system, brake-hung-up
- fact: Most likely cause on a riding mower is the hydrostatic drive system.
- fact (FAQ): Not driving when transmission bypass lever not in drive position, brake engaged, axle key missing, air trapped in hydraulic system, or hydraulic oil low, hot or old. Not moving forward: worn drive belt, worn tensioner spring, bad pulley, air in transmission, hot/old/low hydraulic oil.
- safety: Remove spark plug wires and ignition key before repairs.
- tip: If hydraulic checks find nothing, check non-hydraulic items starving the engine of fuel/air: battery and charging system, clogged fuel filter and lines, clogged air filter, dirty carburetor (engine sputtering or shutting off).

## page: causes/bypass-valve-open
- fact: Hydrostatic mowers have a drive release so the mower can free-roll (e.g., pushing onto a trailer). Must be in "operating" position to move. Can be a lever, push/pull button or knob; see owner's manual.

## page: causes/missing-axle-key
- symptom: After a tire change, a small narrow key bar fell out of the axle; wheel won't move without it. Not every mower has one.

## page: causes/worn-drive-belt
- confirm: Belt may have fallen off or not be positioned correctly on pulleys; worn, cracked or broken: replace.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: Tensioner pulleys often hard plastic with center bearing; pulley can break or bearing fail. Keep tensioner arm greased so it moves and doesn't seize. Without belt tension the mower won't go.

## page: causes/missing-idler-arm-spring
- fact: Idler spring tensions drive belt; replace if broken or missing.

## page: causes/low-hydraulic-oil
- symptom: Won't move, or moves very weakly, with old or low hydraulic oil.
- fix: Change oil and filter(s) at manufacturer intervals; many manufacturers have a "break-in" period with an earlier first oil change. Add oil to full when oil is cool; check for leaks.
- brand: Some entry-level zero turns have non-serviceable (sealed) transmissions where fluid can't be changed; take to servicing dealer.

## page: causes/hot-hydraulic-fluid
- symptom: Bad or low fluid can't lubricate, causing friction and overheating; can cause more extensive damage.
- pro: Take to dealer if hydraulic pump leaks, or mower runs fine cold but stops when hot.

## page: causes/air-in-hydraulic-system
- fix: After changing fluid bleed all air. On most mowers raise rear drive tires off the ground and let them spin forward and reverse until no excessive noise and wheels move at normal speed. Procedure varies by manufacturer and model; follow operator's manual.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: Raise rear drive wheels off ground. step 2: Run wheels forward and in reverse until noise stops and wheels move at normal speed. Follow operator's manual.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: Change oil and filter(s) at manufacturer intervals; early first change in break-in period; level to full when cool.
