## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "pull the PTO switch and nothing happens"
- alias: "blades won't spin under the deck"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: Source summary: worn or stretched deck belt, worn tensioner arm and spring, bad pulley, bad PTO switch, faulty clutch, weak battery, bad safety switch, blown fuse.
- safety: Never reach under the deck until you have followed the Exmark operator's manual precautions: remove ignition key, remove spark plug wires, wait for all parts to stop moving.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- confirm: Worn belt shows cracks, wear, fraying, shiny glazed appearance; may sit deep in the pulley grooves.
- fix: Replace. Even if blades rotate, reduced speed affects cut quality.
- fact: Fast blade speed is required to create suction under the deck to lift grass for an even cut.
- fact: A belt that has fallen off the pulleys will not turn the blades; check belt condition and causes such as bad pulley, worn tensioner arm, missing spring. Source links its Exmark "belt keeps coming off" article.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: Tensioner arm and spring hold idler pulleys inline; a pulley on one side of the bracket, spring on the other. They wear, causing belt to vibrate off. Spring can break or stretch; the bracket hole can wear, affecting tension.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: Bad bearing makes the pulley wobble and no longer sit parallel to the deck; belt can roll off a rocking pulley.
- confirm: Rotate pulley slowly by hand, feel for resistance or listen for bearing noise; grab each side and check for extra play/rocking.
- fix: Replace the pulley assembly if the bearing is bad.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: PTO switch is usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch.
- confirm: Check continuity.
- fix: Replace a switch with a break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: PTO (Power Take Off) clutch transfers engine power to the blades by engaging the drive belt.
- fix: Replace a worn or faulty clutch.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: Battery powers the clutch solenoid; weak battery gives insufficient clutch power.
- confirm: Check battery voltage with a multimeter; fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V. Charge if less.
- fix: If it will not hold a charge, replace it with a 12 V mower battery (hardware, automotive store, or Exmark dealer). Buy a battery with minimum 300 cca (cold cranking amps) to power an electric clutch.
- tip: Bring the old battery; stores typically charge a core fee of $15-$25 unless you supply the old one.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear protective gear for skin (electrical shock) and eyes.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (battery under hood or seat; may need screwdriver to uncover). Do not remove battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect charger cables positive first (red / plus sign) to positive terminal.
- step 3: Attach negative cable (black / negative sign) to negative terminal.
- step 4: Do not touch anything without a rubber coating, to prevent electrocution.
- step 5: Set charger voltage and amperage. Average mower battery is 12 volts. More amps charges faster; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps. A slow charge is best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- brand: Exmark has a seat safety switch under the seat as part of the operator presence system; blades will not turn on without the operator in the seat. A failed seat switch will not sense the operator and the safety system prevents blade engagement.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter, or temporarily bypass the switch to identify a bad one.
- safety: Do not operate a mower without the safety switch installed; always keep safety switches installed and working.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- symptom: problems/blades-wont-engage
- fact: Fuse protects the Exmark electrical system from power surges or a short; blown fuse means no battery power to the clutch.
- fix: Replace with the same size fuse.
- pro: If fuses keep blowing, take it to an Exmark service dealer or repair shop to find the root cause.
