## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- time: not stated
- difficulty: described as easier than you think
- tools: 1 quart engine oil (SAE30 or air-cooled 10W30; check owner's manual); drain pan; socket wrench or spanner wrenches to fit the drain plug; rags or paper towel; rubber gloves; plastic funnel; cardboard/newspaper to protect the work surface
- step 1: gather tools
- step 2: start the engine and warm the oil about 2 minutes then shut off (warm oil flows better); engine is hot so avoid touching it; prepare a hard flat surface covered with cardboard/newspaper
- step 3: unplug the spark plug lead (twist to remove) so the engine can't start if blades are rotated; do not skip
- step 4: gently lay the mower on its side with the carburetor on the high side so oil doesn't run into the carburetor and air filter
- step 5: find the square or hex drain plug on the bottom of the engine (scrape grass from under the deck first if needed); right-hand thread, turn counter-clockwise; some engines have a plug at the bottom of the dipstick tube so the mower need not be tilted; drain into pan; loosen cap or dipstick to speed the flow; while on its side, scrape the deck and inspect blades
- step 6: reinstall drain plug clockwise; return mower upright
- step 7: with a funnel add about 3/4 quart, check dipstick or per manual; top up to full; replace oil cap
- step 8: reattach the spark plug lead
- fact: interval: once a year or every 25 hours for most manufacturers (also written "more frequently if used over 25-50 hours a year; varies by engine maker"); new mower or new engine: first change after the first 5-hour break-in (also written 5-10 hours) to remove machining particles
- fact: push mower oil without a filter gets dirty and contaminants wear the engine
- fact: engine make is usually on a sticker on top of the engine; engines: Kohler, Honda, Briggs & Stratton, or aftermarket "knock-off"
- brand: most mower engines take SAE30 blend or straight 30 weight; Kohler engines need air-cooled 10W-30 because some internal parts don't take SAE blend well
- tip: extractor pump costs $25-$80. Drain plug method pros: fast, cheaper, may get more oil out, can do other checks while draining; cons: messy, must tip mower, risk damaging oil pan/drain plug. Extractor pros: cleaner, no tipping, no risk to plug/pan; cons: may not get all oil out, slower.
- tip: while doing annual service also scrape the deck, inspect blades, replace the fuel filter, inspect and clean or replace the air filter
- safety: recycle used oil - never pour in drain, outdoors or in trash; EPA: re-refining takes about 1/3 energy of refining crude; one gallon used oil yields 2 1/2 quarts of new lubricant versus 42 gallons crude; store in original container or sealed polyethylene container (not soda bottles or milk jugs); take to recycling center, curbside program or auto store

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: lawn mower oil is not the same as car oil; air-cooled engine relies on air and oil to cool; air-cooled oil has more cooling agents than automotive oil
- fact: SAE30 or air-cooled 10W-30; Kohler needs air-cooled 10W-30
- fact: first oil change on a new engine after 5-10 hours (5 hours elsewhere in article)

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: annual service: change oil, scrape deck, inspect blades, replace fuel filter, inspect/clean/replace air filter

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: skipping oil changes causes excessive engine wear and can cause significant damage (links to its own article)
