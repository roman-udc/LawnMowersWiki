## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- safety: Remove spark plug wires and ignition key; wear gloves; take caution under the deck.
- fact: Replace Bad Boy blades once a year (annual service); more often with dirt/gravel. Sharpen after every 25 hours (about twice a year for the average homeowner); more often in sandy/gravel conditions.
- tip: If old blades aren't damaged or excessively worn, sharpen and keep as a spare set.
- fact: New blades need no sharpening; they're sharpened at manufacture, then painted, and the paint wears off the edge in use.
- tools: socket wrench, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, block of wood
- step 1: Disconnect spark plug boot, remove key; wear gloves.
- step 2: Access the underside using ramps or a lift designed for the zero-turn; make sure deck is secure and won't drop; use brake and wheel chocks.
- step 3: Wedge a block of wood between blade and deck housing to stop the blade turning; remove bolts and washers with socket wrench; remove blades.
- step 4: Clean the area under the deck where the blade mounts; if reusing sharpened blades, make sure they're balanced.
- step 5: Install blades with bolts and washers; tighten with a torque wrench to the manufacturer spec in the owner's manual (block of wood to stop rotation). Correct torque avoids the blade coming off.
- mistake: Failing to balance a blade causes vibration and can damage the crankshaft.
- tip: Lawn chemicals may be corrosive to blades and deck; corrosion can fuse blades to deck components, nearly impossible to remove without damage. Remove and reinstall blades frequently.
- fact: Sharpening tools: 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vice; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; gloves.
- step: Clean blade with rag (wire brush for stubborn buildup). Secure in vice; sharpen one side, flip, secure, sharpen other.
- step: File: push file at an angle in one direction along the blade; no sawing motion; remove rough spots/uneven edges.
- step: Drill sharpener: flat guide along flat part of blade with cutting edge slotted in the bevel; move up and down length of edge.
- step: Angle/bench grinder: hold perpendicular to the edge, run along length to level rough spots/nicks; keep it moving to remove a little metal at a time for an even result.
- step: Balance: blade balancer, or nail in wall with head 3/4" to 1" from wall; hang blade centre on nail; it should sit level; if not, shave more metal from the side hanging lower. Balanced = equal weight on each side.
- pro: Seek a pro if not comfortable sharpening/balancing; poor balance or still-worn blade can damage crankshaft/engine. Author (opinion) has his dealer sharpen: cleaner edge. Reasons given: safer; sharpening often cheaper than new blade; pro can grind out large nicks and gouges; blade properly balanced.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- confirm: Bent blade check, removed: match old blade against a new one; a gap means bent. Before removing, rock the blade ends up and down to check spindle housing movement/knocking.
- confirm: Without removing: on a level flat surface measure ground to blade end, rotate blade 180 degrees, measure the other end from the same spot; difference greater than 1/8" means bent, replace; check spindle housings/bearings for damage.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: uneven cut; brown grass tips a couple of days after mowing (dull blade beats the grass instead of cutting).
- fact: Blade bends on hitting a solid object like a tree root or rock.
- fix: Small chips can be smoothed by sharpening; large nicks and gouges mean replace.
- safety: A cracked blade must be replaced right away; it can break off and fly out, risking people, pets, structures.
- tip: Walk the lawn first to remove toys, sticks, rocks. Sand/debris wear blades prematurely (a thicker lawn reduces dirt sucked into deck).

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- symptom: The sail is the high side of the blade; it creates air movement under the deck which, with deck shell and baffles, creates suction to stand grass tall. Dirt wears the sail; when it is thinner than the center of the blade, replace the blade.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: Rock blade ends up and down; movement or knocking in spindle housing means disassemble and replace damaged parts (can happen from hitting a hard object).

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades corrode, wear thin, dull and get damaged; replace annually, sharpen every 25 hours.
