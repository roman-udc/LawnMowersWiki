## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "Toro smells like gas but no wet spot"
- alias: "Toro leaking gas"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Gas evaporates so you smell fuel but see no wet spot. Old fuel degrades components; parts leak when dry, cracked, aged, dirty.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- symptom (bowl gasket): Gasket between carburetor and bowl (looks like a rubber band) wears out; common failure because it's next to the engine and cycles hot/cold, dries and loses its seal.
- fix: Shut off fuel at the shut-off valve under the tank (or clamp the fuel line if none); remove screw at bottom of bowl with a rag ready for gas; remove bowl; replace gasket; reinstall bowl and screw.
- symptom (stuck float): Area by the air intake port is wet with fuel; float regulates gas into the bowl; stuck float lets fuel keep flowing and overflow out of the carburetor.
- fix: Disassemble, clean the parts or rebuild.
- symptom (stuck float needle): works with float to control fuel.
- fix: Gently tap the side of the carburetor with a small rubber mallet or hammer handle end to unstick (temporary, may work a time or two); then disassemble and replace the needle.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Several carb parts gum up and fail (see gasket/float above); steps to clean the carburetor are in a linked article (not in this source).

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- symptom: Old fuel degrades the filter; plastic softens and leaks at seams.
- fix: Replace filter; careful removing the ends from the fuel line - soft plastic can break off inside the hose.
- fact: Ethanol corrosive; use fresh fuel within 30 days or stabilize; regular unleaded 87+ octane, no more than 10% ethanol.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- symptom: Most Toro fuel pumps are plastic; fuel degrades it, leaks at seams and internal damage; can't build pressure.
- fix: Replace; careful not to break soft plastic off the pump ports.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- symptom: Older Toro mowers have metal tanks that rust and develop holes; most today are high-density polyethylene whose seams can fail.
- fix: Metal: can try petroleum-resistant epoxy such as TankWeld by J-B Weld, or replace if available. Poly: best solution is replace the tank.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- symptom: Plastic and metal shut-off valves known to leak; located under the fuel tank if the mower has one.
- fix: Replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- symptom: Lines dry and crack with age; follow the lines from the tank checking each section for wet spots; pay attention to sections held by clamps.
- fix: Replace cracked lines; consider replacing pinch-style clamps. Author likes worm gear clamps (less likely to pinch lines and cause leaks).

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- symptom: Primer bulb may leak when overfilled with fuel after pressing, or where the bulb meets the fuel line. Easy to replace.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: Seal dries and no longer seals; leak is easy to miss because fuel evaporates.
- confirm: Rock the mower back and forth to splash gas up to the cap area and watch for wet spots forming around the cap.
- fix: Replace the gas cap.
