## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Honda riding mower won't turn over or crank"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, blown-fuse, bad-ignition-switch, bad-safety-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-starter-motor
- fact: Starter motor can't get the power it needs to turn the engine.
- safety: Remove the negative battery cable before electrical repairs.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: Keep fully charged, especially in extended storage, to prolong battery life.
- fix: Charge with a 12-volt charger; replace if it won't hold a charge; bring old battery to avoid core fee (dealer, hardware or auto store).

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: Wear protective gear for eyes and skin.
- step 1: Access battery and terminals (screwdriver may be needed); do not remove battery from its casing.
- step 2: Connect positive (red / plus) charger cable to the positive terminal first.
- step 3: Attach negative (black / minus) cable to the negative terminal.
- step 4: Do not touch anything without a rubber coating.
- step 5: Set charger to 12 volts typical; start at 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge is best.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fact: Vibration shakes wiring/components loose; moisture corrodes; corrosion affects continuity.
- fix: Remove battery cables first, then clean with wire brush and baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda). Replace terminals/components that can't be cleaned.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- confirm: Multimeter probes on each prong measuring resistance: near 0 good; infinity bad.
- fix: Replace with same amperage. Repeated blowing: take to dealer or repair shop for root cause.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: key turned, nothing happens.
- confirm: Multimeter on resistance; prongs B (Battery) and S (Starter Solenoid); key at start; one probe each. Good near 0 ohms; bad infinite, replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Riding mower has an operator presence control system; a defective switch can stop it turning over.
- confirm: Test with multimeter; temporary bypass only for troubleshooting.
- safety: Never operate without the safety switch or with it bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- fact: Electromagnetic switch that engages the starter motor. Fails when spring weakens or copper plate corrodes; weak starter, bad battery or bad ground can also make it fail. Needs a fully charged battery before testing. Article links to its solenoid symptom/test article.

## page: causes/bad-starter-motor
- fact: Consider after ruling out battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid; starter can be removed and tested.
- tip: Have a starter/alternator specialist test and rebuild it before buying a new one (author's recommendation).
