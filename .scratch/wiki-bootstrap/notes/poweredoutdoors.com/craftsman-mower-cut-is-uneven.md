## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Craftsman mower cut is bad or uneven"
- alias: "mower leaves strips of uncut grass"
- alias: "grass turns brown a couple of days after mowing"
- alias: "deck is lower on one side"
- alias: "mower leaves a row of grass"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, clogged-deck-chute, unlevel-deck, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, incorrect-path-overlap, bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: cut-quality types: Scalping (close cut in areas; low tire, uneven lawn, unlevel deck, deck height too low); Step cut (ridges; low tire, unlevel deck, bent blade, spindle damage, wrong blade installation); Streaking (strips of uncut grass; worn/dull blades, clogged deck, incorrect paths, mowing too fast, low engine speed); Stringers (patches of uncut grass; worn/dull blades, clogged deck, too fast, low engine speed).
- confirm: check tire pressure first, before measuring for bent blades or an unlevel deck.
- fact: a row of grass left behind: not at full throttle, worn blades, damaged deck shell, clogged deck, or improper overlap.
- fact: uneven/bumpy lawn can scalp; a lawn roller may help; wide decks in ditches give stair-step cuts, use a small-deck push mower there.
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug boot(s) before working under the deck; wear heavy gloves around sharp blades.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: strips of uncut grass or grass browning a couple of days after mowing; dull blades beat up and bruise grass.
- confirm: bent blade, method 1: remove the blade and lay it on top of a new blade.
- confirm: bent blade, method 2: park on flat level surface, measure ground to blade tip on one side, rotate blade 180 degrees, measure the other side; more than 1/8 inch difference means replace the blade.
- fix: sharpen dull blades, replace when needed.
- safety: never straighten a bent blade for reuse; the metal is weakened and the weak area can break off and fly out of the deck at high speed.
- tip: unbalanced blade causes spindle damage and heavy vibration; check balance with a blade balancer or hang the blade on a nail to see if it sits level; shave metal from the heavy side.

## page: causes/blade-installed-upside-down
- fact: Craftsman blades must have the sail pointed toward the deck (up); blade is often stamped to show which side faces up and which faces the ground.
- symptom: upside-down blades beat up the grass and can damage spindle housings.
- fix: remove and install with sail pointing upward.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: tires not at equal pressure make the deck not sit level; one of the first checks. Fill to correct pressure so both sides are equal.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: grass and dirt build up under the deck; Craftsman decks use spinning blades to create air movement and suction that lifts grass for an even cut. Clogging is worse in wet conditions and leaves clumps.
- fix: scrape/clean the deck regularly; avoid wet grass.

## page: causes/unlevel-deck
- confirm: measure each corner of the deck on a flat level surface.
- fix: level side-to-side and front-to-back per owner's manual (procedures vary by model).
- fact: correct pitch has the front of the deck slightly lower than the back.
- see: procedures/level-the-deck

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: wearing heavy gloves, grab each side of the blade and rock up and down; any movement or knock means remove and inspect the spindle housing; movement and knock usually mean replace the bearing in the housing.
- fix: replace damaged spindle or housing.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: turn each deck pulley slowly; resistance or bearing noise means replace the pulley.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- fact: deck belt must be free of cracks and wear and sit securely around pulleys; bad belt/pulleys fail to turn blades at top speed.
- fix: replace worn belts and pulleys.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fix: run at full throttle to keep blades spinning fast.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: too fast pushes grass over instead of standing it up; slow ground speed for thick, tall or wet grass to avoid engine overload.

## page: causes/incorrect-path-overlap
- fact: author left thin strips by not overlapping passes properly; overlap passes so no strips remain.

## page: causes/bent-deck-or-deck-shell
- fact: shell is formed to create suction; hitting trees, rocks, etc. can tweak it.
- fix: repair or replace; replacing the shell means moving components from the old deck to the new one.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- step 1: park on flat level surface.
- step 2: measure ground to blade tip on one side, record.
- step 3: rotate the blade 180 degrees, measure the other side.
- step 4: difference greater than 1/8 inch means replace the blade.

## page: procedures/level-the-deck
- step 1: park on flat level surface, equal tire pressures.
- step 2: measure each deck corner; level side-to-side and front-to-back per manual; front slightly lower than back.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: sharpen via professional service or yourself; replace when needed; install sail up; balance blades.
