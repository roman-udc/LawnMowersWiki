## page: problems/blades-wont-engage
- alias: "SCAG blades won't engage or turn on"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-pto-switch, bad-pto-clutch, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, blown-fuse
- fact: check deck components, clutch, switches, and what powers the clutch.
- safety: remove ignition key and spark plug wires; wait for all parts to stop moving.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: belt slips on the pulleys, blades won't turn; worn belt has cracks, fraying, shiny glazed appearance; may sit deep in the pulley grooves.
- fix: replace; even if blades rotate they may spin slowly and hurt cut quality; fast blade speed is needed to create suction under the deck to lift grass.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: tensioner arm and spring hold idler pulleys in line; usually a pulley on one side of the bracket and spring on the other; they wear causing the belt to vibrate off; spring can break or stretch; the bracket hole can wear.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: a bad bearing lets the pulley wobble, no longer sitting parallel to the deck; belt can roll off a rocking pulley.
- confirm: slowly rotate the pulley by hand feeling for resistance or listening for bearing noise; grab each side and check for extra play rocking back and forth. A good pulley sits securely.
- fix: replace the pulley assembly.

## page: causes/bad-pto-switch
- fact: PTO switch is usually a knob that lets battery voltage power the clutch; when it fails blades won't turn.
- confirm: check continuity; replace if break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- fact: PTO clutch transfers power from engine to blades by engaging the drive belt; replace a worn or faulty clutch that fails to power blades.

## page: guides/how-clutches-work
- fact: PTO clutch engages the drive belt to transfer engine power to blades (source links a clutch article).

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: battery powers the clutch solenoid; weak battery gives the clutch too little power.
- confirm: multimeter; fully charged 12-volt reads about 12.7 volts; below: charge.
- fix: if it won't hold a charge, replace; 12-volt batteries at hardware or automotive stores or a SCAG dealer; bring the old battery; core fee typically $15-$25 without it.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- safety: protective gear for skin and eyes; don't touch non-rubber-coated parts.
- step 1: access battery (under hood or under seat); screwdriver may be needed; don't remove the battery from its casing.
- step 2: connect charger positive (red, plus) first to the positive terminal.
- step 3: connect negative (black) to the negative terminal.
- step 4: set 12 volts; start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps; slow charge best.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: SCAG has a seat switch under the seat as part of its operator presence system; blades won't engage without the operator in the seat; a failed seat switch fails to sense the operator and blocks the blades.
- confirm: test with multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify.
- safety: do not operate without the safety switch; always keep switches installed and working.

## page: causes/blown-fuse
- fact: no power from battery to clutch may mean blown fuse; fuse protects the electrical system.
- fix: replace with same size fuse; if it keeps blowing, take to a SCAG service dealership or repair shop to find root cause.

## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: belt fallen off: bad pulley, worn tensioner arm, missing spring.
