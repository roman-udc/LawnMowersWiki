## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Big Dog zero turns require unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, no more than 10% ethanol. Wrong gas can cause performance problems and may void manufacturer warranty.
- fact: Best fuel for a Big Dog is ethanol-free (recreation fuel); regular 87 octane is acceptable.
- fact: Station options: regular, midgrade, premium, diesel, E15 (up to 15% ethanol), E85 (up to 85%), REC90 (ethanol-free). Avoid E15 and E85 (over 10% ethanol).
- fact: Gas can begin to break down as quickly as 30 days after purchase. Only buy what you can use within 30 days; otherwise stabilize with a fuel additive.
- fact: Ethanol effects: attracts moisture to the fuel system; water and ethanol separate from the gas and sink to the bottom of the tank; leaves varnish and gummy deposits that clog fuel passageways; highly corrosive to fuel components and engine.
- fact: Ethanol-free fuel (recreation fuel) is the best option; it doesn't break down like ethanol fuels; costs more than regular/premium. Sources: 4-cycle fuel in canisters (hardware, automotive, online; TruFuel example linked) and REC-90 at select fuel stations (pure-gas.org lists stations).
- tip: Use only fresh gas; store in a dry area so water doesn't mix in; refuel in a well-ventilated area away from combustibles.
- opinion: Author buys high-quality low-ethanol unleaded and adds fuel additive to each tank; prefers Sea Foam Motor Treatment (keeps fuel stable, reduces moisture, cleans fuel system); use it when storing excess fuel and when storing the mower with fuel in the off-season.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Store fuel in a dry area; fuel degrades from about 30 days; use a fuel additive (Sea Foam Motor Treatment per author) for stored fuel and off-season storage with fuel in the mower. Don't buy excess fuel to sit long.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: Wrong (high-ethanol or old) gas causes fuel system and engine damage; Big Dog spec: 87+ octane, max 10% ethanol.
