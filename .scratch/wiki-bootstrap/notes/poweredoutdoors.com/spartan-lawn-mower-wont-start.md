## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Spartan zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- symptom: not getting the air, spark and fuel required for cylinder combustion
- brand: most Spartan mowers are gas-powered zero turns; some are diesel (article focuses on gasoline)

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: forgot to buy fuel, fuel leak using more than usual, or faulty gauge
- fix: check tank, leak and gauge; add fresh fuel

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas breaks down in as little as 30 days; ethanol (often corn) attracts moisture causing premature corrosion; ethanol/moisture leaves gummy deposits clogging lines and carburetor components
- brand: Spartan requires unleaded gas minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol; do NOT use E15 or E85 (up to 15% and 85% ethanol); ethanol-free is best but pricey
- tip: buy only what you use in 30 days; otherwise stabilize with Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL
- fix: drain tank with a fuel siphon pump; refill with fresh gas mixed with Sea Foam or STA-BIL

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: E15 and E85 must not be used in Spartan mowers (see old-fuel)

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Spartan cap is vented to equalize tank pressure; no vent or plugged = vacuum, fuel can't flow
- confirm: loosen or remove cap and run; if it starts and runs fine replace the cap; let it run to see if it dies starved of fuel; shuts off after a while and restarts only with cap removed = bad cap
- fix: new Spartan fuel cap

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: fouled plug, incorrect gap or loose wire cause intermittent fire
- fix: remove with socket wrench; look for cracked porcelain, burnt electrode, dirty plug; replace if damaged or excessively dirty/dark; a little dirty can be cleaned with wire brush; author usually just replaces it (inexpensive); gap to engine manufacturer spec; securely attach wire

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: lack of air prevents starting; running with very dirty filter can cause overheating damage
- safety: never run without an air filter, even briefly
- tip: start each mowing season with a new air filter; check and clean several times a season; more often in dusty conditions

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: remove filter from housing
- step 2: wipe dirt from housing with a dry clean rag; don't let dirt fall in the intake
- step 3: tap filter against a solid surface; do NOT use compressed air to clean it (damages the filter)
- step 4: hold up to light and check light through paper
- step 5: light visible = reuse; otherwise replace
- step 6: install and reattach housing cover

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: replace annually; sooner if dirty fuel; inline - arrow in direction of fuel flow

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: can't see clogs; use the shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers to start/stop flow; with flow stopped remove the end of a section furthest from the tank into a container lower than the tank; start flow and check
- fix: stop flow, remove line, carburetor cleaner, compressed air, repeat; replace if can't clear; replace dry/cracking lines even if not clogged

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: old gas in the pump degrades it; pump uses engine vacuum to draw fuel
- confirm: check for leaks; verify fuel reaches the pump; disconnect hose from carburetor into a container, start mower, watch for constant or pulsating flow
- fix: replace if no constant or pulsating flow

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: deposits clog the fuel jet and gum small components
- fix: disassemble and clean with carb cleaner including the float bowl and needle; or small engine mechanic; replace if too dirty to clean

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: multimeter about 12.7 volts; charge if less; replace if it won't hold a charge

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: clean terminals in baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) with wire brush; confirm tight connection

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Spartan has several safety switches: won't start without parking brake engaged and levers in the open/outward position; another switch disengages the deck and shuts off the engine if operator not sensed
- confirm: multimeter test; temporary bypass for diagnosis only
- safety: never operate with a bypassed or missing safety switch

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: test with multimeter; replace if bad

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: not the main cause but contributes to a weak battery; battery keeps dying and needs repeated charging
- fact: bad stator or alternator can be the problem
- pro: Spartan dealer or small engine shop to isolate; most parts stores won't take electrical returns; choose a mechanic experienced with charging systems

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: follow operator's manual starting procedures so safety features don't shut the engine off
- fact: choke reduces air to the cylinder for a cold start (higher fuel-to-air ratio); set to off once warm or the zero-turn sputters and dies

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke must be set off when warm or mower sputters and dies
