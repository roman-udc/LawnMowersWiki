## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "my lawn mower is smoking"
- alias: "black smoke from mower"
- alias: "blue smoke from mower"
- alias: "white smoke from mower"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, bad-valve-train, spilled-oil-on-engine, rich-fuel-mixture
- safety: first thing when smoke appears: stop the engine to avoid engine damage. Wait for the engine to cool and remove the spark plug boot(s) before making repairs.
- fact: Source lists causes as plugged air filter, low engine oil, too much engine oil, bad engine gasket, blown seal, oil spill, valve train or piston ring problems. Start with the simple checks before complicated diagnostics (order: air filter, oil level, spark plug oil check, valve train, gasket).
- fact: Smoke colour hints. Black smoke = engine running too rich (more fuel burned than air); first check the air filter (clean or replace a plugged one), and if the filter is fine check for another air-flow restriction. Blue or white smoke = burning excess oil; check oil level first, then piston rings, valve train, engine gasket.
- tip: check engine oil level and air filter before each mowing to avoid large expensive repairs (author points to his Lawn Mower Checklist).
- pro: oil on the spark plug (ring/valve train problem) means taking the engine apart; author recommends a small engine dealer repair shop if not mechanically inclined. Valve train detection (cylinder head removal or leak-down test) should be done by a small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: mower smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking); black smoke from rich running
- confirm: remove the air filter cover (always above the carburetor; usually held by a single screw or two knobs; on some mowers the filter is along the side of the engine next to the carburetor). Pull the filter out carefully so no dirt falls into the intake pipe. Inspect for dirt and grass.
- fact: a clogged filter means the engine cannot breathe; it pulls air from the engine crankcase instead of fresh air, and that air can carry oil up with it; burning that oil creates smoke.
- fix: replace a clogged filter; if it does not look too bad, knock the dirt out and reinstall.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking)
- fact: oil lower than the manufacturer's recommended level can cause extreme heat that burns up small engine parts and makes the engine smoke ("engine is having a meltdown").
- tip: you can try adding fresh oil to see if smoking stops, but at this stage it is most often too late.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: smoking, blue/white smoke (problems/mower-is-smoking)
- fact: too much oil causes excess crankcase pressure, pushing oil back into the cylinder or up to the air intake through the valve train; oil in the cylinder burns off at full engine speed, making smoke.
- fix: drain some oil out of the engine.

## page: causes/worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides
- symptom: smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking); oil build-up on spark plug
- confirm: remove the spark plug (may need a 3/4" or 5/8" socket) and check for excessive oil buildup. Author says plug inspection does not tell much about the type of problem but heavy oil indicates a larger internal engine problem (piston ring or valve train).
- fact: oil on the plug can indicate a ring problem and a score in the cylinder wall; with a scored cylinder the engine brings oil up to the combustion chamber and burns it, so the mower smokes in use.
- fix: either problem means taking the engine apart; the engine may need replacing.
- pro: take to a small engine dealer repair shop if not mechanically inclined.

## page: causes/bad-valve-train
- symptom: smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking); muffler glowing red while the engine runs (burnt valve or timing problem)
- confirm: only by removing the cylinder head or performing a leak-down test, by a small engine mechanic.
- fact: cause is a valve burned by overheating; the valve edges fall apart (a "burnt valve").
- fix: replace the valve and grind the seat. The seat is where the valve contacts the engine block to complete the combustion chamber; both valve and seat are cut at certain angles so the valve seats correctly.

## page: causes/spilled-oil-on-engine
- symptom: smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking)
- fact: a bad engine gasket may leak oil onto the muffler and make the engine smoke as it burns. Find the bad gasket and replace it; difficulty depends on the gasket's location.
- fact: Source lists "oil spill" among the causes of smoke (no further detail).

## page: guides/engine-oil-guide
- fact: regular oil changes are necessary to keep the mower running and avoid shortening engine life.
