## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "push mower is leaking gas"
- alias: "smell of gas in the shed"
- alias: "burnt marks on the lawn"
- causes-named: bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, dirty-carburetor, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-filter, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-fuel-pump, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: gas evaporates leaving no wet signs; "7 places" to look.
- safety: work in a well-ventilated area; gas fumes can be harmful; follow operator's manual.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: bowl gasket sits between bowl and carburetor, looks like a rubber band; next to the engine it heats and cools repeatedly and goes hard and brittle.
- fix: to buy a replacement use the engine model and spec number (not the mower's model/serial number).
- step 1: shut off fuel with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank, or pinch pliers on the fuel line.
- step 2: wipe outside of carb so no dirt enters; have a rag for remaining fuel in the bowl.
- step 3: remove screw at bottom of bowl and the bowl.
- step 4: replace old gasket with new; reinstall bowl and screw.
- step 5: wipe down carb; turn fuel on; check for leaks.
- symptom: leak near the air intake port = stuck float; fuel keeps filling and overflows from carb. Take carb apart to find cause; may clean, rebuild or replace.
- fact: float needle works with float; if stuck it needs repair; take carb apart or have a small engine mechanic rebuild. Tapping gently with a hammer's rubber handle or rubber mallet works a time or two but is only temporary.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: modern gasoline degrades plastic; filter gets soft and leaks at seams; replace cracked/leaking filter. Be careful removing it: plastic ends can be soft and break off.
- fact: not all push mowers have a fuel filter; if none, you can install one. Filter strains dirt out after the tank.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: most push mowers don't have a pump; yours does if the carb sits higher than the tank. Vacuum pump plastic degrades from modern fuel and leaks at seams, can't build pressure.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: tank is HDPE (polyethylene) or metal; poly seams can fail: replace the tank. Metal tank corrodes, rust spots may become a hole; author prefers replacing (rust and fuel system contamination), but on an older mower with the tank discontinued may attempt to repair the rusted spot.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: some push mowers have a shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank; prone to leak; replace once gas comes out of it.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- fact: lines get dry, brittle, crack with age; replace cracked lines; look for tears or punctures where hose clamps attach.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: cap has a rubber gasket that can dry and fail letting fuel seep around the cap; you may only smell gas.
- confirm: rock the mower back and forth to slosh fuel to the cap and watch for a wet spot around the cap; replace the cap if leaking.
