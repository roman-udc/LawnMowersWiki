## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Hustler mower starts then dies / quits in the middle of the lawn"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, bad-safety-switch, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-deck-chute
- fact: Applies to Hustler walk-behind, zero-turn and stand-on. Author once ran out of fuel by not checking the gauge.
- safety: Remove spark plug wire and ignition key before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Ethanol (from corn or other high-starch plants) attracts moisture, causes fuel-system corrosion, evaporates leaving gummy deposits that clog.
- tip: Gas selection/storage: min 87 octane; no more than 10% ethanol (E10), avoid E15 and E85; buy fresh and use within 30 days; use fuel additive; store away from combustibles in a dry location.
- fix: Siphon old fuel into approved container; refill with right gas plus stabilizer. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans; petroleum-based).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- symptom: Inline filter between fuel lines; plugged means mower runs sluggishly and dies.
- fix: Replace; arrow on housing points in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Use fuel shut-off valve to start/stop flow and check each section for flow.
- fix: Turn off fuel, remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air; else new line.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most Hustler zero-turn pumps are vacuum pumps (engine vacuum draws fuel to carburetor); wear or fuel degradation.
- confirm: Check flow into pump via line; then turn off fuel, remove line from carburetor into a container, turn fuel on and start; steady or pulsating flow = OK, else replace.
- fact: EFI Hustler: use a fuel pressure gauge; see operator's manual for spec.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Verify fuel flow to carburetor; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner in intake; start; starts, runs fine then shuts down = carburetor problem.
- fix: Remove and clean carburetor, or small engine shop.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dust and clippings pulled in without a filter wear the engine; always have a clean filter.
- tip: Replace annually for average homeowner; more often for commercial/heavy use. Check before each use and clean as needed; plugged filter can overheat the engine.
- step 1: Clean paper filter: remove element from housing.
- step 2: Wipe housing with clean dry cloth; don't let dirt fall into intake.
- step 3: Tap against hard surface; do NOT use compressed air (damages paper).
- step 4: Hold to light; replace if light doesn't shine through the paper or it's damaged.
- step 5: Reinstall and reattach cover.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Plugged fins cause overheating and dying. Remove dirt from fins, around engine block and shroud; replace broken fins; make sure heat shield is installed correctly for air circulation.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Check oil before each use. Low oil causes friction, crankcase heat, burning oil and parts, overheating and shutdown.
- fix: Adding oil rarely fixes it once shut down from lack of lubrication; likely significant engine damage.
- pro: Experienced small engine mechanic to assess damage.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Excess oil builds crankcase pressure.
- fix: Remove oil via drain plug, oil filter, or fill area with oil evacuator or turkey baster; repeat until level correct.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- fact: Methods: drain plug, oil filter, evacuator or turkey baster from fill tube; keep adjusting until level correct.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: Clean dirty plug with wire brush; replace if very dark or damaged; gap to engine manufacturer spec; secure wires.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Windings separate and short out when hot; no spark; mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: Ohmmeter: break in continuity = replace.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Operator presence system prevents starting unless conditions met (e.g. brake engaged); seat, brake, PTO switches may be bad. Test with multimeter; check manual for other switches; bypass only for troubleshooting.
- safety: Never operate without or with bypassed safety switch.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts airflow for cold starts; if left on after warm-up the mower dies. Set to open/off once warm.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Mower shuts down and won't restart until cap is loosened to let air in. Tank forms vacuum.

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: Plugged deck strains engine, overheats, shuts down; dull blades worsen. Scrape deck regularly, sharpen blades; remove ignition key, disconnect plug wire, protect hands.

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- fact: Hustler: run at high engine speed; don't idle long periods (engine power charges battery). If you keep needing to charge and battery/wiring OK and secure, suspect charging system.
- pro: Experienced mechanic should diagnose charging system; many components, parts-throwing is costly and electrical parts are usually non-returnable.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- pro: See above; mechanic recommended.
