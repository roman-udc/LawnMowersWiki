## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Replace blades with a new set each year (best during annual maintenance to track age). Keep removed blades; if good, sharpen and keep as backup set.
- fact: Sharpen about every 25 hours (about twice a year for average homeowner); more often if mowing more or in dry/dusty conditions.
- fact: New blades are sharpened at the factory and coated with paint; do not remove paint on the edge; ready to install.
- tools: (removal) ratchet, block of wood, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional)
- tools: (sharpening) 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves
- safety: heavy-duty work gloves; park on flat surface, engage parking brake, use wood blocks or wheel chocks; remove ignition key; disconnect spark plug wires; raise deck safely or remove deck for access.
- step 1: Prepare per safety list above.
- step 2: Lodge a block of wood between blade and deck edge to keep the blade from turning; loosen hardware with socket and ratchet; remove hardware and blade.
- step 3: Install new/sharpened blade, again using a wood block to stop spinning. Sails (high sides) must face upward when placing blade center over spindle shaft. Cupped washers: cupped side toward the blade. Torque hardware to John Deere's recommendation in the owner's manual.
- step 4: (sharpen) clean with rag, wire brush for buildup; secure in vise; sharpen one side, flip and do the other.
- step 5: File: push file at an angle in one direction along blade; no sawing motion. Drill sharpener: flat guide along flat portion of blade with cutting edge in bevel slot; move up and down the edge. Grinder: hold perpendicular to edge, run along length, keep moving to remove a little metal at a time.
- step 6: Balance after sharpening: unbalanced blades cause excessive vibration, spindle damage and potential engine damage. Use blade balancer (shave metal off heavy side until equal) or nail on wall (nail head about 1 inch from wall; blade center hole on nail; shave the side that hangs lower until level).
- confirm: bent blade off the mower: lay used blade on a new blade; if it doesn't lie flat, replace.
- confirm: bent blade on the mower: park on hard level surface; measure ground to blade tip, rotate 180 degrees, measure again; more than 1/8" difference = replace.
- mistake: Never straighten a bent blade; weakened metal can break off and fly out.
- tip: If a blade is bent, also check the spindle housing for damage (bent spindle or bearing).
- tip: Lawn chemicals can corrode blade and deck so blades fuse to the deck; remove blades periodically.
- pro: Dealer sharpening: safer, often cheaper than a new blade, grinds out large nicks, properly balanced (less vibration and deck damage). Author keeps a second sharpened set.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: Sail is the high part of the blade that creates air movement under the deck; dirt sucked into deck wears and thins it. Replace blades when sail appears thinner than the center of the blade.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: dull blades cause uneven cut and lawn browning a day or two after mowing.
- fact: Small chips can be sharpened out; large gouge = replace (affects cut and unbalances blade). Bent blade (slightly or significantly) = replace. Cracked blade = replace immediately (metal can fly out).
- fact: Sticks, rocks, toys can chip or bend blade; significant impact causes vibration (unbalanced blades or spindle damage). Sand and dust wear blades faster; thin grass lawn sucks more dirt into deck.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades: replace yearly, sharpen every 25 h.
