## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- symptom: Dull blades: grass tips turn brown a few days after mowing (bruised/torn); strips of grass left behind when blade tips are extremely worn.
- fact: Replace blades once a year at annual service. Sharpen the removed set if still good and keep as a spare for mid-season damage so mowing isn't delayed.
- fact: Sharpen after every 25 hours (about twice a year for the average homeowner); more often if you mow more than average or in dry, dusty conditions.
- fact: New blades are factory sharpened and paint coated; no need to sharpen or remove the paint on the edge.
- safety: Follow owner's manual: remove ignition key, disconnect spark plug wires, set parking brake. Heavy-duty work gloves. Park on a flat surface, engage the parking brake, use wood blocks or wheel chocks to stop rolling. Raise the deck safely or remove it if needed to access the underside.
- tools: ratchet and socket, block of wood, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional)
- step 1: Wedge a block of wood between the blade and deck edge so the blade doesn't move; loosen hardware with socket and ratchet; remove hardware and blade.
- step 2: Install new or sharpened blades, using a wood block to stop the blade spinning while tightening. Sails (high sides) face upward when placing the blade center over the spindle shaft.
- step 3: Cupped washers go with the cupped side toward the blade. Torque hardware to the manufacturer's specification (owner's manual).
- step 4: Reused sharpened blades must be balanced before install.
- tools: DIY sharpening: 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on the wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves.
- step: Clean blades with a rag; wire brush for stubborn buildup. Clamp in a vise; flip and clamp to do the other side.
- step: File - push at an angle in one direction; no sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges.
- step: Drill sharpener - flat guide along the flat portion of the blade, cutting edge slotted into the bevel; move up and down the edge.
- step: Angle/bench grinder - perpendicular to the edge; run the length to level rough spots; keep moving, removing a little metal at a time.
- tip: Balance after sharpening to avoid excessive vibration, spindle damage and potential engine damage. A balancer is inexpensive. Shave metal from the heavy side until equal. Nail alternative: nail head about an inch from the wall, blade center hole on the nail, shave the side that hangs lower until level.
- pro: Author has his dealership sharpen blades (drop off, pick up a couple of days later; keeps a second sharpened set). Reasons: safer, often cheaper than new blades, can grind out large nicks/gouges, blade properly balanced (less vibration and deck damage).
- fact: Annual service checklist listed in the Source: change engine oil and filter; replace spark plugs; remove debris from cooling fan and engine; remove debris from hydro fans and transmission; clean battery terminals and check the battery; replace air filter; replace fuel filter; check the fuel system; check tire pressures; check deck pulleys and belt; scrape the deck; replace or sharpen blades; grease the tractor; check the safety system; wash the tractor.

## page: procedures/measure-a-mower-blade
- confirm: Blade removed: lay on a new blade; if it doesn't lay flat and there's a bend, replace.
- confirm: Blade installed: park on a hard level surface; measure ground to blade tip, rotate 180 degrees and measure again; more than 1/8" difference = replace.
- fact: Sail worn when thinner than the blade center (sand sucked into the deck wears it). Small chips fixed by sharpening; larger chips/gouges mean replace.
- fact: Cracked blade: replace right away (metal can break off and fly out).
- mistake: Never straighten a bent blade; weakened metal prone to crack.
- tip: After finding a bent blade, check the spindle housing for bearing/spindle damage.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: Brown torn grass tips; strips left behind.
- fix: Sharpen or replace; bent = replace.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- fact: Sail is the high side that creates air movement under the deck.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Sand and debris wear blades prematurely; a thin lawn sucks more dirt into the deck than a thick one. Lawn chemicals can corrode blade and deck so blades fuse to the deck; remove blades periodically.
- tip: Walk the lawn first to remove sticks, rocks, toys; impact can chip or bend blades and cause vibration from imbalance or spindle damage.
