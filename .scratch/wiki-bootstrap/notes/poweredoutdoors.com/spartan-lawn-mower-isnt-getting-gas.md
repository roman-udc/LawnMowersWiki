## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Spartan mower turns over but won't start / not getting gas"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- safety: work on the fuel system in a well-ventilated area; fumes are harmful; follow operator's manual.
- fact: an engine not getting enough fuel either won't start or runs sluggish.

## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- fact: insufficient fuel also gives sluggish running; same six causes.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Spartan requires unleaded gas, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol. Ethanol attracts moisture, can corrode fuel system components and leave varnish. Ethanol-free or low-ethanol best. Consume within 30 days; use additive if more than can be used in 30 days.
- fix: drain the tank (fuel siphon pump works great, into a fuel container); refill with fresh fuel with additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (stabilize, reduce moisture, clean the fuel system).

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: inline filter between the fuel lines; replace at least once a year.
- fix: replace; arrow on the plastic housing points in the direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: gummy deposits from old fuel.
- confirm: use the fuel shut-off valve (at the bottom of the fuel tank) to start and stop flow, or hose pinch pliers if there is no valve; find the section that doesn't pass fuel.
- fix: shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner into it, blow out with compressed air; replace if you can't clear it. Replace a dry, cracking line before it leaks.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: plastic or metal pump present when the tank sits lower than the carburetor; a vacuum pump builds pressure using crankcase vacuum. Replace if cracked, damaged or leaking.
- confirm: check fuel reaches the inlet port; remove the fuel line from the carburetor into a container, start fuel flow, start the mower; steady or pulsating stream = working. Replace if damaged or not supplying enough fuel.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: mounted on the top or side of the engine block, usually below or behind the air filter; fuel jet and parts can clog.
- confirm: make sure fuel reaches the carburetor; remove the air filter from the housing, spray a little carburetor cleaner into the intake, start; if it starts, runs and then shuts off, remove and clean the carburetor. If it doesn't start even with this, the problem may not be fuel.
- fix: clean (most mechanical homeowners can); otherwise repair shop.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: tank must vent through the cap or it acts as a vacuum.
- confirm: remove cap, start and run; if it runs fine, install the cap and keep running; if it eventually shuts down with the cap on but restarts and runs as soon as the cap is removed, the cap is clogged.
- fix: try cleaning the cap (doesn't always work); buy a new one.
