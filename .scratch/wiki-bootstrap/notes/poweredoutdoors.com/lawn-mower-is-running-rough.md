## page: problems/engine-runs-rough
- alias: "running rough", "sluggish", "bogging down"
- causes-named: old-fuel, water-in-fuel, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, wrong-choke-setting, mowing-too-fast
- safety: follow operator's manual; wait for moving parts to stop; remove spark plug wires.
- pro: If none solve it, take to a small engine mechanic; internal engine problems are hard to diagnose without proper engine tools.
- tip: Routine maintenance and checking oil before mowing prevents rough running.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas loses combustibility as quickly as 30 days after purchase; most gas has ethanol, which attracts moisture and forms a gummy solution restricting the fuel system.
- fix: Drain the tank with a siphon; refill with fuel for your engine type; stabilize if not consumed within 30 days (Sea Foam Motor Treatment cited).

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- fact: Water in fuel (e.g. mower left in hard rain; anecdote of a child putting a garden hose in the tank) makes the engine sputter because water isn't combustible and the engine has trouble compressing it.
- fix: drain with a siphon and refill with fresh fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dusty conditions plug the filter; engine can't get enough air.
- fix: Remove, check; clean if dirty, replace if extremely dirty, covered in oil, or not in good condition.
- step 1: Remove the air filter cover (held by snaps, wing nuts, knobs or clips).
- step 2: Carefully remove the filter without letting dirt fall into the intake.
- step 3: Wipe out the housing, again keeping dirt out of the intake.
- step 4: Tap the paper filter against a solid surface using the plastic/rubber edge to loosen dirt. Do NOT use compressed air (damages paper).
- step 5: Hold up to light; if you can't see light through the paper or it is oil-covered, don't reuse.
- step 6: Install filter and reattach cover.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: rough/intermittent running; plug covered in carbon or oil.
- fix: replace when tip very dark, porcelain cracked or electrode burnt; make sure gap correct and wire attached securely; remove with socket wrench. Author prefers new plug over cleaning; if cleaning use a wire brush.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Filter strains fuel; if not changed or fuel is dirty it restricts flow; engine runs sluggish (too much air vs fuel).
- fix: replace; arrow on the filter faces fuel flow direction: toward the carburetor, away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Gummy deposits from old fuel restrict flow; sluggish or no running. (Locating the clog is covered in another article.)

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carb sits on top or side of the engine block, usually below/behind the air filter. Deposits clog passageways and make small parts stick; wrong air/fuel mix.
- confirm: if plug and air filter are good and fuel reaches the carb, the carb is a likely cause.
- fix: clean the carburetor; many homeowners can do it, or use a repair shop.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cap must vent; a broken or clogged vent creates a vacuum, fuel can't leave the tank; mower runs sluggish and eventually shuts down.
- confirm: remove the cap while it runs (keep dirt out of the tank); if runs fine, then sluggish and dies a while after reinstalling cap = vent problem.
- fix: replace the cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Plugged deck loads the engine; it bogs and runs rough; dull blades worsen it. Scrape with deck scraper or wire brush; avoid wet grass.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts air for cold starts; must be adjusted once warm or the mower runs rough/stops from too little air.
- fix: check choke position while running; clean choke and linkage if it won't open/close.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: Slow ground speed in thick/tall grass. For tall grass cut at the tallest height first then lower for the next cut; very tall grass is better handled with a brush cutter or rotary mower.
