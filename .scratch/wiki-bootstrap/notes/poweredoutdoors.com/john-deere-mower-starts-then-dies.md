## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "John Deere starts and then shuts off, dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, clogged-air-filter, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-deck (unmapped)
- fact: Typically air or fuel restriction (plugged filters, clogged lines, bad fuel, dirty carburetor), or bad plug or coil.
- tip: Routine annual maintenance prevents these: check oil level and air filter, scrape deck, sharpen blade.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gasoline starts to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol and other additives decay and separate it. Ethanol attracts moisture, leaving sticky residue that clogs fuel system.
- fix: Drain and flush tank; refill with fresh fuel with stabilizer and cleaner (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment). Option: ethanol-free fuel sold as recreation fuel or REC-90, or in canisters at hardware stores; pricey.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Gummy residue from old fuel clogs jets.
- confirm: Remove air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, try to start; if it starts but won't keep running, take the carburetor apart and clean.
- fix: Clean it (link to John Deere carb cleaning article), have a shop clean it, or install a new carburetor.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Use shut-off valve or crimp fuel lines to start/stop flow while checking sections for clogs; spray carb cleaner into the line to loosen, then compressed air; if it won't clear, replace the line.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: Plugged or broken fins let the engine overheat and shut down after running. Clean around fins, replace broken ones, remove debris around engine; careful, engine hot.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Pressure builds in crankcase as crankshaft and rod push through extra oil; overheats and shuts down. Oil can enter cylinder via valve train, burn, and smoke, clogging air filter and starving engine.
- fix: Drain until dipstick reads between the full lines: drain plug, oil filter, oil evacuator, turkey baster (don't reuse for cooking). Check/replace air filter if clogged by smoke. If problems persist, small engine mechanic should check for internal damage.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Sluggish running, dies after running a while. Check and clean several times per mowing season.
- fix (paper): remove filter, keep dirt out of intake, wipe housing, tap on solid surface, no compressed air, hold to light; reuse if you see light; replace if no light, damaged, or oil-covered.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Carbon-buildup plug gives intermittent running; may spark enough to start but not keep running.
- fix: Clean; replace if excessively dark or damaged. Replace annually at service. Make sure plug wires are secure and gap per engine manufacturer.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Mower dies once it gets hot; windings separate and short, plugs get insufficient voltage.
- confirm: Ohm meter for break in continuity; replace if found.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- symptom: Choke restricts air for cold starts; if left on after warm-up engine gets more fuel and less air and shuts down.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- symptom: Plugged vent makes tank vacuum restricting fuel; mower dies after starting.
- confirm: Loosen or remove fuel cap and start; if it runs, replace the cap; run a short time to see if it shuts down again (then likely the cap). Clearing may not work.
