## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Toro mower shaking excessively"
- alias: "deck vibrates when blades engaged"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, bad-pto-clutch, dull-or-bent-blade, debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- safety: remove spark plug boot/wires so engine can't start while under the deck; wait for all parts to stop
- tip: check every option; strangest things cause vibration (author found a wood chip wedged in a pulley, belt hitting it rattled the whole deck; also little rocks, wood chips, tree debris)

## page: causes/engine-mount-loose
- fix: check engine mounting bolts (motor mounts) are tight; replace missing, worn or broken bolts; they reduce vibration when running
- tip: push mower: check them if vibration begins when blades engage

## page: causes/bad-pto-clutch
- symptom: electric clutch contains a ball bearing that can fail and cause vibration; manual-engagement clutch with belts and pulleys has linkage bushings that wear
- fact: push mowers have no electric clutch but have a belt engagement lever to check

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: unbalanced blade (worn more on one side, from sand or incorrect sharpening) vibrates
- confirm: after replacing blades make sure right length and centre hole size, installed correctly; check balance with a blade balancer or hang on a nail
- fix: balanced blades

## page: causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle
- confirm: look at the top of the blade shaft (spindle shaft) for string, wire or other material
- fix: remove and check no additional damage

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: bearings in spindle housing (quill assemblies on smaller units); with work gloves grab both blade ends and rock up and down; movement means bearing bad
- fix: replace bearing only or whole cutter housing assembly if available

## page: procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly
- step 1: remove belt from the pulley: put something narrow between belt and groove of the bad pulley, turn the belt by hand, let object follow to pulley and pry belt off
- step 2: remove pulley: socket on top nut, normally regular thread so counterclockwise; may be set screws (allen/hex) on the shaft; may need a puller
- step 3: remove spindle: 4 to 8 bolts hold it; remove and assembly drops out the bottom of deck
- step 4: replace bearing: housing on flat hard surface; sometimes a snap ring holds it, other times replace whole housing; if bearing replaceable, replace it, grease it, reassemble in reverse
- step 5: bolt housing to deck, install pulley, make sure everything tight with no movement; reinstall belt and turn by hand to check it's on all pulleys

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- fact: check belts and pulleys, and bearing/bushing on the engagement linkage; remove deck covers if fitted

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: keep belt/pulley area clean; dry grass packed on top of deck can build heat and cause a fire
- fact: debris lodged in mower or pulleys causes vibration

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- safety: dry grass packed in the belt housing area on top of the deck can build heat and start a fire; clean belt and pulley area during cleaning
