## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: Interval: change Husqvarna blades at least once a year; sharpen every 25 hours (about twice a year for average homeowner). New blades come sharpened and balanced; no sharpening needed.
- fact: Sand, gravel, rocks dull/ding blades prematurely. Thinly grass-covered lawn causes more wear than thick grass (more dirt collected under deck).
- safety: Always remove the spark plug boot(s) and ignition key before working under the deck. Wear safety glasses and work gloves (sharp edges).
- tools: (removal) ratchet, work gloves, safety glasses, torque wrench, cordless impact gun (optional)
- tools: (sharpening) 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; work gloves
- step 1: Remove spark plug boot and ignition key.
- step 2: Remove blade bolt with socket and ratchet; use a cordless impact gun if stubborn. Alternative for stubborn bolts: place a block of wood over the bolt head and hit it with a hammer to loosen.
- step 3: Remove bolt, blade and any washers. Reinstall in opposite order. If reusing blades, ensure balanced before reinstalling.
- step 4: (sharpen) wear glasses and gloves; clean blade with rag, wire brush for stubborn buildup; secure blade in vise; sharpen one side then flip and secure to do the other.
- step 5: (file) push the file at an angle in one direction along the blade; do not use a sawing motion; remove rough spots and uneven edges.
- step 5b: (drill sharpener) power the drill; place the flat guide along the flat portion of blade with cutting edge in the bevel slot; move up and down the blade edge until no rough spots or nicks.
- step 5c: (grinder) hold angle grinder perpendicular to the blade edge; run along length to level rough spots/nicks; keep moving so it removes a little metal at a time for an even result.
- step 6: (balance) blade balancer to check equal weight; shave more metal off the heavy side until equal. Or nail-on-wall method: nail head about 1 inch from wall, place blade center hole over nail; remove metal from the side that hangs lower until blade sits level.
- confirm: bent blade without removal: park on flat level surface, measure ground to one blade end, record, rotate 180 degrees, measure other end; difference greater than 1/8" means replace.
- confirm: spindle damage: grab both sides of blade (gloves on) and rock up and down; any movement or knocking = take spindle assembly apart and inspect.
- mistake: Never try to straighten a bent blade; weakens the metal, cracks can develop and break off.
- tip: Lawn chemicals can corrode blades; remove blades occasionally so they don't fuse to the deck.
- tip: Author keeps a second sharpened set on hand and has a local dealership sharpen.
- pro: Professional sharpening: can grind out large nicks/gouges, properly balances (reduces vibration and deck damage), often cheaper than a new blade, safer than DIY.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: poor/uneven cut; lawn may turn brown a couple of days after mowing with dull blades.
- fact: Chips and dings from hitting objects usually sharpen out; large gouges mean replace. Bent blade = replace (see 1/8" measurement). Impact can also damage spindle and cause vibration.
- fact: Sticks, rocks, toys run over can bend or gouge blade. Walk the yard before each mowing and remove items.

## page: causes/worn-blade-sail
- symptom: wear on top of the sail looks thinner at ends than toward center; source says this is normal wear from sand and dirt the blade sucks up in regular mowing.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: Blades: replace at least yearly, sharpen every 25 h; cracked blade must be replaced (can break off and throw metal, injure a person or damage house siding).
- fact: Sandy lawn wears blade edges quickly; check more frequently.

- fact: Damaged blades can cause bad or uneven cut.
