## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Country Clipper zero turn won't start"
- fact: engine isn't getting enough air, fuel or spark.
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, wrong-choke-setting, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-safety-switch, incorrect-starting-procedure, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- safety: follow operator's manual; remove spark plug wire before repairs.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "mower won't even turn over"
- causes-named: weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, incorrect-starting-procedure, faulty-charging-system

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: fill with fresh unleaded gas, octane 87 or higher, ethanol no greater than 10%. Author has seen people forget to check the tank.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can begin to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Ethanol attracts moisture; ethanol/water mix evaporates leaving varnish and gummy deposits; ethanol not good for small engines. Never use more than 10% ethanol.
- brand: Country Clipper requires unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol.
- fix: remove old fuel with a siphon pump or other method; fill gas can with fresh fuel and add additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment (cleans fuel system, reduces moisture, stabilizes); add to tank; if it starts let it run through. If still no start, keep going down the list.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts airflow to start cold engine; choke on/closed for cold start; once warm move to off/open or the engine shuts down. Choke not on: cold engine won't start; choke not off: warm engine won't start.
- fix: choke engaged for cold start, off for warm start; move to off once engine warms.
- confirm: if choke position correct and airflow problems continue, check plugged air filter, stuck choke plate, worn choke cable.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: engine fails to start from lack of air; also may overheat, smoke, and cause engine damage.
- fix: clean paper filter, replace if very dirty or damaged.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Paper filter: remove from housing, do not let dirt fall into intake.
- step 2: wipe dirt from housing with clean cloth.
- step 3: tap filter against hand or solid surface to knock off dirt.
- step 4: hold to light; if light isn't visible through every area, or filter is damaged/torn/no longer seals, replace.
- step 5: install filter.
- step 6: Foam pre-filter (if used; works with paper filter): replace if dark spots, brittle, or torn. Wash in water and mild detergent, rinse until water clear, squeeze dry (do not wring out to avoid tearing), lay flat until completely dry, install.
- mistake: never add oil to the pre-filter; oil damages the primary paper filter.
- tip: other filter types: see operator's manual.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: mower has a fuel pump when the tank is lower than the carburetor; pump has three ports: inlet, outlet, and a port connected to a line off the crankcase that pressurizes the pump.
- confirm: look for small cracks or fuel leaking; cracks/leaks mean replace.
- confirm: verify fuel reaches pump: shut fuel valve or clamp; disconnect line from pump inlet into a container lower than the tank; open valve/unclamp; fuel should flow; if not, check lines and filter for blockage.
- confirm: verify pump output: reconnect inlet line, remove fuel line from carburetor, put in container, start mower and watch; steady or pulsating flow expected. Replace pump if unable to pump consistently.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: old fuel leaves gummy deposits; dirty fuel may plug filter. Fix: replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: test flow by stopping and starting fuel flow, checking each line section.
- fix: shut off fuel, remove line, spray carb cleaner in tube and blow compressed air through until clear (cleaner loosens, air dislodges); repeat as needed; replace line if clog can't be removed.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: carburetor gives proper gas-air mix; varnish from old fuel clogs passages and makes internals stick.
- fix: most times cleaning is enough; rebuild or replace if damaged parts or extremely dirty.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: 12-volt battery should read 12.7 V or greater; test with multimeter; below 12.7 V charge it. If it won't hold charge, replace.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: clean corroded terminals in baking soda solution of 2 cups water with 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda, scrub with wire brush.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Country Clipper uses several safety switches; faulty ones prevent starting.
- confirm: test with multimeter, or temporarily bypass to identify bad switch.
- safety: do not operate a mower without safety switches installed; always keep them installed and working.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: remove and inspect for carbon buildup, cracked porcelain insulator, burnt electrode; replace if any; gap to engine manufacturer spec; securely attach plug wire. Loose wire or wrong gap causes starting/intermittent running problem.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- confirm: test with multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: coil provides voltage to the spark plug.
- confirm: after verifying plug is good, check coil continuity with multimeter; replace if break in continuity.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum when turning key; a wire on the solenoid getting hot, smoking or melting.
- fact: solenoid is an electromagnetic on-off switch that actuates the starter motor.
- fix: test per procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid; replace if bad.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: specific starting steps for safety, e.g. brake must be set. Refer to operator's manual to avoid triggering safety features that block starting.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: tank is vented through the gas cap; plugged cap makes vacuum so fuel doesn't reach the carburetor.
- confirm: loosen gas cap to let air in; if mower now starts, cap may be the problem. Confirm by tightening cap and running; if it starves, dies, and won't restart until the cap is loosened again.
- fix: replace cap no longer venting.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: not the main reason for no-start but can cause a weak battery; bad stator or alternator among electrical parts.
- pro: have a small engine mechanic identify the actual cause; otherwise guessing gets expensive.
