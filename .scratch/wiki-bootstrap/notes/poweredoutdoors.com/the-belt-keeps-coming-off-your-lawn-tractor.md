## page: problems/belt-keeps-coming-off
- alias: "lawn tractor deck belt keeps coming off"
- causes-named: worn-or-stretched-belt, belt-guard-or-keeper-missing, worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing, bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing, bad-belt-tensioner-spring, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: also damaged brackets (see below).
- safety: follow owner's manual precautions.
- tip: prolong belt life: clean deck after each mowing; check for rubbing on deck components; keep oil and rust off the belt; periodically check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing failure; fix or replace damaged brackets or keepers.

## page: causes/worn-or-stretched-belt
- symptom: replace a belt with shiny glazed appearance, stretched, loose, or cracked.
- symptom (shredded): likely to snap; usually installed incorrectly or rubbing a deck component. Check routing (decal on mower, owner's manual diagram or parts diagram), then look for an out-of-place bracket with a shiny worn look; adjust or replace; if bracket has a bushing, may replace the bushing or need the bracket assembly.
- fact: OEM deck belts run $80 to $250.

## page: causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing
- fact: belt keepers/guides are rods sitting between 1/8 in and 1/4 in from the pulley; belt doesn't touch them; extra precaution.
- symptom: slack belt hits the keeper and bends the rod, raising the chance of the belt coming off.
- fix: straighten with a hammer or other tools; if it can't be reshaped to sit correctly, replace.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- symptom: bad bearing pulley doesn't sit flat; shifts, belt slips and comes off.
- confirm: grab the pulley to check vertical movement (not level with deck); slowly spin; resistance or bearing noise or not flat and parallel means replace.

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: work gloves; grab each end of the blade and rock up and down for movement or a knock; then remove the spindle housing, inspect bearing, replace if bad; repeat for each spindle housing.
- fix: some tractors let you rebuild the housing with a new bearing; others need the whole spindle housing assembly.

## page: causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring
- fact: tensioner bracket and spring hold the idler pulley in line, pulley on one side of bracket and spring on the other; the spring or its hole in the bracket can wear, vibrating the deck and the belt off; replace bracket or spring if broken or worn.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: debris stuck under tensioner or built up stops it moving, losing proper tension.
- safety: dry debris on the deck can catch fire from belt friction heat; clean the deck after each mowing.
- fact: debris in pulley grooves stresses the belt stretching around it; belt can come off or snap; clean pulleys so the belt sits in the grooves.

## page: causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt
- fact: rust from deck and pulleys on the belt dries it, prone to cracking and coming off; oil from a leak on the deck makes the belt slip; belt builds heat when it can't grip the pulleys.
- fix: repair oil leak or remove rust; replace very rusty pulleys; then replace the belt.
