## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Dixon zero turn won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Engine doesn't start when it isn't getting air, fuel or spark. Guide is for gas Dixons (Dixon also makes diesel; separate article).

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- symptom: Owners forget to check; may have forgotten last fill or have a fuel leak using more gas than usual.
- fix: Add fresh gas; check for fuel leaks and repair.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Dixon requires unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol (sold as Regular or E10). Avoid E15, E30, E85 (up to 15%, 30%, 85% ethanol).
- fact: Old gas develops varnish and gummy deposits that clog fuel system components. Ethanol attracts moisture, which corrodes fuel parts; the ethanol/water mix sinks to the bottom of the tank and burns extremely hot in the engine, causing engine damage.
- fact: Gasoline can begin to break down in as little as 30 days; buy only what you can use in that time, or stabilize it (Sea Foam Motor Treatment; STA-BIL a good alternative).
- fix: Siphon old gas out with a fuel siphon pump; fill a gas can with fresh gas and mix in a stabilizer.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: Dixon: unleaded, 87 octane minimum, max 10% ethanol; avoid E15/E30/E85.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: Gas breaks down in as little as 30 days; stabilize with Sea Foam Motor Treatment (author's preference) or STA-BIL if you hold more than you use in 30 days.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Intermittent starting problems. Look for buildup of dirt, carbon and oil, burnt electrode, broken porcelain.
- confirm: Check gap per engine manufacturer spec and that the plug wire is securely attached.
- fix: Clean a dirty plug with a wire brush; very dark or damaged plug = replace. Set gap, reattach wire.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: Plugged filter restricts air; may keep mower from starting, or if it starts, run rough or overheat.
- mistake: Running without an air filter, even briefly; risks engine damage.
- fix: Inspect; clean or replace.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove the air filter housing cover (clips, wing nuts or hand knobs).
- step 2: Carefully remove the filter without letting dirt fall into the intake.
- step 3: Wipe dirt out of the housing, again keeping it out of the intake.
- step 4: Tap the filter against a hard surface to knock dirt out. Never use compressed air on a paper filter; it damages it.
- step 5: Hold the paper element up to a light. Light shines through = reuse; no light, damaged, or oil-soaked = replace.
- step 6: Install filter and cover.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: A pump is needed when the carburetor sits above the tank; it uses crankcase vacuum to draw fuel uphill. Replace when it can't do this.
- symptom: Visible small crack in housing or fuel leaking from the seams.
- confirm: First verify fuel arrives at the pump inlet line. Then, with flow stopped (shut-off valve, fuel clamps or hose pinch pliers), remove the line from the carburetor into a container, restore flow and start the mower; watch for a steady or pulsating flow. Insufficient flow = replace pump.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Filter strains dirt out of fuel; change annually or more often with dirty fuel. Clogged filter restricts flow and mower may not start.
- fix: Replace; the arrow on an inline filter points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Old fuel leaves gummy deposits that restrict the line.
- confirm: Stop and start fuel flow while checking sections: shut off flow (shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank or pinch-off pliers), choose a section, remove the end furthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump), turn on flow. Good flow: shut off and reattach. No flow: clogged.
- fix: Stop flow, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner in, blow through with compressed air until free of debris and gummy residue; repeat as needed. Replace the line if the clog won't clear.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: The carburetor has a small bowl on the bottom that stores some gas; old gas leaves varnish that clogs the fuel jet and makes small parts malfunction. The carburetor regulates the gas mixed with air for combustion.
- fix: Take it apart and clean with carburetor cleaner, including the float bowl and needle; replace if too dirty to clean adequately.
- difficulty: Doable if a little mechanical.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: The cap is designed to vent so tank pressure equalizes; without venting, a vacuum forms and fuel can't leave the tank. Fix: replace a cap with a plugged vent.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- symptom: Bad battery, loose cables or corroded terminals stop it starting.
- confirm: Multimeter; want about 12.7 volts. Below that, charge. If it won't hold a charge, replace.
- fix: Clean corroded terminals with a baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) and a wire brush; check terminals attached securely.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Same baking soda/wire-brush cleaning; confirm terminals not corroded and securely attached.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Manufacturer fits switches to prevent the deck running without the operator present; there may also be a switch that prevents starting when the parking brake isn't engaged and the speed levers aren't in the open/outward position. See the operator's manual for all switches and locations.
- confirm: Test with a multimeter or temporarily bypass to identify a bad switch.
- safety: Never operate without a safety switch; never run with one bypassed.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: Key turned and mower doesn't start or even turn over.
- confirm: Multimeter test; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: The coil provides voltage so the plug fires.
- confirm: After verifying the plug is good, check coil continuity with a multimeter; a break in continuity = replace.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: Charging system unable to keep the mower running can make it die while mowing, and drain the battery so it won't start next time. Bad stator or alternator.
- fix: Take it to a repair shop. Author's reason: without experience you'll throw parts at it; most shops don't allow returns on electrical parts.
- pro: yes - charging system faults go to a shop.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Dixon safety features require setting the parking brake and putting speed bars in neutral to start. The choke must be set correctly for a cold engine: choke on restricts air so the engine burns more fuel to start cold; once warm, disengage it to allow airflow.
- fix: Follow the Dixon operator's manual.

## page: procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower
- step: Set the parking brake; speed bars (levers) in neutral; choke on for a cold engine, disengage once warm (see operator's manual).
