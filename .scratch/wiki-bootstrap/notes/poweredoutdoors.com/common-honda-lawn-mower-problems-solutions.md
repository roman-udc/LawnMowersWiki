## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Honda lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, weak-or-dead-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-safety-switch, bad-ignition-switch, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter
- symptom: fuel, air or spark problems from fuel restriction, failed mechanical parts, plugged filters, dirty fuel components, loose wire connections or failed electrical parts
- brand: Honda push mowers (pull start mowers have a recoil)
- fact: empty tank - fill with fresh fuel
- fact: bad/old fuel breaks down over time, prone to clogging system; drain tank, refill fresh, use an additive like Sea Foam to stabilize and help clean the fuel system
- fact: faulty/clogged fuel cap - vent clogs, tank forms vacuum restricting flow; clean the cap vent or replace the cap
- fact: bad spark plug - dirty or broken, bad connection, wrong gap; replace, secure connection, gap to manufacturer spec
- fact: plugged air filter - remove and clean; replace if bad
- fact: plugged fuel filter restricts flow - replace
- fact: blocked fuel lines - deposits and dirt; use carb cleaner and compressed air to clear; replace the line if necessary
- fact: dirty carburetor from bad fuel/ethanol deposits - clean components; replace if needed
- fact: bad battery or loose/dirty terminals - charge, replace if it won't hold a charge, tighten cables, clean corrosion
- fact: bad safety switch - test and replace
- fact: bad ignition switch or coil - test and replace a bad switch
- fact: broken recoil on pull-start mower - check if it can be restrung or parts such as the pulley replaced; replace assembly if needed

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Honda mower loses power and dies"
- alias: "dies while mowing"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: Honda starts then dies when it overheats or has a fuel or air restriction
- fact: ignition coil can stop working when hot as the windings separate and short out; test the coil for a break in continuity; replace the coil
- fact: bad fuel, plugged air filter, clogged fuel lines, dirty carburetor - same fixes as above (drain/fresh fuel with Sea Foam; clean filter; carb cleaner and compressed air; clean all carburetor components or replace)

## page: problems/engine-dies-when-hot
- causes-named: bad-ignition-coil
- fact: ignition coil windings separate and short out when the mower is hot

## page: problems/mower-is-smoking
- alias: "Honda mower smoking"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, worn-piston-rings-or-valve-guides, spilled-oil-on-engine
- symptom: smoking when engine gets hot from lack of airflow or low crankcase oil; valve train and piston ring problems make it hot or push oil into the cylinder to burn off
- confirm: check for engine oil leaks dripping onto the muffler
- fact: plugged air filter - restricts air, engine hot, burns more fuel than air required for combustion and smokes; clean or replace
- fact: too much oil in crankcase - pressure builds, oil gets into the cylinder through the valve train; drain a little oil to the manufacturer's recommended level
- fact: too little oil - friction and extreme heat; take to a small engine repair shop to inspect for damage; adding oil is often too late
- pro: internal problems (piston ring, valve train, engine gasket) - take to local small engine repair shop

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: smoking (problems/mower-is-smoking)
- fact: excess oil causes pressure and oil enters the cylinder via the valve train
- fix: drain a little oil to the recommended level

## page: problems/mower-is-vibrating
- alias: "Honda mower shaking"
- causes-named: engine-mount-loose, dull-or-bent-blade, bent-crankshaft, loose-steering-linkage, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Honda vibrates normally when operating; vibration intensifies with loose/broken parts, worn engagement linkages, bad clutch, or debris stuck in the mower
- fact: loose or missing engine mounting bolts - they reduce vibration; secure and replace missing bolts
- fact: unbalanced or damaged blades - replace damaged blades; blades must be balanced before installing
- fact: bent crankshaft - occurs when blades hit a solid object; check when blades are not the cause; mechanic must remove the engine to replace it
- fact: loose linkages and bushings wear - tighten and replace worn parts
- fact: debris lodged in deck or mower - even the smallest items cause significant vibration; check closely
- fact: bad deck components can cause shaking: unbalanced blades or damaged crankshaft

## page: problems/uneven-cut
- alias: "Honda mower cuts uneven"
- causes-named: wrong-tire-pressure, dull-or-bent-blade, blade-installed-upside-down, bad-tire, wrong-cutting-height, bent-crankshaft, wrong-engine-speed, mowing-too-fast, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- tip: check tire pressure first (a low tire makes the mower not sit level), then the deck
- fact: causes listed in a table: worn mower blade, bent blade, blade installed wrong, damaged wheel, incorrect cutting height adjustment, bent crankshaft, engine speed too low, ground speed too fast, clogged mower deck

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "I smell fuel but can't find the leak"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- symptom: fuel odor but no visible leak, because fuel evaporates after the mower has sat a while
- confirm: unless there is a large leak with a puddle, check each fuel component for leaks
- safety: a fuel leak must be fixed; a continually leaking mower can damage the lawn and costs fuel
- fact: dirty/failed carburetor - old-gas buildup causes blockages or part failures; clean and replace damaged parts
- fact: old fuel filter plugged - replace
- fact: bad fuel tank - metal tank can develop a rust hole, poly tank can leak at seams; replace; a rust hole can sometimes be repaired if no replacement is available
- fact: bad fuel shut-off valve - metal and plastic valves prone to leaking; replace
- fact: cracked fuel lines - rubber dries and cracks; replace
- fact: cracked primer bulb or poorly attached to the hose - replace
- fact: bad seal in the gas cap lets gas splash out around the cap - replace the cap

- fix: check whether it can be restrung or parts like the pulley replaced; replace assembly if needed
