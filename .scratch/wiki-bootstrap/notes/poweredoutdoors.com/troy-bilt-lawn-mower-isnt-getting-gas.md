## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Troy-Bilt mower isn't getting gas / fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: no fuel means sluggish running or no start; with a fuel restriction the engine can't power the blade, gives a poor cut, and likely bogs down and dies.

## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- fact: same fuel-restriction causes give sluggish running and bogging.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas older than 30 days begins to break down; ethanol attracts moisture, causing corrosion, varnish and gummy deposits. Use unleaded 87 octane minimum, max 10% ethanol (E10). Never use more than 10%; avoid E15 and E85 (up to 15% and 85% ethanol).
- fix: drain with a siphon pump; refill with fresh gas treated with a fuel system cleaner and stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment. Use within 30 days of purchase or add stabilizer.
- brand: Troy-Bilt - most current mowers have a 4-cycle engine; some 2-cycle still in use. 2-cycle: gas and oil are mixed before filling. 4-cycle: straight gas, never mixed with oil, separate fill ports for gas and oil. Wrong fuel can damage the engine.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: wrong fuel type (2-cycle mix in a 4-cycle or vice versa, >10% ethanol) damages engine.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: strains fuel leaving tank; replace annually, more often with dirty/old gas.
- fix: shut off fuel supply, remove old inline filter, install new with the arrow on the housing pointing in direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: shut off fuel supply (shut-off valve if any, or pinch pliers); pick a section; remove the end furthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank (gas can't run uphill without a pump); turn flow on; watch; stop. Good flow - reattach; otherwise remove the line.
- fix: spray carburetor cleaner into the line, blow compressed air through; repeat until clear; reinstall; if you can't remove the blockage replace the line.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: fuel jet clogs and components stick from old fuel.
- confirm: first make sure fuel reaches the carburetor (if not, check fuel filter and lines). Then remove the air filter and spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake port and start. Do NOT use starter fluid (Source links an article on why). If it starts, runs, then dies, the carburetor is restricting fuel and must be cleaned, rebuilt or replaced.
- fix: disassemble and clean if mechanical; small engine mechanic can do it; or replace the carburetor assembly.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: loosen the cap and start; if it starts and runs strong the cap may be the problem; keep running and tighten the cap; if it runs rough and shuts down after a while, vent is clogged.
- fix: replace the cap with a new Troy-Bilt gas cap.

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- tip: keep the fuel system clean: fresh unleaded with max 10% ethanol (ethanol-free best); additive like Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL Fuel Treatment to stabilize and reduce moisture; replace the fuel filter once a year, more often with dirty fuel or heavier-than-average use.

## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: know your engine type (2-cycle vs 4-cycle) to use the right fuel; 87 octane min, 10% ethanol max.
