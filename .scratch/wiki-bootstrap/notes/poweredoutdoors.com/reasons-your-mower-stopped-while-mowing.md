## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "mower died while mowing"
- symptom: engine isn't getting correct air and fuel
- causes-named: old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, clogged-air-filter, overfilled-oil, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: unleaded decays after about 30 days; ethanol (corn-derived) attracts moisture which leaves gummy residue on evaporation
- fact: use 87 octane or higher, ethanol 10% or less; premium/recreational fuel not required
- tip: use within 30 days; else add Sea Foam to stabilize and reduce moisture

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: regulates fuel and air; deposits from bad fuel are the usual culprit; most can be cleaned; replace if excessive buildup
- tip: clean only if mechanically inclined (many small parts), else local mechanic

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step 1: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner in air intake, start; if it fires up but still won't stay running go inside the carb
- step 2: gather pliers, screwdrivers, sockets, ratchets
- step 3: photograph the carb, especially how linkage and springs go back
- step 4: remove throttle and choke cables if fitted
- step 5: undo filter housing and the nuts/screws holding the carb
- step 6: remove springs slowly without stretching; twist carb a bit if needed; don't tear the gasket between engine block and carb
- step 7: remove bottom screw from the float bowl (holds gas; rag ready)
- step 8: remove bowl without damaging the o-ring; no carb cleaner or other chemical on the o-ring (it stretches, can't reuse)
- step 9: inspect stem (hangs from carb centre) for clogged holes; plugged holes won't draw fuel to the jet; clean with thick wire, flashlight helps; rinse with carb cleaner
- step 10: inspect for hard crusty white buildup (fuel additives incl. ethanol); remove as much as possible; nearly impossible to get all
- step 11: reassemble in reverse order using the photo
- step 12: add fresh fuel plus stabilizer, let bowl fill, start; with a pull cord it may take several pulls

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap must vent; broken cap creates vacuum
- confirm: take cap off and run the mower; if it no longer dies, new cap
- safety: keep dirt/debris out of tank while cap is off; run without deck engaged to avoid dust and grass
- mistake: don't mow with cap off or leave it off; allows debris and water in the tank; only run without it for the test

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- symptom: restricted air; engine runs very hot and often overheats; running a plugged filter can cause significant engine damage
- confirm: remove filter, tap paper filter on hard surface, hold to light; no light means replace
- fact: check frequently

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: engine smokes and stops while mowing; smoke clogs air filter so engine looks elsewhere for air; can cause internal damage or engine replacement

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: also check plugged fuel lines or bad fuel filter when other items don't solve it
