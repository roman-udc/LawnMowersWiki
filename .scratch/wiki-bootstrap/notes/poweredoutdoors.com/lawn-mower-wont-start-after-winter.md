## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "mower won't start after winter"
- fact: untreated fuel or very cold/wet storage contributes.
- causes-named: fuel-valve-off, weak-or-dead-battery, fouled-spark-plug, old-fuel, water-in-fuel, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, wet-electrical-components (corroded/rodent-damaged wiring)
- step 1: make sure the fuel shut-off valve is open (may have been shut for storage).
- step 2: check battery voltage; charge if low; replace if it won't hold charge.
- step 3: remove and inspect spark plug; replace if corroded or very dirty.
- step 4: old fuel sitting without a good stabilizer must be replaced with fresh; also replace if water in the tank.
- step 5: check fuel flow: remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into the intake, start; if it runs a bit then shuts down, fuel restriction; check fuel lines, fuel pump (if any), carburetor.
- safety: remove spark plug wire(s) before repairs.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- fact: you may have shut the fuel valve before storage; open it so fuel flows.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can degrade as soon as 30 days after purchase; ethanol attracts moisture; ethanol/water mix separates and sinks to tank bottom; water is not combustible; leaves varnish and gummy deposits restricting flow.
- fix: drain tank and add fresh fuel; add additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment to clean and remove moisture.

## page: causes/water-in-fuel
- symptom: water mixed in the fuel (from ethanol separation) means the mower won't start; replace fuel.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop flow with shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers; remove line end from the fuel pump inlet port (or carburetor if no pump) into a container lower than the tank; start flow and check.
- fix: spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air, repeat; reinstall. If can't unclog or cracked, install new line of the same length and diameter.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: fuel bowl stores a little fuel; old fuel leaves sticky crusty deposits; jet clogs, small components stick.
- fix: disassemble and clean with carb cleaner; rebuild or replace if damaged or can't clean.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: plug can corrode over winter or long sitting.
- fix: remove with socket wrench; replace if corroded, very dark or damaged; if good but dirty/carbon-covered, wire brush; confirm gap to engine maker spec; reattach wire.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: a battery left in the cold can freeze and no longer hold charge; a fully charged battery won't freeze.
- fact: freezing temperature chart (charge: 12 V reading / 6 V reading / freezing temp): 100%: 12.78 / 6.39 / -80 F; 92%: 12.66 / 6.33 / -71.3 F; 85%: 12.57 / 6.28 / -62 F; 62%: 12.27 / 6.13 / -16 F; 50%: n/a / n/a / -10 F; 40%: 11.97 / 5.98 / +5 F; 20%: 11.67 / 5.84 / +19 F; zero charge: +20 F.
- confirm: article says use an "ohmmeter" (sic; voltage measurement); fully charged 12 V reads 12.7 V; charge if less; replace if no hold.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: wear safety gear (eyes and skin; acid/electrical shock).
- step 2: access battery and terminals (screwdriver to uncover body).
- step 3: leave battery in its casing with terminal cables attached.
- step 4: connect positive cable first (red, plus).
- step 5: connect negative (black) next. Mnemonic: positive (red +) is the last one off and the first one on.
- step 6: skin only on rubber coating of cables/clamps.
- step 7: set charger voltage (usually 12 V) and amps: start at 2 amps, work up to no more than 10 amps.
- step 8: if charger has a gauge, keep connected until fully charged. A 10 amp charger usually takes about one hour to fully charge a mower battery.

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- symptom: storing with debris or moisture corrodes components; check safety switches and wiring for good connections.
- fact: rodent damage: nests in mowers over winter, chewed wires; damaged wiring/parts must be replaced.
- tip: rodent deterrent such as Grandpa Gus (packets around mower and a spray for wiring) at next storage.

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: battery: remove it or fully charge it for winter; fully charged won't freeze; if unsure remove and store cool, dry, away from moisture.
- fact: fuel tank: either drain or leave fuel plus stabilizer; quality additive that keeps gas stable long; author likes Sea Foam Motor Treatment or STA-BIL Storage. Stabilizer only works with fresh gas, can't reverse old gas.
- step (drain): siphon the tank into a fuel container; run the mower until it stops from no fuel.
- step (stabilize): add stabilizer to fresh gasoline per bottle; add to tank; run a few minutes so treated fuel reaches the system before storing.
- tip: store free of debris and moisture.

## page: procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas
- fact: see old-fuel and flow-check steps above (spray carb cleaner in intake test).
