## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "lawn mower loses power"
- alias: "engine doesn't sound as strong"
- causes-named: cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, mowing-too-fast, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, dull-or-bent-blade, clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, fouled-spark-plug, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, dirty-cooling-fins
- fact: Power loss mostly from lack of air or fuel; too much load; low oil. If troubleshooting fails, take it to the dealer; a mechanic can determine charging system, electrical or internal engine problem.
- fact: Working the engine harder can also overheat it (links to overheating article).

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- tip: Expect more load with wet, tall, thick grass. Mow dry grass for better cut, less deck buildup, less engine load. Avoid very tall grass; if long, set deck to highest for first cut, lower for the next.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fix: Slow ground speed; also slow on inclines.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- symptom: Grass and dirt under the deck jam blades so they don't spin freely.
- fix: Scrape frequently, especially with damp grass; silicone spray on deck underside helps prevent sticking.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: Dull, rounded blades make the engine work harder. Sharpen; replace damaged blades.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- step (foam): remove carefully (no dirt into intake); wash with dish soap and water, rinse until no soap, air dry (sun is faster); lightly dampen with clean engine oil (not saturated), wring out excess; replace if dry, brittle, or with dark spots.
- step (paper): remove carefully; tap against hard surface; do not use an air compressor to blow it out; hold to light; light shows through = reinstall, no light = replace.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas becomes less effective after about 30 days; deposits create fuel blockages. Most mowers need unleaded 87+ octane, no more than 10% ethanol (ethanol corrosive and attracts moisture); lower is better.
- fix: Drain and add fresh fuel with additive such as Sea Foam to reduce moisture and clean the fuel system.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- symptom: Gummy, crusted spots from bad fuel deposits; most can be cleaned, sometimes must be replaced.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step 1: Remove air filter and spray carb cleaner into the intake; start the engine; if it fires but won't stay running, go inside the carb.
- step 2: Gather pliers, screwdrivers, sockets, ratchets.
- step 3: Photograph the carburetor (linkage and springs) for reassembly.
- step 4: Remove throttle cable and choke cable if present.
- step 5: Undo filter housing and the nuts/screws holding the carb.
- step 6: Slowly remove springs (don't stretch; may need to twist the carb); watch the gasket between engine block and carb so it doesn't tear.
- step 7: Remove the bottom screw from the float bowl (it holds gas; have a rag ready); remove bowl without damaging the o-ring.
- mistake: Do not get carb cleaner or other chemicals on the o-ring; it stretches and can't be reused.
- step 8: Inspect the stem hanging from the carb centre; holes plugged by old fuel won't draw fuel up to the jet; clean with a thick wire (flashlight helps), rinse with carb cleaner.
- step 9: Inspect for hard crusty white buildup (fuel additives including ethanol); remove as much as you can (nearly impossible to remove all).
- step 10: Reassemble in reverse using the photo.
- step 11: Add fresh fuel plus stabilizer, let the bowl fill, start; pull cord may take several pulls.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace a dirty filter.
## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Isolate sections and use clamps to start/stop flow; check fuel runs through each line.
- fix: Remove line, carb cleaner, compressed air or compressor; replace if clog stays or line is dry, cracked, brittle.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: Intermittent spark from carbon, dirt, oil on tip.
- fix: Replace if dirty or damaged; if only dirty and not very dark, wire brush and reuse.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- symptom: Low oil: friction, heat, power loss; heat can melt internal parts, replacing engine.
- tip: Check oil before each use; make a pre-mow checklist.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- symptom: Smoke (bluish-white); pressure pushes oil into cylinder via valve train; thick smoke can plug the air filter. Continued running can cause seal damage, hydrolock, bent piston rod.
- fix: Remove a little oil via oil evacuator, drain plug or turkey baster; check air filter and spark plug.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- fact: Methods: oil evacuator, drain plug, turkey baster.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: Engine air circulation passages clogged: remove the engine shroud and clear grass, leaves, debris. Fins collect dirt that compromises air movement.
- fix: Check for broken fins; remove buildup.
