## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- fact: change blades once a year, more often with sand, gravel, rocks. Sharpen every 25 hours of mowing or twice a year for average homeowner; brown grass tips a couple of days after mowing means sharpen.
- tools (removal): ratchet, work gloves, safety glasses, optional cordless impact gun
- step 1: remove spark plug boot first (do not skip).
- step 2: remove blade nut/bolt with socket and ratchet; most blades are reverse threaded (called right-hand threaded by article), removed counter-clockwise.
- step 3: stuck bolt: block of wood on bolt head, strike with hammer to shock it loose.
- step 4: inspect: sail thinner at ends than center is normal wear (replace if very worn and thin); chips/dings usually sharpened out; large gouges replace; bent (compare to new blade) replace; crack replace (can break off, flying metal, injure people or damage house siding).
- tools (sharpen): 10" flat metal file, drill-powered blade sharpener, or disc/angle/bench grinder; vise; blade balancer or nail on wall; rags; wire brush; safety glasses; gloves
- step 5: wear glasses and gloves; clean blade with rags/wire brush; secure in vise.
- step 6: file: push at 45-degree angle in one direction, no sawing motion; flip and repeat. Drill sharpener: flat guide along flat portion with cutting edge in bevel, move up and down edge; flip. Grinder: hold perpendicular to the edge, keep moving so little metal removed at a time; flip.
- step 7: balance: center hole on nail on wall; if one end hangs lower, sharpen that end until level; or use blade balancer.
- mistake: do not sharpen to a pointed edge; wears faster.
- tip: keep a spare set of blades so you can drop off a set for sharpening without downtime (author's shop does 1 business day, longer in busy season).
- pro: professional sharpening is safer, often cheaper than buying a blade, removes large nicks/gouges, and balances properly.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- symptom: brown grass tips a couple days later.
- fact: sticks, rocks, toys damage blades; damaged blade can cause uneven cut or vibration that can take out a crankshaft seal and cause oil leak.
- fact: sand, dirt wear/dull blades; lawn chemicals can corrode blades/deck making removal nearly impossible, so remove blades periodically.
