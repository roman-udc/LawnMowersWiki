## page: procedures/tune-up
- fact: full service = change engine oil and filter; replace air filter, fuel filter, spark plug; clean around engine and transmission; change transmission oil and filter if required; scrape deck; sharpen blades; grease. Skipping it can shorten mower life.
- time: dealer annual service about 2 hours labor plus parts.
- tools: engine oil and filter; transmission oil and filter if needed (some residential zero-turns have sealed, unserviceable transmissions); air filter; fuel filter; spark plugs; socket or spanner wrenches; drain pan; funnel; filter wrench; rags, work gloves, safety glasses, work surface cover; deck scraper, wire brush or metal putty knife.
- step 1: check the safety system: mower must not start with deck engaged, without brake set, or with levers in open/outward position; operator presence system must shut off the mower when deck engaged and operator not in seat, or parking brake not engaged when operator leaves the seat. Inspect ROPS and seat belt (designed to work together).
- step 2: check battery (see below).
- step 3: change engine oil and filter.
- step 4: change transmission oil and filter if serviceable.
- step 5: replace spark plugs.
- step 6: inspect fuel system, replace fuel filter.
- step 7: check tire pressures and lug nuts.
- step 8: replace air filter.
- step 9: remove dirt and debris from exhaust, engine and transmission areas.
- step 10: check belts, pulleys, spindle housings.
- step 11: scrape the deck.
- step 12: sharpen or change blades.
- step 13: lubricate mower and deck.
- fact: service interval: Husqvarna zero-turn every 50 hours or annually; commercial engines every 100 hours or annually; follow the manual. Between services keep engine, transmission, deck clean and lubricated; check engine and hydraulic oil levels, tire pressures, safety system, damage and loose parts before operating.

## page: guides/routine-service-and-maintenance
- fact: intervals above; pre-use checks: oil levels, tire pressure, safety system, loose parts.
- fact: air pathways around engine and transmission must stay open; buildup on cooling fins, block, engine covers causes overheating shutdown; clean areas around the transmission incl. hydro fan cooling fins and guards.

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- confirm: Husqvarna zero-turn uses a 12-volt lawn and garden battery; voltmeter reading 12.6 V or greater = 100% charged; less: charge.
- tip: check cables tight; clean/replace corroded terminals: baking soda bath of 3 cups water and 2 heaping teaspoons baking soda with wire brush; add dielectric grease to prevent corrosion.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: disconnect cables from battery. Remember: positive cable (red +) is the last one off and the first one on.
- step 2: if battery has removable caps check water level; if plates not covered, add distilled water (well water is not good; city water a little better; distilled best).
- step 3: hook charger to battery; check voltage with meter. 11.8 V takes little charging; 10.1 V needs a longer time; lower voltage = longer charge.
- tip: "slow bake": put a very low battery on a 2 or 4 amp setting (chargers may offer 6/4/2 amps), charge about an hour, re-check voltage; if increasing, charge another hour; if not increasing, buy a new battery.

## page: procedures/change-the-engine-oil
- fact: change engine oil and filter on most residential zero-turns every 50 hours or annually; commercial engines every 100 hours or annually; new engine: change after the initial 5-10 hours; check manual for engine type and interval.
- step 1: lay cardboard/newspaper cover on the work surface.
- step 2: start mower, park on the cover, run 2 to 3 minutes to warm oil (warm oil flows better); caution, engine hot.
- step 3: remove ignition key and spark plug boot(s).
- step 4: clean around the oil plug or oil drain hose (depending on model); place drain pan; remove plug and drain.
- step 5: clean around oil filter; remove with filter wrench; put a light film of fresh oil on the new filter's gasket and install; once drained reinstall drain plug.
- step 6: fill crankcase with the quantity and type recommended by the manufacturer; not too low or too high.
- step 7: reinstall spark plug boots, run engine a few minutes, shut off, let oil cool, recheck level, add if needed.

## page: procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil
- fact: low or old transmission fluid makes the mower seem weak and can cause extensive damage.
- fact: some residential Husqvarna zero-turns have maintenance-free transmissions (no service or level check); if one leaks contact the dealer.
- fact: serviceable transmissions: change oil every 400 hours (most); some need an initial fluid and filter change after break-in; interval varies with fluid; check the manual.
- brand: Parker recommends Parker HT-1000 oil; Hydro-Gear recommends 20W-50 engine oil; other oils possible and affect change interval.
- confirm: if no full change needed, check level and top off; two overflow/expansion reservoirs (one right, one left transmission) on top of mower behind the seat; level must reach the "full cold" line.
- step 1: park on flat surface, remove key, engage parking brake, wait for parts to stop; engine and transmission can be hot.
- step 2: filters sit near each rear wheel, maybe under a guard or skid plate; remove guard; clean around filters (don't introduce dirt).
- step 3: put oil pan under a filter, unscrew, drain; repeat for the other; take old oil to a recycling center.
- step 4: thin layer of fresh transmission oil on the new filters' rubber seal; install clockwise; after good contact with the adapter turn about one more full turn.
- step 5: clean around expansion reservoirs, remove caps; fill with good quality hydraulic oil to the "full cold" line on both tanks. Some transaxles have a vent plug on each: loosen, fill through the reservoir until oil comes out of the vent plug, tighten, continue filling to "cold full" line.
- step 6: purge air (see below); let cool and recheck level.

## page: procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system
- step 1: raise rear of mower on jack stands just enough that the drive wheels no longer touch the ground.
- step 2: open the bypass valve, start the mower, throttle about 1/2, release parking brake.
- step 3: move speed levers forward and reverse slowly about 6 times.
- step 4: repeat with the bypass valve closed until wheels rotate at a normal rate and with normal sound.
- step 5: let it cool, recheck oil level, add if needed.
- fact: air must be removed after an oil change.

## page: procedures/replace-the-spark-plug
- tip: author replaces annually. Remove with socket wrench; install new with correct gap.
- fact: reuse only if not too dark, no burnt electrode, no cracked porcelain; clean carbon with wire brush; check gap with feeler gauge.
- safety: don't reattach spark plug wires until service is complete.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- fact: during service follow fuel lines from tank checking lines, pump, carburetor for leaks; replace dry/cracked/leaking lines; replace a leaking pump; carburetor leak usually fixed by replacing the gasket or cleaning/fixing components.
- step 1: replace filter annually (old gas degrades filter, plugs it).
- step 2: install with the side arrow toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: replace annually; remove old filter without knocking dirt into intake; wipe housing with dry clean cloth; install new Husqvarna filter, reattach cover; clean several times a season. Without clean air engine runs sluggish and may overheat.

## page: causes/wrong-tire-pressure
- fact: drive tire pressures must be equal side to side at Husqvarna's recommended pressure; uneven pressure causes uneven cut and pulling to one side. Lug nuts must be tight.

## page: causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing
- confirm: turn each pulley slowly; resistance or noise = replace; pulley must sit level and parallel to the deck.
- fact: some Husqvarna zero-turns have a removable floor pan to access the top of the deck; otherwise remove the deck.
- fact: worn/cracked/glazed belts: replace (disconnect plug wires first).

## page: causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing
- confirm: gloved, grab each side of blade and rock up-and-down; movement means replace bearing or spindle housing assembly.

## page: procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades
- tip: author replaces blades annually and sharpens old set as a spare to avoid downtime.
- fact: replace if gouged or bent; if sails worn or ends rounded use new blades; balance blades to avoid spindle damage, vibration, uneven cut.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: scrape deck each service with deck scraper, metal putty knife, or stiff wire brush; access underside safely with jack stands; buildup compromises air movement/suction that stands grass up for a precise cut; avoid mowing damp or wet grass.

## page: guides/mower-parts-that-wear-out
- fact: belts, pulleys bearings, spindle bearings, blades, spark plugs, filters (see tune-up).

## page: guides/winterizing-and-storage
- fact: extra winterizing steps: prepare the fuel system and care for battery so it doesn't freeze (links to separate guide).

## page: procedures/lubricate (unmapped)
- step: use high-performance multi-purpose grease: front caster wheel mount (zerk outside the mount or under dust caps), front wheel bearings, deck spindles; check manual for all grease points; wipe off extra.
- mistake: grease on the belt or pulley grooves makes the belt slip, requiring a new belt.

## page: guides/zero-turn-safety
- fact: zero-turns are dangerous; keep safety system and ROPS/seat belt in good order.
