## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Cub Cadet zero turn starts then dies"
- alias: "starts, stalls and dies" / "runs sluggishly and dies"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-air-filter, dirty-cooling-fins, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: dies when fuel system restricted (old gas, plugged filter, clogged line, faulty pump, dirty carb, plugged gas cap), or from dirty spark plug, bad ignition coil, plugged air filter, wrong choke, plugged cooling fins, wrong oil level, plugged deck.
- safety: follow operator's manual; remove ignition key and spark plug wires before repairs.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can go bad within a month; ethanol attracts moisture and leaves gummy deposits and varnish.
- fix: if fuel has sat more than 30 days without an additive, drain the tank. Cub Cadet zero-turns need unleaded gasoline, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol.
- fix: mix fresh gas with Sea Foam Motor Treatment before adding to tank (stabilize, reduce moisture, clean fuel system); then run the zero-turn about 15 minutes to work it through the system.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: replace; arrow on the side of the filter housing must point in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: gummy substance narrows the passageway; engine starves and dies (problems/engine-starts-then-dies).
- confirm: use the fuel shut-off valve to start/stop flow while checking each section of line.
- fix: turn off fuel, remove the line, spray carb cleaner to loosen, blow compressed air through; if the clog won't clear, install a new line of the same length and diameter.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- brand: most Cub Cadet zero-turns use a vacuum pump that uses vacuum off the engine block to move fuel to the carb; can fail from old fuel.
- confirm: confirm fuel flows to the pump through the line; then turn off fuel, remove the line from the carburetor into a container, turn on fuel, start the mower; steady or pulsating flow = pump fine, else replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: varnish from old gas plugs passages and sticks small internal parts.
- confirm: verify fuel reaches carb; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner into the intake; start. If it starts, runs fine, then bogs and shuts off, carburetor is the likely problem.
- fix: remove, disassemble and clean (or small engine shop).

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: replace air filter once a year and clean several times per mowing season; more often in very dusty conditions or commercial use.
- step 1 (paper filter): remove element without letting dirt fall into the intake.
- step 2: wipe dirt out of the housing with a clean dry cloth.
- step 3: tap filter on a hard surface to knock out dirt; do not use compressed air (damages paper).
- step 4: hold to a light; replace if light does not shine through, if damaged or covered in oil; reuse if light shows and in good condition.
- step 5: reinstall filter and cover.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- fact: steps above (paper element): tap, no compressed air, light test, replace annually.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- fix: remove dirt from fins, replace broken fins, clean around the engine block and shroud; make sure the heat shield is correctly installed for air circulation.
- fact: plugged fins let the engine overheat after running a while and die.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: check the oil with the dipstick before each use. Low oil raises friction and crankcase heat, burns oil and parts.
- fix: you can add oil, but usually the engine has already been damaged by lack of lubrication; have an experienced small engine mechanic, Cub Cadet service center, or the engine maker's dealer (Kawasaki, Kohler etc.) assess damage.
- pro: engine damage assessment.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: too much oil builds crankcase pressure and can shut the engine down.
- fix: remove a little oil via drain plug, oil filter or oil fill area using an oil evacuator or turkey baster; repeat remove/add until the level is right.

## page: procedures/drain-excess-oil
- fact: methods: drain plug, oil filter, or from the fill area with an oil evacuator or turkey baster.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fix: clean a dirty plug; replace if very dark or damaged; gap to engine manufacturer spec; secure spark plug wires (loose wires cause sluggish running or dying).

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: winding can separate and short out when hot, so plugs lose spark and mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: ohmmeter check for a break in continuity; replace the coil if a break.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: choke restricts air for a richer mix for cold starts; left on after warm-up the engine dies for lack of air. Choke on/closed to start cold, then off/open when warm.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: cap vent blocked makes tank vacuum; mower may start but dies after running a while.
- confirm: loosen the cap to let air in and see if the engine runs better; if so tighten and see if the problem recurs after running; replace a bad cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: grass under the deck strains the engine, can overheat and shut down; dull blades worsen it. Scrape deck regularly and sharpen blades.

## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- fact: run zero-turn at high engine speed; do not idle long; the engine must run to charge the battery.
- causes-named: bad-battery, faulty-charging-system
- pro: if the charging system is bad, Source highly recommends an experienced mechanic; many components can fail and electrical parts usually can't be returned.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: if battery and wiring are good and tight but you must keep charging, suspect the charging system; see tests in the battery article.
