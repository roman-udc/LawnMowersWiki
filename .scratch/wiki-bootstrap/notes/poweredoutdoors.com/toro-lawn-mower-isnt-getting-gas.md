## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Toro mower isn't getting gas"
- causes-named: old-fuel, fuel-valve-off, clogged-fuel-line, clogged-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Old fuel clogs lines, filter, pump and carburetor; bad cap can also restrict fuel.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- brand: Toro gas mowers require fresh unleaded gasoline with low ethanol content.
- fact: Ethanol attracts moisture; the mix separates, sinks to the bottom of the tank, gums up and corrodes components. Gasoline breaks down as quickly as 30 days after purchase; use fast or stabilize. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment.
- fix: Siphon old fuel into an authorized container; refill with fresh fuel and additive.

## page: causes/fuel-valve-off
- symptom: Start of season, no fuel; shut-off valve may have been closed for off-season storage.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: Shut off fuel, pick a section, remove its end furthest from the tank into a container placed lower than the tank (fuel can't run uphill without a pump), restart flow; no stream = blockage.
- fix: Remove line, spray carb cleaner, blow compressed air, repeat; replace if not cleared.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fix: Replace; arrow on inline filter faces fuel flow, toward carburetor away from tank. Change annually.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Toro carburetor is placed higher than the tank so it needs a pump; most likely a vacuum-style pump that builds pressure off the engine.
- confirm: Inlet check: shut off fuel, remove line from inlet port into container lower than tank, turn fuel on; flow = OK, else find restriction in line or filter. Outlet check: shut off fuel, remove line from carburetor into container, start flow and engine; steady or pulsating = pump fine.
- fix: Replace if no good flow to carburetor.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- confirm: Good flow to carb; remove air filter; spray carb cleaner in intake; start. Runs well then shuts off = disassemble carb. Doesn't start = look elsewhere.
- fix: Clean carburetor; non-mechanical: small engine shop or replace with new.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- confirm: Loosen or remove cap and run; runs well but dies some time after reinstalling cap = cap problem. Replace cap.

## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- fact: Check fuel system for leaks while there: plastic parts gone soft and leaking at seams; dry aged fuel lines beginning to crack; fuel line clamps more likely to puncture the line. Replace leaking or likely-to-leak components.
