## page: problems/mower-is-leaking-gas
- alias: "strong fuel smell in the garage from my mower"
- alias: "Troy-Bilt leaking gas"
- causes-named: dirty-carburetor, bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat, cracked-fuel-filter, bad-fuel-pump, cracked-fuel-tank, bad-shutoff-valve, cracked-fuel-line, cracked-primer-bulb, bad-gas-cap-seal
- fact: Leak may be small and hard to find; by the time you smell it, fuel has evaporated leaving little wet spot.
- safety: Work in a well-ventilated area; take all safety precautions with fuel.
- brand: Troy-Bilt; tank may be metal or HDPE.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Dirty/old fuel gums up the carb and forms deposits; failing carb parts leak.

## page: causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat
- fact: Bowl gasket (rubber-band looking) seals carburetor to bowl; becomes dry and brittle from heat/cold stress near the engine.
- fix: Shut off fuel using the shut-off valve under the tank or clamp the fuel line; remove the screw and bowl (rag for remaining fuel); remove old gasket, place new one on the bowl; reinstall the bowl; tighten screw; wipe the carb; turn fuel on and check for more leaks.
- confirm: More leaks: check float and needle. Leak near the air intake port suggests a stuck float; it regulates fuel into the bowl, otherwise gas overflows out of the carb. Disassemble to find the cause; clean or rebuild.
- confirm: Float needle works with the float; if stuck, may need replacement; try to repair or take to the Troy-Bilt dealer to rebuild. Temporary unstick: gently tap the carb side with the handle end of a hammer; works maybe a time or two.

## page: procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor
- fact: Bowl gasket steps are in causes/bad-carburetor-gasket-or-needle-and-seat above.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-filter
- fact: Fuel degrades plastic of the inline filter; leaks at seams or if cracked; replace. Be careful removing the filter ends from fuel lines since plastic can soften and break. Replacement filter online or at hardware store.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Most Troy-Bilt pumps are plastic; deteriorate internally and leak at seams; a leaking pump can't build pressure; replace.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-tank
- fact: Metal or HDPE tank. Metal tank can rust and develop holes: seal with petroleum-resistant epoxy like TankWeld by J-B Weld, or replace if a replacement is available. HDPE tank seams can fail: best solution replace the tank. Most tanks are HDPE but smaller engines may still have metal.

## page: causes/bad-shutoff-valve
- fact: Many Troy-Bilt mowers have a shut-off valve under the tank; plastic or metal; both prone to leaking; replace if leaking.

## page: causes/cracked-fuel-line
- confirm: Follow lines from the tank looking for wet areas; pay attention to clamped sections.
- fix: Replace cracked lines; consider replacing pinch-style clamps with worm gear clamps (less likely to pinch the line).

## page: causes/cracked-primer-bulb
- fact: If the mower has a primer bulb, check it. Leaks if the plastic gets dry or if overfilled with fuel; the connection to the fuel line also leaks.

## page: causes/bad-gas-cap-seal
- confirm: Wet spot may evaporate. Rock the mower back and forth to splash fuel around the cap; a wet spot means replace the cap. Check if smell remains after the other checks.
