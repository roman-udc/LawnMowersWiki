## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- fact: John Deere 4-stage (4-cycle) mowers need unleaded gas, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol.
- brand: some John Deere push mowers still in use have 2-cycle engines needing a gas and oil mix (not covered; see the author's push mower gas article).
- fact: regular gas (generally 87 octane per US Energy Information Administration) is fine in a John Deere 4-cycle mower if ethanol is no more than 10%.
- fact: lower ethanol is better; read the pump label for ethanol content and the octane label/button.
- mistake: avoid E15 and E85 (15% and 85% ethanol); can cause significant engine damage.
- fact: ethanol details: corn-based; fine in most motor vehicles but damaging to small gas engines; attracts moisture from air causing gas/ethanol to separate; separated ethanol and water sink to the tank bottom and get fed through the engine causing ignition problems; leaves gummy deposits on fuel components; corrosive and can burn hot, overheating the engine.
- fact: gasoline can break down and lose effectiveness in as little as 30 days; buy from a busy station, buy only what can be used in 30 days; store extra in an approved fuel container away from moisture.
- tip (opinion): higher-quality fuel is recommended if in budget; ethanol-free is best but pricey. Ethanol-free sources: 4-cycle fuel canisters (hardware store/online; more expensive than at the pump) and Recreation Fuel REC-90 at some stations (find via pure-gas.org, US and Canada).
- fact: stabilizer: recommended for all ethanol-containing gas; not needed for non-ethanol fuel (doesn't hurt). Mix stabilizer in each fill-up and when storing fuel, per bottle instructions. Author uses Sea Foam Fuel Treatment (opinion).

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: stabilize fuel unused within 30 days to prevent moisture buildup; store in approved container away from moisture; stabilizer each fill-up and for stored fuel (ethanol blends).

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: running the wrong fuel (high ethanol, old) causes starting and running problems and clogging/deterioration of fuel components, and can damage the engine.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas starts breaking down at 30 days; old/bad fuel leads to clogged and deteriorating fuel components.
