## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Cub Cadet isn't getting fuel"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor
- fact: A lack of fuel can make the mower not start or die after mowing a while. Running the right gas and always fresh gas reduces clogging and damage in the fuel system.

## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor
- fact: Dies after mowing for a while is listed as a symptom of fuel starvation.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- symptom: Fuel can begin breaking down as soon as 30 days after purchase and loses combustible properties. Ethanol attracts moisture; the moisture/ethanol mix can separate, leaving gummy deposits that clog filter, lines and carburetor.
- fix: Drain the tank (a siphon works well) into a fuel container; take the old fuel to a recycling facility for disposal. Refill with fresh fuel and add a fuel additive to stabilize and clean the system (author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment).
- brand: Source links a best-gas-for-Cub-Cadet article.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Change the fuel filter annually during routine service. It filters dirt out of fuel; unchanged, it plugs.
- fix: Replace the filter. An arrow on the plastic housing shows flow direction; install with the arrow pointing away from the tank and toward the fuel pump and carburetor.

## page: procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter
- step 1: Replace the filter; note the flow arrow on the housing.
- step 2: Arrow points away from the fuel tank and toward fuel pump/carburetor.
- tip: Annual replacement at routine service.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Gummy deposits from running old ethanol fuel clog lines.
- confirm: Isolate the clogged section: use the fuel shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank to start/stop flow; if no shut-off valve, clamp the line with hose pliers. Find the section that doesn't flow.
- fix: Shut off fuel, remove the line, spray carburetor cleaner into the hose and blow it out with compressed air. If it can't be cleared, replace the line. Also a good time to replace a dry, cracking line before it leaks.
- tools: hose pliers, carburetor cleaner, compressed air

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Plastic or metal pump is fitted when the tank sits lower than the carburetor. A vacuum pump builds pressure off the crankcase and pushes fuel up to the carburetor.
- symptom: Cracks, damage or leaks mean replace.
- confirm: Step 1 - verify flow to the pump: stop flow (shut-off valve or crimp), remove the fuel line from the pump inlet and put it in a container lower than the fuel tank, start flow. Fuel running into the container means flow to the pump is good; reattach. If no flow, check filter and lines for clogs and check the container is lower than the tank (gravity).
- confirm: Step 2 - with flow confirmed, remove the line from the carburetor and put it in a container, start flow, and start the mower. A steady or pulsating stream of fuel from the line means the pump is working.
- fix: Replace a pump that's damaged or doesn't deliver a stream.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carburetor regulates the air/fuel mix; mounted on the top or side of the engine block, usually below or behind the air filter. A dirty one can clog or stick components including the fuel jet.
- confirm: Remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake, start the engine. If it starts and then won't keep running, remove the carburetor and take it apart for cleaning.
- fix: See procedures/clean-the-carburetor.

## page: procedures/clean-the-carburetor
- step: Quick test - air filter off, carb cleaner into intake, try starting. If it runs briefly then dies, remove and disassemble the carburetor to clean (Source links a Cub Cadet carb-cleaning article).
