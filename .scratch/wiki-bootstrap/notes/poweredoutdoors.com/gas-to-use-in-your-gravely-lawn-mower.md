## page: guides/choosing-the-right-gas
- brand: Gravely gas mowers require unleaded gasoline, minimum octane 87, maximum ethanol 10%. Higher ethanol damages the engine. (Gravely also makes electric and diesel mowers; article covers gas only.)
- fact: regular gas generally has octane 87 (per US Energy Information Administration) and typically 10% ethanol (E10); regular is OK once you confirm ethanol is not above 10%.
- fact: pump labels show ethanol 10%, 15%, 85% (E10, E15, E85); never use above E10; lower is better.
- fact: ethanol is corn-based, more environmentally friendly, fine in most vehicles but damaging to small gas engines.
- fact: ethanol gas attracts moisture; gas separates from ethanol; separated ethanol and water sink to the tank bottom and get fed through the engine causing ignition problems; ethanol leaves gummy deposits on fuel components; ethanol is corrosive and burns hot, which can overheat the engine.
- fact: ethanol-free fuel is best but can cost up to $2.00 more per gallon (recreation fuel at the pump) or more as canisters.
- fact: Recreation Fuel REC-90 is the ethanol-free gas at some stations (not all); pure-gas.org locates stations in the US and Canada.
- fact: 4-cycle fuel canisters (ethanol-free) sold at hardware stores/online.
- fact: no stabilizer needed in REC-90 or canned 4-cycle fuel (no ethanol), but it won't hurt.
- opinion: author recommends higher quality fuel if affordable ("more expensive is not always better").

## page: guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers
- fact: gasoline can start breaking down 30 days after purchase; buy what can be used in 30 days.
- fact: store unused gas in an approved fuel container in a dry place away from water and weather.
- fact: if it can't be used in 30 days, add a stabilizer. Author uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment: stabilizes, minimizes moisture buildup, and cleans the fuel system.

## page: causes/wrong-fuel
- fact: using gas with ethanol above 10% (E15/E85) damages the engine and fuel system (Gravely).
