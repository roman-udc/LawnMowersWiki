## page: problems/battery-keeps-dying
- alias: "John Deere battery is dead every time I go to mow"
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, faulty-charging-system, parasitic-battery-drain, bad-battery
- fact: headline: loose or corroded cables, corroded terminals, failed charging system, key left on, or bad battery.
- safety: wear protective gear for skin and eyes around the battery; follow operator's manual.
- fact: battery life: most last at least 3 years with proper care; replacing more often suggests a connection or battery problem.
- tip: care: keep cables clean and tight; battery securely held down; don't let it freeze, keep fully charged in cold weather. Most batteries fail from vibration (plates break up), so make sure battery is secure in the tray.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- confirm: follow the red (positive) cable from battery to solenoid and on to the starter; all secure. Corrosion looks white or green.
- fix: disconnect negative cable first to break the circuit (a wrench touching frame can arc and spark, potentially blowing up the battery); then positive. Mnemonic: positive is last off and first on.
- fix: clean with baking soda paste: 4 cups water with about six rounded tablespoons baking soda; spread on corrosion (it foams), rinse with water (outdoors, messy); wire brush speeds up; cola also works.
- safety: always wear safety glasses and skin protection.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- confirm (test): volt-ohm meter set to 12 V; engine off, red lead on positive, black on negative: should read 12.4 to 12.8 V (some higher). Write it down. Set parking brake, start engine, run about 3/4 throttle, read again: 13.2 to 13.9 V or higher means alternator is charging. If the reading doesn't rise, charging system fault (bad regulator or bad stator/alternator).
- fact: some systems are 15 amp, others 20 amp, by manufacturer and engine.
- pro: have a professional (John Deere dealer or local mechanic) find root cause; parts are expensive.
- tip: the voltage test can be done anytime if cables are clean; may also reveal a bad connection.

## page: causes/bad-alternator
- fact: bad regulator or stator/alternator are the named possibilities for no voltage rise.

## page: causes/parasitic-battery-drain
- fact: key left on drains little unless an oil light is on at the dash or a 12 V accessory cord is plugged into an accessory port. Charge the battery if it went dead.

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- fact: battery that won't charge above 12 V after an hour or two on a charger is bad; replace.
- fact: new batteries are usually fully charged; if long on a shelf, charge first.
- tip: stores usually charge a core fee of $15 to $25 unless you hand over the old battery; take the old one with you. 12 V lawn and garden batteries are at John Deere dealers, auto stores, most home improvement stores.
- fact: size: at least 300 cold cranking amps if the deck uses an electric clutch; about 250 cca OK without one, bigger is better; check it fits the holder.

## page: procedures/charge-the-battery
- step 1: disconnect cables (positive last off, first on). If battery has removable caps, check water level; if plates are not covered add distilled water (well water is bad, city water a little better).
- step 2: connect charger: red to positive (+), black to negative (-).
- step 3: check voltage with meter: 11.8 V needs little charge; 10.1 V needs a long time; lower reading = longer charge.
- fact (method one, "slow bake"): for a very low battery use 2 or 4 amp setting for about an hour, check that voltage is rising, then continue another hour.
- fact (method two): if reading isn't too low, charge at up to 6 amps about half an hour to reach about 12 V, remove charger, start mower and keep throttle 3/4 to full so it charges while running; check reading after mowing.
- see: procedures/test-the-battery

## page: procedures/test-the-battery
- step: engine-off battery voltage should read 12.4-12.8 V; see charging system test above for running readings 13.2-13.9 V.

## page: procedures/jump-start-the-battery
- tip: confirm both batteries are 12 V; park car next to mower and shut the car engine off.
- step 1: red positive cable to the mower's positive terminal, then to the car battery's positive.
- step 2: black negative cable to the car battery negative; connect the other black end to the mower frame.
- step 3: set mower brake and start the mower; run a few minutes; don't start the car.
- step 4: remove cables in the opposite order of installation; the mower's alternator keeps it charged; check with a meter.

## page: guides/battery-care-and-charging
- fact: battery that is not fully charged can freeze in cold weather and be damaged; keep charged.
- fact: see also the series on charging times (linked, not in this Source).
