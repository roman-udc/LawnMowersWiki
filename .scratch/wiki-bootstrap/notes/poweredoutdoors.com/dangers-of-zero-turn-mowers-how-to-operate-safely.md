## page: guides/zero-turn-safety
- fact: Zero-turns are dangerous because they are engine powered, move fast, turn quickly, and can roll over on hills, uneven terrain, near water, and retaining walls. Compact size doesn't make them less dangerous than tractors, skid steers, excavators.
- fact (statistics cited from others): CPSC estimated 35,000 consumers treated in ERs 2012-2014 for riding-mower injuries; average 90 consumer deaths per year 2008-2010 related to riding mowers, many from falling off, falling under, or being run over. OSHA documents 16 employee deaths in 2019 and 19 in 2018 operating lawn mowers (most from rollovers on too-steep hills, near embankments or water). One death: thrown off and run over with blades engaged; operator presence controls unavailable or possibly disabled so blades stayed engaged with no operator in seat. Amputee Coalition cited re pediatric amputations from mower accidents.
- safety rules (numbered in Source):
  1. Don't operate on slopes greater than 15 degrees; drive up and down the slope, not across.
  2. Don't operate in wet conditions; stay away from slopes when grass is wet (harder to control).
  3. Stay a minimum of 5 feet from bodies of water; use a handheld trimmer or push mower there.
  4. Stay 5 feet from embankments, retaining walls, ditches and other quick terrain changes.
  5. Don't change direction or speed quickly (unstable, loss of control); keep steady speeds.
  6. No children, pets, or others in the area; blades throw material from the discharge chute (rocks, stones, branches); stop the mower if anyone enters.
  7. Don't discharge toward a wall or obstruction (ricochet).
  8. Disengage blades when not mowing, and each time before stepping off to clear an obstruction.
  9. Wear safety glasses and hearing protection; gas mower noise about 90 decibels; OSHA recommends hearing protection at that level.
  10. Wear the seat belt along with ROPS (fixed or foldable). If you temporarily remove/fold ROPS for low overhead obstacles, reinstall as soon as clear; ROPS must be used with seat belt; if ROPS is removed or folded down, do NOT use the seat belt. ROPS prevents the operator getting stuck or injured under the mower in a rollover.
  11. Never allow a passenger (don't mow with a child on your lap; delay mowing until someone can supervise the child).
  12. Always operate during daylight.
  13. Never leave unattended: disengage blades, set parking brake, engine off, remove key.
  14. Wear closed-toe shoes; no sandals.
- fact: Safety features every mower must have: operator presence control system (seat safety switch; shuts off blades when operator not in seat); ROPS (bar above operator station; must be used with seat belt); seat belt; wheel weights or counterweights when necessary per manufacturer guidelines (often needed with a rear-mounted bagger); high seat back.
- fact: Pre-mow checklist: remove sticks, rocks, toys, obstructions; remove people and pets; ROPS upright; seat belt works; blades not damaged or cracked (a piece could break off and eject from the deck).
- fact: American Academy of Pediatrics recommends children be at least 12 to operate a walk-behind push mower and at least 16 for a riding mower (guideline; parent judges maturity; review dangers of reaching under the deck with blades engaged).
