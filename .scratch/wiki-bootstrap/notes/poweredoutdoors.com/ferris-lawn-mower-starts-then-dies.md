## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Ferris starts then dies"
- alias: "mower quits after starting"
- alias: "mower dies while mowing"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-line, bad-fuel-pump, dirty-carburetor, clogged-fuel-filter, weak-or-dead-battery, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, old-fuel, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-choke-setting, faulty-charging-system
- safety: Remove the spark plug wire and let the engine cool before repairs.
- fact: Ferris zero-turn and walk-behind mowers.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Ethanol (made from corn or other high-starch plant) attracts moisture to the fuel system: corrosion, part degradation, varnish. Ethanol and water mix can separate from gasoline and run extremely hot through the engine.
- tip: Fuel tips: consume within 30 days; unleaded, minimum 87 octane; never more than 10% ethanol, avoid E15, E30, E85 (up to 15%, 30%, 85% ethanol); store away from combustibles; approved container in a dry location; add a stabilizer.
- fix: Drain the tank with a fuel siphon pump; mix a fuel additive such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment with gasoline (stabilize, clean the system, reduce moisture) and refill.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Filter keeps dirt out of the intake. Plugged: engine stops running.
- mistake: Never run without an air filter, even to finish a job; it is insurance for the engine.
- tip: Replace annually for the average homeowner, more often for heavy/commercial use; check and clean through the season.
- fix: Paper element: remove from housing; wipe dirt out of housing and cover with a clean cloth without letting dirt fall into the intake; tap against a solid surface; hold to a light - reuse if light shines through; replace if no light, very dirty, wet or damaged; install and refit cap.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step 1: Remove the air filter from the housing.
- step 2: Wipe dirt from housing and cover with a clean cloth; keep dirt out of the intake.
- step 3: Tap the filter on a solid surface.
- step 4: Hold to a light; reuse if light passes.
- step 5: Replace if no light, extremely dirty, wet or damaged.
- step 6: Install filter and refit cap.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Strains fuel leaving the tank; clogged filter starves the engine so it runs sluggish and dies.
- tip: Replace annually (homeowner), more often for heavy/commercial use. If dirty fuel was put in the tank, drain it and replace the filter.
- fix: Install new filter with arrow in direction of fuel flow, toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fact: Gummy deposits from old fuel block the line and the mower shuts down while mowing.
- confirm: Shut off flow with the fuel shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers. Isolate a section; remove the end furthest from the tank into a container; start flow. Container must be lower than the tank since fuel can't run uphill without the pump. If flow is good, shut off, reattach and check other sections.
- fix: Find the blocked section, shut off flow, remove it, spray carburetor cleaner to loosen, blow compressed air through, repeat; replace the line if it can't be cleared.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Old gas sitting in the pump degrades it; it can no longer push enough fuel to the carb.
- confirm: Check for leaks; verify fuel reaches the pump; disconnect the hose from the carburetor into a container, start the mower and watch for constant or pulsating flow.
- fix: Replace if no constant or pulsating flow.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Carb regulates fuel mixed with air; old fuel sitting in it forms gummy and hard deposits, sticking or clogging components.
- confirm: First confirm fuel reaches the carb. Remove air filter, spray carb cleaner into the intake, start. If it starts and then dies after burning the cleaner, disassemble and clean the carb.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: Starts then dies once engine gets too hot.
- fact: Fins dissipate heat from block and cylinder head; plugged fins restrict air.
- fix: Let the engine cool first. Remove debris from fins, replace broken fins, clean debris around engine and shroud, make sure the heat shield is securely attached.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil builds crankcase pressure; can overheat and shut down.
- fix: Remove oil through the drain plug or filter, or suck it out the fill area with an oil evacuator or turkey baster; keep adjusting until oil is at the full line on the dipstick.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Leak or oil burning; lack of lubrication makes friction heat that can overheat the engine so it dies, even melt internal parts.
- pro: If the engine shut down from lack of oil, significant damage likely; take it to an experienced small engine mechanic to tear down and test.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Dirty/fouled plug causes sluggish running and dying; also damaged, wrong gap or loose wire.
- fix: Clean the tip; replace if very dark, burnt electrode or damaged. Gap to engine manufacturer specs; attach plug wire securely. Plugs inexpensive; replace annually.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- fact: Coil winding can separate and short when hot, so plug gets no voltage; mower dies after running a while.
- confirm: Ohmmeter check for a break in continuity. Replace the coil if broken.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke reduces airflow to start a cold engine (richer mix). Once warm it must be off/open or the mower will die.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Tank vents through the cap; plugged vent makes a vacuum preventing fuel leaving the tank.
- symptom: Mower quits and won't restart until you loosen the cap; may hear the vacuum release.
- fix: Clear the clog or replace with a new fuel cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Deck plugged with debris makes the engine work harder; can overheat and shut down.
- fix: Scrape the deck regularly; avoid wet grass; deck spray can minimize buildup (not a miracle product).

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: Battery charged but mower dies because the battery isn't charging.
- fix: Run at high engine speed; don't idle long (engine power charges the battery). Confirm battery and wiring good and secure. If you must keep recharging, suspect the charging system; replace a bad battery.
- pro: Have an experienced mechanic diagnose charging system failures; electrical parts get expensive and usually can't be returned.
