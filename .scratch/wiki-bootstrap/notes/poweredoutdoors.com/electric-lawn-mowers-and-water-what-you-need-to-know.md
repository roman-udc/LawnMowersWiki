## page: guides/mower-got-wet
- fact: Electric mowers are not waterproof; outer shell and battery casing make them water-resistant only. Heavy rain/wet conditions can let water in.
- fact: Potential water entry points: battery housing is typically a hinged compartment with latch (cracks/crevices let water in); some mowers have cooling vents for battery/motor (spraying water up vents while cleaning or mowing in bad weather lets water reach damaging places).
- fix: Latch battery casing securely every time battery is replaced; replace casing if latch or hinge is broken.
- safety: Do not turn on a soaked mower; it short-circuits the electrical system and damages the motor (can be beyond repair).
- step 1: Dry as quickly as possible; can take up to 8 hours depending on model and exposure time.
- step 2: Drying aids: remove water-resistant cover per manufacturer guidelines for airflow; remove battery and dry it first; leave in the sun; use fans; hairdryer for crevices and small areas.
- step 3: When dry, disconnect battery and apply electric contact cleaner to connections and terminals (grease or lubricant optional).
- step 4: Test wiring insulation resistance with a megohmmeter; if value below 100,000 ohms, let the mower dry further.
- step 5: When dry, gradually add voltage and retest resistance; user manual should say how much voltage to apply. Even small voltage applied to damp wiring can diminish battery's voltage capability.
- fact: Severe water damage can shorten electric mower lifespan, but it can usually be made to function again.

## page: guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety
- safety: Mowing in rain is a bad idea, especially electric: one deeper puddle or sudden downpour can ruin it. Corded mower in rain also risks electrocution of the operator.
- fact: Wet or damp grass: risk of puddles and wet clippings entering mower; extra strain on motor/engine shortens life; wet grass cuts poorly, dull blade may rip grass and roots; wet grass clumps so mower can't pick up clippings. (Lawn-care content, minor.)
- fact: High humidity: corrosion/rust faster. Using in humidity won't damage it; storing outside or in a humid room will. Check battery before each use if accessible; a corroded battery can be replaced but starting with a corroded battery will likely mean replacing the mower.
- fact: Storage: do not store outside (heavy rain damages/limits life; if unavoidable, keep under a deck with a tarp or mower cover); do not store in a shed (leaks, humidity); garage or basement is best, dry and cool.
- step 1 (clean prep): Unplug from external power; remove battery if possible; remove attachments (bagger, dethatcher).
- step 2 (clean): Don't clean right after use; cold or lukewarm water on hot engine/battery damages it. Wipe grass-exposed areas with warm soapy water; avoid water on engine/internal wiring (small exposure okay); brush underside, especially big clumps; let dry completely before reinstalling battery or using.
- fact: Dirty mower: blade dulls or clogs with clumps; mold can grow and spread to lawn.
- fact: Robotic mowers are the exception: waterproof, charge on a dock meant to stay outside in any weather.

## page: guides/cleaning-the-mower
- fact: See clean-prep steps for electric mowers above (unplug, remove battery, don't wash hot, avoid engine/wiring, dry fully).

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- fact: Soaked mower should not be powered; test insulation resistance (>= 100,000 ohms per Source threshold) after drying.
