## page: problems/engine-starts-then-dies
- alias: "Kubota lawn mower starts then dies"
- alias: "mower sitting dead in the middle of the lawn"
- causes-named: old-fuel, clogged-air-filter, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, dirty-cooling-fins, overfilled-oil, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, fouled-spark-plug, bad-ignition-coil, wrong-choke-setting, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, debris-on-deck-or-pulleys, faulty-charging-system
- safety: Remove ignition key and spark plug wires when working; caution around a hot engine; wear eye protection; protect hands from sharp components.
- fact: Also mentions a dull blade worsening a plugged deck.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Gas corrodes and gums the fuel system. Ethanol (made from high-starch plants like corn) attracts moisture, speeding corrosion and leaving varnish that restricts flow; an under-fed engine runs sluggish and may shut down.
- fact: Gas can break down in as little as 30 days.
- fact: Stabilizers (Sea Foam Motor Treatment, STA-BIL) keep fuel stable from 30 days up to 2 years; read the product label for duration. Author uses Sea Foam in every tank.
- fix: Drain with a fuel siphon pump, refill with fresh fuel, add additive to stabilize, reduce moisture and clean the system.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Dusty mowing conditions plug filters. Running without a clean filter can overheat and shut the mower down.
- tip: Replace annually, clean regularly, check before each mowing (a minute or two).
- step 1: Remove filter; wipe remaining dirt from housing and cover without dropping dirt into intake.
- step 2: Tap lightly on a solid surface.
- step 3: Hold to light; reuse if light shines through; replace if no light, oil-covered or damaged.
- step 4: Install filter and reinstall the cap.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- tip: Replace annually.
- fix: Replace; arrow on the side must point in the direction of fuel flow, i.e. toward the carburetor and away from the tank.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- symptom: Kubota dies while mowing.
- confirm: Shut off flow with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank or pinch-off pliers; remove the line from the fuel pump into a container; restore flow; if flow is good, reattach and check other components. If not, check line sections and filter.
- fix: Shut off flow, remove the blocked section, spray carburetor cleaner, blow compressed air, repeat; replace the line if it won't clear.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Old fuel sits in the carburetor and forms gummy hard deposits that restrict components.
- confirm: Confirm fuel reaches the carburetor; remove air filter; spray carburetor cleaner into intake; start; if it runs then dies, disassemble and clean.

## page: causes/dirty-cooling-fins
- symptom: Starts then dies when the engine gets hot.
- fact: Cooling fan pushes air around engine block and cylinder head; plugged or broken fins restrict airflow.
- fix: Remove debris in fins, replace broken fins, clean debris around engine and shroud, ensure heat shield is attached securely. Caution: hot engine.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: Too much oil builds crankcase pressure; mower can overheat and shut down.
- fix: Remove oil via drain plug or oil filter, or with an oil evacuator or turkey baster through the fill area (don't reuse the baster for cooking). Check dipstick; add or remove until at the full line.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: Low oil (from a leak or burning oil) causes friction and heat, can overheat and kill the engine; heat can melt internal parts.
- pro: If it shut down from lack of oil, significant damage is likely; take to an experienced small engine mechanic to tear down and test.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- symptom: runs sluggish and dies; also damaged, wrongly gapped or loose-wired plug.
- fix: Clean the tip; replace if very dark, burnt electrode or damaged; gap per engine manufacturer's spec; attach wires securely. Replace annually.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- symptom: dies after running a while, when hot.
- fact: Coil winding can separate and short out when hot so plugs can't get voltage.
- confirm: Ohmmeter check for a break in continuity.
- fix: Replace if there is a break.

## page: causes/wrong-choke-setting
- fact: Choke restricts air for cold starts; once warm it must be moved to allow more air or the Kubota dies after starting.
- fix: Put the choke lever in the correct position.

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Kubota tank vents through the gas cap; plugged vent makes a vacuum.
- confirm: Run with and without cap for periods. If it shuts down with the cap on and won't start, loosen the cap and retry; if it starts and runs, suspect the cap. Tighten again to confirm it dies and won't restart until the cap is loosened.
- fix: Replace the fuel cap.

## page: causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys
- fact: Plugged deck makes the engine work harder to turn blades and can shut it down; dull blades make it worse.
- fix: Scrape deck regularly; sharpen blades; avoid wet mowing; a deck spray can minimize but not prevent buildup.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- symptom: battery not charging (Kubota).
- fact: Run at high engine speed; don't idle for long periods since the engine's power charges the battery.
- confirm: If you keep having to charge the battery and battery/wiring are good and secure, suspect the charging system (links to battery-drain article for tests).
- pro: Have an experienced mechanic diagnose and repair the charging system; many components, parts are expensive and usually non-returnable electrical parts.
