## page: problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start
- alias: "Snapper lawn mower won't start"
- causes-named: empty-fuel-tank, old-fuel, plugged-fuel-cap-vent, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-air-filter, bad-fuel-pump, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, bad-ignition-coil, bad-recoil-starter, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Won't start when the engine isn't getting fuel, air or spark.

## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- causes-named: loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-ignition-switch, bad-starter-solenoid, bad-safety-switch, faulty-charging-system, incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: All Snapper mowers except recoil manual-start push mowers need a battery to start.

## page: causes/empty-fuel-tank
- fix: Fill with gasoline, octane 87 or higher, ethanol no greater than 10%.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: Breaks down within 30 days; ethanol and moisture residue clogs lines, filter, carburetor; add stabilizer to fuel in the tank and in storage containers when not using within 30 days.
- fix: Remove old fuel, flush the tank, add fresh fuel plus additive (author: Sea Foam Motor Treatment).

## page: causes/plugged-fuel-cap-vent
- fact: Cap vents air; plugged vent forms a vacuum restricting fuel.
- confirm: Run the mower for a while with the cap off, then on, compare.
- fix: Clean the vent; if not possible, replace the cap.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- fact: Fouled plug causes intermittent starting/running; also damaged plug, incorrect gap, loose wires.
- fix: Remove and inspect for carbon buildup or cracked porcelain insulator; replace and gap to manufacturer spec.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: Restricted air may stop starting, overheat, shut down, and damage the engine.
- step (paper): remove; wipe housing with a dry clean cloth without dirt into intake; tap on a solid surface; hold to light; reuse if light passes else replace.

## page: causes/bad-fuel-pump
- fact: Used when the carburetor sits higher than the tank; old fuel deteriorates pump components.
- confirm: Inspect vacuum pump for cracks/external fuel (then it can't make pressure). Stop/start fuel flow with the shut-off valve (if any) or clamps to verify fuel reaches the pump; remove the carburetor fuel line into a container, start the mower; steady or pulsating flow = OK; else replace.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: Change annually at routine service; arrow on the side points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- fix: Remove the line, spray carb cleaner, blow through with compressed air until free of dirt and gummy residue; or replace.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fact: Ethanol and additives leave gummy substances that clog small parts. Clean (if mechanical) or have a shop do it; replace if very bad.

## page: causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables
- fix: Confirm cables and terminals are secure; clean corrosion with baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda).

## page: causes/weak-or-dead-battery
- confirm: Multimeter; about 12.7 V needed; charge if lower; replace if dead or won't hold a charge.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-switch
- symptom: nothing happens when key turned or it doesn't feel right. Check with multimeter; replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-ignition-coil
- confirm: Confirm a good plug first; check coil continuity with an ohm meter; replace if there's a break.

## page: causes/bad-recoil-starter
- fact: Manual-start push mowers use a recoil; if the rope is no longer wrapped you may restring it; broken pulley, spring, or clips may need replacing. Price parts versus the whole assembly; replacing the recoil may be more cost-effective.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: click or hum on key turn; wire at the solenoid gets hot, smokes or melts. Test (linked article) and replace if bad.

## page: causes/bad-safety-switch
- fact: Operator presence switches kill the engine when the operator leaves the seat; a faulty one may not recognize the operator. Temporarily bypass to identify.
- safety: Never operate without safety switch; many people are injured annually rolling mowers or falling off.

## page: causes/faulty-charging-system
- fact: Bad stator or alternator among other electrical parts can leave a weak battery. Test with an ohmmeter (linked article).
- pro: Have a professional dealership (factory-trained technicians) identify it; electrical parts usually can't be returned.

## page: causes/incorrect-starting-procedure
- fact: Snapper safety features prevent starting unless the starting procedure is followed; check the operating manual.
