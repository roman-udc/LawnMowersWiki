## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "mower won't turn over, just clicks or hums when I turn the key"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, weak-or-dead-battery, bad-wiring-or-ground, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-starter-motor
- fact: If the solenoid tests good but the Husqvarna still won't turn over or crank, check the battery, cables, starter switch, and safety switches.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: (problems/engine-wont-crank) A click or hum when the ignition key is turned and the mower doesn't start.
- symptom: A wire gets hot and begins to smoke or melt.
- fact: A solenoid is an electromagnet on/off switch that engages the starter motor to turn the engine. Usually mounted on the starter motor but not required to be; some are mounted closer to the battery. Every electric-start Husqvarna engine has one.
- fact: Internals are a spring and a copper plate. It can go bad if the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes. It can also fail as a result of a weak starter, bad battery, or bad ground.
- fact: Identification: may be round or square, 3 or 4 posts, some attached at the top of the starter. The battery positive (+) cable attaches to one side; follow the positive cable from the battery to find it.
- confirm: Likely bad when the starter motor turns the engine over with the solenoid bypassed.
- mistake: Before replacing the solenoid, check for loose or corroded wires or a bad ground; these can keep the solenoid from getting the power it needs.
- brand: Husqvarna; wiring schematics differ between mowers; some include lights and 12-volt ports. 3- and 4-post solenoids shown with a typical schematic.

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver (long), continuity light, wrenches for loose wires, needle nose pliers (if screwdriver doesn't work), battery charger (optional)
- step 1: Make sure the battery is fully charged: voltmeter should read at least 12.3 volts.
- step 2: Prepare to start: set parking brake, mower in neutral, key to the on position.
- step 3: Bypass the solenoid: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the two cables (cable from the battery and cable to the starter). A spark is common. If the engine turns over while bypassed, the solenoid is likely bad. Needle nose pliers can be used if the screwdriver does not work well.
- step 4: Bench test for a starter-mounted solenoid: remove the starter from the engine; attach the battery charger negative (-) clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid. Quick bench test.
- safety: Spark when bypassing is normal; always wear safety gear; follow Husqvarna operator's manual precautions.
- pro: If not comfortable, contact a Husqvarna dealership or repair shop.
