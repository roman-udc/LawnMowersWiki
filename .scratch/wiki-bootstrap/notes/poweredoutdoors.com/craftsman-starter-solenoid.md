## page: problems/engine-wont-crank
- alias: "Craftsman mower clicks or hums but won't turn over"
- alias: "mower just clicks when I turn the key"
- causes-named: bad-starter-solenoid, bad-battery, loose-or-corroded-battery-cables, bad-wiring-or-ground, bad-starter-motor
- fact: engine won't turn over; only a humming or clicking sound.

## page: causes/bad-starter-solenoid
- symptom: clicking or humming when the key is turned and the mower doesn't start; the wire gets hot and begins to smoke or melt (problems/engine-wont-crank).
- fact: a solenoid is an electromagnet on/off switch actuated to engage the starter motor. Usually mounted on the starter motor but not required; some mowers have it nearer the battery. Every Craftsman with electric start has one.
- fact: inside are a spring and a copper plate. Goes bad if the spring gets weak or the copper plate corrodes; can also fail from a weak starter, bad battery or bad ground.
- fact: identification: may be round or square; 3 or 4 posts; some sit on top of the starter. Positive (+) battery wire attaches to one side, so follow the positive cable from the battery to find it. Wiring schematics vary; some include lights and 12-volt ports.
- confirm: bypass the solenoid; if the engine turns over, the solenoid is likely bad.
- confirm: check for loose or corroded wires or a bad ground BEFORE replacing the solenoid; these keep the solenoid from getting power.
- tools: volt-ohm meter, screwdriver, continuity light, wrenches for loose wires, needle nose pliers (if screwdriver does not work), battery charger (optional).

## page: procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid
- step 1: confirm the battery has a full charge: at least 12.3 volts on the voltmeter.
- step 2: set the parking brake, make sure the mower is in neutral, turn the key to ON.
- step 3: lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid touching the two cables (cable from battery and cable to starter) to bypass it. A spark is common. If the engine turns over, the solenoid is likely bad. Needle nose pliers can jump it if the screwdriver does not work.
- step 4 (starter-mounted solenoid): remove the starter from the engine; attach the negative (-) charger clamp to the starter case and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid; a quick bench test.
- safety: sparks possible when bypassing; always wear safety gear; follow the operator's manual.

## page: causes/bad-battery
- fact: weak battery can make the solenoid appear bad; test battery first (at least 12.3 volts).

## page: causes/bad-wiring-or-ground
- fact: bad ground or loose/corroded wires can keep the solenoid from working.

## page: causes/wet-electrical-components
- fact: Source points to water in the electrical system as another reason a mower may not start after being left in the rain (links to a separate article).

## page: procedures/replace-the-starter-solenoid
- fact: only replace after checking battery charge, loose/corroded wires and ground.
