## page: problems/engine-loses-power
- alias: "push mower loses power / sluggish / bogs"
- causes-named: clogged-air-filter, old-fuel, fouled-spark-plug, clogged-fuel-filter, clogged-fuel-line, dirty-carburetor, wrong-engine-speed, wrong-oil-or-low-oil, overfilled-oil, mowing-too-fast, cutting-wet-or-tall-grass, clogged-deck-chute, dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: a push mower loses power when air or fuel isn't reaching the engine or it is overworked; an underperforming mower also cuts badly.
- tip: air filter is the first thing the author checks.
- pro: if none of these fix it, take it to a small engine mechanic; internal engine problems are hard to diagnose without proper tools.
- tip: routine maintenance and checking oil before mowing prevents power-loss problems.

## page: causes/clogged-air-filter
- fact: replace annually at a full service and clean a couple of times through the season; more often in dusty conditions or heavy use. Never run without an air filter, even briefly; dirt can score the cylinder and damage piston rings, a repair bill that may exceed the cost of a new mower.

## page: procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter
- step (paper) 1: remove filter from housing.
- step (paper) 2: wipe dirt from housing with a dry cloth; don't let dirt fall into the intake.
- step (paper) 3: tap on a solid surface.
- step (paper) 4: hold to light; reuse if light shows through, replace if not.
- step (paper) 5: reattach cover.
- step (foam) 1: remove from housing; wipe out the housing; don't let dirt in the intake.
- step (foam) 2: inspect; if dry, brittle, has a dark spot, or is torn, buy a new one.
- step (foam) 3: wash with mild dish soap and water until the rinse water runs clear.
- step (foam) 4: air dry (sun speeds it).
- step (foam) 5: lightly saturate with foam filter oil, whole filter covered but not dripping; blot excess with a paper towel.
- step (foam) 6: install and reattach cover.

## page: causes/old-fuel
- fact: gas can break down as soon as 30 days after purchase; use or stabilize. Ethanol attracts moisture, gumming the fuel system.
- tip: buy from a busy gas station; unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10 percent ethanol (lower ethanol is better); add stabilizer to reduce moisture; store leftover fuel in a cool dry area away from the elements.
- fix: remove old fuel with a fuel siphon; refill fresh gasoline for 4-cycle engines. Older push mowers with a 2-cycle engine need the correct oil-to-fuel ratio.

## page: causes/fouled-spark-plug
- confirm: inspect tip for carbon, dirt, oil. Replace dirty or damaged (recommended); only dirty and not very dark - wire brush and reuse. Fouled plug gives intermittent spark and power loss.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-filter
- fact: not all push mowers have a fuel filter. If present, check it; replace if plugged; arrow on the side points in the direction of fuel flow.

## page: causes/clogged-fuel-line
- confirm: stop fuel flow with pinch pliers or the shut-off valve (if present); pick a section; remove the end furthest from the tank; place it in a container lower than the tank (gravity flow; fuel can't run uphill without a pump); start flow; good flow = reattach; poor flow = clear the blockage.
- fix: with flow off, remove the section; spray carburetor cleaner to loosen; blow compressed air through; repeat until clear; install a new line if it can't be cleared.

## page: causes/dirty-carburetor
- fix: disassemble and clean to remove gumming and hard crusty deposits; check components are in working condition; if it still fails to supply fuel, replace the carburetor assembly; a small engine shop can do it.

## page: causes/wrong-engine-speed
- fact: push mower deck needs full engine power; throttle must be at "fast" with the deck engaged.

## page: causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil
- fact: low oil means friction and heat, which causes power loss; check before each mowing. Fix by filling to the correct level (dipstick lines) and finding why it's low; if not caught early there could be serious engine damage needing an experienced small engine mechanic.

## page: causes/overfilled-oil
- fact: overfilling pushes oil to the cylinder through the valve train; bluish-white smoke; thick smoke can plug the air filter; check the air filter and plug. Continued running can cause seal damage, hydrolock and a bent piston rod.
- fix: remove a little oil with an oil evacuator, drain plug, or turkey baster.

## page: causes/mowing-too-fast
- fact: engine works harder in thick lawns; adjust speed to conditions to avoid overworking or overheating.

## page: causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass
- fact: strains the engine and gives poor cut; dry grass reduces clippings collecting under the deck. For taller grass: cutting height to highest for the first cut, then lower the deck for a second cut. For extremely tall grass author recommends a walk-behind brush cutter (rentable at a local rental center).

## page: causes/clogged-deck-chute
- fact: scrape regularly; under-deck suction from blade airflow stands grass up for a good cut; clogging causes power loss and a bad cut.

## page: causes/dull-or-bent-blade
- fact: dull blades magnify power loss with a packed deck; sharpen at least after every 25 hours of use, more often in sandy or gravel conditions.
