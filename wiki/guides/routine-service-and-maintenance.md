# Routine service and maintenance

## Summary

What an owner should check before each mow, during the season, and at the yearly full service. Use it when a customer asks how often to service the mower, or when a Problem traces back to skipped maintenance (old oil, a dirty air filter, a caked deck, dull blades). Regular checks and routine service prevent many problems or catch them before they become costly repairs, and neglecting oil and filter changes may void the remaining warranty on some brands.

## Key facts

**Before each mow (about five minutes):**

- Fuel level (check it in a well-ventilated area).
- Engine oil level, with the mower on level ground, engine off and key removed. Don't run with too little or too much oil.
- Air filter: a dirty filter makes the engine run rich, smoke and overheat.
- Blades and deck: look for dull blades and grass buildup (engine off, never reach under the deck while anything is moving).
- Tire pressure on air-filled tires, belts for visible cracks, and on riding and zero-turn mowers the throttle, choke and blade-engagement linkages.
- Safety system: on a riding mower the engine should shut down and the blades should not engage when the operator leaves the seat. Check the roll bar (ROPS) mounting and the seatbelt latch where fitted. If any safety check fails, don't mow until it is repaired.
- Clear debris from the cooling fins, around the engine, under the hood, on the deck and around the transmission. Debris near the engine or transmission causes overheating.
- Look for fuel and oil leaks.

**A typical hour-based schedule (one riding-mower example; always check the owner's manual for the model):**

| Interval | Tasks |
|---|---|
| Every 10 hours | Lubricate pedal pivot points; clean battery terminals; clean hood and dash air vents. |
| Every 25 hours | Lubricate axles and rims; clean the air filter; check blades. |
| Every 50 hours | Change engine oil and oil filter; change fuel filter and air filter; check belts and pulleys. |
| Every 100 hours | Check hardware; check the spark plug. |

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/a-guide-to-the-craftsman-riding-lawn-mower.md)

**How often to do a full service:** Sources give different intervals by mower and brand.

- Push mowers: every 50 hours or once a year. Push mowers have no oil filter.
- Riding mowers and zero-turns: every 100 hours or once a year, whichever comes first, in the general service guides. Several brand guides use 50 hours or once a year instead, with oil every 50 hours and before storage.
- Most small-engine makers call for an oil change every 25 to 50 hours, depending on the engine and how hard it is worked. A seasonal checklist and a parts-maker guide say to change oil at least once a year.
- New engines get an early first oil change (see the [Engine oil guide](engine-oil-guide.md) for the break-in interval).
- Average homeowners should service at least twice a season, and maintain the deck at least twice a season (more often if the lawn is heavily watered or fertilized, since moisture and fertilizer eat at the deck).
- Commercial use needs more frequent service.

**What a full service covers:** change the engine oil (and filter where fitted), replace the spark plug, replace the air filter and fuel filter, check the fuel system for leaks, clean the cooling fins and engine, clean the transmission cooling fans and fins, clean battery terminals and debris around wiring, check tire pressure, clean and scrape the deck, check belts, pulleys, blades, spindles and deck level, test the safety system, check hardware, grease the fittings and lubricate pivot points, then wash and dry the mower. **Zero-turn hydraulic (hydrostatic) system:** check the hydraulic fluid level regularly and change the hydraulic oil and filters at the maker's interval. Intervals vary widely by model. ## Recommendations

1. Do the five-minute pre-mow check every time: fuel, oil, air filter, blades, tires, belts, linkages, safety system. Clear debris from the engine and deck.
2. Change the engine oil on schedule (see the [Engine oil guide](engine-oil-guide.md)).
3. Do a full service at least once a year, at the start of the season, using the owner's manual for the model's intervals. At the start of the season also replace old oil (oil can thicken or degrade in storage), empty old fuel and refill with fresh gas, charge and test the battery, and grease all fittings.
4. Mid-season: check the oil before each use, clean the deck regularly, check blades for chips and dullness, tighten hardware loosened by vibration, check belts and pulleys, clean the air filter more often in dusty conditions, and clean corroded battery terminals with a wire brush.
5. Scrape the deck and lubricate spindles and caster wheels several times a season.
6. On zero-turns, keep the transmission's cooling fans, fins and guards clean and check the hydraulic fluid.

> **⚠ Safety:** Before working on blades or under the deck, remove the spark plug wire (boot) and the ignition key, wait for all parts to stop, and wear heavy gloves. Set the parking brake and watch for a hot engine and transmission.

## Tips & life hacks

- Air filter care: a paper filter can be tapped on a hard surface and held up to a light; if light shows through, reuse it, otherwise replace it. A foam filter with brown spots, tears or brittleness gets replaced; otherwise wash it in water and mild dish soap, rinse, and lay it flat to dry. See [Clean or replace the air filter](../procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.md).

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** Oil a washed foam filter lightly, with foam filter oil or clean engine oil ([S47](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/weed-eater-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), expert-blog; [S31](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-checklist-for-a-successful-cut.md), expert-blog) vs never oil a foam **pre-filter** that sits over a paper filter, because oil damages the paper element ([S48](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/country-clipper-zero-turn-mower-wont-start.md), expert-blog). The checklist Source reconciles them: oil only a primary foam filter, never a pre-filter. Check the owner's manual.

- Check the air filter monthly on push mowers, and several times a season on riding mowers.
- Run the engine a few minutes before changing oil: warm oil drains better.
- Keep a simple service log of what was replaced and when.
- Stock common parts (filters, plugs, blades, belts) to avoid downtime in the mowing season.
- Use gas within 30 days; gas left in the machine leaves sediment in the carburetor and fuel line.
- To make belts last: clean the deck after each mowing, keep oil and rust off the belt, and check pulleys and spindle housings for bearing wear.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the pre-mow check (and running out of fuel mid-lawn, or mowing with a failed safety switch).
- Washing with a high-pressure washer: it can damage sealed bearings. A garden hose is enough; then dry the mower completely in the sun or with a blower, since moisture harms electrical parts.
- Cleaning a dirty oil filter instead of replacing it.
- Letting debris build up around the engine and transmission, which leads to overheating shutdowns. See [Engine overheats](../problems/engine-overheats.md) and [Dirty cooling fins](../causes/dirty-cooling-fins.md).
- Running with unequal tire pressures: the deck sits unlevel and cuts unevenly.
- Mowing with dull blades: they tear grass instead of cutting it. See [Dull or bent blade](../causes/dull-or-bent-blade.md) and [Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md).
- Running on a clogged air filter. See [Clogged air filter](../causes/clogged-air-filter.md).
