# Choosing the right gas

## Summary

Which gas to put in a gas mower: octane, ethanol content, and the difference between straight gas for 4-cycle engines and the gas-and-oil mix for 2-cycle engines. Use it when a customer asks what fuel to buy, or when [wrong fuel](../causes/wrong-fuel.md) or [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md) is a suspected Cause. For keeping gas fresh, see [Fuel storage and stabilizers](fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md).

## Key facts

- **The standard spec:** unleaded gasoline, **87 octane or higher**, **no more than 10% ethanol** (sold as regular or E10). Every brand and mower type in the Sources gives this same spec for 4-cycle gas engines.
- **Regular gas is fine** if the pump shows 10% ethanol or less. Regular is generally 87 octane.
- **Never use E15, E30 or E85** (up to 15%, 30% and 85% ethanol). Most riding mower engines cannot use E85, and many can use only up to 10% ethanol (E10). Check the owner's manual.
- **Read the decal on the pump** for octane and ethanol. Pump handle colors are not consistent between stations.
- **Why ethanol hurts small engines:** it attracts moisture from the air. Over time the ethanol-and-water mix separates from the gas and sinks to the bottom of the tank, where it gets fed through the engine and causes ignition problems. It leaves gummy deposits that clog the fuel system, is corrosive to fuel parts, and burns hot, which can overheat and damage the engine. It can also soften plastic filters. See [Water in fuel](../causes/water-in-fuel.md).
- **Ethanol-free gas is best** but costs more. Some stations sell it as recreation fuel or REC-90 (often $2.00 to $3.00 more per gallon). Hardware stores sell it in cans of 4-cycle fuel, the most expensive option but handy for small mowers. pure-gas.org lists stations that sell it (US and Canada).
- **Diesel and other fuels:** some riding mowers, zero-turns and tractors run on diesel, and some Exmark models run on propane. Never put diesel in a gas mower or gas in a diesel one. Check the operator's manual.
- **2-cycle vs 4-cycle:** most mowers today are 4-cycle and take straight gas. Some older push mowers (for example Craftsman, Honda, Toro, Troy-Bilt, John Deere, Lawn-Boy and Snapper) are 2-cycle and need a gas-and-oil mix.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** The general and brand gas articles all give a minimum of 87 octane ([S33](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-the-type-of-gas-lawn-mowers-use.md), [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gas-lawn-tractors-use.md), expert-blog). But the 2-cycle mixing steps in the Lawn-Boy and push-mower articles, and the tips list in the Simplicity article, say **minimum 89 octane** ([S21](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gas-to-use-in-your-lawn-boy-mower.md), [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gas-to-use-in-a-push-mower.md), [S17](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gas-simplicity-lawn-mowers.md), expert-blog). Each of those articles says 87 everywhere else. Tell customers 87 or higher unless their manual says otherwise.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice (E30 vs E35):** The push-mower gas article ([S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gas-to-use-in-a-push-mower.md), expert-blog) lists E15, E30 and E85 in one section and E15, **E35** and E85 in another. The other Sources say E30. The rule doesn't change either way: nothing above 10% ethanol.

## Recommendations

1. **Confirm the engine type and power source first.** Is it 4-cycle or 2-cycle? Gas or diesel? Check the fuel cap, the fill ports and the operator's manual:
   - An original 2-cycle fuel cap lists the gas-to-oil ratio (16:1, 32:1, 40:1 or 50:1) or shows a fuel-and-oil can.
   - A 2-cycle engine has **one** fill port, for the mix. A 4-cycle engine has **two**: one for engine oil, one for gas.
   - The operator's manual says whether the engine takes mixed or straight gas.
2. **4-cycle engines:** use straight unleaded, 87 octane or higher, with 10% ethanol or less. Never put a gas-and-oil mix in a 4-cycle engine.
3. **2-cycle engines:** mix unleaded gas (87+ octane, 10% ethanol max) with a premium 2-cycle oil certified ISO-L-EGD and JASO M345 FD. Use the ratio on the cap or in the manual.
   1. Use the chart below to work out how much mix you need.
   2. Pour the gas into an approved gas can.
   3. Add the 2-cycle oil.
   4. Put the cap back on and shake gently to mix.
   5. Pour the mix into the mower's tank.

   | Ratio | 1 gal gas | 2 gal gas | 2.5 gal gas |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 50:1 | 2.6 oz oil | 5.2 oz oil | 6.4 oz oil |
   | 40:1 | 3.2 oz oil | 6.4 oz oil | 8.0 oz oil |
   | 32:1 | 4.0 oz oil | 8.0 oz oil | 10.0 oz oil |
   | 16:1 | 8.0 oz oil | 16.0 oz oil | 20.0 oz oil |

   Ratios vary by manufacturer. Lawn-Boy 2-cycle mowers use 32:1. Lawn-Boy mowers older than 1971 need 16:1.
4. **Buy fresh gas** from a busy station, and buy only what you'll use within 30 days. Gas can begin to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Stabilize anything you'll keep longer (see [Fuel storage and stabilizers](fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md)).
5. **If the local gas has more than 10% ethanol, buy it somewhere else.** If you can't find ethanol-free gas or gas with 10% ethanol or less, add a fuel treatment to help slow separation.
6. **Safety:** refuel in a well-ventilated area, preferably outdoors, away from combustibles and heat. Let the engine cool for a few minutes before you take off the gas cap, and don't overfill.

## Tips & life hacks

- Buy the best-quality, lowest-ethanol gas you can afford, but more expensive isn't always better. Some authors prefer premium unleaded or REC-90 because less ethanol leaves fewer carburetor deposits.
- Ethanol-free fuel (REC-90 or canned 4-cycle fuel) needs no stabilizer. Adding some won't hurt.
- The engine maker's own 2-cycle oil is fine, and pre-measured bottles make mixing easy. A 5.2 oz bottle in 2 gallons of gas or a 6.4 oz bottle in 2.5 gallons gives 50:1. A 6.4 oz bottle in 2 gallons gives 40:1. Canned 2-cycle premix (50:1 or 40:1) is also sold.
- Don't leave gas outside in a plastic can. It collects moisture and loses potency.

## Common mistakes

- **Putting straight gas in a 2-cycle engine.** The engine gets no lubrication and will most likely seize and die. This ruins the engine.
- **Putting a gas-and-oil mix in a 4-cycle engine.**
- **Using E15, E85 or any blend over 10% ethanol.** It can cause significant engine and fuel system damage. The wrong gas may also void the manufacturer's warranty.
- **Putting diesel in a gas mower, or gas in a diesel mower.**
- **Choosing gas by pump-handle color** instead of reading the decal on the pump.
- **Running old gas.** Its gummy residue clogs the filter, fuel lines and carburetor, so the engine runs sluggishly or not at all. See [Old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md) and [Clean the carburetor](../procedures/clean-the-carburetor.md).
