# Cleaning the mower

## Summary

How to clean a mower after mowing and more thoroughly from time to time, including the deck, engine area and belt area, without damaging bearings or electrics. Use this Guide for routine-care questions and whenever a Problem's fix says to clean or scrape the deck. Grass clippings and dirt hold moisture, which speeds up rust and corrosion. Clippings caked under the deck disturb airflow, dull the cut and make the engine work harder.

## Key facts

- Clean off debris after every mowing and wash the mower now and then. Debris left on the mower causes [vibration](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md) and corrosion.
- Grass buildup under the deck cuts airflow. That shows up as poor bagging and discharge ([clogged deck or chute](../causes/clogged-deck-chute.md)), an [uneven cut](../problems/uneven-cut.md), and extra load on the engine. A clogged deck can strain the engine enough that it [dies after starting](../problems/engine-starts-then-dies.md).
- Buildup mainly comes from [cutting wet or tall grass](../causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass.md) and from mowing at low engine speed ([wrong engine speed](../causes/wrong-engine-speed.md)). Full throttle moves the most air under the deck.
- Spindle and wheel bearings are sealed, but not against high pressure. A pressure washer is fine on a low setting away from the deck spindles, pulleys and engine. Use a garden hose around those parts.
- **Fire risk:** dry grass packed into the belt area on top of the deck can build up heat and start a fire. Clean the belt and pulley area too ([debris on deck or pulleys](../causes/debris-on-deck-or-pulleys.md)).

## Recommendations

**After every mowing:** blow off grass and debris with a leaf blower, an air compressor with a blow gun, or a soft brush. Wear safety glasses. This keeps clipping moisture off the mower and out of the belt and pulley area.

**Periodic wash (gas mowers):**

1. Let the engine cool first. Never spray cold water on a hot engine.
2. Wet the mower and wash it with a mild, grease-cutting dish soap that won't harm paint or plastic, using a sponge. Don't put dish detergent in a pressure washer.
3. **Engine and recoil housing:** use low pressure or stand well back. Water and the carburetor don't mix.
4. **Top of the deck and wheels:** on riding mowers and tractors, remove the belt covers and clean underneath them. Use a garden hose, not a pressure washer, around the spindle housings and pulleys.
5. **Under the deck:** disconnect the spark plug boot(s) first. Tip a push mower's front up onto something firm. Lift a riding mower or zero-turn with a floor jack and jack stands, ramps or a lift. Scrape off caked grass with a deck scraper, putty knife or wire brush, including the discharge deflector. Then rinse.
6. Rinse, then dry the mower completely with a leaf blower or in the sun. Never put it away wet.
7. **Lubricate afterwards** to prevent rust. On push mowers, spray the wheel axles and any wet cables. On riders and zero-turns, spray the cables and seat hinges, and grease the steering and deck before storage.

**Deck washout port** (on some decks): connect a garden hose, turn the water on, start the mower and engage the blades for several minutes. Then turn the water off, move the mower to a dry hard surface and run the blades a few more minutes to dry the deck. Point the discharge away from people, pets, buildings and vehicles. The port often plugs, and it is messy.

**Battery and corded electric mowers:** unplug the mower or remove the battery, and take off attachments such as the bagger. Wait until the mower has cooled after use. Brush the top of the deck with a stiff brush and wipe the clipping areas with warm soapy water. Clean the underside with compressed air or a brush, then wipe it with a damp cloth. Keep water off the motor, internal wiring and cooling vents. Let it dry completely before reinstalling the battery. See [Electric mower care and safety](electric-mower-care-and-safety.md).

## Tips & life hacks

- Without a washout port, spray stuck clippings out through the discharge chute with a garden hose. A washout port kit can be added to some decks.
- After scraping, spray the underside of the deck with a silicone deck spray. It makes a slick surface that reduces sticking and rust and makes cleanup faster. Scrape off dried clippings first so the spray coats evenly. It reduces buildup but doesn't stop it completely.
- Mow only when the grass is dry, because wet clippings turn into a thick paste. Cut no more than 1/3 of the grass height, and mow long grass two or three times.
- Scrape the deck at every service, and [sharpen the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md) at the same time. Dull blades make a clogged deck worse.
- While the shroud is off, clean the engine's [cooling fins](../procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins.md) ([dirty cooling fins](../causes/dirty-cooling-fins.md)).
- Wash a push mower's underside away from the driveway and the visible part of the yard, because it gets messy.

## Common mistakes

- Pressure washing at high pressure around the deck spindles, pulleys or engine. It damages sealed bearings and gets water into the carburetor.
- Spraying cold water on a hot engine, or washing an electric mower right after use.
- Storing the mower wet or with clippings on it, which leads to rust, corrosion and electrical failures. See [Winterizing and storage](winterizing-and-storage.md) and [Mower got wet](mower-got-wet.md).
- Leaving grass packed in the belt area, which is a fire risk and can contribute to a [belt that keeps coming off](../problems/belt-keeps-coming-off.md).
- Letting the deck stay caked, which causes [poor bagging](../problems/not-bagging-grass.md) and a poor cut.
