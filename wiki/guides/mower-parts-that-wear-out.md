# Mower parts that wear out

## Summary

Which mower parts wear out with normal use, how often they usually need replacing, the main blade types, and which spare parts are worth keeping on hand. Use it when a customer asks what to replace at the annual service, what to stock before the season, or whether an old mower is worth repairing.

Sources: [S26](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/what-are-the-riding-mower-parts-that-require-replacement-most-often.md), [S25](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-mower-parts-and-supplies-to-stock-up-on-video.md), [S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-replacement.md)

## Key facts

| Part | Typical replacement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blades | Replace once a year (every 1–3 seasons per one maker); sharpen every 25 hours | Sand, dirt, rocks, sticks and toys wear and damage them; lawn chemicals corrode them. A cracked or damaged blade must be replaced. See [Dull or bent blade](../causes/dull-or-bent-blade.md). |
| Blade (deck) belt | End of each cutting season, or when excessively worn | Wears and can slip after a season; cracked, glazed, stretched or shredded belts should be replaced. See [Worn or stretched belt](../causes/worn-or-stretched-belt.md). |
| Drive belt | Yearly on riding mowers per one maker; about 3–4 years under ideal conditions per another Source | See [Worn drive belt](../causes/worn-drive-belt.md). |
| Spindle (mandrel) bearings | When worn; often after a season | See [Bent spindle or bad spindle bearing](../causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing.md). |
| Pulley and idler bearings, idler spring/bracket, tensioner, belt keepers | When worn | See [Worn pulley or idler bearing](../causes/worn-pulley-or-idler-bearing.md). |
| Air filter, oil filter, spark plug, fuel filter | After every season (spark plug once a year or per the manual) | Engine tune-up parts. See [Tune-up](../procedures/tune-up.md). |
| Battery | Every 3–5 years | See [Battery care and charging](../guides/battery-care-and-charging.md). |
| Tires | When tread is thin, or with dry rot | Tire sealant can fix small leaks instead. |
| Battery mowers | — | Batteries, blades, chargers and power cords are the regular replacements. |

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** drive belt life. One Source says replace the ground drive belt yearly ([S26](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/what-are-the-riding-mower-parts-that-require-replacement-most-often.md), manufacturer); another says a drive belt lasts three to four years under ideal conditions, longer if the mower is kept clean and inspected yearly ([S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-how-long-a-mower-drive-belt-should-last.md), expert-blog).

**Blade types.** Not every type fits every mower; check the owner's manual.

- **High-lift:** tall sail; the best, most precise cut and stripes; standard on most new mowers and the one to use for bagging. Suits thick grass kept at least 3 inches tall. See [Not bagging grass](../problems/not-bagging-grass.md).
- **Low-lift:** shorter sail; for dry, dusty or sandy conditions and short grass. It moves less air, so less dirt is drawn into the deck to wear the spindle seals.
- **Mulching:** curved shape with no straight edge; chops clippings finely. May need extra baffling and a chute blocker (makers sell optional mulch kits).
- **Shredding (gator):** jagged teeth on the sail; makes smaller clippings and shreds leaves.

**Rough costs (one Source, U.S.):** a replacement belt about $20–$60 (see the belt-price callout below); tires $20–$80; spark plugs from about $2; batteries from about $40. An average mower lasts about 7–10 years; push-mower engines about 500–700 hours, larger riding-mower engines up to 1,000 hours, commercial engines often 2,000 hours or more. Poor maintenance can halve that.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice (belt prices):** about $20 to $60 ([S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-replacement.md), expert-blog) vs $60 to $250 ([S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/exmark-belt-keeps-coming-off-or-breaking.md), expert-blog) vs $80 to $250 for OEM riding-mower deck belts ([S21](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-belt-keeps-falling-off-your-riding-mower-fixed.md), expert-blog).

## Recommendations

1. **Replace the blades at the annual service** and sharpen them every 25 hours (more often on sandy or gravelly lawns). Step by step: [Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md); to match a new blade, [Measure a mower blade](../procedures/measure-a-mower-blade.md).
2. **Replace the engine tune-up parts every season:** air filter, oil filter, spark plug and fuel filter.
3. **Inspect belts, pulleys and spindle bearings at every service**, and replace cracked, glazed or stretched belts. See [Replace the deck belt](../procedures/replace-the-deck-belt.md) and [Replace the spindle assembly](../procedures/replace-the-spindle-assembly.md).
4. **Keep spares on hand**, especially with a big lawn. Worth stocking: blades, a spare set of mandrels (spindles), blade and drive belts, deck spray (a nonstick coating that keeps clippings from sticking under the deck), fuel stabilizer, and tire sealant (seals punctures up to 1/4 inch). Nice to have: air filters, spark plugs, engine oil, an oil filter, a blade removal tool and flywheel keys.
5. **Look parts up by model number.** Engine parts may be on a separate parts list, looked up by the engine model number. See [Customer intake](../guides/customer-intake.md).
6. **Repair or replace the mower?** Consider replacing it with extensive engine or transmission damage, constant out-of-warranty repairs, or a repair bill close to the price of a new mower.

## Tips & life hacks

- When you fit new blades, sharpen the old set (if not damaged or badly worn) and keep it as a spare. A blade damaged mid-season can then be swapped at once, and blades sent out for sharpening don't leave you without a mower.
- Remove and refit the blades regularly so lawn chemicals and corrosion don't fuse them to the spindle.
- Walk the lawn before mowing and pick up sticks, rocks and toys. An impact can chip or bend a blade, cause vibration, or damage the spindle.
- A thick lawn draws less dirt into the deck than a thin one, so blades last longer.
- A universal blade removal tool holds the blade while you undo the bolt; it's easier and safer than a block of wood or your hand.
- Keep the deck clean and greased; a deck spray helps mulching and discharge.
- Many simple repairs (tire, seat switch, spark plug, air filter, fuel filter, ignition switch, ignition coil) take a beginner 15–45 minutes.

## Common mistakes

- Running a cracked blade. It can break and throw metal, injuring someone or damaging the house.
- Fitting a blade type the mower isn't designed for.
- Leaving blades on for years until chemicals and rust fuse them to the spindle.
- Waiting for a belt to snap instead of replacing it when it's cracked or glazed. See [Worn blade sail](../causes/worn-blade-sail.md) and [Uneven cut](../problems/uneven-cut.md) for what worn deck parts do to the cut.
