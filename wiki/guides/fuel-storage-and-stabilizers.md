# Fuel storage and stabilizers

## Summary

How to keep mower gas fresh: how long gas lasts, how to store it, and how to use fuel stabilizers and other fuel additives, including how much to add. Use it when a customer asks about storing gas or additives, or when [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md) is a likely Cause. For which gas to buy, see [Choosing the right gas](choosing-the-right-gas.md). For the full off-season routine, see [Winterizing and storage](winterizing-and-storage.md).

## Key facts

- **Gas goes stale fast.** It begins to go stale about 30 days after you pump it. Stale gas is a big reason mowers won't start after sitting, and it can make a mower sputter, hesitate or not start.
- **Old fuel leaves a gummy, varnish-like residue** that clogs the fuel filter, fuel lines and carburetor, and degrades fuel system parts. That leads to blockages and leaks. Ethanol in the gas attracts moisture, which makes things worse. See [Water in fuel](../causes/water-in-fuel.md).
- **A fuel stabilizer keeps gas fresh longer.** It keeps the gas from absorbing water and going bad. Some products claim they keep fuel stable for up to two years.
- **A stabilizer must go into fresh fuel.** It cannot reverse fuel that has already degraded or separated.
- **Ethanol-free fuel** (REC-90 or canned 4-cycle fuel) needs no stabilizer, though adding some won't hurt.
- **Heat matters too.** Gasoline expands when hot. Storing a mower or gas can in direct sun or a hot shed pushes vapor past the seals.
- **Diesel** also needs care: store it away from moisture, light and heat. Diesel stored a long time can form solids and condensation.

## Recommendations

1. **Buy only what you'll use in 30 days.** A 2-gallon can is big enough for a push mower, and a 5-gallon can for a riding mower. Buy from a busy station.
2. **Use the right container.** Use an approved gas can designed for unleaded gas, because some other plastic containers break down. Sears Parts Direct recommends plastic over metal: rust inside a metal can contaminates the fuel, making the engine hard to start or making it sputter and die. Pick a can with a tight-sealing spout or cap to limit fumes.
3. **Store it cool, dry and away from combustibles**, indoors and out of the weather. Gas left outside collects moisture and loses potency.
4. **Add stabilizer to any gas you won't use within 30 days.** Add it while the gas is fresh, following the label for the stabilizer-to-fuel ratio. Some authors add it to every tank.
5. **Rotate your gas.** Empty the can you're using before refilling it, and use the oldest gas first. Never top up old gas with fresh.
6. **At the end of the season,** either stabilize a full tank and run the engine a few minutes (Sears Parts Direct says about 10 minutes) so treated fuel reaches the carburetor, or drain the tank and run the engine until it stops. Don't leave untreated gas in the tank over the off-season. Leftover gas in a can can go into a car or other gas equipment. Don't let a can sit for months. See [Winterizing and storage](winterizing-and-storage.md).
7. **Even if the mower is rarely used,** change the fuel at least every three months.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** Sears Parts Direct says **never pour fuel stabilizer directly into the mower's gas tank**: mix it into the gas can instead ([S58](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md), manufacturer). Several expert-blog articles say to add stabilizer to the fuel in the tank and run the engine a few minutes ([S54](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winterize-your-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/dont-skip-winterizing-your-husqvarna-zero-turn.md), [S55](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winterize-your-toro-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog). Mixing it into the can before fueling satisfies both.

## Tips & life hacks

- **Dosing a stabilizer or fuel treatment (per Source, check the product label):**
  - Sea Foam: a 16 oz can treats up to 16 gallons. Use **1 oz per gallon** for routine fuel maintenance, or **2 oz per gallon** when cleaning out the fuel system (more is okay). Measure it into a 1-gallon can of gas.
  - A fuel system cleaner made for lawn mowers: typically **two teaspoons per gallon**.
  - A cleaner made for cars (such as Redex): use it at **half strength**. Too much can strip the lining of a mower's small fuel tank.
- **Products named in the Sources:** Sea Foam Motor Treatment (the authors' favorite: stabilizes, reduces moisture, cleans the fuel system), STA-BIL (a common alternative; one author thinks it works less well with ethanol fuels), Lucas Safeguard Ethanol Fuel Conditioner, and Archoil AR 6200 (mainly for diesel but usable with gas). These are the authors' opinions, not tests.
- **A fuel system cleaner** is the easiest way to clear dirt and deposits from the fuel system, short of taking the engine apart or paying a shop.
- **Octane boosters** aren't needed in a small mower engine. They won't hurt, but they waste money.

## Common mistakes

- **Adding stabilizer to gas that has already gone stale.** It can't undo the damage. Use fresh gas instead (see [Start a mower with old gas](../procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas.md)).
- **Buying more gas than you'll use in a month**, or topping up old gas with fresh.
- **Storing gas outside, in a non-approved container, or in a rusty metal can.**
- **Leaving untreated gas in the mower over winter.** It gums up the fuel system and causes starting and running problems in spring. You may then need to [clean the carburetor](../procedures/clean-the-carburetor.md) or [clean the gas tank](../procedures/clean-the-gas-tank.md).
- **Using diesel-only additives** (diesel system cleaners, diesel emissions reducers) in a gas mower.
- **Pouring in a car-strength fuel cleaner at full dose.** Use half strength in a mower.
