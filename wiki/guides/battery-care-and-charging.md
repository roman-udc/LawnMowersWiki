# Battery care and charging

## Summary

How to keep a mower battery charged, clean and alive: how long batteries last, what the voltages mean, how to charge safely, and how to protect the battery from freezing over winter. Use it when a customer asks how to look after or store a battery, or after a [battery that keeps dying](../problems/battery-keeps-dying.md) has been fixed and they want to stop it happening again. Most of it is about the 12 V lead-acid battery in riding and zero-turn mowers; the paragraph on battery (cordless) mowers covers their lithium-ion packs.

Sources: [S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md), [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-battery-charging-times-explained.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/caring-for-your-electric-lawn-mower.md)

## Key facts

- **Life:** a mower battery typically lasts at least 3 years. Vibration is the main killer, followed by freezing.
- **Voltage:** most riding mowers use a 12 V battery; some older mowers (from about 1980 or earlier) use 6 V. A fully charged 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V; about 11.5 V is almost dead. A healthy battery at rest reads about 12.4–12.8 V; with the engine running, the charging system should show about 13.2–13.9 V. Before testing a starter solenoid, the battery should read at least 12.3 V.
- **Size:** a mower with an electric blade clutch needs about 300 cold cranking amps (CCA); about 250 CCA otherwise. A core charge of about $15–$25 is typical when buying a new battery.
- **The battery drains in storage.** A key left on, corroded or loose terminals, a faulty charging system and age all speed this up. Older batteries lose charge faster; a battery that drains too quickly should be replaced. See [Parasitic battery drain](../causes/parasitic-battery-drain.md) and [Faulty charging system](../causes/faulty-charging-system.md).
- **Once the engine runs, the mower's own charging system recharges the battery.** Repeated jump starts can damage the charging system, so a charger is preferred, and a 6 V battery must be charged, not jumped.
- **Freezing:** a 100% charged battery will not freeze in normal winter storage. A battery that is not fully charged can freeze and be ruined: the lower the charge, the higher the temperature at which it freezes. A half-charged battery can freeze solid overnight at −10 °F. If the temperature falls under 20 °F and the battery has less than 20% charge, it may freeze. A frozen battery is unlikely to charge again and should be replaced.

| Charge | 12 V reading | 6 V reading | Freezes at about |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | 12.78 V | 6.39 V | −80 °F |
| 92% | 12.66 V | 6.33 V | −71.3 °F |
| 85% | 12.57 V | 6.28 V | −62 °F |
| 62% | 12.27 V | 6.13 V | −16 °F |
| 50% | – | – | −10 °F |
| 40% | 11.97 V | 5.98 V | +5 °F |
| 20% | 11.67 V | 5.84 V | +19 °F |

## Recommendations

1. **Charge slowly, with the right charger.** Use a charger of 10 A or less on a mower battery; 7 A or less is gentler and is called the best balance. Fast 20 A chargers shorten charging but can damage the battery, especially if used regularly. A trickle charger (1–3 A) is the gentlest option. Step by step: [Charge the battery](../procedures/charge-the-battery.md).
2. **Charge before winter storage and again before the first spring start.** Batteries lose charge over the off-season.
3. **For winter, either remove the battery or keep it fully charged.** Removing it is called the best way. To remove it: wear gloves and eye protection (battery acid), turn the mower off, remove the hold-down brackets or straps, take off the black negative (−) cable first, then the red positive (+). Store it in a cool, dry place away from anything flammable and away from damp rooms. If it is stored with the mower, disconnect the cables and keep it fully charged; a trickle charger left on over the cold months keeps it charged so it doesn't freeze.
4. **Keep terminals clean and tight.** Clean corrosion with a wire brush and a baking soda solution (about 2 cups of water to 3 heaping tablespoons of baking soda), then coat the terminals with dielectric grease or petroleum jelly. Clean before storage and again before spring use. See [Loose or corroded battery cables](../causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables.md).
5. **Keep the battery firmly held down.** Vibration shortens its life.
6. **Test before you replace.** After winter, check the connections, charge the battery, then measure the voltage with a multimeter set to DC (red lead to +, black to −). See [Test the battery](../procedures/test-the-battery.md). Test the battery and charging system once a year.
7. **Replace the battery** if the case is cracked, leaking or bulging, the terminals are badly corroded or damaged, it won't hold a charge after charging, or it has frozen. Replace loose or broken cables too. See [Bad battery](../causes/bad-battery.md) and [Weak or dead battery](../causes/weak-or-dead-battery.md).

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** how long a full charge takes. One Source says a 10 A charger needs about one hour and a trickle charger eight hours or more ([S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md), expert-blog); another says that, charged slowly, a 6 V battery can take up to 6 hours and a 12 V up to 12 hours, and a trickle charger up to 24 hours ([S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-battery-charging-times-explained.md), expert-blog). The time depends on how flat the battery is and on the charger's amps.

**Battery (cordless) mowers:** keep the lithium-ion pack charged to at least 20% at all times, including over winter; lithium-ion packs degrade if left flat. Charge it an hour or two before mowing, since a full battery left sitting loses some charge. Replace a pack that is swollen, leaking, runs hot, charges slowly, won't charge even on a new charger, or was run to zero and won't recharge. More in [Electric mower care and safety](../guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety.md).

## Tips & life hacks

- Put the charger on well before mowing season starts, so the first mow isn't held up.
- Pull the key when the mower is parked: a key left in the switch can draw standby current and drain the battery.
- Some chargers shut off by themselves; if yours doesn't, unplug it when the battery is full to avoid overcharging.
- Hook up a trickle charger in this order: charger unplugged, red clamp to +, black clamp to −, check the voltage setting, then plug it in.
- Keep the battery dry and store the mower out of rain and snow. On battery mowers, keep blades sharp: dull blades draw more current.
- A combined jump starter and charger lets you both jump and maintain the battery. After a jump, let the mower run so its charging system can recharge the battery. See [Jump-start the battery](../procedures/jump-start-the-battery.md).

## Common mistakes

- Leaving a partly charged battery in an unheated shed or garage over winter. It can freeze, crack and never charge again.
- Leaving the battery connected through a long storage: slow drain plus cold ruins it.
- Charging with a charger over 10 A.
- Jump-starting over and over instead of charging, which can damage the charging system.
- Replacing a "dead" battery after winter without first cleaning the terminals, charging it and testing the voltage.
- Letting a lithium-ion battery-mower pack sit flat over winter.
