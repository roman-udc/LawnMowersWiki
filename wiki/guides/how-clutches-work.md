# How clutches work

## Summary

How the electric blade clutch on a riding or zero-turn mower works, and the signs that it is failing. Use it when a customer asks what the clutch does, or alongside [Blades won't engage](../problems/blades-wont-engage.md) and [Bad PTO clutch](../causes/bad-pto-clutch.md). The clutch can be one of the most expensive mower parts, so confirm it is the fault before replacing it.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/a-look-into-how-lawn-mower-clutches-work.md)

## Key facts

- **What it is:** the electric clutch, also called the power take-off (PTO) clutch, is where the engine's power is passed to the blades. It engages the belt that drives the blades, and it also brakes the blades when switched off.
- **How it is powered:** the dashboard PTO switch feeds battery power to the clutch. When it engages you should hear it click on under the deck.
- **Parts:** an electromagnet, a rotor with a plate inside it, a flat pulley on top connected by a shaft through the middle, a brake pad, and assorted plates, bolts, springs and pulleys. Several clutch types exist with slightly different parts, but they all do the same job.
- **Engaging:** the engine spins the rotor. With the clutch off, the rotor spins freely. Switching it on energizes the magnet, which pulls against the plate in the rotor and starts the pulley turning the blades.
- **Braking:** when switched off, the magnetic pull is released and the brake pad presses against the pulley to slow the blades.

## Recommendations

Signs the clutch may need replacing:

- The blades don't spin when the clutch is switched on.
- The mower won't start when the blades are engaged.
- The deck doesn't spin fast enough, or the blades vibrate too much and slow down as they start.
- Unusual sounds, rough running or vibration (see [Mower is vibrating](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md)).

Before replacing it, test it (see [Test the PTO clutch](../procedures/test-the-pto-clutch.md)):

1. If you don't hear the click, check the power reaching the clutch with a multimeter. It should get about 12 volts. Too little means the fault is elsewhere (for example the [PTO switch](../causes/bad-pto-switch.md) or wiring), not the clutch.
2. With power present, measure the electromagnet's resistance: it should read between 1 and 3 ohms. A high reading means too much resistance; a low reading may mean a wiring problem.
3. Inspect the brake pad and hub for buildup, corrosion or obstructions that stop the pulley making good contact. Replace a worn brake pad.
4. Inspect plates, bolts, springs and pulleys for wear and make sure everything is tight. Then replace the damaged parts or the whole clutch; the mower's dealer usually has the part.

## Tips & life hacks

- Listen for the click under the deck when you flip the PTO switch: no click is the first clue.
- Clutch mounting torque is generally 50-60 ft-lbs, but each machine differs: find the spec for the model before taking it apart.
- Some clutches can be taken apart for inspection.

## Common mistakes

- Buying a new clutch without confirming it is the fault: low voltage at the clutch means the problem is elsewhere.
