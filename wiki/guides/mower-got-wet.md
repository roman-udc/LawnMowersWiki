# Mower got wet

## Summary

What to do after a mower has been left out in the rain or soaked, for both gas and battery mowers. Use this Guide when a customer says the mower won't start, or runs badly, after rain. Water can get into the engine oil, the fuel tank and the electrical parts (ignition switch, spark plug wire and boot, safety switches). A gas riding mower is built to handle light rain and usually has no lasting damage, but after heavy rain the moisture must be removed. Battery and corded electric mowers are only water-resistant, not waterproof. See also [Electric mower care and safety](electric-mower-care-and-safety.md).

## Key facts

- Water in the electrical system is a common reason a mower won't start after rain (see [wet electrical components](../causes/wet-electrical-components.md)). On a riding mower this can look like a bad starter solenoid, so dry and check the electrical parts first.
- **Oil:** milky white oil means water is in the oil. Change it, because running on it can cause major internal engine damage. Water in the oil usually got into the crankcase through the air filter.
- **Fuel:** the gas cap has a vent, so hard rain can get into the tank. Water doesn't burn, so the mower runs rough or won't run at all (see [water in fuel](../causes/water-in-fuel.md)).
- **Electric mowers:** water gets in through the battery compartment (cracks, or a latch that isn't closed) and through the motor and battery cooling vents. Turning on a soaked electric mower can short the electrical system and damage the motor beyond repair. A brief shower is usually fine. Drying a soaked mower can take up to 8 hours. Severe water damage can shorten an electric mower's life, but it can usually be made to work again.

## Recommendations

**Gas mowers**

1. **Dry the mower,** especially the wiring, switches, cables and other electrical parts. Use a leaf blower or an air compressor with an air gun, then leave it in the sun on a warm, breezy day.
2. **Check the oil** with a flashlight. If it looks milky, [change the engine oil](../procedures/change-the-engine-oil.md).
3. **Check the air filter.** If water got into the oil, the filter is probably wet too. Replace a wet paper filter, because drying it in the sun doesn't fully restore it. A foam filter can be wrung out, dried in the sun, oiled with air filter oil, wrung out again and reinstalled. See [clean or replace the air filter](../procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.md).
4. **Check the fuel tank for water.** If you find any, siphon the fuel into a safe container and take it to a recycling center. [Replace the fuel filter](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter.md) if the mower has one, and refill with fresh fuel. If you can't drain all of it, a fuel additive can help with the moisture (see [Fuel storage and stabilizers](fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md)).
5. **Check the carburetor.** If the mower was run with water in the tank, water is probably in the carburetor too. Remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and take off the bowl. Catch the gas and water in a rag, then reinstall the empty bowl. See [clean the carburetor](../procedures/clean-the-carburetor.md) if it still runs badly.
6. **Displace water from the electrical parts.** Spray a water-displacing lubricant such as WD-40 into the ignition switch. Pull off the spark plug wire and spray the boot and wire. Spray the safety switches too, since water collects there.
7. **Start it.** It may take a pull or two, or a couple of turns of the key. It may smoke at first, and dirty liquid (rainwater, oil and dirt) may drip from around the engine. Both should stop once the engine reaches operating temperature.
8. **Lubricate the cables.** Spray the choke and throttle cables, and any wires that got wet, so the lubricant gets inside the cable housing. Otherwise the cables can rust or freeze in cool weather.

**Battery and corded electric mowers**

1. Don't turn it on until you are sure it is dry.
2. Remove the battery (or unplug the cord) and dry the battery first. Remove the water-resistant cover if the manufacturer allows it, so air can flow.
3. Dry the motor and wiring with a handheld blower, fans or a hairdryer for small gaps and crevices, or leave it in the sun on a dry, breezy day.
4. Once it is dry, apply electrical contact cleaner to the connections and terminals.
5. Optional, for experienced users: test the wiring insulation with a megohmmeter. Below 100,000 ohms, let it dry longer.
6. Latch the battery compartment securely every time. Replace the compartment cover if the latch or hinge is broken.

## Tips & life hacks

- The best prevention is storing the mower indoors in a garage, pole barn or shed. If it has to stay outside, use a waterproof (marine-grade) cover. Never store it in the house, the basement or next to anything flammable. See [Winterizing and storage](winterizing-and-storage.md).
- In humid areas, one author adds a fuel additive every other fill-up to keep condensation out of the fuel (single blog report).
- Mowing in light rain is possible, but wet grass clumps, builds up under the deck, gives a poor cut and leaves deeper tire tracks. See [Cleaning the mower](cleaning-the-mower.md).

## Common mistakes

- Trying to start a soaked electric mower before it is dry, which can short the electrics and ruin the motor.
- Running the engine on milky oil instead of changing it.
- Drying a wet paper air filter and reusing it.
- Spraying water up an electric mower's cooling vents while cleaning it.
- Replacing the starter solenoid or other parts before drying the electrical system and checking it again ([engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md), [engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md), [battery mower won't start](../problems/battery-mower-wont-start.md), [engine runs rough](../problems/engine-runs-rough.md)).
- Leaving the mower out in heavy or steady rain.
