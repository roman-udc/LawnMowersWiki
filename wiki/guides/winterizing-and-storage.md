# Winterizing and storage

## Summary

How to get a gas mower ready for the off-season, or for any storage longer than about a month, and where to keep it. Use this Guide when a customer asks how to put a mower away for winter. Also use it when a mower that was stored without preparation won't start in spring. Skipping winterizing leads to rust, corrosion, gummed fuel, a frozen or flat battery, wiring chewed by rodents, and trouble starting and running in spring. Fuel choice and stabilizer dosing are in [Fuel storage and stabilizers](fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md). Battery freeze risk and charging are in [Battery care and charging](battery-care-and-charging.md). Oil type is in the [Engine oil guide](engine-oil-guide.md).

## Key facts

- **Fuel:** gas can start breaking down within about 30 days. For storage longer than 30 days, use one of two methods. Drain the tank and run the engine until it stops. Or add fuel stabilizer to fresh fuel and run the engine a few minutes so treated fuel reaches the carburetor (one manufacturer Source says 10 minutes). Untreated fuel attracts moisture and gums up the fuel system, which causes [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md) problems in spring. Stabilizer only works in fresh gas. It can't fix gas that is already old. For details, see [Fuel storage and stabilizers](fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md).
- **Battery:** a fully charged battery will not freeze. Either keep it fully charged or remove it and store it somewhere cool and dry, away from combustibles and freezing temperatures. See [Battery care and charging](battery-care-and-charging.md).
- **Moisture and debris:** grass and dirt hold moisture, which rusts the deck and corrodes electrical parts. Wash, scrape and dry the mower completely before storage. See [Cleaning the mower](cleaning-the-mower.md).
- **Rodents:** mice and squirrels nest under the hood and chew wiring harnesses and filter housings. On many mowers the wires have a soy-based coating that rodents eat. Wiring repairs can cost thousands.
- **Tires:** underinflated tires left sitting get flat spots, which cause vibration. Flat spots usually work out with use, but sometimes the tire must be replaced.
- **Where to store:** a dry garage, pole barn or shed is best. Never store a mower that has fuel in it inside the house or basement, or near open flames or sparks such as wood stoves, water heaters, ranges and clothes dryers. Fuel fumes are flammable.

## Recommendations

Work roughly in this order. Park on flat, level ground with the parking brake set. Put cardboard under the work area to catch drips. The engine may still be hot and the blades are sharp.

1. **Change the engine oil** and filter if the oil is dirty or it has been about 50 hours since the last change. Run the engine a couple of minutes to warm the oil, then [change the engine oil](../procedures/change-the-engine-oil.md). If the mower has a serviceable hydrostatic transmission and the service interval is due, [change the hydraulic oil](../procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil.md). Many entry-level transmissions are sealed and can't be serviced.
2. **Disconnect the spark plug boot(s)** before working under the deck so the engine can't start. Don't skip this step.
3. **Service the deck.** Lift it on jack stands, not pneumatic stands (they can shift), or remove it as the manual describes. Grab each blade by its ends and rock it to check the spindle bearings. Movement or noise means the spindle needs repair. [Sharpen or replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md), and never reuse a bent, sail-worn or gouged blade. Scrape the underside. Take off the belt covers and clean out debris under the pulleys. Replace a belt that is worn, glazed or shredded. Turn each pulley by hand and replace any that is rusty, noisy or stiff.
4. **Replace the maintenance items** once a year: the [spark plug(s)](../procedures/replace-the-spark-plug.md), [air filter](../procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.md) and [fuel filter](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter.md). The arrow on the fuel filter points the way fuel flows. A [tune-up](../procedures/tune-up.md) covers all three. Check the fuel lines, fuel pump and carburetor for leaks so fumes don't fill the storage area.
5. **Clean the engine.** Remove the shroud if needed. Clear debris from the [cooling fins](../procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins.md), the engine and the exhaust, and from around the hydrostatic transmission fan.
6. **Fuel:** drain the tank or add stabilizer (see Key facts).
7. **Battery:** [charge it](../procedures/charge-the-battery.md) fully (12.6 V or more is 100%) or remove it. To remove it, put on gloves and eye protection, turn the mower off and take off the hold-downs. Disconnect the black negative cable first, then the red positive. Clean corroded terminals with a wire brush and a baking soda solution (2 cups water, 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda). Then apply a thin coat of dielectric grease.
8. **Tires:** inflate them to the pressure on the sidewall or in the manual. A couple of psi extra is fine, since tires lose pressure in the cold.
9. **Lubricate.** Grease every grease fitting (wheels, front axle, deck, idler arm pivots and some casters). Spray a lubricant on cables and linkages.
10. **Check safety systems and hardware.** Test the safety interlocks as the manual describes. On zero-turns, inspect the rollover bar and seat belt. Tighten loose hardware and order any missing parts.
11. **Wash and dry the mower completely.** Use a garden hose, with no high-pressure washing near bearings. Dry it in the sun or with a leaf blower. Never store a mower wet.
12. **Store it in a cool, dry place.** Indoors, cover it with a tarp or drop cloth to keep dust off. Outdoors, raise it off the ground and use a cover (see the conflict below). Under an overhang or deck, rain falls through the deck boards and snow blows in.
13. **Protect against rodents** with a pet-safe deterrent. Options include repellent packets around the mower plus a spray on the wiring, mothballs around the mower, or traps.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** Expert blogs recommend a heavy-duty waterproof or marine-grade cover for outdoor storage ([S23](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winterize-your-lawn-mower-top-storage-for-your-mower.md), [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-storage.md), expert-blog) vs the manufacturer Source, which says to use a breathable, vented cover and not a plastic one, because plastic traps condensation and causes rust ([S27](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-maintenance-checklist-by-season.md), [S28](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/storing-your-lawn-tractor-for-winter.md), manufacturer).

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** Expert blogs say a fully charged battery can stay installed and connected ([S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-storage.md), expert-blog) vs the manufacturer Source, which says to remove it, or to leave it in place with the cables disconnected, because a connected battery in storage corrodes and loses power. It also says a cold garage or shed can shorten battery life ([S28](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/storing-your-lawn-tractor-for-winter.md), [S27](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-maintenance-checklist-by-season.md), manufacturer).

## Tips & life hacks

- On zero-turns with air-filled front caster tires, set the tires on cardboard to keep them off cold concrete.
- To protect the cylinder from rust, remove the spark plugs and pour 1 ounce of oil into each plug hole. Run the engine for about 5 seconds to spread the oil, then install new spark plugs, gapped correctly.
- If the engine wasn't run dry, clamp the fuel lines while the fuel filter is off to prevent spills.
- After scraping the deck, spray the underside with a deck spray to protect it from rust. Lightly sand paint chips on the deck and tractor and apply touch-up paint.
- On a lawn tractor, clean out debris underneath, especially around the steering plate below the brake pedal, where it can stop the pedal from moving freely.
- A battery that is removed for winter can be put on the charger a couple of times during storage. Keep it out of damp places such as under sinks and in laundry rooms.
- Before buying a shed, measure the mower to make sure it fits through the door.
- If time is short, the oil change and spark plug replacement can wait until spring.
- Many owners don't drain the tank. They fill it, add stabilizer as the bottle directs and run the engine a few minutes. Either method works if done properly.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice (where to add stabilizer):** add stabilizer to the fuel in the tank (most expert-blog Sources) vs mix it into fresh gas in a gas can and never pour it straight into the mower's tank ([S26](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md), manufacturer). The full Source list is in [Fuel storage and stabilizers](fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md).

## Common mistakes

- Pushing the mower into a corner and ignoring it until spring. Common results in spring: the [engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md) or the [engine runs rough](../problems/engine-runs-rough.md) because of gummed fuel, and the [battery keeps dying](../problems/battery-keeps-dying.md) because it is a [weak or dead battery](../causes/weak-or-dead-battery.md).
- Storing the mower wet, which leaves [wet electrical components](../causes/wet-electrical-components.md) and causes rust. See [Mower got wet](mower-got-wet.md).
- Leaving old gas in the tank, or adding stabilizer to gas that is already stale.
- Using a high-pressure washer around the deck and spindles. Sealed bearings are not sealed against high pressure.
- Skipping rodent protection. Chewed wiring harnesses show up as [bad wiring or a bad ground](../causes/bad-wiring-or-ground.md).
- Leaving tires underinflated, which causes flat spots and makes the [mower vibrate](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md) (see [wrong tire pressure](../causes/wrong-tire-pressure.md)).
- Working under the deck with the spark plug wires still connected.
