# Reading wiring schematics

## Summary

What a wiring schematic (wiring diagram) is and how it helps with electrical faults on a riding or zero-turn mower. Use it to explain the idea to a customer before sending them to the step-by-step [Read a wiring schematic](../procedures/read-a-wiring-schematic.md). The manufacturer videos behind this Guide use household-appliance examples; only their general method is kept here.

Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/wiring-schematic-symbols-explained-video.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-read-a-wiring-schematic-video.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/using-a-wiring-schematic-to-trace-a-current-video.md)

## Key facts

- A schematic is a map of the wiring and of how current flows through the electrical parts. Lines are wires; symbols are components (switches, lights, motors and so on). Following the lines from one symbol to the next shows where a failure could be.
- Wire colors are labeled on the lines with abbreviations, and a color chart on the schematic itself decodes them.
- Current flows in a loop (a circuit). A broken wire or failed part anywhere in the loop stops it, so tracing the circuit with a multimeter narrows down which part has failed.
- Knowing how to read the schematic helps you buy the right part the first time.
- **Starting circuit on a riding mower:** the red battery cable goes to one large post of the starter solenoid; a black wire on the other large post feeds the starter motor. A small wire through the ignition switch energizes the solenoid's coil when the key is turned to start, which closes the contact and sends battery power to the starter. See [Bad starter solenoid](../causes/bad-starter-solenoid.md).
- Starter solenoids come in 3-post and 4-post versions with different schematics. Not all schematics are the same for every mower: basic ones leave out options such as lights and 12-volt ports.

## Recommendations

1. Get the schematic for the exact model: it may be in the owner's manual, on a sticker on the machine, or on the model's parts-diagram page online.
2. Find the circuit for the fault (for example, the starting circuit when the [engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md)) and follow it from the battery through each part and wire.
3. Trace that circuit with a multimeter until only one part is left untested: that is the likely culprit. The step-by-step method is in [Read a wiring schematic](../procedures/read-a-wiring-schematic.md); for the solenoid itself, [Test the starter solenoid](../procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid.md).
4. Repair or replace broken wires or bad connections. See [Bad wiring or ground](../causes/bad-wiring-or-ground.md).

## Tips & life hacks

- A multimeter is the essential tool for electrical troubleshooting.
- Check that the schematic you have matches your mower's options: a diagram for the base model may not show lights or accessory ports.

Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/wiring-schematic-symbols-explained-video.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bad-ariens-mower-solenoid.md)

## Common mistakes

- Using a schematic for a different model and assuming the wiring is the same. Not all mower schematics match.
- Replacing a part before tracing the circuit, when a broken wire was the real fault.
