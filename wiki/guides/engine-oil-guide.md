# Engine oil guide

## Summary

Which oil a gas mower engine takes, how much, and how often to change it. Use it when a customer asks what oil to buy, how much to add, or when to change it, and when a Problem points at [wrong or low oil](../causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil.md) or [overfilled oil](../causes/overfilled-oil.md). Oil cools, lubricates and cleans the engine; skipping oil changes shortens engine life and can cause serious engine damage.

## Key facts

- **Mower oil is not car oil.** Mower engines are air-cooled and rely on air and oil to stay cool. Use an air-cooled engine oil that is high in zinc; car oil has little zinc because car engines are liquid-cooled.
- **What oil does:** it cools internal parts, cushions moving parts against friction, and cleans carbon and broken-down oil out of the oil passages. Old oil breaks down and loses its cooling agents and detergents; overheated oil turns black and thick and bakes onto internal parts.
- **Usual grades:** most small-engine makers call for SAE30 or 10W-30. 10W-30 can replace SAE30 and works better in colder temperatures. Kohler engines need air-cooled 10W-30 (because of their hydraulic lifters).
- **Temperature matters:** in hot weather a thicker oil such as 20W-50 may be needed. In cold weather a thinner oil such as 5W-30 may be needed, especially on riding mowers used for snow work: oil that is too thick keeps parts from moving freely and makes the engine hard to start. Engine makers publish viscosity-versus-temperature charts (the Sources point to Kawasaki's); always check the owner's manual.
- **Typical capacities:** most push-mower engines take about 3/4 quart (about 3/4 to 1 quart); a V-twin about 2 quarts; large commercial "big block" engines (about 34 hp and up) about 3 quarts; one Craftsman riding mower about 2 1/2 quarts. Always look up the exact capacity for the model.
- **How often:** most small-engine makers say every 25 to 50 hours, depending on the engine and how hard it works; many service guides use every 50 hours and before storage. Change it at least once a year, and a couple of times a year for heavy use or large lawns.
- **New engines** need an early first oil change (the break-in change). Most Sources say after the first 5 hours.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** First oil change on a new engine at 5 hours ([S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-oil-in-a-push-mower-in-8-easy-steps.md), expert-blog, in its summary; also [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-simplicity-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S25](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog) vs after 5-10 hours ([S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-oil-in-a-push-mower-in-8-easy-steps.md), expert-blog, later in the same article) vs within 7-10 hours ([S26](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md), expert-blog). Safest answer: change it at 5 hours unless the owner's manual says otherwise.

## Recommendations

1. Check the oil level before every mow, with the mower on level ground and the engine off.
2. Use the grade the owner's manual names, in air-cooled engine oil. If unsure, SAE30 or 10W-30 fits most engines; adjust for very hot or very cold weather using the engine maker's chart.
3. Change the oil on schedule (25 to 50 hours, and before storage), and do the first change on a new engine early. See [Change the engine oil](../procedures/change-the-engine-oil.md).
4. Know the crankcase capacity and add oil about 1/2 quart at a time, checking the dipstick between pours; add only a little at a time when close to full.
5. If the engine smells hot from running low, top it up and then do a full oil change, since overheated oil may have thickened.

## Tips & life hacks

- If the customer is always adding oil, the engine is either leaking, burning or using oil. Look for leaking gaskets and the oil filter seal ([Mower is leaking oil](../problems/mower-is-leaking-oil.md)); a plugged air filter makes the engine burn oil; using oil points to overheating or worn valves or rings, which needs a repair shop.
- Check twice, and add only what the engine maker calls for.
- A replacement engine costs roughly $200 to $2,700 depending on make and horsepower, so regular oil changes are cheap insurance.

## Common mistakes

- Using car oil instead of air-cooled engine oil.
- Overfilling: too much oil can damage the engine and cause smoking. See [Overfilled oil](../causes/overfilled-oil.md) and [Drain excess oil](../procedures/drain-excess-oil.md).
- Using the wrong viscosity: it can burn and smoke ([Mower is smoking](../problems/mower-is-smoking.md)), run hot ([Engine overheats](../problems/engine-overheats.md)), or make a cold engine hard to start ([Engine hard to start when cold](../problems/engine-hard-to-start-when-cold.md)).
- Running on old, dirty oil until it breaks down.
