# Engine cooling times

## Summary

How a mower engine cools down after mowing, how to help it cool, and how long to wait before storing or working on it. Use it when a customer asks how long to let the mower cool, or whether it is safe to park it in the garage right after mowing. For an engine that runs too hot, go to [Engine overheats](../problems/engine-overheats.md).

Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/7-things-that-can-cause-a-lawn-mower-to-overheat.md)

## Key facts

- **There is no fixed cooling time.** It depends on whether the engine is air- or liquid-cooled, the engine's size, the age of the oil, the outside temperature and how thick the grass was. Smaller, cleaner, air-cooled engines cool faster than larger, dirtier ones. The Sources give no exact times.
- **Air cooling** (most push and riding mowers): a fan pushes air across the cooling fins. Debris clogging the fins slows cooling.
- **Liquid cooling** is less common and found on large mowers with heavier engines. It cools better but costs more and weighs more; use the proper coolant and keep it at the right level.
- **What slows cooling or causes overheating:** low or old oil, a dirty engine, debris blocking air paths in or around the block, damp or thick grass, old parts, and hot weather (an engine is more likely to overheat at 100 degrees than at 75).
- **Signs of overheating:** smoke or steam from the engine, the engine sound changing to chugging, or the mower shutting down.

Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md)

## Recommendations

1. Let the engine cool before putting the mower in a garage or storage unit. Park it outdoors if you can.
2. On a riding mower with the engine under a hood, lift the hood to expose the engine.
3. Let the engine run briefly with no load so normal cooling begins: idling spreads heat evenly through the oil and lets the cooling fan push air across the engine. About 10 to 30 seconds of idling is usually enough; idling much longer heats the oil and engine further. Lowering speed for the last few minutes of mowing also helps.
4. Wait until the engine has cooled before repairs, and check the oil only after the engine has been off a good while, to avoid burns.

## Tips & life hacks

- If the oil looks dark and grainy, it is too old: it sticks to parts and burns, adding heat. Change it and see whether the engine cools faster. See the [Engine oil guide](engine-oil-guide.md).
- Keep the outside of the engine clean. Grass and grime on top block cooling, and dried grass next to a hot engine is a fire risk.
- To clean the fins: turn off the engine and pull the spark plug wire, remove the engine screen or cover per the manual, then use a brush and/or mild soap on the cooling fins and pick out large debris. See [Clean the cooling fins](../procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins.md).
- Thick, tall or wet grass makes the engine work harder and run hotter. See [Cutting wet or tall grass](../causes/cutting-wet-or-tall-grass.md).

Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md)

## Common mistakes

- Parking a hot mower in a closed garage straight after mowing.
- Idling far too long to "cool it down": it heats the oil further.
- Blowing the fins out with compressed air or poking with long brushes: this pushes debris deeper into the engine. See [Dirty cooling fins](../causes/dirty-cooling-fins.md).
- Building homemade cooling rigs: they risk voiding the warranty. Follow the owner's manual.
- Running on low oil: see [Wrong oil or low oil](../causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil.md) and [Engine dies when hot](../problems/engine-dies-when-hot.md).
