# Electric mower care and safety

## Summary

Care and safety for electric mowers: battery (cordless) mowers and corded mowers that plug into a household outlet. Use it when a customer asks how to look after an electric mower, whether they can mow in the rain, how to store or clean it, or what dangers to watch for. For charging and storing the battery pack itself, see [Battery care and charging](../guides/battery-care-and-charging.md). For a mower that won't start, go to [Battery mower won't start](../problems/battery-mower-wont-start.md).

## Key facts

- **Two kinds.** A battery mower runs on a built-in pack charged when not in use and has no cord. A corded mower plugs into an outlet, typically a GFCI outlet (a GFCI adapter can be used if the home has none). Battery run time limits one; the cord limits where the other can go.
- **Low maintenance, not no maintenance.** Electric mowers need no oil changes or fuel, and are quieter than gas, but blades, battery, cables, springs, bolts and screws, and the cord still need checking at the start of the season and in heavy-use months.
- **Parts that get replaced regularly:** batteries, blades, battery chargers and power cords.
- **Water is the main enemy.** Mowers shed light rain, but in long rain water gets in through the motor's cooling vents and through the small gap of a hinged battery cover. It can reach the motor, wiring and battery connectors, short the mower out and stop it starting. A damaged electrical system may stop the mower turning on, or leave it running weakly with a poor cut. With enough water, wiring insulation can fail and shock the operator; smoke can be a sign of a short. See [Wet electrical components](../causes/wet-electrical-components.md).
- **Corded mowers and rain:** a standard 120 V household outlet can kill. A corded mower used in the rain risks electrocution. A battery mower is generally safer than a corded one.
- **Humidity speeds corrosion and rust.** Using the mower in humid weather is fine; storing it outside or in a humid room is not. Starting with a corroded battery may mean replacing the mower.
- **Other dangers:** fast blades; battery mowers are generally heavier than corded ones and can tip on slopes; a damaged battery can leak corrosive chemicals; a corded mower adds a trip hazard and the risk of running over the cord and exposing live wires. Debris on the lawn can be thrown, or can clog the mower so it overheats, shuts down or catches fire.
- **Corded mowers need the right extension cord.** A corded push mower needs a cord that can supply 13–15 amps; a 6–10 amp cord may stop it starting or staying on and makes both mower and cord overheat, a fire hazard.
- Robotic mowers are the exception on water: they are built to stay outside on their charging dock in any weather.
- **Run time and battery life.** A battery typically runs a mower for about an hour before it needs recharging ([S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/a-guide-to-choosing-a-cordless-lawn-mower.md)); an electric riding mower typically needs around 4 hours to recharge ([S21](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/should-i-get-an-electric-riding-mower.md)). A well-kept mower battery generally lasts about five years ([S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/a-guide-to-choosing-a-cordless-lawn-mower.md)).

## Recommendations

1. **Remove the power before touching the blade or deck.** Take out the battery pack(s) and the safety key, or unplug the cord. Wear gloves. See [Safety key missing](../causes/safety-key-missing.md).
2. **Don't mow in the rain, and avoid wet grass**, even if the manual calls the mower waterproof. Check the owner's manual: virtually every maker warns against it. Wet footing also makes slips onto a running mower more likely.
3. **Read the manual** for a new mower's safety features; safety standards differ between makers.
4. **Check the blades often.** Brush off dirt with a dry cloth; replace blades that start to rust, are chipped or cracked. If a blade is abnormally dull or makes an unusual noise when cutting, stop using the mower at once. See [Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md).
5. **Look after the battery.** Each season clean and inspect it, clean dirt off the terminals on both sides so it contacts the mower's posts, check for corrosion, and make sure it charges and holds a charge. Keep it at 20% charge or more, even in winter. Replace a charger that has had water damage. See [Bad charger](../causes/bad-charger.md) and [Battery not seated](../causes/battery-not-seated.md).
6. **Corded mowers:** inspect the power cord and extension cord before every use for cracked insulation or bare wire, and replace a damaged cord. Use a UL-listed, three-prong, outdoor-rated extension cord rated for the mower, and plug into a GFCI outlet. Don't yank cords out of the socket. Know where the cord is at all times. If the mower won't run, check that the cord is fully plugged in, that the outlet works (test it with a lamp), and the household breakers.
7. **Store it dry and cool.** A garage or basement is best. Don't store it outside (if there's no choice, keep it under a deck with a tarp or cover) or in a leaky, humid shed. Keep mower and cord dry in rainy seasons: wet cords have been known to cause fires.
8. **Clean it safely.** Unplug it or remove the battery and attachments first. Wait until the motor and battery have cooled. Wipe grass-covered areas with warm soapy water, keep water away from the motor and wiring, brush clumps from under the deck, and let it dry completely before refitting the battery. See [Cleaning the mower](../guides/cleaning-the-mower.md).
9. **Dress for it:** eye protection, proper shoes and long pants. Clear the lawn of debris first. On slopes, mow across, not up and down. Children and young adults need a walk-through and training before using any mower.
- If the mower has two separate batteries, charge both before mowing. Don't leave a lithium-ion battery on the charger all the time, and don't run it very low: both wear it out faster ([S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/a-guide-to-choosing-a-cordless-lawn-mower.md)).

## Tips & life hacks

- Charge the battery an hour or two before mowing so it starts full.
- Empty the bag and clear clippings from under the deck after every use; wet clippings left under the deck cause rust and mold. See [Clogged motor vents](../causes/clogged-motor-vents.md) for keeping the motor's air flowing.
- If you must mow damp grass: raise the deck, use the side chute instead of the bag or mulcher, take narrower passes, clear under the deck between passes, and keep a spare battery or recharge halfway, since wet mowing drains the battery faster.
- Replacing blades about every six months even if they look fine is one author's general advice.
- On some electric-start gas push mowers the charger plugs into a port on the handle near the battery.
- Self-repair can void an electric mower's warranty; if nothing obvious is found, an authorized service center is the next step.

## Common mistakes

- Mowing in the rain, or leaving the mower out in it. See [Mower got wet](../guides/mower-got-wet.md).
- Using a light 6–10 amp extension cord on a corded mower.
- Running over the cord, or pulling it from the socket by the cord.
- Hosing down the motor, or washing the mower while it is still hot.
- Working on the blade with the battery or safety key still in.
- Storing the mower outside or in a damp shed.
- Cleaning a battery-powered mower with a pressure washer or garden hose. Use air and a moist cloth instead ([S21](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/should-i-get-an-electric-riding-mower.md)).
