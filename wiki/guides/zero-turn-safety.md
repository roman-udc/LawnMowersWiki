# Zero-turn safety

## Summary

How to operate and service a zero-turn mower safely: slopes, water, the safety interlock system, the rollover protection system (ROPS) and seat belt, and who should not be near or on the mower. Use it when a customer asks whether their zero-turn can handle a hill, asks about bypassing a safety switch, or is about to work on the mower.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/dangers-of-zero-turn-mowers-how-to-operate-safely.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/zero-turn-lawn-mowers-are-they-good-on-hills.md)

## Key facts

- **Zero-turns are dangerous.** They are engine powered, move fast, turn quickly, and can roll over on hills, uneven ground, near water and near retaining walls. Their compact size doesn't make them safer than larger machines. Riding-mower accidents (falling off, falling under, being run over, rollovers on steep slopes or near water) injure tens of thousands and kill dozens of people a year in the U.S., per government figures cited by the Source.
- **They are not good on hills.** There isn't much weight on the front, and the drive can't keep one side driving properly when weight shifts to the side. Because a zero-turn steers and stops with its drive wheels, on wet grass going downhill you may be unable to steer or stop.
- **Safety features every mower must have:** an operator presence control (seat safety switch) that stops the blades when you leave the seat; ROPS (the bar over the operator); a seat belt; wheel weights or counterweights where the maker calls for them (often needed with a rear bagger); and a high seat back.
- **The interlock system:** the engine should not start unless the parking brake is set, the PTO (blade switch) is off and the steering levers are in neutral, and the engine should shut down if the blades are engaged and the operator leaves the seat. See [Bad safety switch](../causes/bad-safety-switch.md).
- **ROPS and seat belt work together.** Wear the seat belt whenever ROPS is up. If ROPS is folded or removed, do not wear the seat belt.
- **A zero-turn won't roll freely** without opening the transmission bypass, because the hydrostatic drive holds it. See [Bypass valve open](../causes/bypass-valve-open.md).
- Gas mower noise is about 90 decibels, a level at which hearing protection is recommended.
- Child age guideline cited by the Source (American Academy of Pediatrics): at least 12 for a walk-behind mower and at least 16 for a riding mower, with the parent judging maturity.
- **Slopes over 15 degrees are dangerous** on a zero-turn: the chance of sliding or rolling over goes up, so cut those areas with a push mower or trimmer ([S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/best-zero-turn-mower-tires-for-hills.md), [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/choosing-between-a-residential-commercial-zero-turn-mower.md)). Keep 5 feet away from water, retaining walls, drop-offs and other unlevel ground ([S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/choosing-between-a-residential-commercial-zero-turn-mower.md)).

## Recommendations

1. **Slopes of 15 degrees or less only.** Steeper ground needs another method: a string trimmer for small banks, a walk-behind mower for larger ones. Drive up and down slopes, not across.
2. **Stay away from wet grass on slopes.**
3. **Keep at least 5 feet from water, embankments, retaining walls, ditches and sudden drops.** Use a trimmer or push mower there.
4. **Keep steady speeds.** Don't change direction or speed suddenly.
5. **Keep children, pets and other people out of the area**, and stop the mower if anyone comes in. Never carry a passenger, and never mow with a child on your lap. Don't discharge toward walls or obstacles, where thrown objects can ricochet.
6. **Wear the seat belt with ROPS up.** If you fold ROPS down to pass under a low branch, put it back up as soon as you are clear.
7. **Disengage the blades** whenever you're not cutting, and every time before getting off to clear an obstruction.
8. **Before leaving the mower:** blades off, parking brake on, engine off, key out.
9. **Wear** safety glasses, hearing protection and closed-toe shoes (no sandals). Mow in daylight only.
10. **Never bypass the interlock** except briefly while troubleshooting, and never run the mower with a safety feature not working. Check the interlock, ROPS and seat belt at every service. See [Replace the seat switch](../procedures/replace-the-seat-switch.md) and [Start a zero-turn mower](../procedures/start-a-zero-turn-mower.md).
11. **Before any repair:** park on flat, level ground, remove the key and pull the spark plug wires.
- Don't mow a hill after rain or in the early morning with dew on the ground: damp grass can make the tires slip ([S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/best-zero-turn-mower-tires-for-hills.md)).

## Tips & life hacks

- Pre-mow checklist: pick up sticks, rocks and toys; clear people and pets; ROPS up; seat belt working; blades not cracked or damaged (a broken piece can be thrown from the deck).
- On gentle slopes, wide turf tires hold better, and slightly lower tire pressure can help; too much air makes the tires spin and slide downhill, even in reverse. One author runs about 10–12 psi for gradual hills (his practice, not a spec; follow the tire and mower maker's pressure).
- Don't climb with the levers all the way forward: ease the ground speed back and keep the throttle between three-quarters and full, so the hydraulic pump keeps pressure and the engine stays cool.
- Keep the mower clean and stored dry to prevent corrosion.

## Common mistakes

- Mowing a slope steeper than 15 degrees, or any slope when the grass is wet.
- Mowing right up to a pond, ditch or retaining wall.
- Wearing the seat belt with ROPS folded down, or leaving ROPS folded after passing an obstacle.
- Disabling the seat switch or other interlock and leaving it that way. One reported death involved an operator thrown off and run over with the blades still engaged, the operator presence control possibly disabled.
- Letting a child ride along.
