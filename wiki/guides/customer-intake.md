# Customer intake

## Summary

The basic questions to ask a customer before diagnosing any Problem or recommending a part. Ask them once, at the start of the conversation. Problem pages assume these answers are known and only ask the questions that separate one Cause from another.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-measure-a-mower-blade.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/replace-exmark-mower-deck-parts.md)

## Key facts

Ask these questions. Each one changes what you look up or recommend.

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What brand is the mower? | Narrows which Brand notes apply and which parts catalog to search. |
| What is the mower's model number? | The model number is the easiest way to find the right replacement part. Parts such as cutter housings and spindles vary between a brand's different mower decks. |
| What is the serial number? | Together with the model number, the serial number pins down part fitment: dealers ask for both to get the correct part the first time. It also encodes the build date, and a dealer can look up warranty coverage and recall notices by serial number. |
| What engine is it (engine make, and the engine model and type/spec number)? | Engine parts are often listed separately from the frame and deck parts and are looked up by the engine model number. Don't confuse the engine's numbers with the mower's model and serial numbers. |
| What kind of mower is it: push, self-propelled, riding or zero-turn? And is it gas, battery or diesel? | Mower type and Power source decide which Problems, Causes and procedures apply. Riding mowers, for example, have parts (fuel solenoid, starter solenoid, seat switch) that push mowers don't. |
| How old is the mower? | Age helps decide what is worth fixing. Larger machines see more wear over time, and age plays a role in expected reliability. If the customer doesn't know, it can often be decoded from the serial number. |
| When did it last run, and has it been stored since? | A mower that sat for a month or more with gas in the tank points toward [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md) and a varnished carburetor. |

Where to find the numbers:

- **Walk-behind (push and self-propelled) mowers:** on the top or side of the deck, near the rear wheels, or on the engine housing.
- **Riding mowers and lawn tractors:** on a tag under the seat, on the frame beneath the seat platform, near the rear wheels or rear frame, on the deck, or near the engine or battery tray. Model and serial number usually appear together on the same tag.
- **Engine:** the engine has its own model and type/spec numbers, stamped or labeled on the engine itself. Some riding mowers list part numbers for engine maintenance parts under the hood.

## Recommendations

1. Ask for the brand, model number and serial number first, before discussing any part.
2. Ask for the engine make and the engine model and type/spec numbers whenever the fix might need an engine part (spark plug, filters, carburetor, gaskets, ignition coil).
3. Confirm the Mower type and Power source, then go to the matching Problem page in the index.
4. Ask how old the mower is and when it last ran. Use the answers to rank the likely Causes and to judge whether a repair is worth it.
5. If the model tag is missing or unreadable, say so plainly. Some parts, such as blades, can still be matched by measuring the old part.

## Tips & life hacks

- Ask the customer to send a photo of the model/serial tag and of the engine label. It avoids misread digits.
- If the customer doesn't know the mower's age, the build date is often encoded in the serial number. The format varies by brand and model line. On some Craftsman tractors, for example, it depends on the first three digits of the model number.
- If the serial number doesn't decode cleanly, the engine's manufacture date is a good estimate, since the engine is usually built shortly before final assembly.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-find-the-age-of-your-craftsman-riding-lawn-mower-or-lawn-tractor.md)

## Common mistakes

- Giving the engine's model number as the mower's model number, or the other way round. They are different numbers on different labels, and parts are looked up by different ones.
- Recommending a deck part from the model number alone, without the serial number, when the part varies between decks.
- Skipping "when did it last run". A mower stored with gas in the tank often has [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md), a common reason it won't start.
