# Start a mower with old gas

Fixes: [Old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md).

Old gas has to be drained and replaced; it won't burn well and it gums up the fuel system. After that, check the fuel filter and fuel line for restrictions. The carburetor is the most likely part to be damaged by old fuel.

Sources: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md)

## Before you start

- How long has the gas been in the mower? Does the engine start and then quit? A mower that starts for under five seconds and then stalls likely has a carburetor that needs cleaning; one that starts and stalls after about 30 seconds likely has bad or water-saturated fuel.
- Is it a diesel or 2-cycle engine? Don't use the carburetor-cleaner starting trick (step 5) on those.

Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md)

## Safety

- Wear safety glasses and work in a well-ventilated area.
- Remove the spark plug wire before you work near the intake.
- Carburetor cleaner is very flammable.
- Dispose of old gas at a recycling facility.

Sources: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md)

## Tools & parts

- Fuel siphon pump and an approved fuel container
- Fresh gas and a fuel additive (the expert Source uses Sea Foam Motor Treatment, mixed per the bottle)
- Fuel pinch-off pliers, carburetor cleaner, compressed air (for the fuel line check)
- New fuel filter or fuel line if needed
- Screwdriver or Torx driver to remove the air filter housing

Sources: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md)

## Steps

1. Siphon the old gas out of the tank into a fuel container.
2. Refill with fresh gas plus a fuel additive or stabilizer. Start the engine and let it run until it's no longer sluggish. If it won't start or stay running, go on.
3. Check the fuel filter for a clog and replace it. Install the new filter inline between the fuel lines, with the arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow. See [Replace the fuel filter](replace-the-fuel-filter.md).
4. Check the fuel line flow. Stop the fuel (shut-off valve near the bottom of the tank, under the seat on some zero-turns, or pinch pliers), put the end furthest from the tank into a container lower than the tank, and restart the flow. Poor flow means a clog: clear it with carburetor cleaner and compressed air, or replace the line with one of the same length and diameter. See [Replace the fuel line](replace-the-fuel-line.md).
5. To get a hard-starting engine going in the meantime, spray carburetor cleaner into the intake: remove the air filter housing and filter, check the housing for water and debris and clean it, then spray carburetor cleaner into the intake port under the filter (or into the spark plug port) for only 2 to 3 seconds. Reassemble, set the throttle to the midpoint if you can, set full choke, press the primer bulb a few times if there is one, reattach the plug wire and start. If it starts, shut it off and refit the filter and housing. If not after a few pulls, repeat once; then call a professional.
6. If the engine still runs poorly, the carburetor needs cleaning, rebuilding or replacement: old fuel clogs the jet and freezes small parts. See [Clean the carburetor](clean-the-carburetor.md).

## Tips & common mistakes

- Use carburetor cleaner, not starter fluid. Starter fluid is very dry with no lubrication value, and too much or too often can damage the cylinder. If you must use starter fluid, spray it on a rag and hold the rag to the intake. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md)
- Too much carburetor cleaner can damage the engine: 2 to 3 seconds is enough. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md)
- If the mower needs carburetor cleaner to start every time, take it to a professional; it's an emergency measure, not a fix. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md)
- If the carburetor sits higher than the tank, the mower has a fuel pump: check for steady or pulsating flow out of the carburetor line, and replace the pump if there's none. Source: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md)
- A plugged gas cap vent can mimic old fuel: if the engine runs with the cap off but quits shortly after you put it back on, replace the cap. Source: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md)

- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Fuel storage and stabilizers](../guides/fuel-storage-and-stabilizers.md).

## Verify the fix

The engine starts and keeps running on fresh fuel without carburetor cleaner, and it's no longer sluggish.
