# Level the deck

Fixes: [Unlevel deck](../causes/unlevel-deck.md). Step 1 also corrects [Wrong tire pressure](../causes/wrong-tire-pressure.md), which can mimic an unlevel deck. Also used after [Wrong cutting height](../causes/wrong-cutting-height.md) is corrected if the deck still sits unlevel.

## Before you start

- Find the owner's or operator's manual for your deck model. Leveling specs and adjustment points vary by manufacturer and model; the steps below are the common pattern and the brand examples the Sources give.
- Set all tire pressures to spec, equal left and right. Uneven tire pressure can mimic an uneven deck.
- Some zero-turn decks are hard to level: a trained dealer can take up to two hours, and one Source does not recommend it for the end user. Consider a dealer if the adjusters are hard to reach.

## Safety

> **⚠ Safety:** Park on a level surface, set the parking brake, disengage the blades, shut off the engine and remove the key before measuring. Wear work gloves: you will be handling the blade tips.

Sources: [S18](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-level-a-riding-lawn-mower-deck-for-an-even-cut-video.md)

## Tools & parts

- Wrench set (two wrenches to hold the adjustment nut while tightening the jam nut)
- Ruler or tape measure
- Tire pressure gauge and compressed air supply
- Work gloves
- Owner's manual for the deck spec

No parts are normally needed: leveling adjusts the existing deck hangers, lift links, leveling rods or linkages and stabilizer bracket.

## Steps

1. Park on a flat, level, hard surface and inflate all tires to the recommended pressure, equal left and right.
2. Set the deck height. Sources differ on the position (see the conflict below); use the one your manual gives.
3. **Measure side to side.** Turn the blades so they point side to side and measure from the ground to the outside blade tip on the right, then on the left. (Some Sources measure from the ground to the bottom of the deck at all four corners instead.) The manufacturer Source says the two sides should be within 1/8 inch.
4. **Adjust side to side** at the adjuster your deck uses, then re-measure and repeat until level:
   - Lift link adjustment nut (riding mower): clockwise raises the left side, counterclockwise lowers it; one full turn is about 3/16 inch.
   - Left adjustment rod under the rear fenders (some riding mowers): loosen the upper nut (don't remove it), turn the lower nut clockwise to raise the left side or counterclockwise to lower it, then tighten the upper nut.
   - Zero-turn adjustment bolt (one on each side of the deck): loosen the jam nut, move the bolt up or down until the readings match, retighten the jam nut.
5. **Measure front to rear (pitch or rake).** Turn one blade so it points front to back (on the right side, the blade nearest the discharge chute) and measure from the ground to the front tip and to the rear tip. On most decks the front should sit slightly lower than the rear; some models want it even. See the conflict below for the numbers.
6. **Adjust the pitch** at the front link or stabilizer bracket: loosen the jam nut (or outer nut), turn the adjustment nut (or inner or lock nut) to raise or lower the front, re-measure and repeat. On the manufacturer Source's mower, one full turn of the front link adjustment nut is 1/8 inch.
7. Hold the adjustment nut with one wrench and tighten the jam nut with a second wrench.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** front-to-rear spec. Front 1/8 to 1/2 inch lower than the rear ([S18](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-level-a-riding-lawn-mower-deck-for-an-even-cut-video.md), manufacturer; [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-husqvarna-riding-mower.md), expert-blog) vs front 1/4 to 3/8 inch lower on riding mowers and lawn tractors ([S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-craftsman-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-cub-cadet-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-simplicity-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-lawn-tractor.md), [S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-your-troy-bilt-lawn-mower-is-cutting-uneven-fixed.md), expert-blog) vs 1/16 to 1/4 inch on zero-turns ([S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-your-troy-bilt-lawn-mower-is-cutting-uneven-fixed.md), expert-blog). ## Brand notes

- Craftsman, Cub Cadet and Simplicity riding mowers: side to side at the left adjustment rod under the rear fenders; pitch at the stabilizer bracket (loosen the outer nut, tighten the inner nut to raise the front, loosen it to lower), front 1/4 to 3/8 inch lower than the rear.
- Husqvarna riding mowers: deck in its highest position; side to side at the lift link adjustment nut on the side of the mower (turn left to lower, right to raise); pitch at the front link (loosen the jam nut, turn the adjustment nut left to lower the front, right to raise), front 1/8 to 1/2 inch lower.
- Troy-Bilt riding mowers: side to side by loosening (not removing) the hex cap screw on the left deck hanger bracket and turning the adjustment gear behind it (clockwise raises); pitch at the stabilizer bracket, front 1/4 to 3/8 inch lower. Troy-Bilt zero-turns: jam nut and adjustment bolt on each side; pitch at the lock nut and jam nut on the front hanger bracket (may be behind a cap), front 1/16 to 1/4 inch lower.
- John Deere zero-turns, Snapper, Toro, Kubota and Ariens: level side to side with the front slightly lower than the rear; exact spec in the operator's manual.
- Exmark and Cub Cadet: a trained dealer can take up to two hours; one Exmark deck takes a factory-trained mechanic 1.5 to 2 hours and is not recommended for the end user.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Don't skip the tires: uneven pressure can mimic an uneven deck.
- Loosen jam nuts and lock nuts; don't remove them.
- Recheck after every adjustment and repeat until the readings are in spec.

## Verify the fix

Re-measure: the sides should match (within 1/8 inch per the manufacturer Source) and the front should sit lower than the rear by your manual's spec. A level deck distributes the mower's weight evenly and reduces pulling to one side.
