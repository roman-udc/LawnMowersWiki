# Clean the battery terminals

Fixes: [Loose or corroded battery cables](../causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables.md).

Corrosion (a white or green buildup) on the battery terminals and cable ends breaks continuity, so the starter doesn't get the power it needs. Disconnect the battery, scrub the terminals and cable ends clean with a wire brush and baking soda, then reconnect and protect them.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-wont-turn-over.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-clicks-but-doesnt-turn-over-video.md)

## Before you start

- Is the battery charged? One Source cleans the cables once the battery has checked good; see [Test the battery](test-the-battery.md).
- Where is the battery? On a riding mower it is usually under the hood or seat; you may need a screwdriver to uncover it.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-clicks-but-doesnt-turn-over-video.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md)

## Safety

- Wear safety glasses and gloves (rubber-coated gloves in one Source).
- Remove the negative cable first to break the circuit. If the circuit isn't broken and the wrench touches the frame or other metal, the battery can arc and throw sparks that could blow it up.
- The positive (red) cable is the last one off and the first one on.

## Tools & parts

- A wrench or screwdriver for the terminal clamps.
- A wire brush (best for heavy corrosion); a toothbrush or cotton swabs for light dirt and corrosion.
- Baking soda and water, or a store-bought battery terminal cleaner. Cola soda also removes corrosion.
- A shop rag or clean cloths.
- Dielectric grease or petroleum jelly to protect the terminals afterwards.
- Replacement terminals or cables if they are too corroded or damaged to clean.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-clicks-but-doesnt-turn-over-video.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start-just-clicks-solved.md)

## Steps

1. Turn the ignition off, put on gloves and safety glasses, and get to the battery.
2. Loosen the negative clamp and remove the negative cable from the battery, then remove the positive (red) cable.
3. Scrub the battery terminals and cable ends with the wire brush.
4. If brushing doesn't remove it all, apply baking soda and water to the corrosion. It foams or bubbles as it dissolves the corrosion. When it stops bubbling, brush off what's left with a toothbrush.
5. Rinse or wipe the terminals and cable ends clean (rinsing is messy, so do it outdoors), then dry them with a clean cloth.
6. Replace any terminals or cables too corroded or damaged to clean, and any cables that keep coming loose or are broken.
7. Reconnect the positive (red) cable first, then the negative, and tighten both clamps securely.
8. Coat the terminals thinly with dielectric grease or petroleum jelly to slow new corrosion.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice (cleaning mix):** 2 cups water to 3 heaping tablespoons baking soda ([S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-wont-turn-over.md), expert-blog; [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start-just-clicks-solved.md), expert-blog) vs a paste of 4 cups water with about six rounded tablespoons ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), expert-blog) vs one part baking soda to one part water ([S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md), expert-blog) vs sprinkle dry baking soda on the terminals and moisten it ([S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-clicks-but-doesnt-turn-over-video.md), manufacturer).

## Tips & common mistakes

- Clean the terminals before storing the battery for winter and again before using it in spring; dirt and moisture make terminals corrode faster, especially in cold, wet months.
- For winter storage, one Source cleans the terminals until the metal is bright, greases them and leaves the cables disconnected so they don't touch the terminals.
- Vibration shakes cables loose, so check they are tight whenever you clean them.

## Verify the fix

- The engine should crank. If it still won't, measure voltage on the red post of the starter solenoid (red probe) against the negative battery terminal (black probe): it should read more than 12 volts. If it doesn't, replace the red battery cable. Don't let the probe touch both solenoid posts at once. Next suspect: [Test the starter solenoid](test-the-starter-solenoid.md).
