# Change the engine oil

Fixes: [Wrong oil or low oil](../causes/wrong-oil-or-low-oil.md).

Old oil gets dirty with particles that abrade engine parts, and skipping oil changes causes excessive wear and can cause significant engine damage. The job is a drain and refill, plus a new filter on engines that have one. The Sources call it easier than you think.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-change-the-oil-in-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-oil-in-a-push-mower-in-8-easy-steps.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-is-smoking.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md)

## Before you start

- **Is it due?** Change it as often as the owner's manual says, and at least once a year. Also change it if it's dirty, if it looks milky white (water got in), and before winter storage so the engine has fresh oil in spring.
- **Is it a new mower or a new engine?** The first change comes early, after the break-in, to remove machining particles.
- **What engine is it?** The make is usually on a sticker on top of the engine. The engine maker's manual gives the oil type and the crankcase capacity.
- **Push mower:** does the engine have a drain plug? Many don't; then you tip the mower or use an extractor. Some have a plug at the bottom of the dipstick tube. Push mowers have no oil filter, so their oil gets dirty faster.
- **Riding mower or zero-turn:** does it have a drain plug, a drain valve, or a drain port with a hose? Does it have an oil filter?

## Safety

- Warm the engine in a well-ventilated area. The engine and oil are hot afterwards: don't touch the hot engine.
- Park on a level surface, set the parking brake and remove the ignition key.
- Disconnect the spark plug wire (all of them on a multi-cylinder engine) so the engine can't start while you work or rotate the blade. Don't skip this, and keep it disconnected until you've finished all your maintenance.
- When you tip a push mower, keep the carburetor on the high side so oil doesn't run into the carburetor and air filter.
- Recycle used oil: never pour it down a drain, on the ground or in the trash. Store it in the original or another sealed oil container (not soda bottles or milk jugs) and take it to a recycling center, curbside program or an auto parts store that accepts it.

## Tools & parts

- Oil drain pan (or a container with a spout or cover), plus a closed container for carrying the used oil
- Socket wrench, spanner or allen wrench to fit the drain plug (square or hex, in varying sizes)
- Oil filter wrench or strap wrench (engines with a filter)
- Plastic funnel, rags or paper towels, rubber or work gloves
- Cardboard or newspaper to protect the work surface
- Drain hose, tube or sleeve for engines with a drain valve or port (many riding mowers come with one)
- Optional: an oil extractor or evacuator pump, which pulls the oil out through the fill tube
- Fresh air-cooled engine oil of the type and amount in the owner's manual
- New oil filter, if the engine has one

## Steps

1. Lay cardboard or newspaper on a flat, level surface. Run the engine 2 to 3 minutes to warm the oil (warm oil flows better), park on the cover and shut it off.
2. Remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire or wires.
3. Clean around the oil fill, the drain plug and the filter, so dirt can't fall into the crankcase.
4. Drain the oil. Remove the fill cap or dipstick to vent the engine so it drains faster.
   - **Push mower, no drain plug:** set the pan beside the mower, remove the fill cap, and gently tip the mower on its side with the carburetor on the high side so the fill hole lines up with the pan.
   - **Push mower with a drain plug:** tip it the same way (or back on its handle), scrape deck debris from around the square or hex plug on the bottom of the engine, and unscrew it counterclockwise into the pan. Reinstall it clockwise when the oil stops, and set the mower upright.
   - **Riding mower or zero-turn with a drain plug:** the plug is on the side of the engine near the bottom, under the hood (an older riding mower may need a side panel off). Put the pan under it, remove it, and reinstall it once fully drained. A drain sleeve around the plug guides the oil into the pan.
   - **Drain valve or port with a hose:** uncap the valve, attach the drain hose or tube with its other end in the pan, push the valve in and turn it counterclockwise to open. When it stops draining, close the valve, remove the hose and refit the cap.
   - **Extractor:** pump the oil out through the fill tube without tipping the mower.
5. **Oil filter (if fitted):** have a rag ready, turn the old filter counterclockwise with the filter wrench and let its oil drain into the pan. Wipe a light film of fresh oil on the new filter's seal, then spin it on by hand.
6. Refill through the fill hole or dipstick tube with a funnel, with the oil type in the manual. Stop short of the full amount (some oil stays in the engine after draining), wait a minute for it to settle, then check the dipstick. Add a little at a time and recheck until it reads at the top of the full mark. Don't overfill; if you do, remove a little.
7. Wipe up any spilled oil, so it doesn't smoke as it burns off a hot engine.
8. Reconnect the spark plug wire(s), run the engine a few minutes, shut it off, let the oil settle and cool, recheck the level and top up if needed.

## Brand notes

- **Oil type:** most push mowers use SAE30 or 10W-30 (about 3/4 to 1 quart of SAE30). Kohler engines need air-cooled 10W-30, because some internal parts don't take SAE blend well. Most gas engines in Craftsman mowers use 10W-30 air-cooled oil. ## Tips & common mistakes

- **Don't overfill.** Know the capacity, add about 1/2 quart less than it (or 1/2 quart at a time), then add a little at a time, checking the dipstick until it reaches the mark. Too much or too little oil can damage the engine and cause running problems. Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-husqvarna-riding-mower.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/a-guide-to-the-craftsman-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S27](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-gravely-zero-turn-mower.md), [S30](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-tune-up-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md), [S33](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-bad-boy.md), [S34](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-john-deere.md), [S35](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-kubota-mower.md)
- **Drain plug vs extractor:** the plug is faster and cheaper and may get more oil out, but it's messy, you have to tip the mower and you can damage the plug or oil pan. An extractor ($25–$80) is cleaner with no tipping, but slower and may not get all the oil out. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-oil-in-a-push-mower-in-8-easy-steps.md)
- A drain plug can look like a large bolt head hidden under dirt and grass. A drain hose is cleaner than a plug, and one can be added to many engines: ask the engine dealer. Sources: [S21](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-draining-oil-from-your-lawn-mower.md), [S22](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-lawn-tractor.md)
- While a push mower is on its side, scrape the deck and inspect the blades. Good annual-service companions: a fuel filter and an air filter check. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-oil-in-a-push-mower-in-8-easy-steps.md)
- Oil filters: use the OEM filter from the owner's manual, or an aftermarket one through a cross-reference website. Source: [S25](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/what-if-you-dont-change-oil-in-your-mower.md)

- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Engine oil guide](../guides/engine-oil-guide.md).

## Verify the fix

- After the run and cool-down, the dipstick reads at the full mark: not lower, not higher.
- No drips at the drain plug, valve or filter, and no spilled oil left on the engine.
- Check the level again before each use.
