# Test the battery

Fixes: [Weak or dead battery](../causes/weak-or-dead-battery.md), [Faulty charging system](../causes/faulty-charging-system.md), [Bad alternator](../causes/bad-alternator.md) (running-voltage check). Also rules out [Loose or corroded battery cables](../causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables.md) and is the first check before blaming a [Bad starter motor](../causes/bad-starter-motor.md).

A quick voltage reading tells you whether the battery is charged, needs a charge, or is too weak to crank the engine. Most riding and zero-turn mower batteries are 12 volts. A second reading with the engine running checks the charging system.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-husqvarna-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-battery-keeps-dying.md)

## Before you start

- Is the battery 12 volts? Most mower batteries are; check the battery label or owner's manual.
- Can you reach the terminals? The battery sits under the hood or under the seat.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-husqvarna-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md)

## Safety

- Turn the ignition off before you test, and wear work gloves and goggles.
- Keep the meter probes on the battery terminals only, red to positive (+) and black to negative (-).

Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-battery-keeps-dying.md)

## Tools & parts

- Multimeter or voltmeter set to DC volts
- Work gloves and goggles

Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md)

## Steps

1. Turn the ignition off and reach the battery.
2. Set the meter to DC volts (the 12 V range if it has one).
3. Touch the red probe to the positive (+) terminal and the black probe to the negative (-) terminal.
4. Read the voltage with the engine off:
   - about 12.7 V: fully charged
   - 12.4 to 12.8 V: normal resting reading
   - about 11.5 V: almost dead
5. If the reading is low, charge the battery (see [Charge the battery](charge-the-battery.md)) and test again.
6. To check the charging system, start the engine, run it at about 3/4 throttle and read the terminals again. It should read 13.2 to 13.9 V or higher. A lower reading points to the charging system.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** the threshold for "charged" differs. Most Sources say a full 12 V battery reads about 12.7 V and anything less should be charged (for example [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-zero-turn-wont-start.md), expert-blog); the service guides say 12.6 V or more is 100% charged ([S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog); the manufacturer video only says over 12 V is good ([S5](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md), manufacturer); the starter-solenoid tests ask for at least 12.3 V before testing ([S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bad-kawasaki-starter-solenoid.md), expert-blog).

## Tips & common mistakes

- A battery that won't hold a charge, or won't charge above 12 V after an hour or two on the charger, needs replacing. Sources: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-lawn-mower-battery-keeps-dying.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-zero-turn-mower-problems.md), [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/murray-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)
- While you're at the battery, make sure the cables are tight, and clean or replace corroded terminals. The Husqvarna guide cleans them with a wire brush in a bath of 3 cups water and 2 heaping teaspoons baking soda, then adds dielectric grease. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-husqvarna-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md)

## Verify the fix

A charged battery reads about 12.6–12.7 V with the engine off, and 13.2–13.9 V or more with the engine running at 3/4 throttle. If it reads well but the engine still won't crank, test the starter solenoid next (see [Test the starter solenoid](test-the-starter-solenoid.md)).
