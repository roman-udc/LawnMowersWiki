# Replace the flywheel key

Fixes: [Sheared flywheel key](../causes/sheared-flywheel-key.md).

The flywheel key is a small rectangular metal piece that keeps the crankshaft and flywheel aligned when the flywheel nut is tightened. It shears when the blade hits something hard enough to knock the flywheel out of alignment, which protects more expensive engine parts. A broken or sheared key must be replaced.

## Before you start

- Is it a riding mower or a push mower? The detailed steps below are for a riding mower engine with a blower housing. On a push mower, the key sits under the nut on top of the crankshaft.
- Do you have a flywheel puller and a torque wrench? Both are needed on a riding mower engine.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/honda-mower-is-vibrating.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-is-vibrating.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)

## Safety

- Repairs to power equipment can be hazardous. Use the proper tools and safety gear, and don't go ahead unless you're confident; some repairs should be done by a qualified technician.
- Park on a level surface, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Wear work gloves.
- Disconnect the battery's negative cable and the spark plug wire before you work on the flywheel.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)

## Tools & parts

- Wrench set and socket wrench set
- Flywheel puller
- Torque wrench
- Slot screwdriver, hammer or mallet
- Nylon rope to lock the piston (or an impact wrench instead)
- Work gloves
- New flywheel key (manufacturer-approved part)

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)

## Steps

1. Disconnect the battery: park on a level surface, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Lift the seat, remove the bolt on the negative cable and tuck the cable away from the post.
2. Remove the engine blower housing: lift the hood (or remove it for more room), remove the air duct screws and the air duct, turn the air filter cover knobs counterclockwise and take off the cover and filter, remove the air filter housing screw, then remove the front and rear blower housing bolts and lift the housing off.
3. Remove the flywheel screen cap. Disconnect the spark plug wire and remove the plug. Feed nylon rope into the spark plug hole to jam the piston so the crankshaft can't turn, leaving some rope out so you can pull it back. (An impact wrench avoids the rope.) Remove the flywheel bolt, washer and screen, and pull off the flywheel fan.
4. Loosen the flywheel: thread the flywheel bolt back into the crankshaft, set the flywheel puller above it and thread the puller bolts into the holes in the flywheel. Tighten them a little at a time, alternating, so the flywheel rises evenly until it breaks free. If it won't, tap the top of the flywheel with a mallet.
5. Remove the puller and bolt, pull the flywheel off the crankshaft, and clean the remains of the old key out of the crankshaft and flywheel.
6. Push the flywheel onto the crankshaft with its notch lined up with the crankshaft slot. Insert the new key into the slot and push it flush with the top of the crankshaft.
7. Reinstall the fan, screen and washer, thread the flywheel bolt in by hand, then torque it to 100 ft-lbs. Pull out the rope, reinstall the spark plug and wire, and refit the screen cap.
8. Reinstall the blower housing (line up the air diverter), its bolts, the air filter housing screw, the filter and cover (knobs clockwise) and the air duct. Lower the hood.
9. Reconnect the negative battery cable and lower the seat.

On a push mower, the Sources give a shorter version: remove the nut on top of the crankshaft, inspect the flywheel and the key in the keyway, and replace the key if it's damaged.

## Brand notes

- **Briggs & Stratton engines** in Craftsman, Husqvarna, Toro, MTD, Murray, Cub Cadet, Troybilt and Ariens riding mowers and lawn tractors: the riding mower steps above apply. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- **Honda push mowers:** remove the nut on top of the crankshaft and check the key in the keyway. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/honda-mower-is-vibrating.md)

## Tips & common mistakes

- Raise the flywheel evenly by alternating the puller bolts; don't crank one side all the way. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- Leave a tail of rope outside the spark plug hole so you can pull it out before starting. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)

## Verify the fix

The engine should start and run normally once the new key restores the timing. A mower that vibrated because of a damaged key should run smoothly.
