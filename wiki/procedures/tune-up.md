# Tune up the engine

Fixes: [Fouled spark plug](../causes/fouled-spark-plug.md), [Clogged air filter](../causes/clogged-air-filter.md), [Clogged fuel filter](../causes/clogged-fuel-filter.md).

A tune-up is routine engine maintenance: change the engine oil and oil filter, and replace the air filter, spark plug and fuel filter. Then check the ignition, carburetor, battery, throttle and choke controls, and clean the engine cooling fins. Do it at least once a year. An engine that misses its tune-ups runs poorly: it can run rough or misfire, stall when driving, make small engine noises, and cut unevenly. A full annual service adds the deck, blades, belts, tires and lubrication on top of the tune-up.

## Before you start

- When was the last tune-up or service? Most Sources say once a year, or every 50 or 100 operating hours, whichever comes first (see the conflict below).
- Gather the parts first. Many riding mowers have a sticker under the hood that lists the tune-up part numbers. Confirm the correct spark plug for the engine.
- Does the engine have two cylinders? Then it takes two spark plugs and has two plug wires.
- Is it a diesel? Diesels have no spark plugs.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice on the service interval:** every 50 hours or once a year ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-craftsman-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-push-lawn-mower.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-cub-cadet-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-husqvarna-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog) vs every 100 hours or once a year for riding mowers and zero-turns in general ([S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog). The Husqvarna Source uses 100 hours only for commercial engines. Follow the owner's manual.

## Safety

- Park on a flat, level surface in a well-ventilated area, set the parking brake (chock the wheels if you like) and remove the ignition key.
- Disconnect the spark plug wire or wires so the engine can't start, and leave them off until the tune-up is finished.
- The engine, oil and transmission may still be hot after warming up.
- Wear rubber gloves for oil work, heavy work gloves near blades, and safety glasses.
- Use jack stands or similar lock-in-place supports if you raise the mower.

## Tools & parts

- Engine oil and a new oil filter (SAE30 or 10W-30 on the push mower in one Source; use the owner's manual spec)
- Air filter (inner and outer, or the foam pre-filter and paper filter, where the engine has both)
- Spark plug (one per cylinder)
- Inline fuel filter
- Socket or spanner wrenches, spark plug wrench, gap tool
- Oil filter wrench (a strap wrench), oil drain pan or a container with a spout and cover, funnel, a drain tube if the mower has a drain valve
- Two hose pinch-off pliers and a shop rag for the fuel filter
- Rags, rubber and work gloves, safety glasses, cardboard or newspaper under the mower

## Steps

1. Run the engine a few minutes to warm the oil (warm oil drains better), then park on a level surface, set the parking brake, remove the key, lift the hood and disconnect the spark plug wire or wires.
2. Change the engine oil and oil filter. On the riding mower in the manufacturer video: remove the lower dash cover, take the cap off the oil drain valve, fit the drain tube with a pan under it, push in and turn the valve counterclockwise and pull it out to open it, and remove the dipstick. When it stops draining, push the valve in and turn it clockwise until the pin locks, then remove the tube and refit the cap and cover.
3. Add oil through the dipstick tube, stopping several ounces short of the manual's capacity (some oil stays in the engine). Wait a minute, check the dipstick, and top up a little at a time to the full mark.
4. Replace the air filter: turn the cover knobs counterclockwise, remove the cover and pull the old filter off the intake tube. Lift it out carefully so no dirt falls into the intake, and wipe out the housing. Slide the new pre-filter over the new filter, fit it to the intake tube, refit the cover and turn the knobs clockwise.
5. Replace the spark plug: remove it with the spark plug wrench (a 3/4" or 5/8" socket on many engines), set the new plug's gap with a gap tool to the manual's spec (0.03" on the video engine), thread it in and tighten it with the wrench. Don't reconnect the wire yet.
6. Replace the fuel filter: clamp the fuel line on both sides of the filter with pinch-off pliers, put a rag underneath, release the hose clamps and pull the lines off. Fit the new filter with its arrow pointing toward the carburetor and away from the tank, reconnect the lines and release the pliers. Check the fuel lines for dry, brittle or leaking sections.
7. Remove the engine shroud and clean dirt and debris from around the engine, the exhaust and the cooling fins; also clean the hydro fans and transmission area on hydrostatic mowers.
8. Check the ignition, carburetor, battery (12.6 V or more is fully charged; clean corroded terminals), and the throttle and choke controls.
9. Refit the dash cover, reconnect the spark plug wire or wires and lower the hood.
10. Run the engine a few minutes in a well-ventilated area, shut it off, wait a minute for the oil to settle, recheck the dipstick and top up to the full mark.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Replace the tune-up parts (air filter, oil filter, spark plug, fuel filter) after every season. Sources: [S18](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/what-are-the-riding-mower-parts-that-require-replacement-most-often.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-fuel-filter.md)
- A filter too dirty to pass air can damage the engine; a paper filter can be tapped clean and reused if light shows through it, otherwise replace it. Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-push-mower-step-by-step.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-cub-cadet-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- Dirty or wrongly gapped spark plugs cause intermittent running and starting problems. Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-cub-cadet-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-push-mower-step-by-step.md)
- Use worm gear clamps instead of pinch-style clamps when you replace fuel lines. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-craftsman-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- A full annual service also covers the safety interlock check, tire pressures and lug nuts, deck cleaning and scraping, belts, pulleys and spindles, blades, greasing and washing. Set tire pressure to the owner's manual, which can differ from the maximum on the sidewall. Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-simplicity-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-gravely-zero-turn-mower.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-push-lawn-mower.md)
- Before storing for the off-season, remove the fuel or add a stabilizer and run the engine a couple of minutes, and fully charge the battery or remove it. Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-push-mower-step-by-step.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-lawn-tractor.md)
- If you can't find a problem or aren't comfortable doing the work, take the mower to a dealer or an experienced small engine mechanic. Source: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md)

## Verify the fix

After the final oil check, the engine should start readily and run smoothly without misfiring, stalling or surging, and the oil should sit at the full mark.
