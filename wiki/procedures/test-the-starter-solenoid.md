# Test the starter solenoid

Fixes: [Bad starter solenoid](../causes/bad-starter-solenoid.md). Rules out [Loose or corroded battery cables](../causes/loose-or-corroded-battery-cables.md) and [Bad wiring or ground](../causes/bad-wiring-or-ground.md) first. A good solenoid with a starter that still won't spin points to a [Bad starter motor](../causes/bad-starter-motor.md).

When you turn the key to start, the starter solenoid sends battery current to the starter motor. If the engine won't turn over, or you hear a click but nothing happens, these tests show whether the solenoid is the fault before you buy one.

## Before you start

- Is the battery fully charged? It must read at least 12.3 V on a voltmeter, or the test means nothing (see [Test the battery](test-the-battery.md)).
- Are the battery cables and solenoid wires tight and clean, and is the ground good? Many "bad solenoids" are only a loose wire or a bad ground: check them before you replace the solenoid.
- Is the solenoid a separate part on the frame, or mounted on the starter? Starter-mounted solenoids get a bench test instead.

## Safety

- Bypassing the solenoid often throws a spark. This is common, but be careful and always wear safety gear.
- Set the parking brake and put the mower in neutral before any test that can crank the engine.
- Follow the precautions in your operator's manual. For electrical repairs, disconnect the black (negative) battery cable first, then the red.
- If you aren't comfortable diagnosing it safely, have a dealer or repair shop do it.

## Steps

**Bypass test (solenoid on the frame)**

1. Confirm the battery reads at least 12.3 V.
2. Set the parking brake, put the mower in neutral and turn the key to ON.
3. Lay a long screwdriver across the solenoid so it touches both large terminals: the cable from the battery and the cable to the starter. Use needle-nose pliers if the screwdriver doesn't bridge well.
4. If the engine turns over while the solenoid is bypassed, the solenoid is probably bad: replace it (see [Replace the starter solenoid](replace-the-starter-solenoid.md)).

**Voltage test at the starter post**

1. Disconnect the black starter cable from the solenoid post and attach the red meter probe to that post.
2. Clip the black probe to the negative battery terminal.
3. Hold the key in START: the solenoid should click. Read the voltage, then turn the key off.
4. More than 12 V means the solenoid works; no voltage means replace it.

**Coil voltage test (engine won't turn over and no click)**

1. Pull the two small wires off the solenoid's coil spades.
2. Set the meter to DC volts and clip the probes to the two wire connectors (or have a helper turn the key).
3. Turn the key to START, note the reading, then turn it off.
4. Battery voltage (over 12 V) means the coil gets power but the solenoid isn't closing: replace it. 0 V means a break in the circuit to the coil; the solenoid is likely fine, so test the coil circuit.

**Bench test (solenoid mounted on the starter)**

1. Remove the starter from the engine.
2. Attach a battery charger's negative (-) clamp to the starter case, and touch the positive (+) clamp to the big post and the exciter wire on the solenoid.
3. Watch whether the starter runs with the solenoid.

## Verify the fix

After replacing the solenoid, the engine cranks when the key is turned to START. On the voltage test, the starter post reads more than 12 V with the key held in START.
