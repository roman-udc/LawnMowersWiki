# Replace the spindle assembly

Fixes: [Bent spindle or bad spindle bearing](../causes/bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing.md). Also referenced from [Debris wrapped around blade spindle](../causes/debris-wrapped-around-blade-spindle.md) when that damage reaches the spindle.

The spindle assembly (spindle housing, cutter housing or, in Sears guides, the mandrel assembly) is the part on the deck that the blade attaches to and spins on. This page covers both ways to fix it: rebuilding the housing with a new bearing, where your deck allows it, and swapping the whole assembly. Sears's own troubleshooting articles point to this repair.

Sources: [S10](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-mandrel-assembly-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/mower-deck-vibrates.ltrtr140.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/won-t-cut-grass.ridtr01.md)

## Before you start

- Can your housing be rebuilt? Some manufacturers allow only rebuilding the housing, some sell only the whole assembly (sealed bearing), some both. Exmark and some Cub Cadet housings are sealed and replaced whole; most Kubota decks take a bearing alone.
- Have the deck's model and serial number ready; housings and spindles vary by deck. Disassemble first to see which parts you need, and bring the old assembly to the dealer if unsure.
- What the replacement includes varies (some include the blade shaft and bearings), so check the parts diagram for your model.

## Safety

- Park on level ground, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s) (both on a 2-cylinder engine).
- Raise the deck on jack stands if working under it, and wear work gloves: blade edges are sharp.
- On riding mowers with a spring-loaded attachment lift lever: once the rear lift links are disconnected, the lever has more tension. Don't use it until the deck is back on.
- Sears warns that power-equipment repairs can be hazardous: use proper tools and safety gear, and don't go ahead unless you're confident; some repairs are best left to a qualified technician.

## Tools & parts

- Tools: wrench or socket set (an impact gun helps on the pulley nut), pliers, a wood block to hold the blade, torque wrench (optional per Sears; Exmark says torque to factory spec), a puller if the pulley is stuck, a soft mallet or wood block for a stuck shaft, grease, work gloves, jack stands.
- Parts: new spindle housing assembly, or a replacement bearing if your housing can be rebuilt; new mounting bolts are recommended. Use a manufacturer-approved part.

## Steps

Most Sources do this with the deck on the mower; Sears's guide removes the deck first (see Brand notes).

1. **Take the belt off the pulley.** Slip something narrow between the belt and the groove of the bad pulley, turn the belt by hand so the object follows the belt onto the pulley, and pry the belt up and off.
2. **Remove the pulley.** Put a socket on the nut on top of the pulley; it is normally a standard thread, so turn it counterclockwise. Hold the blade with a wood block between blade and deck so it doesn't spin. Some mowers also have allen or hex set screws holding the pulley to the shaft; these can be stubborn and need a puller.
3. **Remove the blade** (mounting bolt underneath).
4. **Remove the spindle housing.** Take out the bolts or nuts holding it to the deck (4 to 8 depending on the mower) and the assembly drops out of the bottom of the deck.
5. **Rebuild or replace.** Set the housing on a flat, hard surface. If it can be rebuilt (sometimes a snap ring holds it together), replace the bearing and grease it. If not, replace the entire housing with its bearings. On housings where the shaft comes out separately: remove the washer and pull the shaft; if it sticks, remove the key (the small bar on the side of the shaft) and it should drop through the bearings; if still stuck, put the nut back on the thread and tap it with a soft mallet or wood block, or buy a new shaft with the housing.
6. **Clean the seat.** Scrape grass from around the deck hole so the new housing sits flat on the deck.
7. **Install the housing.** Start the threads by running the mounting bolts into the new housing's holes, then back them out. Fit the housing into the deck opening from underneath and tighten the bolts a little at a time in a star (cross) pattern so it pulls down evenly. Tighten firmly, or torque per the owner's manual.
8. **Refit the shaft and pulley.** Where the shaft is separate, insert it from the bottom of the housing and fit the key in its groove. Fit the pulley (with the belt properly routed), washer beveled side down, and the nut; block the blade and tighten firmly or to factory spec.
9. **Refit the blade.** Correct side facing up (the lower side is clearly labeled), hand-tighten, block it, then tighten firmly. Use sharp, balanced blades.
10. **Refit the belt** and turn it by hand to check it's on all the pulleys and in the belt keepers. Make sure everything is tight with no movement.
11. Reconnect the spark plug wire(s).

## Brand notes

- **Sears guide (Craftsman, MTD, Troy-Bilt, Husqvarna, Ariens, Cub Cadet, Snapper riding mowers and lawn tractors):** removes the deck before swapping the mandrel. Lower the deck, disengage the blades, roll the belt off the engine pulley and out of the keepers, unpin the front lift link support, unclip both suspension arms and rear deck brackets, release the blade cable from the deck bracket and its spring from the idler arm, and slide the deck out. Flip the deck to reach the blade bolt. Reinstall in reverse, check belt routing over all pulleys and keepers (see the owner's manual), and reconnect both plug wires on 2-cylinder tractors.
- **Exmark:** the cutter housing is replaced as a complete assembly (sealed bearing); 6 or 8 mounting bolts depending on model. The same steps work for all three housings; only the center housing differs, with a double-stacked pulley and a usually longer shaft. Torque the housing bolts and pulley nut to factory spec.
- **Cub Cadet:** sealed housings are replaced whole; others can be rebuilt.
- **Kubota:** the bearing can be replaced in the housing on most decks; some models need the full housing assembly.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Use new mounting bolts.
- Shaft threads can be damaged on disassembly; take the old one apart before ordering so you know what you need.
- While the belt is off, spin each pulley slowly by hand: resistance or noise means replace that pulley.
- > **⚠ Conflicting advice:** Sears says simply "tighten firmly" and lists a torque wrench as optional; the Exmark guide says torque the housing bolts and pulley nut to factory spec from the owner's manual. When in doubt, use the manual's torque values.

## Verify the fix

- With the key out and plug wires off, rock each blade by both ends again: no movement and no knocking.
- Turn the belt by hand and check it sits on every pulley; check that the housing, pulley and blade are tight with no movement.
- The blade shaft should spin freely, and the deck should run without vibration with the blades engaged.
