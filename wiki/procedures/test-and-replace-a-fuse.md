# Test and replace a fuse

Fixes: [Blown fuse](../causes/blown-fuse.md). A fuse that keeps blowing points to [Bad wiring or ground](../causes/bad-wiring-or-ground.md) or a [Bad ignition switch](../causes/bad-ignition-switch.md).

A riding mower fuse protects the electrical system. A blown one cuts power and the engine won't crank. Testing and swapping the fuse is quick; a fuse that blows again means a short you have to find.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-tell-if-a-fuse-is-blown.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-riding-lawn-mower-blown-fuse-video.md)

## Before you start

- Where is the fuse? It sits in the wire harness, often in a fuse block near the battery or behind the dash. On some models you remove the battery and battery box to reach it beside the solenoid.
- What does it look like? Either a cylindrical fuse (silver ends, clear middle, thin element through it) or a flat automotive fuse (metal prongs into a holder, clear body, thin element).
- What amperage is it? The replacement must match.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-tell-if-a-fuse-is-blown.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md)

## Safety

- Turn the key off and wear work gloves.
- Never fit a fuse of a higher amperage.

## Tools & parts

- Multimeter (resistance and DC volts)
- Several spare fuses of the same amperage
- Work gloves
- Wiring diagram and parts diagram, to trace a short (the owner's manual often has both)

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-riding-lawn-mower-blown-fuse-video.md)

## Steps

**Test and replace**

1. Pull the fuse out of its holder.
2. Look at the element: if it's burned and separated, the fuse is blown.
3. To be sure, set the multimeter to resistance and touch a probe to each prong. Near 0 ohms means good; infinite means blown.
4. Replace a blown fuse with one of the same amperage.

**If the new fuse blows: trace the short**

1. With the key off and the fuse out, set the meter to DC volts and put the probes in opposite sides of the fuse holder. Voltage here means a short in the wire between the fuse holder and the ignition switch, or in the switch: unplug the switch and recheck. No voltage then means the switch is shorted (replace it); voltage still there means repair the wire.
2. If there was no voltage, install a new fuse, turn the key to RUN and back off, then pull the fuse. If it blew, the short is in the ignition switch, the run-circuit wire or a harness on it. If it didn't blow, the short is intermittent: wiggle each harness and retest; the harness that blows the fuse when wiggled has the shorted wire.
3. Test the ignition switch: unplug it and pull it from the dash. In RUN it should read near 0 ohms between B and A1 and infinite between B and every other spade. Anything else means replace it (see [Replace the ignition switch](replace-the-ignition-switch.md)).
4. Narrow it to one harness: unplug the engine and chassis harnesses from the dash harness, fit a new fuse and briefly key to RUN. A blown fuse means the short is in the dash harness. Reconnect the engine harness and repeat; if it blows, the short is in the engine harness; if not, it's in the chassis harness. Repair or replace the harness (see [Read a wiring schematic](read-a-wiring-schematic.md)).

## Tips & common mistakes

- A fuse that keeps blowing has a root cause, a short circuit or a bad component. If you can't find it, have a dealer, repair shop or mechanic diagnose it. ## Verify the fix

The new fuse stays intact after you turn the key to RUN and back off, and the engine cranks.
