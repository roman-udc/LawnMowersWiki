# Sharpen and replace the blades

Fixes: [Dull or bent blade](../causes/dull-or-bent-blade.md), [Blade installed upside down](../causes/blade-installed-upside-down.md), [Bad blade adapter or loose blade](../causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade.md).

Dull blades tear the grass instead of cutting it, so the tips turn brown a day or two after mowing; very worn tips leave strips of uncut grass. Dull blades also strain the mower. Most Sources say to put on new blades once a year (more often with sand, gravel or rocks) and to sharpen about every 25 hours of mowing, which is about twice a year for an average homeowner. Sharpen more often in sandy, dry or dusty conditions.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** replace blades once a year ([S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-sharpen-your-lawn-tractor-blades.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/change-sharpen-your-push-mower-blades.md), expert-blog; once per season or when dull or damaged, [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/what-are-the-riding-mower-parts-that-require-replacement-most-often.md), manufacturer) vs most blades need replacing every 1–3 seasons depending on mowing frequency and terrain ([S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-measure-a-mower-blade.md), manufacturer).

## Before you start

- Is the blade worth sharpening? Inspect it first:
  - **Sail** (the raised back edge that lifts the grass): some wear at the ends is normal from sand and dirt. Replace the blade when the sail is thinner than the center of the blade.
  - **Tips:** replace a blade whose tips are rounded or very thin.
  - **Chips and dings:** small ones sharpen out. Large nicks and gouges mean a new blade.
  - **Bends:** lay the old blade on a new one, or measure it on the mower (see [Measure a mower blade](measure-a-mower-blade.md)). A bent blade must be replaced.
  - **Cracks:** replace a cracked blade at once.
- While the blade is still on, put on heavy gloves, grab each end and rock it up and down. Play or knocking points to the spindle housing or its bearing, not the blade.
- Other signs it's time for a new blade: ragged or torn grass, more vibration than usual, or a blade that has already been sharpened a few times.
- New blades come sharpened and painted from the factory. Don't sharpen them or grind the paint off the edge; it wears off in use.
- Which blade fits? Use the model number, or measure the old blade if the tag is gone ([Measure a mower blade](measure-a-mower-blade.md), [Customer intake](../guides/customer-intake.md)).

## Safety

- Stop the mower from starting: turn the blades (PTO) off, shut the engine off, remove the key and pull the spark plug boot(s) off. Don't skip the plug boot. On a battery mower, take the battery out; on a corded mower, unplug it. Wait for moving parts to stop.
- Park on a flat, level surface, set the parking brake and chock the wheels. To get under a zero-turn, use ramps or a lift made for it and make sure the deck can't drop.
- Wear work gloves (blade edges are sharp) and safety glasses, especially while sharpening.
- On a push mower, turn the fuel valve off and tip the mower so the air filter is on the high side.
- Never straighten a bent blade and reuse it. Straightening weakens the metal; it can crack and a piece can break off and fly out.
- A cracked blade can break apart while mowing and throw metal, injuring people or damaging siding.
- Tighten the blade bolt to the right torque. A loose blade can come off.
- Riding mower deck removal: after the rear lift links are off, the spring-loaded lift lever is under more tension. Don't use it until the deck is back on.
- Repairs can be hazardous. Some should be done only by a qualified technician.

## Tools & parts

- To remove and install: socket and ratchet (or wrench set), a block of wood or a blade removal tool to hold the blade, a torque wrench, work gloves and safety glasses. A cordless impact gun helps loosen stubborn bolts (not for tightening).
- To sharpen: a 10" flat metal file, a drill-powered blade sharpener, or a disc, angle or bench grinder; a vise; rags and a wire brush. A stone and sandpaper also work on small electric-mower blades.
- To balance: a blade balancer (inexpensive), or a nail in the wall.
- Parts: new mower blade(s), manufacturer-approved; new cupped washers if the old ones are worn or flat.

## Steps

**Remove the blade**

1. Make the mower safe (see Safety). On a riding mower, lift the hood and disconnect the plug wire(s); remove the deck or raise it to reach the blades. On some tractors the blade can be changed without removing the deck.
2. Wedge a block of wood between the blade and the deck, or clamp a blade removal tool over the blade and the deck edge, so the blade can't turn.
3. Loosen the blade bolt or nut with a socket and ratchet. For a stuck bolt, use an impact gun, or put a block of wood on the bolt head and hit it with a hammer to shock it loose.
4. Remove the bolt, washers (and on some mowers a splined bushing or blade boss) and the blade. Keep the hardware in order. Replace cupped washers that are worn or flattened: a flat washer lets the blade slip.
5. Clean the area under the deck where the blade mounts. This is a good time to scrape the deck.

**Sharpen**

6. Clean the blade with a rag, and a wire brush for stubborn buildup.
7. Clamp it in a vise. Sharpen one cutting edge, then flip it and do the other. Take the same amount of metal off each edge.
   - **File:** push the file along the edge in one direction only, at an angle, with no sawing motion, until the rough spots and uneven edges are gone.
   - **Drill sharpener:** lay the flat guide on the flat of the blade with the cutting edge in the bevel slot, and run it up and down the edge.
   - **Grinder:** hold it perpendicular to the edge and keep it moving along the length, taking a little metal off at a time.
8. Don't sharpen to a pointed edge; it wears faster.
9. Wipe the blade down to remove metal filings.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** the file angle is given as "an angle equal to the slope of the blade" ([S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/change-sharpen-your-cub-cadet-mower-blades.md), expert-blog), 25 to 30 degrees ([S17](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-change-sharpen-your-craftsman-mower-blades.md), expert-blog) and 45 degrees ([S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/inspecting-your-lawn-mower-blades-saves-you-time-money.md), [S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-the-way-the-blade-goes-on-a-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog).

**Balance**

10. Balance every sharpened blade before it goes back on. Use a blade balancer, or hang the blade by its center hole on a nail in the wall (nail head about 3/4" to 1" out from the wall).
11. The end that hangs lower is heavier. Take metal off that end until the blade sits level. The nail method is less accurate than a balancer.

**Install**

12. Put the blade on with the sails (high sides) pointing up toward the deck. Many blades are marked on the bottom side.
13. Fit the cupped washers with the cupped side toward the blade, then the bolt (and bushing, if used). Hand-tighten.
14. Hold the blade with the wood block or removal tool and tighten the bolt with a torque wrench to the owner's manual spec. If the manual gives none, tighten it firmly. Don't tighten with an impact gun; it can overtighten and damage the spindle.
15. Remove the block or tool, reinstall the deck if you took it off (check the belt runs over every pulley and through every keeper), and reconnect the spark plug wire(s).

## Tips & common mistakes

- Skipping the balance step. An unbalanced blade shakes the mower and can damage the spindle bearings, spindle housing, deck, crankshaft or engine.
- Straightening a bent blade. Replace it.
- Tightening the bolt with an impact gun, or to the wrong torque.
- Blades go out of balance from uneven wear by sand, or from taking more metal off one end when sharpening.
- Lawn chemicals can corrode blades and fuse them to the deck. Take the blades off now and then.
- Walk the yard first and pick up sticks, rocks and toys; they bend and gouge blades. Sand wears blades faster.
- Keep a spare sharpened set. Install new blades at the annual service and sharpen the old set as spares, so a damaged blade doesn't stop the mowing.
- A dealer or shop can sharpen for you: the Sources say it's safer, often cheaper than a new blade, a pro can grind out large nicks and gouges, and the blade comes back balanced. Expect to leave them a day or a couple of days. If you aren't comfortable sharpening and balancing, use a pro.
- Do the blades when the deck is already off or open: at the annual service, at winterizing, or when you scrape the deck. A plugged deck gives an uneven cut and loads the engine.
- A thin coat of oil before winter storage helps keep electric-mower blades fresh.

## Verify the fix

- The blade hangs level on the balancer or nail before it goes on.
- The sails point up toward the deck and the bolt is torqued.
- Rock each blade end by hand (gloves on): no play or knocking.
- On the next mow the grass is cut cleanly, the tips don't turn brown a couple of days later, no strips are left between passes, and the mower doesn't vibrate more than usual.
