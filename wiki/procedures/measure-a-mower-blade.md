# Measure a mower blade

Fixes: [Dull or bent blade](../causes/dull-or-bent-blade.md), [Bad blade adapter or loose blade](../causes/bad-blade-adapter-or-loose-blade.md).

Two jobs use a tape measure on a blade: checking whether a blade is bent, and sizing a blade so the right replacement can be ordered. The wrong size blade can cut poorly, hit the deck, rattle loose or damage the mower, and blade size isn't printed on the mower. For how to take the model number and size details from a customer, see [Customer intake](../guides/customer-intake.md).

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-measure-a-mower-blade.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-mower-cut-is-uneven.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/change-sharpen-your-zero-turn-mower-blades.md)

## Before you start

- Do you have the model number? It is the easiest way to find the right blade. Measure only when the model tag is missing, for example on a hand-me-down or garage-sale mower. You can also search by the OEM part number stamped on the old blade.
- Where's the model tag? Walk-behind: top or side of the deck, near the rear wheels, or on the engine housing. Riding: under the seat, near the rear wheels, on the deck, or near the engine or battery tray.
- Checking for a bend? You can do it with the blade on the mower (no removal), or off the mower against a new blade.
- On a push mower, first make sure the wheels are equal and undamaged and the cutting-height settings match.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-measure-a-mower-blade.md), [S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-is-cutting-uneven.md)

## Safety

- Disconnect the spark plug wire, or unplug or remove the battery of an electric mower, before you reach under the deck or take the blade off.
- Wear work gloves. Blade edges are sharp.
- Tighten the blade securely when you put it back.
- Replace any blade that is bent, chipped or cracked; a cracked blade can break apart and throw metal.

## Tools & parts

- Tape measure or ruler
- Flat, stable surface (a hard, level floor for the on-mower check)
- Work gloves
- Blade removal tool or a wrench for the blade bolt
- A new blade of the same model, for the off-mower bend check
- Blade balancer, or a nail in the wall, for the balance check

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-measure-a-mower-blade.md), [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kobalt-electric-mower-bad-cut.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/change-sharpen-your-honda-lawn-mower-blades.md)

## Steps

**Check for a bend with the blade on the mower**

1. Park the mower on a hard, flat, level surface and make it safe.
2. Measure from the ground to the tip of one blade end. Write it down.
3. Turn the blade 180 degrees and measure from the same spot on the ground to the other tip.
4. If the two readings differ by more than 1/8", the blade is bent: replace it. Repeat for each blade.

**Check for a bend with the blade off the mower**

5. Lay the old blade on top of a new blade of the same model. If it doesn't lie flat, or there's a gap, it's bent: replace it. No new blade to hand? Take the old one to a dealer.

**Size a blade for a replacement**

6. Remove the blade (see [Sharpen and replace the blades](sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md)). Clean it; it must be straight, since bends and warping throw off the measurements.
7. Lay it flat and measure diagonally from the tip of one cutting edge to the tip of the other. That is the full length. Don't measure straight across the center; it gives a shorter, wrong length. Blade length is usually a little less than the deck width (a 22-inch mower may use a 21-inch blade). On a riding mower, measure each blade on its own.
8. Check the center hole. Round: measure the diameter in inches or millimeters. 5-point or 6-point star: count the points. H-pattern or bow tie: the shape must match exactly. A hole that doesn't match won't fit the spindle, or could fly loose.
9. Optional: measure the width at the widest part, and note if the blade is unusually slim or heavy-duty.

## Tips & common mistakes

- While the blade is on, grab each end (heavy gloves) and rock it up and down. Movement or knocking means a spindle or bearing problem, not a bent blade. After finding a bent blade, check the spindle housing too.
- Never straighten a bent blade; the weakened metal can crack.
- Look at the rest of the blade while you have it: a sail thinner than the blade's center, or rounded tips, means replace; small chips sharpen out, large nicks and gouges mean replace; a cracked blade must be replaced at once.
- Check balance: hang the blade by its center hole on a blade balancer, or on a nail sticking 3/4" to 1" out of the wall. Take metal off the end that hangs lower until it sits level. Uneven wear from sand, or taking more off one end when sharpening, puts a blade out of balance.
- Check the rest of the mower too, including the deck and drive system.

## Verify the fix

- Both tip readings are within 1/8" of each other, or the blade lies flat on a new one.
- The replacement blade matches the old one's diagonal length and center-hole shape, and fits the spindle.
