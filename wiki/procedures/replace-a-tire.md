# Replace a tire

Fixes: [Bad tire or wheel](../causes/bad-tire.md).

## Before you start

- Can the tire be repaired instead? A puncture only in the tread can usually be fixed with tire sealant: [Repair a flat tire](repair-a-flat-tire.md). Replace the tire when it is too damaged to repair, has a sidewall hole, or the tread is worn thin.
- Front or rear? Replace a front tire with one the same size as the other front tire, and a rear tire with one the same size as the other rear tire. Rear tires need deep tread for traction.
- The steps below are for a riding-mower rear tire. The guide applies to Craftsman, MTD, Husqvarna, Murray, Troy-Bilt, Toro, Snapper, Yard-Man, Weed Eater, Ariens, John Deere and Southern States lawn tractors and riding mowers.

## Safety

- Repairs can be hazardous: use the proper tools and safety equipment. The Source says some repairs are for a qualified technician only.
- Park on a level surface, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire (both wires on a 2-cylinder engine).
- Block the front tires and support the frame on a jack stand, not the jack.
- Wear work gloves and safety goggles.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-rear-tire.md)

## Tools & parts

- Floor jack or bottle jack, jack stand, wood blocks.
- Slot screwdrivers, pry bars (blunt, to protect the bead), pliers, valve stem core removal tool.
- Liquid detergent and water, compressed air supply, anti-seize compound.
- Optional: ratcheting tension straps, to seat a stubborn bead.
- Parts: a new tire, manufacturer-approved.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-rear-tire.md)

## Steps

1. Park on a level surface, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off and remove the key. Put on gloves and goggles. Lift the hood, disconnect the spark plug wire (both on a 2-cylinder engine) and lower the hood.
2. Block the front tires so the mower can't roll. Jack under the frame to raise the rear wheel, set a jack stand under the frame and remove the jack.
3. Pull off the axle cover, remove the retaining ring and then the washer. Pull the wheel off the axle and remove the square key.
4. Remove the valve cap and valve stem core to let out the remaining air. Break the bead and pry the tire off the rim on one side, then the other.
5. Clean the rim. Lay the new tire flat, lubricate the top bead with liquid detergent and pry the rim into the top side of the tire. Flip it, lubricate the other bead and pry the remaining side onto the rim. Use blunt tools so you don't damage the bead.
6. Fill with air to seat the bead, reinstall the valve stem core and fill the tire. If it won't fill, put ratcheting tension straps around the outer edge of the tread on each side (or one strap around the center of the tread) and tighten them to force the beads outward; fill to seat the bead, reinstall the core, add just enough air to keep the beads seated, then carefully release and remove the straps.
7. Slide the wheel onto the axle with the key slots in the rim and axle lined up. Put anti-seize on the square key and push it into the rim. Reinstall the washer, snap the retaining ring onto the axle and push the axle cover onto the wheel.
8. Jack under the frame, raise it, pull the jack stand, lower the mower and remove the wood blocks.
9. Raise the hood, reconnect the spark plug wire (both on a 2-cylinder engine) and lower the hood.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Prying with sharp tools can damage the bead: use blunt tools.
- If the tire won't take air, the bead isn't seated: use tension straps as in step 6.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-rear-tire.md)

## Verify the fix

The bead is seated all the way around, the tire holds air, and the wheel is held on by the key, washer and retaining ring.
