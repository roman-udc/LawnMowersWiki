# Rebuild the carburetor

Fixes: [Dirty carburetor](../causes/dirty-carburetor.md).

The carburetor blends air and fuel. When it's clogged, dirty or leaking and cleaning alone hasn't fixed it, rebuild it with a manufacturer-approved rebuild kit: gaskets, seals, a hinge pin, a needle valve and other small parts (you probably won't use every part in the kit). A stuck float or float needle is also fixed by a rebuild. If the carburetor is too corroded, dirty or damaged to rebuild, replace it instead: see [Replace the carburetor](replace-the-carburetor.md).

This page also covers the smaller job of replacing just the bowl gasket, for a carburetor that leaks gas where the bowl meets the body.

## Before you start

- Have you cleaned the carburetor already? Rebuild when cleaning fails: too much buildup, or stuck or damaged small parts. See [Clean the carburetor](clean-the-carburetor.md).
- Compare the price of a rebuild kit with a new carburetor. Sometimes there's little difference, and replacing is the better choice.
- Find the engine make, model and spec number before you buy parts. Many mowers use another manufacturer's engine, so the mower's model and serial number won't find the right kit or gasket.
- Leaking gas only at the bowl? Try the bowl gasket replacement first. If it still leaks, check the float and needle.

## Safety

- Repairs to power equipment can be hazardous. Use the proper tools and safety gear, don't go ahead unless you're confident, and leave some repairs to a qualified technician.
- Park on a level surface in a well-ventilated area, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool.
- Wear work gloves and safety goggles. Disconnect the battery's negative cable first.
- Gasoline drains from the line and the bowl: catch it in a fuel-safe container or rag and wipe up spills.

## Tools & parts

- Wrench set, nut driver set, punch set, hammer, pliers
- Hose pinch-off pliers
- Carburetor cleaner, compressed air, a thin wire or paper clip
- Shop rags and a fuel-safe container
- Petroleum jelly
- Safety goggles and work gloves
- Carburetor rebuild kit (manufacturer-approved). The manufacturer guide uses the fuel bowl gasket, engine intake (mounting) gasket, float hinge pin, needle valve and air intake O-ring from the kit.
- For a bowl gasket job only: a new bowl gasket and a rag.

## Steps

### Full rebuild (riding mower)

1. Disconnect the battery: park, set the brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Lift the seat, remove the bolt on the negative cable and tuck the cable away from the post.
2. Remove the engine blower housing: lift the hood (or remove it for more room), remove the air duct screws and duct, turn the air filter cover knobs counterclockwise and remove the cover and filter, remove the air filter housing screw, then remove the front and rear blower housing bolts and lift the housing off.
3. Disconnect the fuel line: clamp it with pinch-off pliers, put a rag under the carburetor end, release the clamp and pull the line off. Wipe up any fuel.
4. Remove the carburetor: remove the air intake nuts, detach the breather tube and remove the air intake, and disconnect the fuel solenoid wire. Photograph the linkage connections. Remove the mounting studs, disconnect the throttle rod and spring and the choke rod, and pull the carburetor off. Put it on a clean work surface in a well-ventilated area.
5. Take it apart: unthread the solenoid valve, remove the float bowl (drain the fuel into a fuel-safe container), punch out the float hinge pin, and remove the float and needle valve. Remove the float bowl gasket, engine intake gasket and air intake O-ring.
6. Clean: clean the float bowl and gasket surfaces with carburetor cleaner and a rag. Use a paper clip to scrape off old gasket and O-ring residue. Clean every hole, especially the main and idle jets, with carburetor cleaner and a paper clip. Let it dry completely, at least 10 minutes.
7. Reassemble with new parts: put a little petroleum jelly on the new float bowl gasket and install it, install the new needle valve, refit the float with the new hinge pin, attach the float bowl with the solenoid valve, and install the new air intake O-ring.
8. Install: reconnect the choke rod, then the throttle rod and spring. Put the new intake gasket on the engine, attach the carburetor with the mounting studs and tighten them firmly. Connect the fuel solenoid wire, attach the breather tube and the air intake, and install the intake nuts.
9. Reattach the fuel line with its hose clamp and remove the pinch-off pliers.
10. Reinstall the blower housing (line up the air diverter), its bolts, the air filter housing screw, the filter and cover, and the air duct. Lower the hood.
11. Reconnect the negative battery cable and lower the seat.

Sources: [S19](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-rebuild-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)

### Replace the bowl gasket only

The bowl gasket looks like a rubber band and seals the bowl to the bottom of the carburetor. Sitting next to the hot engine, it dries out, hardens and leaks.

1. Shut off the fuel with the shut-off valve under the tank, or crimp the fuel line with pinch pliers.
2. Wipe the dirt off the outside of the carburetor so nothing falls in when the bowl comes off.
3. Hold a rag under the bowl for the fuel left in it, remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and take the bowl off.
4. Remove the old gasket and fit the new one. Keep carburetor cleaner and other material off the new gasket.
5. Reinstall the bowl and tighten the screw that holds the bowl to the carburetor.
6. Wipe the carburetor down to remove spilled fuel.
7. Turn the fuel back on and check for leaks. If it still leaks, check the float and needle.

### Stuck float or float needle

A leak near the air intake port points to a stuck float: it no longer stops fuel entering the bowl, and the excess overflows. The float needle works with the float and can stick too. Take the carburetor apart to find the cause, then clean it, replace the needle, or rebuild it. If it's too worn to rebuild, replace the carburetor.

## Brand notes

- **Briggs & Stratton engines** on Craftsman, Troybilt, Husqvarna, MTD, Poulan, Toro, Murray, Snapper, Yard-Man and John Deere riding mowers and tractors: the full rebuild steps apply. Source: [S19](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-rebuild-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Craftsman:** Craftsman uses other makers' engines. Buy a rebuild kit online, from a Craftsman dealer or from an authorized engine dealer, using the engine model and spec. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-craftsman-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Kawasaki:** have the engine model and spec ready; kits come from a Kawasaki small engine dealer, a local equipment dealer or online. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-kawasaki-carburetor.md)
- **John Deere, Cub Cadet:** a rebuild kit may be available from the dealer; compare its cost with a new carburetor. Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-your-cub-cadet-carburetor.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-a-john-deere-carburetor.md)

## Tips & common mistakes

- Photograph the linkage before you take the carburetor off; it's the easiest way to reconnect it correctly. Source: [S19](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-rebuild-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- Some owners free a stuck float needle by tapping the side of the carburetor gently with the handle end of a hammer or a rubber mallet. It works only a time or two: it's temporary. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas.md), [S17](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas.md)
- Find the replacement bowl gasket by the engine's model and spec number, not the mower's. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md)
- If you'd rather not do it, a small engine mechanic or dealer can rebuild the carburetor. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md), [S17](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas.md)

## Verify the fix

The engine should start and run smoothly without surging or stalling, and no fuel should leak from the bowl or the air intake once the fuel is turned back on.
