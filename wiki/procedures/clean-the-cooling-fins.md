# Clean the cooling fins

Fixes: [Dirty cooling fins](../causes/dirty-cooling-fins.md).

The cooling fins carry air around the engine block and cylinder. When they clog with debris, air can't circulate and the engine overheats. Cleaning them, and the area around the engine and under the cover, is yearly maintenance (every fall).

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/7-things-that-can-cause-a-lawn-mower-to-overheat.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-engine-overheats.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-cub-cadet-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-gravely-zero-turn-mower.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-husqvarna-riding-mower.md)

## Before you start

- Check the owner's manual for how the engine screen or cover comes off on your model.
- Are any fins broken or damaged? Those need replacing, not just cleaning.

Sources: [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-engine-overheats.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-engine-overheats.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md)

## Safety

- Turn the engine off and let it cool first: the engine and fins can be hot.
- Unplug the spark plug wire before you reach in.

Sources: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ferris-lawn-mower-starts-then-dies.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/why-your-riding-lawn-mower-overheats-and-how-to-cool-it-down.md)

## Tools & parts

- Soft brush or small bristle brush
- Mild soap (optional)
- Compressed air: one Source uses it, another says not to (see Tips & common mistakes)
- Replacement fins, if any are broken

Sources: [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-engine-overheats.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/why-your-riding-lawn-mower-overheats-and-how-to-cool-it-down.md)

## Steps

1. Turn off the engine, let it cool, and unplug the spark plug wire.
2. Remove the engine cover, screen or shroud as the owner's manual describes.
3. Pull out the large, visible debris.
4. Brush the dirt, grass and oil off the cooling fins, using mild soap if needed. Be gentle: bent fins don't cool well.
5. Replace any broken or damaged fins.
6. Clean the dirt and debris from the engine cover and from around the engine block, the shroud and the air intake.
7. Put the cover back on and make sure the heat shield is installed correctly and attached securely.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Don't spray water into the engine or wash it with a hose: that pushes dirt and water into the engine area.
- Do it every year. If you don't, have a repair shop clean the fins as part of regular maintenance.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** use a soft brush or compressed air ([S13](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/why-your-riding-lawn-mower-overheats-and-how-to-cool-it-down.md), manufacturer) vs don't use compressed air or long brushes ([S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-engine-cooling-times-explained.md), expert-blog).

## Verify the fix

The Sources give no separate test. With the cover off, the fins should be free of packed dirt, grass and oil, no fins should be broken, and the heat shield should be in place. Air can then circulate around the block and cylinder again.
