# Replace the deck belt

Fixes: [Worn or stretched belt](../causes/worn-or-stretched-belt.md), and the new belt that's needed after fixing [Bad belt tensioner spring](../causes/bad-belt-tensioner-spring.md), [Belt guard or keeper missing](../causes/belt-guard-or-keeper-missing.md), [Oil or rust on belt](../causes/oil-or-rust-on-belt.md) or [Wrong belt](../causes/wrong-belt.md).

Replace a belt that is shiny or glazed, burnt, frayed, cracked or snapped, squeals when the deck is engaged, or lets the blades slip. Fix whatever made the old belt fail first, or the new one will go the same way. On a push mower the belt is the drive belt, reached from under the deck. On many riding mowers (Cub Cadet, Craftsman and others) the deck has to come out from under the mower; on many zero-turns you only lift the foot pan and remove the belt covers. A homeowner can do it with basic tools.

## Before you start

- Which mower and which belt? Steps vary by model; keep the owner's manual handy for its diagrams.
- Get the correct belt: read the part number off the old belt and buy a duplicate, or look it up by model number in the parts diagram. The authors recommend an OEM belt; auto-parts-store matches perform worse and wear faster.
- Note the routing before the old belt comes off: use the decal on the deck or the manual's diagram, or photograph the belt.

## Safety

- Let a recently run engine cool for 10-15 minutes: a hot muffler can ignite fuel vapors.
- Remove the ignition key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s); on a battery mower, remove the battery.
- Set the parking brake, turn the PTO (blades) off and lower the deck to its lowest position.
- Wear gloves and safety glasses: the blades and deck edges are sharp. Take care reaching under the deck.

## Tools & parts

- Push mower: screwdriver or nut driver set, ratchet or socket set, a block of wood, two sandwich bags (or packaging tape), gloves, goggles, the new belt.
- Riding mower: socket wrench set, screwdriver set, pliers (cotter pins), a torque wrench, gloves, goggles, the new belt. Cub Cadet decks also use a ratchet in the idler bracket's square hole.
- A phone camera for the routing.

## Steps

**Push mower (drive belt):**

1. Work on a stable, flat surface; a workbench where the mower can be strapped down is ideal. Unplug the spark plug boot and seal the fuel tank: lay a sandwich bag over the opening and screw the cap back on (or tape the cap and its vent), so fuel doesn't seep out when the mower is tipped.
2. Tilt the mower back on its handle and secure the handle. Wedge the block of wood between the blade and the deck.
3. Holding the blade with a gloved hand, loosen the blade bolt and remove the bolt, blade and blade support.
4. Remove the bottom drive cover screws and the debris shield bolts (bag and label them), and twist the shield off the crankshaft pulley.
5. Roll the belt off the crankshaft pulley and remove the pulley. If the belt was already off the pulley, it was knocked off or too loose.
6. Stand the mower upright, release the height adjustment lever if there is one near the drive, and remove the top drive cover screws and cover.
7. Unhook the belt keeper on top of the transmission, pull out the old belt, and slip the new belt over the transmission pulley and through the deck opening.
8. Reassemble in reverse: belt keeper, drive cover, height lever and screws. Tip the mower back, wrap the belt around the crankshaft end and refit the crankshaft pulley with its indentations aligned, rolling the belt into the groove. Refit the debris shield, bottom cover, blade support, blade and bolt; tighten the bolt by hand, wedge the blade with the block, then torque it.
9. Remove the block and the bag or tape on the fuel tank, and reconnect the spark plug (or battery).

**Riding mower or lawn tractor (deck comes out):**

1. Remove the belt from the engine (PTO) pulley and release it from its keepers. On a Cub Cadet, turn a ratchet in the square hole of the idler bracket to move the deck drive pulley forward first; on a manual PTO, loosen (don't remove) the idler pulley hardware and remove the rod over the engine pulley.
2. Disconnect the deck: the front lift rod or link support (pin and washer or cotter pin), the left and right suspension or lift arms and rear deck brackets (clips, cotter pins), and the blade (PTO) cable and its spring from the idler arm.
3. Make sure nothing else attaches the deck, and slide it out from under the mower.
4. Remove the belt covers, photograph the routing, and take the old belt off the idler and spindle pulleys (you may need to loosen the left idler pulley nut).
5. Fit the new belt around the outer spindle pulleys, then the idler pulleys, using the photo or diagram. Retighten any idler hardware you loosened and refit the belt covers and guards.
6. Slide the deck back under and reconnect the arms, brackets, lift rod, blade cable and spring; check every belt guide, clip and pin is back.
7. Pull the belt from the right side onto the engine pulley, narrow side of the V into the groove, slowly rotating the pulley until it rolls on.
8. Rotate the belt by hand to check it sits in every pulley with no slack, then reconnect the spark plug wire(s).

**Zero-turn with belt covers (Exmark example):**

1. Lift the foot pan and remove the belt covers.
2. Follow the routing decal (or your photo). Most Exmark decks have a self-tensioning spring that stays on; older units and the Quest use a tension spring you remove first and refit after routing.
3. Start at the pulley nearest the engine, wrap the belt around the spring-loaded tensioner pulley and the blade pulleys in the decal pattern, leaving the last blade pulley.
4. Pull the belt tight with both hands and push it into the last pulley's groove with your palm, walking it around as you slowly turn the pulley.
5. Grease the deck, the idler arm and the hydraulic belt tensioner so both tensioner arms have grease in the cavity.

## Brand notes

- **MTD family (Troy-Bilt, Rover, WOLF-Garten, Remington, Yard Machines, Columbia, Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Boelens):** the MTD guide applies to mowers with these stamps; steps may vary by model. Belts are sold at the local MTD dealer or online.
- **Cub Cadet and Simplicity riding mowers:** the deck must be removed to change the belt; the deck removal steps (idler bracket ratchet, lift arms, front lift rod) are the same in both Sources.
- **Craftsman:** look up the belt by model number at SearsPartsDirect.com; the steps are a guide for most models, so check the model's operator's manual.
- **Exmark:** no tools are needed to reach the belt on most zero-turn decks, except the entry-level Quest and older models.
- **Standard decks that don't need removal:** set the deck as low as possible and take off the covers; release tension and pull the belt from the left spindle pulley; remove the idler spring (hardware above the idler arm) and the idler; remove the retaining belt keeper and take the belt off the clutch pulley under the engine; install in reverse, finger-tightening the idler first, then tightening it securely.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Photograph the routing before removing the old belt: wrong routing found only after the deck is back on means a lot of rework.
- Fix what shredded or threw the old belt first (a rubbing bracket, a bad bearing, debris), so you don't go through several belts.
- Check the blade while it's off; sharpen or replace it.
- A deck belt should be sturdier than a drive belt; one author suggests kevlar cords rather than polyester for a deck belt.
- A homeowner's deck belt can last up to four years with proper maintenance; a damaged deck, debris and heavy use shorten it. Replace the blade belt at the end of the cutting season or when it is worn.

## Verify the fix

- Rotate the belt around the pulleys a couple of times to check it rides in every groove, and tug it to check the tensioner spring reacts.
- Before refitting the belt covers, start the engine and engage the deck two or three times to confirm the belt stays on; then install the covers.
