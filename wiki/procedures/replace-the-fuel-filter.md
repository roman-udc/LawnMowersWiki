# Replace the fuel filter

Fixes: [Clogged fuel filter](../causes/clogged-fuel-filter.md), and the damage from [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md), and a leaking [cracked fuel filter](../causes/cracked-fuel-filter.md).

The fuel filter keeps dirt out of the carburetor. Old gas leaves gummy buildup that plugs it; a clogged filter lets too little fuel through, so the engine runs rough or stops. A cracked or leaking filter must be replaced too. Most Sources replace it once a year, at the yearly tune-up or service, or when it's plugged.

## Before you start

- Does the mower have an inline fuel filter? Not all push mowers have one.
- Does the mower have a fuel shut-off valve? If not, you'll need pinch-off pliers or a clamp on the fuel line.
- Find the arrow on the filter housing: it shows the direction of fuel flow.

Sources: [S22](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-push-lawn-mower.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-loses-power-bogs-down.md), [S29](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kubota-mower-loses-power.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md)

## Safety

- Park on a level surface in a well-ventilated area, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Wear work gloves.
- Disconnect the spark plug wire (both wires on a two-cylinder engine).
- Stop the fuel flow before you open the line, keep a rag under the filter, and wipe up spilled fuel.
- Repairs can be hazardous: go ahead only if you're confident; some repairs are for qualified technicians.

## Tools & parts

- Two hose pinch-off pliers (or the fuel shut-off valve)
- Pliers or needle-nose pliers for the hose clamps
- Shop rag and work gloves
- New inline fuel filter (manufacturer-approved), from a dealer, online or a hardware store
- Worm gear clamps, if you're replacing pinch-style clamps

Sources: [S45](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-fuel-filter.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/big-dog-zero-turn-wont-start.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start.md), [S42](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-exmark-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md), [S24](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)

## Steps

1. Park, set the brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Disconnect the spark plug wire or wires.
2. Get to the filter. On the riding mower in the manufacturer guide, remove the lower dash fastener and pull off the lower dash.
3. Stop the fuel: close the shut-off valve, or clamp pinch-off pliers on the fuel line on each side of the filter. Put a rag under the filter.
4. Release the hose clamps (needle-nose pliers help) and slide the filter ends out of both fuel lines. Wipe up spilled fuel.
5. Note the arrow on the new filter. Install it between the lines with the arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow: toward the carburetor (or fuel pump) and away from the tank.
6. Clamp both lines securely to the new filter, then release the pinch-off pliers or open the shut-off valve.
7. Reinstall the lower dash if you removed it, reconnect the spark plug wire or wires, and lower the hood.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Remove the old filter carefully: its plastic ends can soften and snap off inside the fuel hose. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md), [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ferris-lawn-mower-leaking-gas.md), [S32](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-is-leaking-gas.md), [S42](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-exmark-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md)
- Use worm gear clamps instead of pinch-style clamps to avoid damaging the line, and check the fuel lines while you're there. Source: [S24](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- Replace the filter at least once a year, at routine service or when winterizing. Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md), [S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/fix-lawn-mower-starts-then-dies.md), [S43](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-lawn-tractor-is-leaking-gas.md), [S40](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winterize-your-cub-cadet-lawn-mower.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-isnt-getting-fuel.md), [S48](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)
- While the fuel system is open, follow the lines from the tank and check the lines, pump and carburetor for leaks. Source: [S34](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-husqvarna-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md)

## Verify the fix

With fuel turned back on, there are no leaks at the filter or clamps, and the engine starts and runs without stumbling or stopping.
