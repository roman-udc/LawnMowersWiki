# Clean the gas tank

Fixes: [Dirty fuel tank](../causes/dirty-fuel-tank.md).

Clean a metal gas tank when the mower has sat for a few months, the gas is more than three months old, there is debris in the tank, or the engine isn't performing. Only one Source covers this job.

Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md)

## Before you start

- Read the operating manual for your mower.
- Is the tank metal, and is there rust inside? Rust needs the extra vinegar soak (see Steps).
- Have a safe way to store the drained gas and to take used gas and cleaner to a hazardous waste site.

Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md)

## Safety

- The dangers are fumes, fire and hazardous chemicals. Work in a well-ventilated area, away from flames and heat, and don't smoke near the tank.
- Wear a face mask, and safety glasses when you use harsh chemicals. Keep a water hose nearby to dilute spilled chemicals.
- Remove the spark plug and disconnect the mower battery so the engine can't start: gas fumes can remain even with the tank removed.
- Store drained gas safely.

Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md)

## Tools & parts

- Face mask and safety glasses
- Fuel container or canister, and a clamp for the fuel line
- Flashlight
- Carburetor cleaner, a small pin and rags (for the carburetor)
- Acetone-based cleaner, or a few handfuls of small rocks or pebbles and a little solvent
- Vinegar (for rust)
- Fresh gasoline and fuel additive
- Replacement parts as needed: carburetor bowl, main gasket, needles and nut, fuel filter, fuel line, gas tank, spark plug

Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md)

## Steps

1. Set up a safe work area (see Safety).
2. Remove the spark plug and disconnect the battery.
3. Drain the tank: set the fuel container directly below the tank, clamp the fuel line to the carburetor, detach the fuel line and let the tank drain completely. Shine a flashlight inside and look for cracks, holes, debris and rust. Some damage can be repaired; extreme cases need a new tank.
4. Clean the carburetor (see [Clean the carburetor](clean-the-carburetor.md)).
5. Rinse the tank with a small amount of fresh gasoline, not the gas you drained. Don't rinse it with water. Take the rinse gas to a hazardous waste site.
6. Clean the metal tank with an acetone-based cleaner: add some, shake the tank, and pour it out into a container you can dispose of. Acetone evaporates quickly, so it doesn't leave condensation. Alternatively, remove the empty tank, add a few handfuls of small rocks or pebbles and a little solvent, shake it side to side and up and down, and rinse. Repeat until no more rust rinses out.
7. If there is rust, fill the tank with vinegar after the acetone clean and leave it overnight. Finish cleaning in the morning and let the tank dry completely.
8. Check the fuel filter and fuel lines. Replace the filter (once a year) and any line with cracks or dry rot (see [Replace the fuel filter](replace-the-fuel-filter.md) and [Replace the fuel line](replace-the-fuel-line.md)).
9. Put everything back together, replacing the parts that need it, and reconnect the spark plug and battery.
10. Fill the tank with fresh fuel.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Most fuel lines are 1/4" or 5/16" rubber hose. The author uses worm-gear clamps rather than pinch-style clamps to cut down on leaks.
- Dispose of old fuel properly; don't use it in other tools or vehicles.
- To keep rust from coming back, swirl a baking soda and water mix in the tank, dispose of it, flush with soapy water, then add a small amount of diesel to coat the tank with a light oil.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** the same Source says never to rinse the tank with water, because water causes problems for the carburetor, spark plug and engine ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md), expert-blog), but in its pebble method says kerosene or diesel works as the solvent and "even water works" ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md), expert-blog).

With fresh fuel in, the mower should start. If it still doesn't, the problem is somewhere else.
