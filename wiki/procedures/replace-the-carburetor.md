# Replace the carburetor

Fixes: [Dirty carburetor](../causes/dirty-carburetor.md).

The carburetor mixes gas with air before it goes into the cylinder. Water damage, dirt, debris or gummed-up gasoline can unbalance the mix, so the engine won't start, surges, or runs rough and stalls; a damaged carburetor can also leak gas. Replace it when cleaning and rebuilding don't fix it: when it's corroded, has damaged parts, or old fuel has done too much damage. If it isn't beyond repair, rebuild it instead: see [Rebuild the carburetor](rebuild-the-carburetor.md).

## Before you start

- Have you tried cleaning the carburetor? See [Clean the carburetor](clean-the-carburetor.md).
- Compare the price of a rebuild kit with a new carburetor: when they're close, replacing is the better choice.
- Order by the engine make, model and spec number, not the mower's model or serial number. Many mowers use another manufacturer's engine.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-craftsman-lawn-mower-carburetor.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-kawasaki-carburetor.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-your-cub-cadet-carburetor.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-a-john-deere-carburetor.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-riding-mower-carburetor.md)

## Safety

- Repairs to power equipment can be hazardous. Use the proper tools and safety gear, go ahead only if you're confident, and leave some repairs to a qualified technician.
- Park on a level surface in a well-ventilated area, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Wear work gloves.
- Disconnect the battery's negative cable first. Clamp the fuel line and catch dripping fuel with a rag.

Sources: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)

## Tools & parts

- Wrench set, nut driver set, pliers
- Hose pinch-off pliers
- Shop rag and work gloves
- New carburetor (manufacturer-approved) and a new intake gasket

Sources: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)

## Steps

1. Disconnect the battery: park, set the brake, turn the ignition off, remove the key and let the engine cool. Lift the seat, remove the bolt on the negative cable and tuck the cable away from the post.
2. Remove the engine blower housing: lift the hood (or remove it for more room), remove the air duct screws and duct, turn the air filter cover knobs counterclockwise and remove the cover and filter, remove the air filter housing screw, then remove the front and rear blower housing bolts and lift the housing off.
3. Disconnect the fuel line: clamp it with pinch-off pliers, put a rag under the carburetor end, release the clamp and pull the line off. Wipe up spilled fuel.
4. Remove the carburetor: remove the air intake nuts, detach the breather tube and remove the air intake, and disconnect the fuel solenoid wire. Photograph the linkage connections. Remove the mounting studs, disconnect the throttle rod and spring and the choke rod, and pull the carburetor off. Remove the old intake gasket and clean the mounting surface.
5. Install the new carburetor: reconnect the choke rod, then the throttle rod and spring. Put a new intake gasket on the engine, attach the carburetor with the mounting studs and tighten them firmly. Connect the fuel solenoid wire, attach the breather tube and the air intake, and install the intake nuts.
6. Connect the fuel line to the carburetor and remove the pinch-off pliers.
7. Reinstall the blower housing (line up the air diverter), its bolts, the air filter housing screw, the filter and cover, and the air duct. Lower the hood.
8. Reconnect the negative battery cable and lower the seat.

## Brand notes

- **Briggs & Stratton engines** on Craftsman, Troybilt, MTD, Husqvarna, Murray, Snapper, Weed Eater, Toro and Ariens riding mowers and tractors: the steps above apply. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Craftsman:** Craftsman uses other makers' engines; you need the engine model and spec to order. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-craftsman-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Kawasaki:** have the Kawasaki engine model and spec ready. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-kawasaki-carburetor.md)
- **Ferris:** a carburetor with a stuck float that is too worn to rebuild can be replaced. Source: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ferris-lawn-mower-leaking-gas.md)
- **Gravely, John Deere, Cub Cadet:** order by the engine model and spec number. Sources: [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-your-cub-cadet-carburetor.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-a-john-deere-carburetor.md)

## Tips & common mistakes

- Photograph the linkage before you disconnect it. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- Always fit a new intake gasket and clean the mounting surface first. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)

## Verify the fix

The engine should start easily and run without surging, rough running or stalling, and no gas should leak from the carburetor.
