# Drain and refill the fuel tank

Fixes: [Old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md), [Water in fuel](../causes/water-in-fuel.md), [Wrong fuel](../causes/wrong-fuel.md).

Remove bad gas (old, water-contaminated or the wrong type) from the tank and replace it with fresh gas treated with a fuel additive. For a full clean of a rusty or dirty metal tank, see [Clean the gas tank](clean-the-gas-tank.md) instead.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-sputtering-solved.md)

## Before you start

- What kind of mower is it? On a push mower you can tip the mower or pull the fuel line off the carburetor; on a riding or zero-turn mower a fuel siphon pump is the easiest way.
- Is there water in the tank? Shine a flashlight into the tank: water is heavier than gas and sinks to the bottom, and you may see the gas and water separate. If there is water, you also need to drain the fuel lines and carburetor bowl.
- Which fuel does the engine need? 4-cycle engines use regular gas, 87 octane or higher, with no more than 10% ethanol. 2-cycle engines need a gas and 2-cycle oil mix.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-sputtering-solved.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-acts-like-its-running-out-of-gas.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-quits-when-hot.md)

## Safety

- Wear safety glasses and work in a well-ventilated area.
- Drain the fuel into an approved fuel storage container, and take the old fuel to a recycling center.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-your-mower-wont-start-after-leaving-in-the-rain.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-quits-when-hot.md)

## Tools & parts

- Fuel siphon pump (hand pump with a hose)
- Approved fuel container
- Flashlight and rags
- Fresh gasoline and a fuel additive (Sources name Sea Foam Motor Treatment and STA-BIL)
- Fuel filter, if the mower has one and there was water in the fuel

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-sputtering-solved.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/when-your-riding-mower-gets-wet.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-your-mower-wont-start-after-leaving-in-the-rain.md)

## Steps

1. Drain the tank into an approved container. With a siphon hand pump, put one end of the hose in the fuel tank and the other in the fuel container, then pump to start the flow. On a push mower you can instead tip the mower to drain the gas, or remove the fuel line from the carburetor and let it drain into a container.
2. If there was water in the fuel: also drain the fuel lines and the carburetor bowl (remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and the bowl, with a rag ready to catch the gas and water, then refit the empty bowl). Air can dry out water that remains. Replace the fuel filter if the mower has one (see [Replace the fuel filter](replace-the-fuel-filter.md)).
3. Mix fresh gas with a fuel additive in a fuel can, following the directions on the bottle, then add it to the tank.
4. Start the mower and let it run so the gas and additive work through the fuel system, until it no longer runs sluggishly.
5. Take the old fuel to a recycling center.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Gas starts to break down after about 30 days. Buy fresh gas and use it within that time.
- If you can't drain all of the fuel, a fuel stabilizer additive such as STA-BIL or Sea Foam can help.
- The author runs a fuel additive every other fill-up (or every tank) to keep condensation out of the tank, especially in humid areas or near water.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** add the additive to the fresh gas in a fuel can before it goes in the tank, and never pour fuel stabilizer directly into the mower gas tank ([S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md), manufacturer; [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-sputtering-solved.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-quits-when-hot.md), expert-blog) vs fill the empty tank with fresh gas that has the additive mixed in, without saying where to mix it ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), expert-blog). Mixing in a can satisfies both.

## Verify the fix

The mower starts and runs without running sluggishly or sputtering. If it still doesn't start or keeps running sluggishly, keep troubleshooting the fuel parts old fuel can clog: the fuel filter, fuel lines and carburetor.
