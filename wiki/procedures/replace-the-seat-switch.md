# Replace the seat switch

Fixes: [Bad safety switch](../causes/bad-safety-switch.md).

The seat switch is a safety device under the seat that senses the operator. It shuts off the engine if nobody is in the seat when the brake is released or the mower starts moving, and on many mowers the blades won't engage without it. A broken or unplugged switch stops the engine even with you seated. Test it first, then replace it with a manufacturer-approved part if it fails.

## Before you start

- Is the switch's wire harness plugged in securely? Lift the seat and check. A loose harness just needs reconnecting.
- Does the switch test bad (see the multimeter test in Steps)? If it passes, look for a short in the wire harness instead.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-dies-when-you-release-the-brake-video.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/zero-turn-mower-blades-wont-engage-or-turn-on.md)

## Safety

- Park on a level surface, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off and remove the key. Wear work gloves.
- Disconnect the negative battery cable before you work.
- Repairs can be hazardous; don't proceed unless you're confident, and leave some repairs to a qualified technician.
- The seat switch is an essential safety device. Unplugging it doesn't bypass it: with the harness unplugged, the engine always shuts off when you release the brake.

## Tools & parts

- Wrench set
- Slot screwdriver
- Multimeter (resistance setting), for the test
- Work gloves
- Replacement seat switch (manufacturer-approved)

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-seat-switch-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-dies-when-you-release-the-brake-video.md)

## Steps

1. **Disconnect the battery.** Lift the seat, remove the bolt holding the negative cable to the battery and tuck the cable away from the post.
2. **Remove the seat.** Remove the retaining bolt and washer from the seat bottom. Press the seat adjustment lever and slide the seat fully forward. Release the locking tab (a slot screwdriver helps) and pull the seat off the bracket, holding the adjustment lever against the seat bottom so it doesn't fall. Remove the adjustment lever.
3. **Remove the switch.** Disconnect the wire harness, rotate the switch counterclockwise (about a quarter turn) and pull it out of the seat. Replace it if it's visibly damaged.
4. **Test the switch.** Set the multimeter to resistance and touch a probe to each of the two inside spades: it should read near 0 ohms with the plunger released and infinite resistance with the plunger pressed in. Repeat on the two outside spades: near 0 ohms released, infinite pressed. If any reading fails, replace the switch.
5. **Install the new switch.** Push it into the seat bottom, rotate it clockwise to lock, and connect the wire harness.
6. **Reinstall the seat.** Refit the adjustment lever and hold it while you snap the seat back into the bracket. Reinstall the retaining bolt and washer.
7. **Reconnect the battery.** Reconnect the negative cable to the negative terminal and lower the seat.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Replacing the seat switch is one of the easy DIY repairs, and a broken seat switch is one of the riding-mower parts replaced most often. Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/easy-diy-riding-lawn-mower-repairs.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/what-are-the-riding-mower-parts-that-require-replacement-most-often.md)
- If the switch passes every resistance check, a short in the wire harness can still shut the engine off when you release the brake: check the harness and repair damaged wires. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-dies-when-you-release-the-brake-video.md)

## Verify the fix

Sit on the seat, start the engine and release the brake: the engine should keep running. On a zero-turn, the blades should now engage with you seated.
