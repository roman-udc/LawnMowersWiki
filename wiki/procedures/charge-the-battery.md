# Charge the battery

Fixes: [Weak or dead battery](../causes/weak-or-dead-battery.md). A battery that charges but keeps going dead points to a [Faulty charging system](../causes/faulty-charging-system.md); a full charge is also the first step before you test for a [Bad starter solenoid](../causes/bad-starter-solenoid.md).

Charge the battery at the start of the mowing season, before winter storage, or whenever it's unexpectedly dead (for example when the ignition was left on). It can be charged without taking it out of the mower. If it won't take or hold a charge, replace it.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/charging-a-battery-in-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/dead-battery.ltrtr141.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start-just-clicks-solved.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-blades-wont-engage.md)

## Before you start

- What voltage is the battery? Most riding mowers use 12 V batteries; some built before 1980 (the 1980s, per one Source) use 6 V. Use a charger that matches.
- Does the charger have amp settings or a trickle setting? A slow charge at low amps is best.
- Read the battery voltage first (see [Test the battery](test-the-battery.md)): the lower the reading, the longer the charge. At 11.8 V it needs little time; at 10.1 V it needs a long time.

## Safety

- Wear eye and skin protection: batteries hold acid and can give an electric shock.
- While the charger is connected, touch only the rubber-coated parts of the cables and clamps.
- Unplug the charger from the outlet before you connect or disconnect the clamps.

## Tools & parts

- Battery charger matching the battery voltage, with an output of 10 amps or less (a trickle-charge setting helps with a very weak battery)
- Voltmeter or multimeter
- Screwdriver, if a cover hides the battery
- Safety glasses and work gloves
- Distilled water, for batteries with removable caps

## Steps

1. Reach the battery and terminals (under the hood or under the seat; you may need a screwdriver to uncover it). Leave the battery in its casing.
2. If the battery has removable caps, check the water level. If the plates aren't covered, add distilled water. Well water is not good; city water is a little better; distilled is best.
3. With the charger unplugged, connect the red (+) charger clamp to the positive terminal first, then the black (-) clamp to the negative terminal.
4. Set the charger to the battery voltage (usually 12 V) and the amperage. More amps charge faster, but a slow charge is best: start at 2 amps and work up to no more than 10 amps.
5. Plug the charger in. At 10 amps a battery should fully charge in about an hour; lower settings take longer. For a very low battery, charge at 2 or 4 amps for about an hour, check that the voltage is rising, and if so charge another hour. If it isn't rising, replace the battery.
6. If the charger has a gauge or shuts off automatically, leave it connected until it shows full. Some chargers don't shut off: unplug them yourself so the battery isn't overcharged.
7. Unplug the charger, then remove the clamps and close up the battery cover or seat.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** whether to disconnect the mower's battery cables before charging. The service and battery-drain guides say disconnect them first (positive is last off and first on) (for example [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-riding-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog); the "clicks but won't start" guides say leave the battery in its casing with the terminal cables attached (for example [S51](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-clicks-but-wont-turn-over.md), expert-blog); the manufacturer video charges with the battery in the mower and doesn't mention the cables ([S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/charging-a-battery-in-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md), manufacturer).

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** the maximum charge rate. 10 amps or less, and more than 10 amps can damage the battery ([S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/charging-a-battery-in-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md), manufacturer, and most expert-blog Sources) vs 7 amps or less, called the best balance ([S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-battery-charging-times-explained.md), expert-blog) vs "no more than 7-10 amps" ([S27](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-wont-start-no-click.md), expert-blog).

## Tips & common mistakes

- If you need to mow and the battery isn't very low, charge at up to 6 amps for about half an hour to reach about 12 V, remove the charger and start the mower. Keep the throttle at 3/4 to full so the battery charges while you mow, then check the reading afterwards. Sources: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bad-boy-battery-keeps-dying.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-lawn-mower-battery-keeps-dying.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-battery-keeps-dying.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-battery-keeps-dying.md), [S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-battery-keeps-dying.md)
- A trickle charger delivers 1–3 amps and can take up to 24 hours for a full charge. Most are automatic; put one on well before the mowing season and keep it on over the cold months so the battery doesn't freeze. Source: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-battery-charging-times-explained.md)
- A fully charged battery won't freeze in storage: charge it fully before winter, or remove it to cool, dry storage. Sources: [S70](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winterize-your-lawn-mower-top-storage-for-your-mower.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/dead-battery.ltrtr141.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/charging-a-battery-in-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md)
- A slow 2-amp charge is best but may postpone mowing by a day. Source: [S27](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-wont-start-no-click.md)
- A battery that dies quickly after charging, or won't hold a charge, needs replacing. Bring the old one when you buy a new 12 V battery (hardware or auto parts store, or a mower dealer), or you'll pay a core fee, averaging about $20. Sources: [S63](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-blades-wont-engage.md), [S38](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/hustler-mower-blades-wont-engage.md), [S43](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-blades-wont-engage-or-turn-on.md), [S57](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/snapper-blades-wont-engage.md), [S71](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kawasaki-engine-wont-start.md)
- A battery that charges fine but keeps going dead points to the charging system, not the battery. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start-just-clicks-solved.md)
- A charger is the usual fix for a dead battery; jump-starting is the stopgap (see [Jump-start the battery](jump-start-the-battery.md)). Sources: [S72](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md), [S63](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md)

## Verify the fix

After charging, a 12 V battery should read about 12.6–12.7 V or more with the engine off (see [Test the battery](test-the-battery.md)), and the mower should crank. Check the reading again after mowing.
