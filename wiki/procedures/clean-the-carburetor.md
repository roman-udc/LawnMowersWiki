# Clean the carburetor

Fixes: [Dirty carburetor](../causes/dirty-carburetor.md), and the damage from [old fuel](../causes/old-fuel.md).

The carburetor regulates the fuel-to-air mixture. Old fuel, ethanol and fuel additives leave gummy and white crusty buildup that clogs the fuel jet and sticks small parts. A dirty carburetor makes the engine hard to start or start then die, backfire (running lean), sputter and run rough, surge, use too much fuel, or blow black smoke (running rich).

## Before you start

- Don't take the carburetor apart unless you have to. First confirm that fuel reaches it: check the fuel line for clogs and kinks, and the fuel filter and fuel pump if the mower has them.
- Then do the quick test: remove the air filter, spray carburetor cleaner into the air intake and start the engine. If it fires up, runs and then dies, the carburetor needs cleaning. If it doesn't start at all, the problem may not be fuel: check for an air restriction or no spark.
- Is the owner comfortable with small parts? If not, a small engine repair shop can clean or rebuild it.

## Safety

- The bowl holds gasoline: keep a rag ready to catch it.
- Shut off the fuel supply before you start.
- Follow the operator's manual's safety instructions, and consult a professional if you're not sure.

## Tools & parts

- Pliers, needle-nose pliers, screwdriver
- Socket and ratchet set
- Carburetor cleaner
- A thick piece of wire and a flashlight
- Rags or a clean cloth
- A phone or camera for photos
- Fuel pinch-off pliers, if there's no fuel shut-off valve
- Replacement gaskets, only if one tears or gets contaminated
- Fresh fuel and a fuel stabilizer or additive

## Steps

1. Take photos of the carburetor before and during the teardown, especially the linkage and springs. There are many small parts.
2. Shut off the fuel with the shut-off valve at the bottom of the tank, or crimp the fuel line with pinch-off pliers. Remove the fuel line from the carburetor.
3. Detach the throttle and choke cables from the carburetor (not every mower has them).
4. Remove the air filter housing and the nuts and screws that hold the carburetor.
5. Remove the springs slowly so they don't stretch; a stretched spring must be replaced. You may need to twist the carburetor a little. Don't tear the gasket between the engine block and the carburetor; replace it if it tears.
6. Wipe the dirt off the outside of the bowl. Hold a rag under it, remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and lower the bowl off. Don't damage the bowl gasket (it looks like a rubber band), and don't get carburetor cleaner or anything else on it: it stretches and can't be reused.
7. Find the stem that hangs down from the center of the carburetor. Its holes clog with old fuel gum, and then it can't draw fuel up to the jet. Use a flashlight to find the holes, clear them with a thick wire, and rinse with carburetor cleaner.
8. Check the carburetor body and parts for gummy deposits and white crusty buildup, and remove as much as you can with carburetor cleaner and a clean cloth. It's almost impossible to get all of the crust out.
9. Make sure the float and float needle move freely and are in good order.
10. Reassemble in reverse order using your photos: springs, filter housing, throttle and choke cables, fuel line.
11. Turn the fuel back on, fill with fresh gas mixed with a fuel stabilizer or additive, let the bowl fill, and start. A pull-cord engine may take several pulls.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** most Sources reuse the gaskets and needles unless they're torn, contaminated or stuck ([S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-push-mower-carburetor.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-zero-turn-mower-carburetor.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-your-cub-cadet-carburetor.md), expert-blog) vs one Source that replaces both needles and the main gasket as part of every cleaning ([S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md), expert-blog).

## Brand notes

- **Craftsman, Husqvarna, Toro:** these brands use other manufacturers' engines. Have the engine model and spec number ready to order a replacement carburetor or rebuild kit (online, from the brand's dealer, or from an authorized engine dealer). Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-craftsman-lawn-mower-carburetor.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-husqvarna-mower-carburetor.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-toro-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Kawasaki engines:** a clogged fuel jet or stuck float are the typical failures, mostly from old fuel; use fresh fuel with low ethanol. Order parts by the engine model and spec. Source: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-kawasaki-carburetor.md)
- **John Deere, Cub Cadet:** if parts are corroded or damaged, replace the carburetor; order by the engine model and spec number. Sources: [S17](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-a-john-deere-carburetor.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-your-cub-cadet-carburetor.md)
- **Older two-cycle push mowers:** use good fuel at the correct fuel-to-oil ratio after cleaning. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-push-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Ferris:** the bowl gasket can be replaced on its own, without a full cleaning; see [Rebuild the carburetor](rebuild-the-carburetor.md). Source: [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ferris-lawn-mower-leaking-gas.md)
- **Gravely:** cleaning may free a stuck float. Source: [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas.md)
- **Black Max, Husqvarna, Toro, Kubota:** these Sources point to separate carburetor-cleaning articles; rebuild or replace if it's still bad. Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/black-max-mower-wont-start.md), [S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md), [S24](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-only-runs-choke-on.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/carburetor-is-not-getting-fuel.md)

## Tips & common mistakes

- If cleaning doesn't work, small parts may need replacing or old fuel may have damaged the carburetor too much: see [Rebuild the carburetor](rebuild-the-carburetor.md) and [Replace the carburetor](replace-the-carburetor.md). Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-push-mower-carburetor.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-zero-turn-mower-carburetor.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-husqvarna-mower-carburetor.md)
- Throwing parts at the mower gets expensive. If you're not sure the carburetor is at fault, get a repair shop diagnosis. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-zero-turn-mower-carburetor.md)
- After the job, don't let gas sit long; add a stabilizer such as Sea Foam Motor Treatment. Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-craftsman-lawn-mower-carburetor.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-toro-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- Cleaning the carburetor periodically extends its life; a repair kit lets you replace internal parts about once a year. Source: [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md)
- If no fuel comes out when you take the carburetor off, the fuel line may be blocked. Source: [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-clean-your-metal-lawn-mower-gas-tank-in-9-easy-steps.md)

## Verify the fix

With fresh fuel in the bowl, the engine starts (a pull-cord engine may take several pulls) and keeps running without carburetor cleaner, with no surging, sputtering or backfiring.
