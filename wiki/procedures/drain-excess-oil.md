# Drain excess oil

Fixes: [Overfilled oil](../causes/overfilled-oil.md).

Remove a little oil at a time until the dipstick reads at the full line. There are five ways to do it; use whichever your engine allows.

Sources: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-draining-oil-from-your-lawn-mower.md), [S26](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-what-happens-if-you-put-too-much-oil-in-a-lawn-mower.md), [S33](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-kubota-mower.md)

## Before you start

- Does the engine have a drain plug or drain valve? It's on the side of the engine by the dipstick or at the bottom of the oil pan. Many push-mower engines have none.
- Does the engine have an oil filter?
- What level does the manufacturer specify? Aim for the full line on the dipstick.

## Safety

- Remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s) before you start.
- Don't reuse a turkey baster for cooking after it's been in engine oil.

## Steps

1. Remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s).
2. Read the dipstick: pull it, wipe it clean, reinsert it, pull it again and read the level.
3. Remove a little oil with one of these methods:
   - **Drain plug or valve port:** set a drain pan under it, loosen or remove the plug for a quick second, then retighten it so only a little oil comes out.
   - **Oil filter:** loosen or spin off the filter to let a little oil out, with a rag ready, then refit it.
   - **Fill hole (no drain plug):** tip the mower on its side with the carburetor and air filter on the high side, so oil can't run into them, and let a little out of the fill hole.
   - **Oil evacuator:** insert its tube into the fill hole and pump oil out.
   - **Turkey baster:** suck small amounts out through the fill area.
4. Recheck the dipstick. Remove or add a little oil and repeat until the level is at the full line.
5. Check the air filter; replace it if it's oil-covered or plugged with smoke.
6. Wipe up any spilled oil and reconnect the spark plug wire(s).

## Tips & common mistakes

- Several authors prefer a turkey baster or oil evacuator through the fill area: it's the easiest and cleanest method. A turkey baster suits small amounts. Sources: [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-is-blowing-blue-black-or-white-smoke.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-draining-oil-from-your-lawn-mower.md), [S21](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-is-smoking.md)
- Open the drain plug only briefly, or too much oil drains out. Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-mower-loses-power.md), [S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/mower-stopped-running.md), [S27](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-why-your-riding-lawn-mower-loses-power.md)
- To avoid overfilling next time, look up the crankcase capacity, add 1/2 quart at a time, then small amounts near full, checking the dipstick. Source: [S29](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-craftsman-mower.md)

- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Engine oil guide](../guides/engine-oil-guide.md).

## Verify the fix

The dipstick reads within the full lines, no higher and no lower.
