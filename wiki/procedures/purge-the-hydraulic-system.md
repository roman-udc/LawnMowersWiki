# Purge the hydraulic system

Fixes: [Air in the hydraulic system](../causes/air-in-hydraulic-system.md).

Purging (bleeding) removes air trapped in the hydrostatic transmission. Do it after every hydraulic oil change or addition, any time the transmission has been opened or repaired, and before the first use of a new mower. Air left in the system makes the mower slow, noisy or unable to move. The usual method is to raise the drive wheels and cycle them forward and reverse, first with the bypass valves open and then closed, until they turn at normal speed and sound. Steps vary by make and model: check the operator's manual.

## Before you start

- Is it a zero-turn or a lawn tractor? The lever and pedal steps differ (see Steps and Brand notes).
- Is there enough oil in each transmission? Check before you start the engine.
- Can you raise the rear drive wheels on jack stands? That's the best method; if you can't, do it on the ground in a large, open, flat area free of obstructions.

## Safety

- Park on a flat, level surface, set the parking brake and make sure the PTO (blade) switch is off.
- Chock the front wheels so the mower can't roll forward, and support the raised rear securely on jack stands.
- Make sure the transmission has enough oil before you start the engine.
- When you finish, shut off the engine and set the parking brake before you lower the mower.

- Jack stands (or jacks) to raise the rear; wheel chocks.
- Hydraulic oil of the type the manufacturer specifies, to top up afterwards.

## Steps

Zero-turn:

1. Park on a flat surface, chock the front wheels and confirm the oil is full in each transmission.
2. Raise the rear on jack stands just high enough that the drive wheels turn freely.
3. Open the bypass valves (one per side; on many mowers these are the drive release levers at the rear), so the wheels can spin by hand.
4. Start the engine, set the throttle (see the conflict note below) and release the parking brake.
5. Move the drive levers slowly forward and then reverse, about 6 times.
6. Shut off the engine, set the parking brake and close the bypass valves.
7. Start the engine, release the parking brake and move the drive levers forward and reverse about 6 more times.
8. Repeat, with the bypass valves open and then closed, until there's no excessive noise and the wheels turn smoothly at normal speed in both directions.
9. Shut off the engine, set the parking brake and lower the mower.
10. Let the system cool, then check the expansion tanks: air removal lowers the oil level. Top up to the FULL COLD line.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** about 1/2 throttle while cycling ([S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/how-to-service-a-gravely-zero-turn-mower.md), [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-cub-cadet-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/service-a-husqvarna-zero-turn-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog) vs high throttle for the bypass-closed round ([S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bad-boy-zero-turn-wont-move-forward-or-reverse.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kubota-zero-turn-wont-move-forward-or-reverse.md), [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/walker-mower-wont-move-forward-or-reverse.md), expert-blog). Check the operator's manual for your model.

Lawn tractor (Husqvarna steps):

1. On a flat, open area, with the engine off and the parking brake set, disengage the transmission with the rear lever so the tractor rolls freely.
2. Start the engine at slow throttle, release the brake, and press the drive pedal forward for about 5 seconds, then reverse for 5 seconds; repeat several times.
3. Shut off the engine, set the brake and engage the transmission with the rear lever.
4. Repeat with the transmission engaged; then, with 10 feet clear in front and behind, start at about half throttle and drive forward 10 feet and back 10 feet several times.

## Brand notes

- Ariens, Cub Cadet, John Deere and Spartan zero-turns (simple version): level surface, chock the wheels, raise the rear drive tires and let them spin forward and backward until there's no excessive noise and they turn at normal speed.
- Exmark Radius: rear on jack stands, about 1/2 throttle, bypass open, levers forward and reverse about 6 times; close the bypass and repeat about 6 times; check the oil with the engine off and cool.
- Exmark Lazer Z: full throttle, release the parking brake, hold the speed levers all the way forward for one minute; shut off, let the hydros cool and check the level.
- Hustler: about 6 cycles with the bypass valve open, then about 6 with it closed, then check the fluid level.
- Husqvarna zero-turns: fast idle throttle.
- Simplicity: bypass levers go to PUSH/OPEN for the first round and DRIVE/CLOSED for the second.

## Tips & common mistakes

- Expect the oil level to drop as the air comes out: always recheck and top up after purging.
- Repeat the cycles as many times as needed; stopping early leaves air in the system.
- If the purge doesn't help, follow the model-specific procedure in the operator's manual.

## Verify the fix

- The drive wheels turn smoothly at normal speed, forward and reverse, with no excessive noise.
- With the system cool, the oil sits at the FULL COLD line on each expansion tank.
