# Replace the ignition switch

Fixes: [Bad ignition switch](../causes/bad-ignition-switch.md). A shorted switch can also cause a [Blown fuse](../causes/blown-fuse.md).

A worn ignition switch can stop current reaching the starter circuit. Test it first; if it fails, swapping it is an easy repair.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-switch.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/easy-diy-riding-lawn-mower-repairs.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-lawn-mower-wont-turn-over.md)

## Before you start

- Has the switch tested bad? With a multimeter on resistance and the key in START, the B and S terminals should read near 0 ohms; infinite resistance means replace the switch. With the key in RUN, B to A1 should read near 0 ohms and B to every other spade infinite.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-lawn-mower-wont-turn-over.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-riding-lawn-mower-blown-fuse-video.md)

## Safety

- Park on a level surface, set the parking brake, turn the ignition off and remove the key. Wear work gloves.
- Disconnect the negative battery cable before you work.
- Repairs to power lawn-care equipment can be hazardous; don't proceed unless you're confident, and leave some repairs to a qualified technician.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-switch.md)

## Tools & parts

- Wrench set
- Multimeter, for the test
- Work gloves
- Replacement ignition switch (manufacturer-approved)

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-switch.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-lawn-mower-wont-turn-over.md)

## Steps

1. **Disconnect the battery.** Lift the seat, remove the bolt holding the negative cable to the battery and tuck the cable away from the post.
2. **Remove the switch.** Lift the hood to reach the back of the switch. Release the locking tabs and push the switch out through the front of the dash. Disconnect the wire harness from the back (if it's stiff, wiggle the plug while pulling).
3. **Install the new switch.** Connect the wire harness to the back of the new switch, push it into the dash until the locking tabs engage, and lower the hood.
4. **Reconnect the battery.** Reconnect the negative cable to the negative terminal and lower the seat.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-switch.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-riding-lawn-mower-blown-fuse-video.md)

- **Craftsman (manufacturer video):** the switch's contact spades are labelled; in RUN, current goes from terminal B to A1, which feeds the blue run-circuit wire. Source: [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-riding-lawn-mower-blown-fuse-video.md)

## Tips & common mistakes

- A shorted ignition switch can also blow the fuse: if the fuse keeps blowing, test the switch (see [Test and replace a fuse](test-and-replace-a-fuse.md)). Source: [S4](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-riding-lawn-mower-blown-fuse-video.md)

## Verify the fix

Turn the key to START: the engine should crank. If you tested the old switch, the new one should read near 0 ohms across the terminals the key position connects.
