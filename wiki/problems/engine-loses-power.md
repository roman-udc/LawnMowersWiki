# Engine loses power

## Summary

The engine runs, but it bogs down, feels sluggish or doesn't give the power it once did, often the moment the blades are engaged or in heavy grass. The Sources give three reasons: the engine isn't getting enough air or fuel (plugged air filter, old fuel, clogged fuel filter or lines, dirty carburetor, fouled plug); it runs hot (low or overfilled oil, dirty cooling fins, blocked air passages); or it is overloaded (wet or tall grass, mowing too fast, a plugged deck, dull blades). A few cases are electrical or internal engine problems that need a mechanic. Most causes are easy to fix, and fixing them early avoids expensive engine repairs.

## Quick triage

1. **Make it safe.** Remove the ignition key and the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s) before any repair, and follow the operator's manual.
2. **Ease the load.** Slow the ground speed, raise the deck for tall grass, and mow when the grass is dry.
3. **Check the air filter** first; replace it if it's dark, damp or covered in debris ([Clean or replace the air filter](../procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.md)).
4. **Check the fuel.** Gas more than a month or two old can make the engine struggle: drain it and refill with fresh fuel. Then look for a clogged fuel filter ([Replace the fuel filter](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter.md)), clogged lines, a failing fuel pump and a plugged fuel cap vent.
5. **Check the oil** before each mowing: top up low oil, drain excess oil ([Drain excess oil](../procedures/drain-excess-oil.md)).
6. **Clean the deck, blades and cooling fins.** Scrape packed grass from under the deck, sharpen or replace dull blades ([Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md)), and clean the cooling fins and the area under the engine shroud ([Clean the cooling fins](../procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins.md)).
7. **Check the spark plug** ([Replace the spark plug](../procedures/replace-the-spark-plug.md)) and the carburetor ([Clean the carburetor](../procedures/clean-the-carburetor.md), [Rebuild the carburetor](../procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor.md)).
8. **Still losing power with the blades engaged?** Check the blade drive belt and the spindles. If fuel, air, belts and blades all check out, it may be low compression or valve trouble: call a professional.

## Brand notes

- **Briggs & Stratton engines:** the engine can act sluggish, almost like it's going to die, with plenty of fuel in the tank, because fuel isn't reaching it (old fuel, filter, lines, pump, carburetor, fuel cap vent).
- **Husqvarna, Toro:** also check for blocked air passageways: remove grass, dirt and debris from under the engine shroud and make sure the heat shield is securely in place.
- **Craftsman:** too much oil can foul the spark plug with oil, which causes power loss and rough running.
- The brand articles for Bad Boy, Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Ferris, Honda, John Deere, Kubota, SCAG, Spartan and Troy-Bilt list the same air, fuel, heat and load causes.

## Tips & life hacks

- Do the basics (fuel and air) first; they're easy to fix and often the root of the problem. The air filter is the first thing one author checks.
- Check the engine oil before each mowing to catch leaks and engine problems early.
- Fix performance problems early: engine repairs get expensive if they aren't addressed right away.
- Routine maintenance prevents most power-loss problems.

- Air filter, fuel filter, fuel line, fuel pump, gas cap.

## Safety

- Follow the safety precautions in the operator's manual. Remove the ignition key and the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s) before any repair.
- Work on the fuel system in a well-ventilated area, and wear goggles and gloves.
- Clean under the deck and check the blades only with the mower off and the spark plug disconnected.
- If you lack the skills, knowledge or condition to do the repair safely, consult a professional.

## When to send to a technician

- The engine still loses power after the air, fuel, oil, deck and blade checks: internal engine problems are hard to diagnose without the right tools. A dealer or small engine repair shop can find a charging system, electrical or internal engine problem.
- It still bogs or stalls with the blades engaged after fuel, air, belts and blades check out: it may be low compression or valve trouble.
- The engine still has problems after low oil was topped up: running low on oil may have damaged it internally.
