# Battery mower won't start

## Summary

A battery-powered (cordless) mower does nothing when you press the start button and pull the safety bar, or it starts and then shuts down. The Sources group the causes in four areas: not enough power (a flat, failing, wrong-size or badly seated battery, or a charger that isn't charging), a bad electrical connection (a safety switch or key, loose or pinched wires), an overworked motor (a hot battery, clogged vents, grass jammed under the deck, tall or wet grass), and an incorrect starting procedure. Damaged motors and start switches are rarer and are a repair-shop job. This page also covers corded electric mowers, which add the power cord, outlet and breaker.

## Quick triage

1. **Make it safe.** Remove the safety key and the battery before any repair or before reaching under the deck.
2. **Check the battery connection.** Wipe the lid, remove the battery, clean the contacts and the compartment, and slide it back in until it clicks.
3. **Charge the battery** and check the charger: fully plugged into a standard 120 V outlet, plug matching the outlet, lights normal.
4. **Follow the starting steps:** battery in, safety key in, press the safety button, pull the safety bar to the handle; handle unfolded and locked.
5. **Let a hot battery cool** and clean the motor vents with a dry cloth or compressed air.
6. **Clear the deck:** remove grass jammed under the deck and anything wrapped around the blade; raise the cutting height and slow down in tall or wet grass.
7. **Wiggle the wires** at pinch points (where the handle folds) and test the safety switches.

### Corded electric mowers

Corded mowers have no power source value in this wiki yet; their power checks are here.

- A corded push mower needs an extension cord that supplies 13 to 15 amps. A 6 to 10 amp cord may have trouble turning the mower on or keeping it running, and a mismatched cord makes both overheat, with damage and fire risk.
- Check that the outlet and cord connections are fully plugged in and the outlet can supply the power; check the household breakers, and test the outlet with a simple appliance such as a lamp.
- Corded mowers typically plug into a GFCI outlet (a GFCI adapter can be used where the home has none).
- Constant pulling, flexing or yanking can damage the wires inside the power cord; check it with a multimeter. Cords are easily replaced.

## Tips & life hacks

- Charge the battery an hour or two before use so it is fully charged, and track its age; a fully charged battery left sitting loses some charge over time.
- Empty the bag and clear the deck after every use.
- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Electric mower care and safety](../guides/electric-mower-care-and-safety.md).

## Safety

- Remove the safety key and the battery before any repair, and before working on the deck.
- Take care with electric current and sharp blades.
- Never operate the mower without all its safety switches working.

## When to send to a technician

- A damaged motor or start switch: take it to a professional repair shop or the brand's authorized service center; the parts are pricey and the repair may cost more than the mower is worth.
- Nothing on the checklist works.
- Under warranty: contact the manufacturer first.
