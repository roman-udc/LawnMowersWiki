# Engine cranks but won't start

## Summary

The starter or recoil pull spins the engine normally, but the engine never fires and runs. A spinning engine needs air, fuel, spark and compression. When it cranks but won't start, one of them is missing. The usual reasons are a fuel problem (old gas, an empty tank, a clogged fuel filter or line, a plugged gas cap vent, a bad fuel pump or a dirty carburetor), a plugged air filter, a bad spark plug, or a wrong choke setting or starting procedure. Ignition faults, a sheared flywheel key and low compression are less common. On a diesel mower, also think of glow plugs and air in the fuel system.

Most Sources for this page are general "won't start" articles that list both crank and no-crank causes together. If the engine doesn't spin at all, or only clicks, see Related problems.

## Quick triage

1. **The basics before any test:** there is enough fresh fuel, the fuel shut-off valve (if fitted) is open, the bail lever is held or the parking brake is set, the blade (PTO) switch is off on riding and zero-turn mowers, and the choke is on/closed for a cold engine. Most current push mowers have an automatic choke with no lever.
2. **Choke:** set it on/closed for a cold engine and off/open for a warm one. Check that the choke cable moves freely.
3. **Fuel age:** gas can start to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. If it is old, drain the tank and refill with fresh gas and a fuel stabilizer.
4. **Air filter:** pull it out and hold it up to a light. Clean or replace it if it is dirty, torn or brittle. With the filter out, check that the choke plate closes and opens with the lever.
5. **Spark plug:** check that the plug wire is firmly attached. Pull the plug and look for carbon or oil deposits and a cracked insulator.
6. **Gas cap vent:** loosen the cap and try to start. If it starts, the vent is plugged.
7. **Fuel test:** with the air filter removed, spray a short burst into the air intake and try to start. If the engine fires or tries to start, it's a fuel problem (step 8). If not, it's spark or compression (step 9).
8. **Fuel flow:** check the fuel filter, the fuel line, the fuel pump's output and, on riding mowers, the wire on the fuel solenoid. If fuel reaches the carburetor and the engine still won't run, clean, rebuild or replace the carburetor.
9. **Spark and timing:** put a spark plug tester between the plug wire and the installed plug and crank. No glow means a bad plug or ignition coil; on electric-start models also check the battery, wiring and starter solenoid. If there is spark, check the flywheel key (especially after a blade strike), then compression.
10. **Pull-start mowers:** if the recoil is broken, see whether it can be restrung or a broken part such as the pulley replaced; otherwise replace the recoil assembly.
11. **Diesel mowers:** check the glow plugs, and look for air or water in the fuel system.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice (what to spray into the intake in step 7):** use a short burst of starter fluid, in a well-ventilated area ([S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), manufacturer) vs use carburetor cleaner and never starter fluid ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-zero-turn-cranks-but-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/scag-zero-turn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S41](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S44](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start.md), [S59](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S76](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S84](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-wont-start.md), [S101](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-mower-wont-start.md), [S102](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-push-mower-wont-start.md), [S107](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-not-to-use-starter-fluid-on-a-lawn-mower.md), [S117](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-toro-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), all expert-blog). ## Brand notes

- **Husqvarna, John Deere, SCAG with electronic fuel injection (EFI):** test the fuel pump with a fuel pressure gauge against the spec in the operator's manual. The "pull the line and watch the flow" test doesn't apply. Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/scag-zero-turn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- **Husqvarna:** some engines use a foam *primary* filter, not just a foam pre-filter. Wash it, let it dry fully, soak it in clean engine oil and squeeze out the excess. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- **Cub Cadet zero-turn:** with fuel reaching the pump, the pump should give a steady or pulsing flow. If it doesn't, replace the pump. Source: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-zero-turn-cranks-but-wont-start.md)
- **Craftsman and other Sears riding mowers:** the carburetor's fuel solenoid valve shuts off fuel when the engine is killed, to prevent backfire. If its wire harness is disconnected, the valve stays closed. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)
- **Automatic choke:** newer Husqvarna, Toro and Troy-Bilt push mowers and many Cub Cadet push models have a thermostat-controlled choke that is closed when cold and opens as the engine warms, so there is no choke lever to set. Sources: [S44](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start.md), [S59](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S101](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-mower-wont-start.md), [S102](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/troy-bilt-push-mower-wont-start.md), [S117](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-toro-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)

Their brand "won't start" articles cover the gas engines; for a diesel, see the diesel causes in the table. ## Tips & life hacks

- **Fuel split test:** one short burst into the intake tells you whether you are chasing fuel, or spark and compression. Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S53](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/first-check-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S76](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start.md)
- **Don't tear down the carburetor first.** Confirm good fuel flow to it and remove the air filter before taking it apart. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- **Check the simple things first.** A new mower, or one just out of storage, often has its fuel valve closed from transport or storage. Sources: [S53](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/first-check-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S75](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-after-winter.md)
- **Don't guess and throw parts at it.** It gets expensive, and most mower shops won't take back electrical parts. Sources: [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bolens-mower-wont-start.md), [S44](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start.md), [S56](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-mower-wont-start.md), [S80](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/poulan-pro-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)
- In the busy season a dealer repair can take a week or two, so work through the checks on this page first. Source: [S65](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)
- After refilling with fresh, stabilized fuel, let the engine run 10–15 minutes so the treated fuel works through the system. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- If the gas won't be used up before storage, add fuel stabilizer to the tank. Gas absorbs water and loses combustibility over months. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)
- Don't blow out a paper air filter with an air compressor. Tap it against a hard surface instead. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- Before a compression test, check that the crankcase isn't overfilled with oil, and drain it below the max mark if it is. Too much oil can also foul the plug and get into the carburetor and air filter. Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S96](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-cub-cadet-lawn-mower.md), [S97](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-john-deere.md)

## Parts commonly needed

- **Air filter / foam pre-filter:** replace if no light shows through, or if it is torn or brittle.
- **Spark plug:** replace if it is fouled or the insulator is cracked.
- **Fuel filter:** replace it if it hasn't been changed within the last year.
- **Fuel line:** replace it if it is clogged or damaged.
- **Fuel pump:** replace it if fuel goes in but little or none comes out. Check EFI pumps against the pressure spec.
- **Carburetor:** clean or rebuild it first, and replace it if that doesn't restore fuel delivery. To order a replacement you need the engine model and spec number, not the mower's.
- **Fuel cap:** replace it if the vent is plugged.
- **Ignition coil:** replace it if there is no spark with a good plug, or if the spark plug wire is damaged (the wire is part of the coil).
- **Flywheel key:** replace it if it is sheared. Removing the flywheel needs a flywheel puller.
- **Recoil starter:** restring it or replace broken parts such as the pulley; replace the assembly if needed.
- **Glow plug (diesel):** replace a glow plug that no longer heats.

## Safety

- Before any repair, remove the ignition key, take the spark plug wire(s) off, set the parking brake and wait for all parts to stop moving. Follow the safety steps in the operator's manual for your mower, gas or diesel.
- Use starter fluid only in a well-ventilated area, in short bursts. Work with carburetor cleaner in a well-ventilated area too, and take care around a hot engine.
- Drain and siphon gas into a proper container.
- When pulling the fuel line to test flow, shut off the fuel supply first and catch the fuel in a container.
- Protect your hands, skin and eyes when working on the battery, fuel and electrical system.

## When to send to a technician

- The compression gauge reads under about 40 psi (most engines read over 40, and many over 80). Finding the cause needs a leak-down test with special tools. The fix may be a valve adjustment, new piston rings or cylinder repair.
- The engine has an automatic compression release, which makes a plain compression test unreliable. A leak-down test is the better check.
- The charging system is the suspect: have a dealer or small engine mechanic find the actual failure rather than buying parts.
- Air, fuel and spark all check out and the engine still won't fire, or you aren't sure you can do the repair safely.
