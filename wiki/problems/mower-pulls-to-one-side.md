# Mower pulls to one side

## Summary

The mower veers left or right instead of going straight, or (on a zero-turn) feels weak on one side or jerks to one side as it moves off. On a riding mower the complaint can also be that it won't steer one way. The usual cause is simple: unequal tire pressure, so check the tires first. On zero-turns the next suspects are a bad steering damper and tracking that is out of adjustment (one wheel turning faster than the other). Less often, one side of the drive is weak: a dragging brake, air trapped in one of the two transmissions, low or old hydraulic oil, a worn drive belt, or a failing transmission. A walk-behind can pull from a dragging wheel, a deck lower on one side, or an uneven drive cable. And sometimes it isn't the mower at all: slopes, soft ground and thick patches of grass make any mower drift.

## Quick triage

1. **Check the terrain.** Mow the same area in a different direction. If the pull disappears or switches sides, slopes, soft ground or thick grass are to blame.
2. **Check tire pressure.** Fill every tire to the manufacturer's spec (owner's manual or tire sidewall) so the tires on each side match. Even a few pounds of difference can affect steering.
3. **Inspect the wheels.** Spin each wheel by hand and look for wobble, cracks, bent rims or resistance.
4. **Zero-turn: check the dampers**, then adjust the tracking if one tire still turns faster ([Adjust the tracking](../procedures/adjust-the-tracking.md)).
5. **Zero-turn: check for a hung-up brake.** Raise the rear drive wheels on jack stands, disengage the hydros with the bypass levers, release the parking brake and spin each wheel for resistance.
6. **Remove or rebalance any attachment or towed load.**
7. **Walk-behind: level the deck** and, on a self-propelled mower, make sure both drive wheels engage evenly; check the drive cable tension and the drive belt.
8. **Then the hydraulics:** purge trapped air ([Purge the hydraulic system](../procedures/purge-the-hydraulic-system.md)) and check the hydraulic oil level and age ([Change the hydraulic oil](../procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil.md)).

## Brand notes

- **Zero-turn brand tables (Ariens, Bad Boy, Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Exmark, Ferris, Gravely, Husqvarna, Hustler, John Deere, Kubota, SCAG, Simplicity, Snapper, Spartan, Toro, Troy-Bilt):** the same three rows: unequal tire pressure, bad dampers, tracking adjustment (speed adjustment bolt, see the owner's manual).
- **Riding mowers and lawn tractors (Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Husqvarna, John Deere, Kubota, Simplicity, Snapper, Toro, Troy-Bilt):** worn or loose steering components (bushings, washers, gears) can stop the mower steering one way; inspect and replace failed parts.
- **Bad Boy, Ferris, lawn tractors:** a weak hydrostatic transmission (bad drive belt or pulley, old or low hydraulic oil, a leak) makes the mower pull to the weak side. Some transmissions are sealed and can't be serviced; take them to a dealer.
- **Husqvarna zero-turns:** the transmission may fail on one side; consult a Husqvarna dealer.

## Tips & life hacks

- Always start troubleshooting by checking that the tire pressures are equal on each side.
- Don't just compensate with the steering levers: fix the cause, which could be a bigger issue.
- Changing your mowing pattern balances wear on the mower and gives a more even cut.

## Safety

- Raise the mower only on secure jack stands, with the front wheels chocked, before spinning the drive wheels.
- Don't exceed a zero-turn's towing capacity: its transmissions are not designed for heavy loads.
- Follow the safety instructions in the operator's manual, and consult a professional if you don't have the skills to do the repair safely.

## When to send to a technician

- The mower still veers after the easy checks: the problem may be deeper in the drive system or chassis.
- A sealed hydraulic system, a bigger transaxle problem, or a transmission pump or wheel motor failure: take it to a repair shop or dealer.
