# Mower is noisy

## Summary

Mowers are loud by design: they sit low to the ground, hit every bump, and the blades spin fast. Normal is a steady engine hum at idle, a consistent whir with the blades engaged and a smooth, even vibration. Abnormal is a squealing belt, grinding metal, rattling parts, knocking, or a high-pitched noise that changes with engine speed or blade movement. The kind of noise points to the cause: squealing to belts and bearings, grinding to spindle bearings, thudding to the blades, knocking from the engine to low oil or the engine, and a loud or rattling exhaust to the muffler. How well the mower is maintained is one of the biggest factors in how loud it gets.

## Quick triage

1. **Stop mowing** until the noise is found and fixed.
2. **Check the belt first**: a belt that slipped and stuck between the belt guides must be reinstalled correctly around the pulleys; replace it if it is worn, cracked, glazed or stretched.
3. **Check the tensioner spring**, and remove any material wrapped around the blade shaft.
4. **Check the pulley and spindle bearings** for play or noise; replace a bad pulley or spindle assembly.
5. **Check the blades**: each one tight, free of chips or warping, and balanced; check under the deck for debris.
6. **Check the oil level** and the air filter; for small engine noises, a full [tune-up](../procedures/tune-up.md) often helps.
7. **Lubricate moving parts**, especially bearings, to cut squeaking and squealing.

## Brand notes

- **Cub Cadet:** a high-pitched squeal from the deck comes when the belt runs too loose or too tight, when the wrong type of belt is used, or when a pulley has a worn bearing.
- **Zero-turns:** some allow the spindle housing bearing or the pulley bearing to be replaced on its own, instead of the whole assembly.

## Tips & life hacks

- Prevent noise with maintenance: sharpen the blades, inspect the belts, tighten hardware and lubricate moving parts.
- Lubricate bearings especially (WD-40 or similar): it cuts noise and saves bearing replacements.
- When the mower is extremely loud, a tune-up should check the blades, deck spindles, belts, pulleys, rollers, air filter, fuel cap and pull cord.
- Many mowers last 7 to 10 years (one Source's estimate, not true for every maker); if it is still extremely loud after everything has been tried, it may be due for replacement.
