# Battery keeps dying

## Summary

The riding mower or zero-turn battery goes flat again and again: it's dead every time you go to mow, or you have to charge it after every use, or it charges on the charger but drains while the engine runs. The Sources give five checks: loose or corroded battery cables and terminals, dirty or loose wiring connections, a charging system that isn't recharging the battery, a bad battery, and the ignition key left on. Most batteries last at least 3 years with proper care; needing a new one more often points to a connection or battery problem.

## Quick triage

1. **Make it safe.** Wear gear that protects your skin and eyes around the battery, and disconnect the negative (black) cable before any electrical repair.
2. **Turn the key off** and unplug any 12-volt accessory after mowing; charge the battery ([Charge the battery](../procedures/charge-the-battery.md)).
3. **Clean and tighten the cables and terminals**, and make sure the battery is held down securely in its tray.
4. **Test the battery** ([Test the battery](../procedures/test-the-battery.md)); replace a battery that won't charge fully or keeps losing charge.
5. **Run the engine at high speed** rather than idling for long periods.
6. **Test the charging output** with the engine running. If the battery and wiring are good and secure but it still keeps needing a charge, suspect the charging system.

## Brand notes

- **Bad Boy, Cub Cadet, Husqvarna, Hustler, John Deere, SCAG, Spartan, Walker zero-turns and riding mowers:** the brand starts-then-dies articles all say: run at high engine speed and don't idle for long, because the battery charges from engine power; if the battery and wiring are good and secure but it keeps needing a charge, suspect the charging system and have an experienced mechanic diagnose it.
- **Bad Boy, John Deere:** most batteries fail from vibration, which breaks up the plates inside; make sure the battery sits securely in the tray and doesn't bounce.
- **Bad Boy, Troy-Bilt:** some models have a 12-volt accessory port that drains the battery if a cord is left plugged in with the key on.

## Tips & life hacks

- Keep the cables clean and tight and the battery held down.
- Don't let the battery freeze: keep it fully charged in cold weather, since a battery that isn't fully charged can freeze and be damaged.
- Charge the battery fully before winter storage; it can lose charge in the off-season and may need charging again before the first spring start.
- How long a charge lasts: one Source claims a fully charged lawn mower battery typically lasts about one hour, and that a full (especially trickle) charge reduces how often it needs charging. That is this Source's claim; no other Source gives a run time.

## Safety

- Wear gear that protects your skin and eyes when working around the battery.
- Always disconnect the negative (black) cable from the battery before any electrical repair.
- Follow the operator's manual safety instructions, and consult a professional if you lack the skills to do the repair safely.

## When to send to a technician

A charging-system fault: many components can fail, throwing parts at it gets expensive, and electrical parts usually can't be returned. Have an experienced mechanic diagnose and repair it.
