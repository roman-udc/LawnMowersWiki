# Engine dies when hot

## Summary

The engine runs fine at first, then shuts off once it has been running a while and is hot, sometimes with smoke. The Sources name two groups of causes: some make the engine overheat and shut down (oil that is low, wrong or overfilled, plugged cooling fins, a plugged deck or dull blades loading the engine, a dirty air filter), and others make it stop after running a while, which is easy to mistake for a hot shutdown (old fuel, a dirty carburetor, a plugged fuel cap vent, a fouled spark plug). An ignition coil whose windings separate when hot is named by all but one of the Sources that list causes. Don't keep running an engine that gets extremely hot.

## Quick triage

1. **Make it safe.** Let the engine cool and the moving parts stop, remove the key (if used) and the spark plug wire(s) before any check.
2. **Check the oil.** Drain a little if it is overfilled; top up or change to the right air-cooled engine oil if it is low or wrong.
3. **Clean the cooling fins**, around the engine block and under the engine shroud; replace broken fins.
4. **Scrape the deck** and avoid mowing wet grass; sharpen dull blades.
5. **Clean or replace the air filter.**
6. **Drain old fuel** and refill with fresh gas plus a fuel system cleaner and stabilizer; clean the carburetor if the problem continues.
7. **Loosen the gas cap** to test the vent, and check the spark plug.
8. **Test the ignition coil** with an ohmmeter once hot problems point to it; replace it on a break in continuity.

## Brand notes

- **Cub Cadet, Husqvarna, Simplicity:** for the wrong oil type or viscosity, the brand articles say to use an air-cooled engine oil like the oils provided by Kawasaki (the author's overheating article has a viscosity chart).
- **Husqvarna:** with too little oil, take it to a small engine shop or Husqvarna dealership; it is often too late to save the engine.
- **Cub Cadet:** the shutdown might not be overheating at all; the brand's general problems article lists other reasons.
- **Honda:** the ignition coil windings separate and short out when the mower is hot.

## Tips & life hacks

- Scrape the deck regularly and avoid mowing wet grass, so the blades turn easily and the engine isn't loaded.
- Add a fuel system cleaner and stabilizer (for example Sea Foam or STA-BIL) with fresh gas.
- Some causes make the mower stop after running a while rather than overheat; it is easy to confuse the two, so work through the fuel and air checks as well as the cooling checks.

## Parts commonly needed

- Ignition coil.
- Air filter and spark plug.
- Air-cooled engine oil of the right type and viscosity.
- Fuel cap, if the vent is plugged.
- Mower blades (sharpened or replaced).

## Safety

- Don't keep running an engine that gets extremely hot.
- Wait for the engine to cool and all moving parts to stop. Remove the ignition key (if used) and the spark plug wire(s) or boots so the mower can't start or move while you work.
- Disconnect the spark plug wire(s) before draining oil, and follow the owner's manual.
- Beware of hot parts and sharp blades.

## When to send to a technician

- The engine shut down from low oil: an oil change rarely fixes it, and a small engine mechanic should test for internal damage.
- An internal engine problem is suspected (piston rings, valve train, engine gasket): take it to a small engine shop.
- Engine and charging-system problems may be better left to an experienced mechanic at a dealer or repair shop.
