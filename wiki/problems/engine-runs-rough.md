# Engine runs rough

## Summary

The engine keeps running but sputters, misfires, surges, hesitates or bogs down, as if it's running out of gas. The Sources trace it to two groups: the engine isn't getting enough air, fuel or spark (dirty carburetor, fouled spark plug, old or water-contaminated fuel, a clogged fuel filter or line, a fuel cap that no longer vents, a plugged air filter, a wrong choke setting), or the engine is overworked (mowing too fast, not running at full throttle, a plugged deck, dull blades). Sputtering that is dealt with right away usually doesn't mean engine damage; ignored, it can cause significant damage.

## Quick triage

1. **Make it safe.** Park on a flat surface, let the engine cool and all parts stop moving, remove the ignition key and the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s), and follow the operator's manual. Work on the fuel system in a well-ventilated area.
2. **Ease the load.** Slow the ground speed in tall, thick or wet grass, and put the throttle in its highest position when you engage the blades.
3. **Check the air filter and choke.** Clean or replace a plugged filter ([Clean or replace the air filter](../procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.md)). The choke must open once the engine is warm; check the choke lever placement, cable and plate.
4. **Check the fuel.** Drain old fuel and refill with fresh gas and a stabilizer. Look for water at the bottom of the tank. Clear a clogged fuel line with carburetor cleaner and compressed air, or replace it if it can't be cleared or is cracked ([Replace the fuel line](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-line.md)). Replace a plugged fuel filter ([Replace the fuel filter](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter.md)) and a gas cap that no longer vents.
5. **Check the spark plug.** Clean a dirty plug with a wire brush; replace it if it's very dark, worn or damaged, gap it correctly and attach the wire securely ([Replace the spark plug](../procedures/replace-the-spark-plug.md)).
6. **Clean the carburetor.** Remove it to clean it, or rebuild or replace it ([Clean the carburetor](../procedures/clean-the-carburetor.md), [Rebuild the carburetor](../procedures/rebuild-the-carburetor.md)).
7. **Scrape the deck and sharpen the blades.** Remove, sharpen and balance dull blades before reinstalling them ([Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md)).
8. **Tune up the engine each season** to keep it running smoothly ([Tune-up](../procedures/tune-up.md)).

## Brand notes

- **Kawasaki, Craftsman, Husqvarna, Cub Cadet, Toro:** their carburetor-cleaning articles describe a failing carburetor as sputtering, running rough and surging; the Craftsman article adds using too much fuel.
- **Riding mowers:** a poorly running engine may stall when you try to drive the mower, and can leave an uneven cut if it doesn't run smoothly while mowing.
- **Bad Boy, Ferris, Cub Cadet zero-turn, Husqvarna zero-turn, Kubota zero-turn:** the engine is sluggish with the blades engaged if the throttle isn't at its fastest setting.
- The brand articles for Hustler, John Deere, SCAG and Simplicity give the same air, fuel, spark and load checks; the Hustler and Cub Cadet runs-rough articles are near-duplicates.

## Tips & life hacks

- Look first at what limits air, fuel and spark, then at what puts extra strain on the engine.
- Routine maintenance and checking the oil before mowing prevent rough running.
- [Too much oil](../causes/overfilled-oil.md) in the crankcase can foul the spark plug and make the engine run rough.
- If old fuel was the problem and fresh gas doesn't help, check the air and fuel filters next; a gummed carburetor may need rebuilding.

## Safety

- Park on a flat surface, remove the ignition key and the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s), and wait for the engine to cool and all moving parts to stop before repairs. Follow the operator's manual.
- Never reach under the deck until all moving parts have stopped, and protect your hands from the blades.
- Work on the fuel system in a well-ventilated area.
