# Mower won't move

## Summary

The engine runs, but the riding mower or zero-turn won't drive forward or reverse, or it moves slowly and feels weak (a "weak hydrostatic transmission"). Most riding mowers and zero-turns today drive the wheels with a hydrostatic transmission: an engine-driven belt turns a hydraulic pump, and the fluid it pumps turns the wheels. Anything that breaks that chain stops the mower: drive release (bypass) levers left open after pushing the mower, a worn or thrown drive belt, a failed tensioner pulley or idler spring, or hydraulic oil that is low, old, hot or full of air. On a lawn tractor, a missing axle key or a transaxle left in freewheel does the same.

Start with the simple checks. Owners often fear an expensive transmission failure, but the fix is usually a lever, a belt, a pulley, a spring or fresh oil.

## Quick triage

1. **Bypass levers.** Check the drive release (bypass) levers, rods or knobs (one per side on a zero-turn, one at the rear on a lawn tractor) are in the operating position. On a transaxle, check the freewheel control isn't in freewheel. Several Sources say to check this first.
2. **Parking brake.** Make sure the brake isn't left on.
3. **Drive belt.** Engine off: check the pump (or ground) drive belt is on, seated around the pulleys, and not worn, cracked, frayed or glazed.
4. **Tensioner pulley and idler arm spring.** Spin the tensioner pulley for a failed bearing; check the spring is there and not broken or stretched.
5. **Hydraulic oil.** Check the level; change old oil at the interval the manufacturer recommends. Some transmissions are sealed and can't be serviced.
6. **Air.** After an oil change, bleed (purge) the air or the mower won't reach normal speed.
7. **Heat.** If it runs fine cold but goes weak when hot, let it cool and have a dealer check the transmission.
8. **Lawn tractor axle key** after a tire change.
9. **Not the drive at all:** if the engine sputters or shuts off when you try to drive, check the battery and charging system, fuel filter and lines, air filter and carburetor.

## Brand notes

- **Weak hydrostatic transmission (Gravely, SCAG, Snapper, Hustler, Spartan, Toro, Troy-Bilt, Cub Cadet zero-turn, Craftsman):** these roundups list a "weak hydro" separately; the causes are the same drive belt, tensioner pulley (replace it and grease the tensioner arm) and low or old hydraulic oil.
- **Sealed transmissions (Craftsman, Husqvarna, John Deere, Kubota, Snapper, Toro, Troy-Bilt, Bad Boy):** some models, often entry-level ones, have sealed hydraulic systems the owner can't service; take them to the dealer.
- **Zero-turns (Ariens, Husqvarna, Kubota, John Deere):** a pump and wheel motor on each side; the pressure is so high you can't push the mower without bypassing the transmission.
- **Husqvarna:** riding and zero-turn hydrostatic transmissions have different service procedures; see the owner's manual.
- **Kubota and Walker won't-move articles:** the intros say the levers being "in the operating position" stops the mower; the body text of both says they must be in the operating position to move. Follow the body.

## Tips & life hacks

- Don't assume the transmission first: check levers, belt, pulley, spring and oil before paying for a transmission repair.
- Never tow the mower or pull it with other equipment: it can damage the transmission. Open the bypass and push it.
- Purge air from the transaxle before first use and after any transaxle repair.
- Change the hydraulic oil (and filter, where fitted) on the manufacturer's schedule and check the level now and then.

## Safety

- Follow the operator's manual. Turn the engine off, remove the key and the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s), and wait for all parts to stop moving before working on the drive.
- Let the transmission cool before repairs or an oil change; hot hydraulic fluid can burn.
- Work on a flat, level surface. Use the bypass levers only on level ground with the parking brake set.

## When to send to a technician

- The mower runs fine cold but gets weak or stops when hot.
- The hydraulic pump leaks, or the fluid is hot and leaking.
- The transmission is sealed and can't be serviced.
- You lack the skills, tools or condition to do the repair safely.
