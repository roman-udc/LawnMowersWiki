# Engine overheats

## Summary

The engine runs hotter than it should. Usually the first sign is a hot smell (hot metal, burning oil or plastic); the engine may feel unusually hot, start smoking, sputter, or shut off in the middle of mowing and refuse to restart until it cools. The Sources sort the causes into two groups: the engine isn't being cooled or lubricated properly (low, wrong or too much oil, plugged or damaged cooling fins, a missing or loose engine guard, a blocked cooling fan, a dirty air filter), or it is being overworked (plugged deck, dull blades, long, thick or wet grass, mowing too fast). Most causes are easy to find and fix, but keep running a hot engine and the heat can warp metal parts, crack seals, wear gaskets and permanently damage the engine.

## Quick triage

1. **Stop at the first hot smell.** Shut off the engine, remove the ignition key, wait for the engine to cool, and remove the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s) before any repair. Follow the operator's manual.
2. **Check the oil.** Use an oil designed for air-cooled engines, of a viscosity suited to the outdoor temperature, and keep the level full. Drain and refill if it's the wrong oil; change it if it's dark or low; drain some if it's overfilled ([Change the engine oil](../procedures/change-the-engine-oil.md), [Drain excess oil](../procedures/drain-excess-oil.md)).
3. **Clean the cooling fins and around the engine.** Remove the engine cover and clear debris with a soft brush or compressed air; bent fins don't cool well, so be gentle and replace damaged fins ([Clean the cooling fins](../procedures/clean-the-cooling-fins.md)).
4. **Check the engine guard.** Put it back in position, tighten or replace its mounting screws, and replace a missing or damaged guard. On a riding mower, clear debris from the cooling fan and its shroud.
5. **Replace a very dirty air filter** ([Clean or replace the air filter](../procedures/clean-or-replace-the-air-filter.md)).
6. **Lighten the load.** Scrape the deck, sharpen and balance dull blades ([Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md)), slow down, avoid wet grass, and double- or triple-cut an overgrown lawn.

## Brand notes

- **Bad Boy, Cub Cadet zero-turns:** use an air-cooled engine oil that is high in zinc; 10W-30 suits most conditions (SAE30 is also named for Cub Cadet), and very warm conditions may need a different viscosity. Keep the level full.
- **Craftsman, Husqvarna, Kubota:** if the trouble continues after an oil change, running low on oil may have caused extensive damage; take it to the brand's servicing dealer or a small engine dealer for more tests.
- **Electric mowers:** grass and debris caught in the motor's air vents block the air that cools it; the mower can overheat, shut down and potentially catch fire.
- **Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Husqvarna, push mowers, riding mowers:** an overheated engine shuts down, so overheating also shows up as a mower that won't stay running.

## Tips & life hacks

- Don't spray water into the engine when you wash the mower: it pushes dirt and water into the engine area and makes fin clogging worse.
- Debris around the engine blocks airflow; keep the mower clean.
- Prevent overheating: clean around the engine and cooling fins regularly, replace the oil and air filter on schedule, use the oil and fuel grade recommended for the engine, do seasonal maintenance, and in extreme heat mow in the cooler parts of the day.
- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Engine cooling times](../guides/engine-cooling-times.md).

## Safety

- When the engine smells hot, stop running it right away to avoid extensive engine damage.
- Stop the mower, remove the ignition key, wait for the engine to cool, and remove the spark plug wire(s) or boot(s) before repairs. Follow the operator's manual.
- Electric mower: turn it off at once, unplug the cord if it's corded, and let it cool. Check the motor for damage before trying to restart it, and consult a specialist if you're not sure it's safe.
- If you lack the skills to do the repair safely, consult a professional.

## When to send to a technician

- Problems continue after an oil change: low oil may have caused extensive damage; a servicing dealer or small engine dealer can run further tests.
- Low oil may have damaged the engine: have a small engine mechanic diagnose it.
