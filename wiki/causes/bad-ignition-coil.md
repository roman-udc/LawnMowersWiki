# Bad ignition coil

## Summary

The ignition coil makes the energy for the spark at the spark plug. Each time the flywheel magnet passes the coil, the coil sends current to the plug. If the coil fails there's no spark, and the engine can't start. Replacing it is a moderate job: take off the blower housing, swap the coil and set its air gap.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-coil.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

Coils also fail with age and heat. When the coil gets hot its windings can separate and short out, so the plug loses voltage and the engine dies after running a while ([S97](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/why-your-mower-loses-power-when-the-blades-are-engaged.md), [S93](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/zero-turn-mower-quits-when-hot.md)).

## Before you start

- Is the spark plug itself good? Rule out a fouled spark plug first.
- Which engine does the mower have? The manufacturer guide covers Briggs & Stratton engines. Check the air-gap spec for yours.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the engine doesn't fire even with starter fluid. A spark tester shows no spark with a good plug, or the spark plug wire is damaged, cracked or dried out.
- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): two brand articles say that if the key is turned and the mower doesn't start or even turn over, the ignition switch or the ignition coil could be the problem.

## How to confirm

1. Check the spark plug wire. If it is damaged, replace the ignition coil, because the wire is part of the coil.
2. Remove the spark plug and inspect it. If it looks okay, reinstall it.
3. Connect a spark plug tester between the plug and the plug wire, crank the engine and watch the tester.
4. If the tester doesn't spark, the coil probably needs replacing. If it sparks, the coil is good. Check for a sheared flywheel key (spark timing) next, then low compression.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-coil.md)

5. **Continuity test (most Sources):** once the spark plug is known to be good, check the coil with an ohmmeter or multimeter. A break in continuity means the coil must be replaced.
6. Look at the coil wire: cracked, worn or exposed wire can ground out the coil ([S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bolens-mower-wont-start.md), [S77](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-push-mower-wont-start.md)).
7. On a lawn tractor, remove the kill wire. If a known good plug still gets no spark, the coil is the likely cause ([S96](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/lawn-tractor-won-t-start.ridtr04.md)).
8. For a coil that only fails hot, run the mower until it stalls and check for spark immediately ([S97](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/why-your-mower-loses-power-when-the-blades-are-engaged.md)).

## Fix

Replace the coil. The manufacturer rates it difficulty 3 and about 30 minutes.

1. Disconnect the battery and remove the blower housing.
2. Turn the flywheel so the magnet points away from the coil.
3. Unbolt the old coil and disconnect its kill wire.
4. Move the spark plug boot to the new coil and connect the kill wire.
5. Mount the new coil and set the air gap with a feeler gauge.

- **Ignition coil:** use a manufacturer-approved coil for your engine. The spark plug wire comes with the coil. Move the old spark plug boot onto it.

## Tips & life hacks

- **Setting the gap:** turn the flywheel so the magnet lines up with the coil, and put the feeler gauge between them. Loosen the mounting screws so the magnet pulls the coil onto the gauge, then tighten the screws. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-ignition-coil.md)
- Check the plug wire for drying and cracking, which can make it ground out. Source: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

- Always check the spark plug first. Rule out a bad plug before testing the coil. Source: [S96](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/lawn-tractor-won-t-start.ridtr04.md)
