# Air in the hydraulic system

## Summary

Air gets trapped in the hydrostatic transmission when the hydraulic fluid is changed or topped up and the system isn't bled (purged) afterwards, or any time the transmission is opened. The trapped air keeps the mower from moving at normal speed. Riding-mower automatic transaxles can also pick up air bubbles from driving over rough terrain. On a zero-turn each rear wheel has its own pump and wheel motor, so air on one side slows only that wheel and the mower pulls to that side. The fix is easy: purge the air by running the drive wheels forward and reverse while they're off the ground.

## Before you start

- Was the hydraulic fluid recently changed or added, or was the transmission opened for repair? Air is the likely cause if the problem started right after.
- Is the slowness on both sides, or only one wheel?
- Has the mower been driven over rough terrain (riding mowers with an automatic transaxle)?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower won't move](../problems/mower-wont-move.md): the mower moves slowly or not at all, especially right after a fluid change; the transmission is noisy (cavitation) when the wheels turn; the oil gets hot and expands more than normal. On a zero-turn, air on one side can make one wheel weak or dead, so the mower pulls or won't drive straight.

## How to confirm

- Check the timing: slow or no movement that starts after a fluid change points to trapped air.
- Listen with the drive wheels turning: excessive noise from the wheel or transmission is the sign of cavitation from air.
- Raise the rear drive tires off the ground and run them forward and reverse. If they speed up and the noise goes away as you cycle them, air was the problem.

Sources: [S22](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/zero-turn-hydrostatic-transmission-problems.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-zero-turn-mower-wont-move-forward-or-reverse-fix-it.md), [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/mower-wont-move-or-go-forward.md)

## Fix

Bleed (purge) the air from the hydraulic system after every fluid change or addition, and any time the transmission has been opened. On most mowers you raise the rear drive tires off the ground and run them forward and reverse until there's no excessive noise and the wheels turn at normal speed. The exact steps vary by make and model, so follow the operator's manual.

## Brand notes

- Ariens, Cub Cadet, John Deere: on most of their zero-turns, set the mower on a level surface, chock the wheels, raise the rear drive tires and let them spin forward and backward until the noise stops and the wheels move at normal speed.
- Hustler: with the rear tires raised and the mower running, cycle the drive levers forward and reverse about 6 times with the bypass valve open, then about 6 times with it closed, then check the hydraulic fluid level.
- Ariens, Kubota, SCAG, Troy-Bilt: follow the bleeding procedure in the brand's owner's manual.
- Husqvarna: bleeding applies to serviceable transmissions after a fluid change.

## Parts & fitment

No parts are needed to purge air. If the purge lowers the oil level, top up with the hydraulic oil the manufacturer specifies.

Sources: [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/hustler-zero-turn-wont-move-forward.md), [S19](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/simplicity-zero-turn-wont-move-forward-reverse.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Plan the purge as part of every hydraulic fluid change, not as a separate repair: skipping it is what causes this fault.
- Chock the wheels that stay on the ground so the mower can't roll forward while the rear is raised.
