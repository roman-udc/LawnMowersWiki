# Clogged fuel filter

## Summary

The fuel filter strains fuel as it leaves the tank, so dirt and sediment don't reach the carburetor and engine. If it isn't replaced regularly, it plugs up with dirt and with sticky deposits from old gas. Then too little fuel gets past it, and the engine turns over but won't start, or runs rough and stops. Replacing the filter is easy and takes 15 minutes or less.

## Before you start

- When was the fuel filter last changed? More than a year ago makes it a strong suspect.
- Has the mower been running on old or dirty fuel?
- Where is the filter? Most mowers use an inline filter between the fuel lines. If you can't find one, it may sit at the bottom of the fuel tank.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the filter hasn't been changed in over a year, and little or no fuel flows past it to the carburetor.
- A running engine that is short of fuel: it runs sluggishly, or needs the choke on to stay running.

## How to confirm

- Shut off the fuel with the shut-off valve or fuel pinch-off pliers. Pull the line off below the filter, put the end in a container set lower than the tank, and turn the fuel back on. Weak or no flow out of the filter points to a clog (check the line above it too).
- A filter that looks dark from straining dirty fuel is a sign of contaminated fuel.
- If the filter is over a year old, the manufacturer Source simply says to replace it.

## Fix

Replace the old or dirty fuel filter with a new one. For an inline filter:

1. Disconnect the spark plug wire (both wires on a 2-cylinder engine) and let the engine cool.
2. Clamp hose pinch-off pliers on the fuel line on each side of the filter. Put a shop rag underneath to catch drips.
3. Release the hose clamps and pull the hoses off the old filter. Wipe up spilled fuel.
4. Fit the new filter with its arrow pointing in the direction of fuel flow: toward the carburetor, away from the tank. Secure the hose clamps.
5. Remove the pinch-off pliers and reconnect the spark plug wire.

For a filter at the bottom of the tank, drain the tank first.

## Parts & fitment

- **Fuel filter:** match the type (inline or in-tank) and the fuel line size of your mower. Note the flow arrow on the housing.
- Tools: two hose pinch-off pliers, pliers, a shop rag and work gloves.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-isnt-getting-gas.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-fuel-filter.md)

- If the old filter's plastic has gone soft, work carefully: its ends can break off inside the hose ([S38](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-fuel.md)).
- Tools: a shut-off valve, or fuel pinch pliers if there's no valve.

Sources: [S129](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-why-your-riding-lawn-mower-loses-power.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Change the fuel filter at least once a year, for example at the yearly tune-up. Change it more often if you run old or dirty fuel. It's an inexpensive part that protects the fuel system and engine. Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-isnt-getting-gas.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-fuel-filter.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/briggs-and-stratton-engine-isnt-getting-gas.md), [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-isnt-getting-fuel.md), [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-runs-with-choke-on.md), [S16](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/honda-lawn-mower-isnt-getting-gas.md)
- When fitting the new filter, always check the flow arrow on the housing. Source: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md)
- Check the filter before rebuilding or replacing the carburetor, so you don't pay for a part you don't need. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)

- If dirty fuel went into the tank, drain it and replace the filter. Replace it more often than yearly with heavy or commercial use.
- Most push mowers don't use a fuel filter.
