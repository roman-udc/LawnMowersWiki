# Battery not seated

## Summary

On a battery mower the pack has to be latched in its compartment with its contacts touching the compartment contacts. If it was installed wrong, isn't latched, or dirt and debris in the compartment block the contacts, the mower won't start or stops while mowing. The fix is easy: clean the contacts and reseat the battery until it clicks.

## How to confirm

Check that the battery is securely in place. On most models you hear a click as it snaps in and the battery release button springs into place; a battery that hasn't clicked isn't latched.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/battery-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/dewalt-electric-mower-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/reasons-why-your-ego-electric-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ryobi-electric-battery-mower-wont-start.md)

## Fix

1. Wipe around the battery compartment lid before opening it, so dirt doesn't fall in.
2. Press the battery release button and remove the battery.
3. Wipe the battery contacts and clear dirt and debris out of the compartment.
4. Line the battery up with the guides (grooves or rails on its sides) and slide it in until it clicks.
5. Close the compartment lid securely.

## Brand notes

- **DeWalt:** open the battery compartment lid and slide the battery in until the release button springs into place with a click.
- **EGO:** on most EGO mowers you hear a click and the battery release button springs into place; brush out a dirty compartment.
- **Greenworks:** the battery slides into the compartment's grooves and clicks when seated.
- **Sun Joe, Toro, WORX:** the external battery slides into guide rails and snaps in; the lid must close securely.
