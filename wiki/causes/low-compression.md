# Low compression

## Summary

A spinning engine needs fuel, spark and compression to run. If the piston doesn't compress the air and fuel in the cylinder, the engine won't start, even with good fuel and spark. Possible reasons include valves out of adjustment, worn piston rings or a damaged cylinder wall. Finding the exact cause needs a leak-down test, so this is a pro-level fix.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

## Before you start

- Have you confirmed that fuel, air and spark are all fine? Check compression last.
- Is the crankcase overfilled with oil?
- Does the engine have an automatic compression release? Many small engines do.
- What is the compression spec for your engine model?

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the engine doesn't fire even with starter fluid. The spark plug and ignition check out, and a compression gauge reads under about 40 psi. Low compression can also make an engine run rough.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/runs-rough-or-misfires.ridtr03.md)

## How to confirm

1. Check the oil level. If the engine is overfilled, drain oil until the level is below the maximum mark.
2. Remove the spark plug and connect a compression gauge through the spark plug hole. Zero the gauge.
3. Briefly turn the key to start so the starter spins the engine, then read the gauge.
4. Most engines read over 40 psi, and many read over 80 psi. Under 40 psi means the piston isn't compressing air. The test shows that a compression problem exists, but not what causes it.
5. On engines with an automatic compression release, a leak-down test is a better check than a compression test.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

## Fix

Have a service technician do a leak-down test. It uses an air compressor and special tools to find where the air leaks. Depending on what the test finds, the repair may be a valve adjustment, new piston rings or a cylinder wall repair.

## Parts & fitment

- **Piston rings:** worn rings are one possible cause. A technician should confirm this with a leak-down test before any parts are ordered.
- **Tools for the check:** cylinder compression gauge and spark plug wrench. A leak-down tester is used by technicians.
