# Low compression

## Summary

A spinning engine needs fuel, spark and compression to run. If the piston doesn't compress the air and fuel in the cylinder, the engine won't start, even with good fuel and spark. Possible reasons include valves out of adjustment or other valve-train trouble, worn piston rings, a blown head gasket or a damaged cylinder wall. Finding the exact cause needs a leak-down test, so this is a pro-level fix.

## Before you start

- Have you confirmed that fuel, air and spark are all fine? Check compression last.
- Is the crankcase overfilled with oil?
- Does the engine have an automatic compression release? Many small engines do.
- What is the compression spec for your engine model?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the engine doesn't fire even with starter fluid. The spark plug and ignition check out, and a compression gauge reads under about 40 psi. - Engine runs rough or misfires: low cylinder compression makes the engine run rough, and it may not start at all.
- Mower loses power when the blades are engaged: once the basics check out, low compression or valve trouble is the more serious engine problem to suspect.

## How to confirm

1. Quick check: disconnect the spark plug wire and slowly turn the blade backward to the compression stroke. You should feel some resistance. This doesn't prove compression is high enough, so follow up with a gauge or a leak-down test.
2. Check the oil level. If the engine is overfilled, drain oil until the level is below the maximum mark.
3. Remove the spark plug and connect a compression gauge through the spark plug hole. Zero the gauge.
4. Briefly turn the key to start so the starter spins the engine, then read the gauge.
5. Most engines read over 40 psi, and many read over 80 psi. Under 40 psi means the piston isn't compressing air. The test shows that a compression problem exists, but not what causes it.
6. On engines with an automatic compression release (ACR), a leak-down test is a better check than a compression test, unless the engine maker publishes ACR compression specs.

> **Safety:** disconnect the spark plug wire before turning the blade by hand.

## Fix

Have a service technician do a leak-down test. It uses an air compressor and special tools to find where the air leaks. Depending on what the test finds, the repair may be a valve adjustment, a head gasket, new piston rings or a cylinder wall repair. This is a job for an experienced small-engine mechanic or a servicing dealer.

## Parts & fitment

- **Head gasket, valves:** a blown head gasket or valve damage can also cause low compression. Sources: [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bolens-mower-wont-start.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-push-mower-wont-start.md)
- **Piston rings:** worn rings are one possible cause. A technician should confirm this with a leak-down test before any parts are ordered.
- **Tools for the check:** cylinder compression gauge and spark plug wrench. A leak-down tester is used by technicians.
