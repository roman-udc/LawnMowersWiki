# Overfilled oil

## Summary

Too much oil in the crankcase raises the pressure inside it. The crankshaft and connecting rod have to push through the extra oil and can't turn freely, so internal parts are loaded, the engine gets hot and it can overheat and shut down. The pressure also pushes oil through the valve train into the cylinder and up into the air intake. That oil burns off as bluish-white smoke, which can foul the spark plug and plug the air filter, starving the engine of air until it dies. A small-engine oil pan is small, so there is little room for error: a little extra oil matters. Draining the excess is easy. Running on with too much oil can damage seals, blow gaskets, overheat the engine or hydrolock it, and then the repair is pro work or an engine replacement.

## Before you start

- Did the trouble start right after an oil change or after topping up the oil?
- What crankcase capacity does the owner's manual give for this engine? Check the manual (or search online) before adding or removing oil.
- Does the engine have a drain plug or drain valve, an oil filter, or neither? Many push-mower engines have no drain plug.
- Is it a single-cylinder or a twin-cylinder engine? Gasket repairs on a twin cost more because the engine has to come out of the mower.

## How to confirm

- Read the dipstick: pull it out, wipe it with a clean rag, push it back in, pull it out again and read it. The oil should be within the full lines, no higher and no lower. Above the full or max mark means overfilled.
- On a riding mower that cranks but won't start, check the oil level for overfill once you've ruled out an ignition problem.
- Look for the other signs: bluish-white smoke, an oil-soaked or smoke-plugged air filter, an oily spark plug, oil leaking from the engine.

Sources: [S43](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kubota-lawn-mower-is-smoking.md), [S48](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-starts-smoking-after-an-oil-change.md), [S70](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/this-is-what-happens-if-you-put-too-much-oil-in-a-lawn-mower.md), [S78](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/too-much-oil-toro-mower.md), [S90](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/zero-turn-begins-smoking-after-an-oil-change.md), [S95](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)

## Fix

Drain or pull out a little oil at a time until the dipstick reads at the full line, then check the air filter and spark plug: replace the air filter if it's oil-covered or plugged with smoke, and clean or replace an oily plug. If the engine still misbehaves after the level is right, stop running it and have a small engine mechanic check it for internal damage; tearing the engine down yourself may cause more damage.

- [Drain excess oil](../procedures/drain-excess-oil.md) (the air filter and spark plug have their own procedures: clean or replace the air filter, replace the spark plug)

## Parts & fitment

- **Engine oil:** the type and amount the engine manufacturer specifies. Typical crankcase capacities: most push-mower engines about 3/4 quart, a V-twin about 2 quarts, large commercial "big block" engines (about 34 hp or larger) about 3 quarts (per Source, verify against the owner's manual).
- **Air filter:** replace it if it's coated with oil or plugged with smoke.
- **Spark plug:** clean or replace it if it's oil-covered.
