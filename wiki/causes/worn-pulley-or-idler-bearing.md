# Worn pulley or idler bearing

## Summary

Every pulley on the deck (and on the drive) spins on a bearing. A good pulley is secure, flat and parallel to the deck. When the bearing wears or fails, the pulley wobbles, sits higher on one side, shifts side to side with the deck engaged, turns with resistance or noise, or seizes. The belt then runs on an unstable pulley: the mower vibrates, the belt doesn't seat and can roll off, and the blades can't run at full speed, which gives a bad cut. Most often the failed bearing is in an idler pulley.

The deck idler pulley is the one that tightens the blade belt against the blade pulleys when the blade engagement lever is engaged, so a worn idler can also keep the blades from spinning at all. On the drive side, the tensioner pulley that keeps tension on the drive belt is often hard plastic with a bearing in the center: the pulley can wear or break, or the bearing can fail, so the belt loses tension or falls off and the mower won't move.

The fix is usually a new pulley with its bearing, done with common hand tools.

## Before you start

- Is the trouble vibration, a belt that keeps coming off, blades that won't engage, a bad cut, or a mower that won't move? Deck pulleys and the deck idler cause the first four; the drive-belt tensioner pulley causes the last.
- Shut the engine off, take the key out and remove the spark plug boot before touching belts or pulleys.
- Can you reach the pulleys? Remove the belt covers. Some zero-turns have a removable floor pan to reach the top of the deck; otherwise the deck comes off.
- Check belt routing and tension first, and take out any extra slack.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower is vibrating](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md): a pulley that wobbles or sits higher on one side shakes the mower as the belt runs over it; the deck vibrates.
- [Uneven cut](../problems/uneven-cut.md): the pulley shifts side to side with the deck engaged, or a worn idler can't keep the belt tight on the blade pulleys.
- [Belt keeps coming off](../problems/belt-keeps-coming-off.md): the belt doesn't seat on a pulley that isn't flat and parallel, and vibration rolls it off. Usually a bad idler bearing.
- [Blades won't engage](../problems/blades-wont-engage.md): the belt rolls off a rocking pulley, or a worn deck idler can't tighten the blade belt so the blades don't spin.
- [Mower won't move](../problems/mower-wont-move.md): a broken drive tensioner pulley or failed bearing lets the drive belt go slack or fall off. A tensioner pulley that can't keep tension on the drive belt also gives a weak hydrostatic drive.
- Noise: grinding or screeching from the deck usually means a worn pulley or spindle.

## How to confirm

1. With the engine off, the key out and the belt covers off, look at each pulley. It should be secure, flat and parallel to the deck, with neither side higher than the other.
2. Grab each side of the pulley and rock it up and down. Rocking, vertical movement or extra play means a bad bearing.
3. Turn each pulley slowly by hand. It should spin smoothly and silently. Restriction, grinding or bearing noise means replace it. The check is easier with the deck belt off.
4. A pulley that has seized, or a bearing that has blown out of the center of the pulley, needs no test: replace it.
5. While you're there, check the bearing and bushing on the blade engagement linkage, and check the idler bracket for play where it mounts to the deck. Any movement there means a new bracket.
6. Rust on pulleys dries out the belt so it breaks: clean it off, and replace a pulley that is extremely rusty.

## Fix

Replace the pulley. Some pulleys allow the bearing alone to be replaced, but idler pulleys (and riveted pulleys) have a sealed bearing, so the whole pulley and bearing assembly is replaced. SCAG's article says to replace a sealed pulley as a unit rather than fight to replace the bearing. Drive pulleys and spindle housings may have replaceable bearings; a spindle bearing is a different fault (see [bent spindle or bad spindle bearing](bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing.md)). For a drive-belt tensioner pulley, replace it and grease the tensioner arm.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** some articles say to replace the bearing first and the pulley assembly only when the bearing is sealed ([S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-mower-is-vibrating.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bad-boy-lawn-mower-is-vibrating.md), [S88](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/walker-mower-vibrating.md), [S93](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/wright-mower-vibrates-shakes.md), expert-blog), while the manufacturer Sources and most other articles just say replace the pulley ([S108](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/mower-deck-vibrates.ltrtr140.md), [S111](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/won-t-cut-grass.ridtr01.md), manufacturer; [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/belt-keeps-coming-off-your-bad-boy-lawn-mower.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/belt-keeps-coming-off-your-scag-lawn-mower.md), expert-blog). Bobcat, Kubota, Hustler and Cub Cadet articles say it varies by model ([S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bobcat-zero-turn-uneven-cut.md), [S54](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kubota-lawn-mower-cuts-uneven.md), [S45](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/hustler-mower-bad-uneven-cut.md), [S91](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-your-cub-cadet-lawn-mower-cuts-uneven.md)).

## Brand notes

- **Exmark:** an idler pulley assembly is a carriage bolt, lock nut, pulley and spacer. The spacer is sometimes mounted to the deck with an aluminum teardrop-shaped piece, or is a bushing (wider bottom, narrower top) that goes through the pulley. Exmark pulleys are riveted, so the bearing can't be replaced alone; Exmark sells pulleys with the bearing installed. A seized bearing can take out the bushing that holds the pulley (the bushing is not part of the bearing). ## Parts & fitment

- **Idler or deck pulley:** most come as a pulley and bearing assembly; buy that unless your pulley takes a separate bearing.
- **Mounting hardware:** an idler usually mounts with a carriage bolt, spacer (or bushing) and flanged lock nut. Have all of them before you start; a damaged carriage bolt is common.
- **Drive tensioner pulley:** often hard plastic with a center bearing; replace the whole pulley.

## Tips & life hacks

- Keep the tensioner arm and idler bracket greased so they don't seize. The idler bracket is designed to move and spring back: if it can't, the belt slips and the blades slip in heavy or deep grass, and the instant load when the blades are turned on can snap the belt.
- A worn idler bracket (the hole where the spring hooks on wears larger) or a stretched idler spring also causes vibration; replace them (see [Bad belt tensioner spring](bad-belt-tensioner-spring.md)).
- If the belt keeps falling off but isn't worn or stretched, check the tensioner pulley.
- Clean mud and debris out of the pulley grooves; it stretches the belt until it rolls off or snaps.
- A worn, cracked or glazed belt is a separate fault: see [worn or stretched belt](worn-or-stretched-belt.md).
