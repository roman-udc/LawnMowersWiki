# Dirty carburetor

## Summary

The carburetor mixes gas with air for combustion. The usual reason it stops working is old gas. Old gas leaves varnish and gummy, crusty deposits that plug the fuel jet and passages, or make internal parts like the float and needle stick. The engine then doesn't get enough fuel and cranks without starting. Ethanol in the fuel makes this worse. Fix difficulty: moderate (clean or rebuild). A repair shop can clean or rebuild it if you'd rather not handle small parts.

## Before you start

- Has the mower sat with gas in it? Gas can start to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase.
- Push mower or riding mower? A push mower carburetor typically has a bowl held by one screw and a stem in the center. Common Briggs & Stratton riding mower carburetors also carry a fuel solenoid valve on the float bowl.
- Rule out the cheap fuel-supply faults first (air filter, fuel filter, fuel line, fuel pump, fuel solenoid wire), so you don't buy a carburetor you don't need.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): good fuel flow reaches the carburetor, but the engine only fires when carburetor cleaner or starter fluid is sprayed into the intake. It then runs sluggishly and dies once the spray is burned.
- Other signs of a bad carburetor on a running mower: backfiring from running lean, sputtering and rough running, surging, using too much fuel, or only running with the choke on.

## How to confirm

1. Confirm good fuel flow to the carburetor. Check the fuel line running to it, and any fuel filter or fuel pump, for a restriction.
2. Remove the air filter.
3. Spray a short burst into the air intake and try to start the engine.
4. If it starts, runs briefly and dies, the carburetor is the likely cause. If it doesn't fire at all, look for an air restriction or a spark problem instead.

## Fix

Clean the carburetor first, rebuild it if cleaning doesn't work, and replace it if it is too corroded, dirty or damaged to rebuild. A stuck float or float needle can sometimes be freed for a while by tapping the carburetor with a rubber mallet, but it will stick again until the carburetor is taken apart and cleaned, rebuilt or replaced. In outline:

1. Take photos before and during teardown, for reassembly.
2. Shut off the fuel with the shut-off valve or pinch-off pliers, and catch any spilled fuel.
3. Disconnect the throttle and choke linkages, and the fuel solenoid wire if there is one. Remove the carburetor without tearing the gasket between it and the engine.
4. Remove the float bowl. Keep carburetor cleaner off the bowl gasket, or it must be replaced.
5. Clear the holes in the center stem and all the jets (especially the main and idle jets) with carburetor cleaner and a thin wire or paper clip. Remove gum and white crusty buildup, and check that the float and needle move freely.
6. On a rebuild, fit a new float bowl gasket, needle valve, float hinge pin, intake gasket and air intake O-ring. Let the carburetor dry for at least 10 minutes before reassembly.
7. Refill with fresh gas and a fuel stabilizer.

## Brand notes

- **Briggs & Stratton riding mower carburetors** (on Craftsman, Troy-Bilt, Husqvarna, MTD, Poulan, Toro, Murray, Snapper, Yard-Man and John Deere riding mowers and lawn tractors): the fuel solenoid valve threads into the float bowl and must come off before the bowl. The rebuild is rated difficulty 4 and takes about 60 minutes. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-rebuild-a-riding-lawn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Husqvarna, John Deere, SCAG with electronic fuel injection (EFI):** the fuel pump on these is tested with a fuel pressure gauge against the spec in the operator's manual. Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/scag-zero-turn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- **Zero-turns:** detach the throttle and choke cables and the air filter housing, then ease the springs off without stretching them (stretched springs must be replaced). Source: [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-zero-turn-mower-carburetor.md)
- **Cub Cadet:** when the mower only runs with the choke on, a deteriorated gasket behind the carburetor can be letting in extra air; replace it while the carburetor is off. Source: [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-runs-with-choke-on.md)

## Parts & fitment

- **Carburetor rebuild kit:** float bowl gasket, needle valve, float hinge pin, intake gasket and air intake O-ring. Match it to the engine model.
- **Carburetor:** replace it when cleaning and rebuilding fail, or when it is too corroded or damaged. Match the engine model.
- **Carburetor gasket:** a worn gasket behind the carburetor lets in extra air. Replace it when the carburetor is off and the gasket no longer seals.

## Tips & life hacks

- Don't tear down the carburetor until fuel flow to it is confirmed and the intake spray test points at it. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- If a riding mower only runs with the choke on, suspect a dirty carburetor or another fuel restriction. Sources: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-only-runs-choke-on.md), [S15](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-runs-with-choke-on.md)
- It is almost impossible to remove all of the white crusty material. Get off as much as you can. Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-push-mower-carburetor.md), [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-zero-turn-mower-carburetor.md)
- If cleaning doesn't fix it and you aren't sure the carburetor is the fault, have a repair shop diagnose it rather than throwing parts at the mower. Source: [S14](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-zero-turn-mower-carburetor.md)
- To keep it from happening again, use fresh gas with a stabilizer, and never gas with more than 10% ethanol. Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/clean-a-push-mower-carburetor.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-only-runs-choke-on.md)
