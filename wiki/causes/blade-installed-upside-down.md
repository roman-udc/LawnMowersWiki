# Blade installed upside down

## Summary

Every mower blade has a sail (also called the fin): the curved high side at the ends that moves air, lifts the grass upright and tosses the clippings. The sail must point up toward the deck. Installed upside down, the sail faces the ground: the blade beats, tears and bruises the grass instead of cutting it, the sail can hit the ground or high spots, and the impacts can damage the spindles and spindle housings (or the blade motor on battery mowers). It is common right after new blades go on: one Source's author has seen customers' new mowers cut terribly for this reason. The fix is easy: remove the blade and reinstall it "sail up".

## Before you start

- Were the blades just replaced or sharpened, or is the mower new? Upside-down blades are most common on newly installed blades.
- Does the blade have a stamp showing which side faces the ground ("BOTTOM", "THIS SIDE DOWN", "GRASS SIDE", "THIS SIDE FACING GRASS")?

## How to confirm

Make the mower safe first (key out and spark plug wire off, or battery pack removed) and look under the deck.

- The blade is upside down if the sail (fin) points down toward the ground.
- Look for a stamp: the side marked "BOTTOM", "THIS SIDE DOWN" or "GRASS SIDE" faces the ground. Bolt marks on the blade also show which way the bolt goes.
- Check the hardware too: the right parts in the right order per the owner's manual or parts diagram.

## Fix

Remove the blade and reinstall it with the sail pointing up toward the deck ("Sail up!"), using the correct hardware in the correct order, and tighten the nuts or bolt to the operator's manual spec. Balance a resharpened blade before installing it (new blades are already balanced). The steps are in [Sharpen and replace the blades](../procedures/sharpen-and-replace-the-blades.md).

If the mower ran with the blades upside down and hit the ground, check the spindles and spindle housings for damage (see [Bent spindle or bad spindle bearing](bent-spindle-or-bad-spindle-bearing.md)).

## Brand notes

- **Exmark:** OEM blades have the part number on the bottom of the blade; some aftermarket blades are stamped "BOTTOM" or "THIS SIDE DOWN". Source: [S31](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-bad-or-uneven-exmark-mower-cut.md)
- **EGO and other battery mowers (Greenworks, Kobalt, RYOBI, Sun Joe, Toro electric, Worx):** a blade installed sail-down can hit high spots and damage the blade motor. EGO blades may be marked "THIS SIDE DOWN" or "THIS SIDE FACING GRASS". Sources: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ego-electric-lawn-mower-is-cutting-uneven.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/greenworks-lawn-mower-is-cutting-uneven.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/kobalt-electric-mower-bad-cut.md), [S21](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ryobi-electric-mower-bad-uneven-cut.md), [S23](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/sun-joe-electric-mower-bad-cut.md), [S25](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-electric-mower-bad-cut.md), [S30](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/worx-mower-has-bad-uneven-cut.md)
- **Craftsman, Kobalt, Worx:** blades are often stamped to show which side faces up and which faces the ground. ## Parts & fitment

No part is needed unless the blade or spindle was damaged. For bagging and leaf pickup, a high-lift blade (taller sail) moves more air.

Sources: [S17](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/mower-is-not-bagging-grass-solved.md), [S18](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/pick-up-leaves-with-a-mower.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Remember "Sail up!"
- When sharpening, take off one blade at a time, sharpen it and put it back before removing the next, so an installed blade is always there to copy.
