# Old fuel

## Summary

Gas starts to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase. Most gas contains ethanol, which attracts moisture. As it ages, gas loses its combustibility and leaves varnish and sticky deposits that restrict fuel flow and make parts fail. The ethanol and water mixture can also separate, sink to the bottom of the tank, and corrode fuel system parts. Draining the tank and refilling with fresh gas is easy. If the deposits have already clogged the fuel filter, fuel line or carburetor, those need attention too.

## Before you start

- How long has the gas been in the tank? Did the mower sit for a month or more, for example over winter?
- Was a fuel stabilizer added to the gas before storage?
- Is the engine 4-cycle (straight gasoline) or an older 2-cycle push mower (gas and oil mix)?
- Was the gas stored in a metal can? Rust can contaminate it.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the mower sat for months with gas in the tank, and the engine spins but won't fire. If it does start, it may sputter, hesitate, run sluggishly or die.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md)

## How to confirm

- Work out the age of the gas. Gas older than about 30 days, or gas left in the tank for several months of storage, is suspect.
- Look for varnish or sticky, gummy deposits in the fuel system, for example in the fuel filter or fuel line.
- If you suspect old gas, treat it as the cause: drain the tank and refill with fresh gas before chasing other parts.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md)

## Fix

1. Drain the tank with a fuel siphon pump into a fuel container. Take the old gas to a recycling facility for disposal.
2. Refill with fresh gas: unleaded, at least 87 octane, no more than 10% ethanol. Less ethanol, or ethanol-free gas, is best.
3. Add a fuel stabilizer such as Sea Foam to help clean the fuel system and reduce moisture (see the conflicting advice below on where to mix it).
4. If the engine starts, let it run (one Source says 10–15 minutes) so the treated fuel works through the system.
5. If it still won't start, check the parts old gas tends to clog: the fuel filter, the fuel lines, then the carburetor.

Don't top up old gas with new gas. Mixing doesn't fix the deposits old gas leaves behind.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** the poweredoutdoors.com Sources say to fill the tank with fresh fuel and add the stabilizer (S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, S6, S8). The manufacturer Source says to mix stabilizer into the gas can at the label ratio and never pour it directly into the mower's gas tank (S10).

## Brand notes

- **Husqvarna, John Deere, SCAG:** 4-cycle engines use gasoline. Older push mowers may have a 2-cycle engine that uses a gas and oil mixture. - **John Deere:** after refilling, let the engine run 10–15 minutes so the treated fuel works through the fuel system. Source: [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

## Parts & fitment

- **Fresh gasoline:** unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol for 4-cycle engines.
- **Fuel stabilizer:** follow the label for the stabilizer-to-fuel ratio.
- **Fuel filter / fuel line:** replace if old gas deposits have clogged them. Match a replacement line's length and diameter.

## Tips & life hacks

- Buy only what you'll use within 30 days. A 2-gallon can is enough for a push mower, and a 5-gallon can for a riding mower. Source: [S10](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md)
- Store gas in a plastic can, not a metal one. Rust contaminates the fuel. Source: [S10](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md)
- If you won't use up the gas before storage, add stabilizer. Without stabilizer, run the tank empty before storing the mower at the end of the season. Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md)
- Wear safety glasses and work in a well-ventilated area when draining gas. Source: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/start-a-lawn-mower-with-old-gas.md)
