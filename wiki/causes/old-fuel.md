# Old fuel

## Summary

Gas starts to break down as soon as 30 days after purchase and loses its combustibility. Most gas contains ethanol (usually corn-based), which attracts moisture from the air. As it ages, gas leaves varnish and sticky, gummy deposits that clog the fuel filter, fuel lines and carburetor, restrict fuel flow and make parts fail early. The ethanol and water mixture can also separate, sink to the bottom of the tank, corrode fuel system parts and, if it is drawn into the engine, burn hot enough to damage it. Draining the tank and refilling with fresh gas is easy. If the deposits have already clogged the fuel filter, fuel line or carburetor, those need attention too.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** gas starts to go bad in about 30 days (most Sources, for example [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), expert-blog, and [S10](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-keep-lawn-mower-gas-from-going-bad.md), manufacturer) vs gas "more than a month or two old" may be the problem ([S195](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/why-your-mower-loses-power-when-the-blades-are-engaged.md), manufacturer) vs drain and refill if the fuel is over 6 months old ([S194](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/runs-rough-or-misfires.ridtr03.md), manufacturer).

## Before you start

- How long has the gas been in the tank or the gas can? Did the mower sit for a month or more, for example over winter?
- Was a fuel stabilizer added to the gas while it was still fresh? A stabilizer can't reverse gas that has already gone bad.
- What gas went in? Check the octane and ethanol content on the pump label (Regular or E10 is up to 10% ethanol; E15, E30 and E85 are higher).
- Is the engine 4-cycle (straight gasoline, separate fill ports for gas and oil) or an older 2-cycle (one fill port, gas and oil mix)?
- Is it a diesel mower? Diesel also goes bad, with different signs.
- Was the gas stored in a metal can, outdoors, or near moisture or heat?

## Symptoms it produces

## How to confirm

- Work out the age of the gas. Gas older than about 30 days, or left in the tank over winter or for several months, is suspect (see the conflicting advice in Summary).
- Smell and look at it: old gas has a strong, pungent or foul odor and looks darker or more yellow than fresh gas. Compare its color to fresh gas.
- Look for varnish or sticky, gummy deposits in the fuel system, for example in the fuel filter, fuel line or a crusty carburetor.
- Diesel: old diesel looks dark, it leaves more sediment, and the fuel filter clogs more often than normal and turns dark or black.
- If you suspect old gas, treat it as the cause: drain the tank and refill with fresh gas before chasing other parts.

## Fix

1. Wipe around the fuel cap and remove it.
2. Drain the tank into an approved fuel container. A fuel siphon pump is the easiest way, especially on riding and zero-turn mowers. On a push mower you can also tip the mower to drain it, or remove the fuel line from the carburetor and drain into a container. Some Sources also flush the tank.
3. Refill with fresh gas: unleaded, at least 87 octane, no more than 10% ethanol. Less ethanol, or ethanol-free gas, is best. 2-cycle engines need the correct gas and oil mix instead.
4. Add a fuel stabilizer (Sources name Sea Foam Motor Treatment and STA-BIL) to help clean the fuel system, reduce moisture and keep the gas stable. See the conflicting advice below on where to mix it.
5. If the engine starts, let it run so the treated fuel works through the system. Sources give "a few minutes", "a while", 10–15 minutes and about 15 minutes. It may then run without the choke.
6. If it still won't start, check the parts old gas tends to clog: the fuel filter, the fuel lines, then the carburetor. See [Start a mower with old gas](../procedures/start-a-mower-with-old-gas.md), [Replace the fuel filter](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-filter.md), [Replace the fuel line](../procedures/replace-the-fuel-line.md) and [Clean the carburetor](../procedures/clean-the-carburetor.md).
7. Take the old gas to a recycling facility. Don't dump it on the ground.

Diesel mowers: remove the old diesel and refill with fresh; if it still won't start, check the fuel filter and other fuel parts for restrictions left by the dirty fuel.

Don't top up old gas with new gas. Mixing doesn't fix the deposits old gas leaves behind, and a stabilizer only works on fresh fuel.

## Parts & fitment

- **Fresh gasoline:** unleaded, minimum 87 octane, maximum 10% ethanol for 4-cycle engines; premium or recreational fuel isn't required. 2-cycle engines need gas mixed with air-cooled 2-cycle oil at the manual's ratio (2-cycle oil is not engine oil).
- **Fuel stabilizer:** follow the label for the stabilizer-to-fuel ratio and how long it keeps fuel stable (Sources say from 30 days up to 2 years).
- **Fuel siphon pump** and an approved gasoline container for draining.
- **Fuel filter / fuel line:** replace if old gas deposits have clogged them. Match a replacement line's length and diameter.
