# Bad charger

## Summary

A battery mower's pack only charges if the charger is getting power and making contact. The charger may not be fully plugged in, the outlet may not supply enough power, the plug may not match the outlet, the battery may not be seated on the charger's contacts, or the charger or its cord may be damaged. Then the battery never charges and the mower won't start. Chargers are among the parts regularly replaced on battery mowers. The fix is easy: correct the connection, or replace a damaged charger or cord.

## Before you start

- Does the pack charge in an external charger, or in the mower through a charging port? Some EGO and RYOBI units (RYOBI zero-turns) charge in the mower; most push and self-propelled models use an external charger.
- Use only the charger and cord designed for that battery.

## Symptoms it produces

## How to confirm

- Check that the charger cord is fully plugged in, into a standard 120 V household outlet with sufficient power.
- The plug style must match the outlet (a 3-prong plug needs a 3-prong outlet). Never modify the plug.
- On an external charger, make sure the battery pack's contacts engage the charger securely.
- Read the charger's indicator lights against its manual (see Brand notes).
- If you use an extension cord, it must be undamaged and rated for the load.

## Fix

Reconnect the charger properly, or use a suitable outlet. Replace a damaged charger or charging cord. Clean the charger's vents, and keep the charger out of extreme heat or cold.

## Brand notes

- **Toro:** batteries charge in an external charging station made for that battery. LED codes (check your charger's manual): blinking green = charging, solid green = charged, blinking red = battery may be defective, solid red = too hot or too cold; move the charger somewhere without extreme temperatures.
- **EGO:** some units charge in the mower via a charging port, others on a charger. A wrong or damaged extension cord risks overheating both, damage and fire.
- **RYOBI:** zero-turns have a charging port with LEDs and possibly a charge display; lights can show charging, full, failed to charge or defective.
