# Wrong choke setting

## Summary

The choke is a plate in the carburetor throat that restricts airflow, so a cold engine gets the rich mixture (more fuel, less air) it needs to start. A cold engine won't start with the choke off, and a warm engine won't start with the choke on. Once the engine has started and warmed up, the choke must be moved to off/open: left on, the engine gets too much fuel and too little air, sputters and dies. A choke plate that is stuck, or a choke cable that is stretched, worn or out of adjustment, gives the same result even when the lever is set right. Fix difficulty: easy.

## Before you start

- Is the engine cold or warm? Did it die a few minutes after a cold start, once it warmed up?
- Where is the choke lever or knob set, and does the choke plate actually follow it?
- Does the choke cable move freely, or does it feel stretched or worn?
- Does the mower have a manual choke (a lever or knob) or an automatic choke? Many current push mowers have an automatic choke, so having no choke lever doesn't rule out a choke problem.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the choke is off/open on a cold engine or on/closed on a warm one. Or the lever is set right, but the choke plate is stuck or the cable binds.
- [Engine starts then dies](../problems/engine-starts-then-dies.md): the choke was left on after the engine warmed up. The engine runs sluggish, sputters and shuts off for lack of air.
- Engine runs rough: a choke in the wrong position, or stuck open or closed, makes the engine run rough and bog down.
- [Mower is smoking](../problems/mower-is-smoking.md) with black smoke: a choke that stays on or sticks on makes the engine run rich and blow black smoke.

## How to confirm

1. Check the lever: it should be on/closed for a cold engine and off/open for a warm engine.
2. Remove the air filter (or its housing) and find the choke plate (valve).
3. Work the choke lever and watch the plate. It should close fully when the choke is engaged and open fully when it isn't.
4. Check that the choke cable moves freely and isn't stretched or worn.
5. If the choke is set right and the engine still has airflow trouble, also check for a plugged air filter.

## Fix

- Set the choke correctly: on/closed to start a cold engine, then off/open once the engine warms up. Leaving it on after warm-up makes the mower shut down.
- Free a stuck choke plate, choke shaft and linkages with carburetor cleaner. A penetrating lubricant (Sources name WD-40 and Sea Foam Deep Creep) also frees them up.
- Adjust the cable so the choke is closed when the lever is engaged and open when it isn't. Lubricate a sticking choke cable, and replace a stretched, worn or damaged one.
- An automatic choke that doesn't work may be stuck, or the temperature sensor near the muffler that opens and closes it may be faulty. Have a dealer or repair shop fix it.

## Tips & life hacks

- If the engine only keeps running with the choke on, the choke itself isn't the fault. The engine is getting too much air or too little fuel. Look at the carburetor, fuel filter, fuel line, fuel pump, gas cap and carburetor gasket instead. Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-only-runs-choke-on.md), [S21](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-turns-over-wont-start.md), [S39](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-runs-choke-on.md)
- Choke trouble shows up most on cool mornings and in cold weather, when the engine depends on the choke to start. Source: [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-when-cold.md)
- If the mower dies a few minutes after a cold start, make sure the choke lever is in the correct position.
