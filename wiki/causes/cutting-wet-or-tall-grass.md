# Cutting wet or tall grass

## Summary

Wet, long, thick or very tall grass puts a heavy load on the engine (and on the transmission when the blades are engaged). The engine has to work harder, so it bogs down, loses power, runs hot and can overheat. Wet grass is also heavier and stickier: it clumps and builds up under the deck and in the chute and bagging tubes, which restricts airflow, adds more load and spoils the cut and the bagging. A battery mower can overload and shut off. The fix is easy and costs nothing: mow when the grass is dry, mow more often, and take tall grass down in two or three passes.

## Before you start

- Was the grass wet (rain, morning dew), long, thick or very tall when the trouble started?
- How long since the lawn was last mowed?
- Was the mower going fast?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine loses power](../problems/engine-loses-power.md): the engine bogs down and feels sluggish in wet, tall or thick grass, especially with the blades engaged; wet clippings clump under the deck and strain it further.
- [Engine starts then dies](../problems/engine-starts-then-dies.md): a battery mower stops when too-thick, tall or wet grass puts an extreme load on the motor, and may not restart until the load is reduced.
- Engine overheats: long, thick or wet grass overworks the engine and can overheat it.
- Mower doesn't bag grass well: wet grass or wet leaves build up under the deck and stick inside the chute, blower tubes and inlet tube, cutting the airflow that carries the clippings.
- Poor or uneven cut: wet grass clumps on the deck and gives a poor cut.

## How to confirm

The trouble shows up in heavy conditions and goes away in short, dry grass. If the engine bogs or the motor stops only in wet, long or thick grass, ease the load (slow down, raise the deck, wait for dry grass) and see whether it stops. If the mower consistently struggles in tough conditions, easing the load may be all it takes. Look under the deck for wet clippings clumped and stuck to the underside.

## Fix

- **Mow dry grass.** Wait until the dew is gone or the lawn has dried after rain. Dry grass gives a better cut, less buildup under the deck, less load on the engine and less cleaning and scraping later.
- **Mow regularly**, so the grass doesn't overgrow and overwhelm the mower.
- **Double- or triple-cut tall grass.** Set the deck to its highest setting for the first cut, then lower it a little and cut again, instead of cutting an overgrown yard in one pass.
- **Slow the ground speed** in tall, thick or wet grass if you must mow it, and run the engine at full throttle.
- **Battery mowers:** raise the cutting height, slow down, and cut narrower sections in tall or thick grass.

## Brand notes

- **Battery mowers (DeWalt, EGO, SKIL):** thick, tall or wet grass can overload the motor so the mower stops; raise the cutting height and slow down.
- **Electric mowers in general:** wet grass clogs the deck, strains the motor and affects the cut, one of several reasons not to mow in the rain.
- The brand articles for Bad Boy, Bobcat, Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Greenworks, Honda, Husqvarna, Hustler, John Deere, Kubota, SCAG and Toro give the same mow-dry, double-cut advice.

## Tips & life hacks

- For extremely tall grass, use a walk-behind brush cutter, which you can rent at a local rental center.
- In hot weather and harsh conditions, mow in the cooler parts of the day and take breaks.
- Bagging leaves works best when they are dry or only lightly moist; very wet leaves may need a couple of passes.
