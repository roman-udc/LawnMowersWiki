# Worn or stretched belt

## Summary

The deck belt (blade belt) runs around the deck pulleys and spins the blades. It is a wear item: normal use stretches it, and age and rubbing crack, fray and glaze it. A worn belt no longer grips the pulleys, so it slips. The blades then turn slowly or not at all, and a stretched belt has slack that lets it sit loosely on the pulleys, so the deck vibrates and the belt can roll off.

Blade speed matters: fast-spinning blades create the air movement and suction under the deck that stands the grass up for an even cut. A slipping belt lowers blade tip speed, and the cut suffers even when the blades still turn.

The same wear hits other belts: a worn ground drive belt or hydrostatic pump belt slips and the mower moves slowly or poorly, and on a belt-driven bagger the deck belt also drives the blower belt.

The fix is to replace the belt. Most homeowners can replace a riding mower deck belt with a few hand tools (one Source).

## Before you start

- Which belt is it: the deck belt that spins the blades, or a drive belt that moves the mower? Some mowers (some Ferris models) have two deck belts.
- Have the owner's manual, the routing decal on the mower, or the parts diagram ready: routing is the first thing to check on a shredded belt.
- There is no set replacement interval: it depends on how much the mower is used and how well it is kept up.
- A belt that keeps breaking often means something else is wrong, such as a deck packed with clippings or an oil leak.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower is vibrating](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md): a stretched belt has slack and doesn't sit securely in the pulleys, so the deck vibrates; it may also come off.
- [Uneven cut](../problems/uneven-cut.md): the belt slips, blade tip speed drops, and the cut is bad or uneven. Owners often notice only when the lawn looks worse.
- Blades won't engage (no page yet): a badly worn belt slips until the blades don't turn, or turn slowly.
- Belt keeps coming off (no page yet): a worn, loose belt rolls off the pulleys.
- Other signs: squealing or squeaking from the deck, especially when accelerating or engaging the blades; a whine and power loss; a burning belt smell; a mower that moves slowly, stutters, won't climb hills or won't move until the belt warms up (drive belt).

## How to confirm

Remove the belt covers (and on some mowers the floor pan) and look at the belt. Replace it if you see any of these:

- shiny, glazed sides (a slipping belt glazes; no ridges or bumps left);
- cracks, fraying or torn edges, or worn sidewalls;
- a stretched or loose belt that no longer sits tight on the pulleys, with lots of slack between pulleys;
- a thin belt that sits deep in the pulley grooves (more than 1/2" deflection, in one Source; the pulley may be worn too).

If the belt looks good but the deck still vibrates, check its routing and tension and remove the slack. Also check the pulleys for stuck debris, and that pulleys sit parallel to the deck.

**Shredded edges** mean something is damaging the belt, and a shredded belt is weak and likely to break. Find the cause before fitting a new one or you'll keep buying belts:

1. Check the belt is the right one and routed correctly (decal on the mower, owner's manual, or parts diagram).
2. With the deck engaged, look for a bracket or belt keeper the belt rubs: a shiny, smooth, flat spot on the metal shows where.

## Fix

- Replace a worn, cracked, glazed, stretched or shredded belt. Replace it as soon as it shows wear, even if it isn't the main reason the blades won't engage.
- If the belt looks good, correct its routing and adjust the tension to take out the slack.
- For a shredded belt, first stop the rubbing: bend or adjust the bracket or belt keeper back into place, replace a worn bracket bushing, or replace the bracket assembly when it can't be adjusted or is sold only as an assembly. Then fit the new belt.
- If the belt keeps coming off, also check for a bad pulley, a worn tensioner arm, or a broken or missing tensioner spring.

## Brand notes

- **Bad Boy:** pump drive belt tension should be 60 to 65 lbs, set with the jam nuts on the tensioner (per Source, verify against the manual).
- **Cub Cadet:** belts wear faster with aftermarket belts that aren't OEM quality, and faster again when pulleys, the tensioner bracket or its spring fail.
- **Ferris:** some models have one deck belt, others two.
- **Kubota, Ariens:** an incompatible belt can be a safety risk.
- **Snapper, Exmark, Husqvarna, Troy-Bilt, Gravely:** some belt brackets take a replaceable bushing; others are sold only as a bracket assembly.

## Tips & life hacks

- When one mower belt is worn, replace both: the other is probably due.
- Check belts regularly; also check tension springs (they can break) and clean dirt, mud and debris from the pulleys.
- Turn the mower off and inspect the belt as soon as you hear squealing at start-up.
- Debris packed in the deck stresses the belt and can make it snap.
