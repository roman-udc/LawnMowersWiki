# Bad drive control linkage or damper

## Summary

The parts between the operator's controls and the drive wheels have worn, loosened or gone out of adjustment, so the two drive wheels don't get the same command. On a zero-turn, each steering lever has a damper: a small gas shock attached to the bottom of the steering arm that absorbs feedback from the hydro pumps and smooths the control. Dampers wear over time; a bad or loose one makes the mower jerk to one side when moving forward and feel weak on that side. The linkages from the steering levers to the plate on the hydraulic motors can also be out of adjustment ("tracking"), so one wheel turns faster than the other and the mower pulls. On a self-propelled walk-behind, a stretched or unevenly tensioned drive cable can send more power to one wheel. A mis-adjusted neutral position makes a riding mower or zero-turn creep in neutral.

The fix is usually easy to moderate: tighten or replace a damper, or adjust the tracking, the cable or the neutral control.

## Before you start

- Is it a zero-turn (two steering levers) or a self-propelled walk-behind? The parts differ.
- Check the tire pressures first: equal pressure on each side, at the manufacturer's spec. Several Sources say to start there and check the dampers before adjusting the tracking.
- Have the owner's manual at hand: tracking and neutral adjustments vary by manufacturer and model.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower pulls to one side](../problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side.md): a zero-turn jerks to one side when moving forward (bad damper) or steadily pulls because one wheel turns faster than the other (tracking out of adjustment); the side with the bad damper feels weak or unresponsive when you push that lever forward. A self-propelled mower veers when a stretched drive cable sends more power to one wheel.
- [Mower is vibrating](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md): on a push mower, linkages and bushings wear and loosen, causing vibration.
- [Mower creeps in neutral](../problems/mower-creeps-in-neutral.md): the neutral position of the control is mis-adjusted.

## How to confirm

- Watch the mower drive straight forward on level ground. A jerk to one side as it moves off points to a damper; a steady pull with one wheel turning faster points to tracking.
- Check that each damper is secure on the steering arm and that its hardware is tight.
- Rule out the other one-sided causes before adjusting: equal tire pressure, a brake dragging on one side, air in one transmission.
- Self-propelled: make sure both drive wheels engage evenly, and check the drive cable for stretch or uneven tension.
- Creeping: put the controls in neutral and watch for movement.

## Fix

- **Damper:** tighten the hardware if it's loose; replace a bad damper.
- **Tracking:** when one tire moves faster than the other, adjust the speed adjustment bolt to change that tire's rotation speed until both match: [Adjust the tracking](../procedures/adjust-the-tracking.md).
- **Creeping in neutral:** [Adjust the neutral control](../procedures/adjust-the-neutral-control.md).
- **Self-propelled drive cable:** adjust the cable tension; if that doesn't correct it, look deeper in the drive system for wear.
- **Push mower linkages:** tighten the linkages and replace worn parts.

Don't just compensate with the steering levers: fix the cause, which could be a bigger issue.

## Parts & fitment

- **Steering damper:** one on each side of a zero-turn, attached to the bottom of the steering arm. The Sources give no part numbers; match the model.
- **Drive cable (self-propelled):** a stretched or uneven cable gives one wheel more power, especially on models with variable-speed control.
