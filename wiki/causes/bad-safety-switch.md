# Bad safety switch

## Summary

Mowers have an operator presence control system: a set of safety switches that only let the engine start, or keep running, when certain conditions are met. On a riding or zero-turn mower these usually include a seat switch (is someone in the seat?), a brake or parking-brake switch, and a PTO (blade) switch. Some zero-turns also need the steering levers in the open/outward position. On a push mower the main one is the bail lever (safety bar) switch, which kills the engine when you let go of the bar. When one of these switches fails, it can't tell that the condition is met, so the mower won't crank or start. A bad switch can also shut the engine off while mowing or stop the blades from engaging. Testing a switch is easy, and replacing one is easy to moderate.

> **Safety:** never operate a mower with a safety switch removed or bypassed. Bypass a switch only briefly, to identify a bad one while troubleshooting, then replace it. Many people are injured every year by mowers rolling over or by falling off, and a fatality has involved a seat switch that was disabled. ## Before you start

- What kind of mower is it? Push and battery mowers use a bail lever switch, and some also have folding-handle, side-rail and on/off button switches. Riding and zero-turn mowers use seat, brake and PTO switches, and sometimes others.
- Is the starting procedure right? Before testing switches, check the operator's manual: parking brake set, blades (PTO) off, steering levers out where required, operator in the seat.
- Which switches does this model have, and where are they? The operator's manual lists them.
- Does it fail every time or only sometimes? Loose wires on the interlock switches, or a loose brake arm, can cause no-starts that come and go.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): you turn the key and the engine doesn't turn over, even with the parking brake set. Most brand "won't start" and "won't turn over" articles list it. On battery push mowers, the mower won't start because the handle isn't unfolded and locked, the side rails aren't fully extended, or the safety bar isn't pulled to the handle.
- [Engine starts then dies](../problems/engine-starts-then-dies.md): a riding mower that shuts off as soon as you release the brake usually has a broken or unplugged seat switch, or a worn seat that doesn't press the switch. A bad connection, or bouncing off the seat on rough ground, can make the mower keep stalling.
- [Blades won't engage](../problems/blades-wont-engage.md): a seat switch that doesn't sense the operator stops the blades from turning on, and lifting off the seat with the deck on shuts the deck off.
- Engine runs rough: a failing operator presence switch can feel like sputtering.
- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): general "won't start" articles list it: the starting steps are followed correctly (brake set, blades off, operator in the seat) but the mower still won't start. A switch that blocks starting usually stops the engine cranking at all, so check [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md) first.
- [Mower won't move](../problems/mower-wont-move.md): the engine dies when you release the brake to drive off; the seat switch may be unplugged or broken.

## How to confirm

1. **Test with a multimeter.** Most Sources say to check each switch for continuity.
2. **Seat switch (riding mower), per a manufacturer Source:** lift the seat and check the switch harness is plugged in. If it is, unplug it, turn the switch a quarter turn and pull it out of the seat, and replace it if it's visibly damaged. Set the meter to ohms. Across the inner spades it should read near 0 ohms with the plunger released and infinite with the plunger pressed in. The outer spades should read the same way. Any other reading means replace the switch. ([S123](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-dies-when-you-release-the-brake-video.md))
3. **Brake interlock switch:** the switch detects the brake pedal pushed fully down, which is also how it sees the parking brake set. On the Craftsman tractor example: remove the air duct, move the fuel tank aside (drain some fuel if it's too heavy), lower the dash, and unplug the harness from the switch. Across the two prongs with the white wires, near 0 ohms is good and infinite means replace. ([S122](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/lawn-tractor-won-t-start.ridtr04.md), [S124](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md))
4. **Blade (PTO) switch:** remove the clutch lever assembly screws, pull it down slightly, unplug the harness, and measure across the white-wire prongs: near 0 ohms good, infinite replace. ([S124](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md))
5. **Bail lever switch (push mower):** follow the cable from the bail lever down to the switch on the side of the engine. It should be grounded when the lever is released (engine stops) and not grounded when the bar is held against the handle. ([S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bolens-mower-wont-start.md), [S97](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-push-mower-wont-start.md))
6. **Temporary bypass:** you can briefly bypass a switch (on battery mowers, with a jumper wire) to see whether it's the bad one. Troubleshooting only, then replace the switch.
7. **If a switch tests good,** make sure the plate or part that presses it actually makes good contact ([S27](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-wont-start-no-click.md)). On a tractor, check the interlock switch wiring connections, and check for side-to-side play in the brake pedal: the brake arm may not reach the brake interlock switch, a common cause of intermittent no-starts on some tractors. Tighten the brake arm to remove the play. ([S125](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-mower-and-tractor-common-questions.md))

## Fix

Replace the switch that tests bad. If the seat switch harness was unplugged, plug it back in. If a worn or cracked seat keeps the seat switch from being pressed, replace the seat. If the brake arm has side-to-side play, tighten it. On battery mowers, make sure the handle is unfolded and secured and the side rails are fully extended with the clamps locked.

- [Replace the seat switch](../procedures/replace-the-seat-switch.md)

> **Note:** several brand "common problems" tables say "Replace a bad battery" in the safety-switch row. That's a typo in those Sources: the fix is the switch.

## Parts & fitment

- **Seat switch:** a plunger switch that twists a quarter turn into the seat bottom and is tested across two pairs of spades. The manufacturer Source covers it for Craftsman, MTD, Troy-Bilt, Husqvarna, Murray, Snapper, Cub Cadet and Ariens riding mowers.
- **Mower seat:** a worn or cracked seat can stop the seat switch from being pressed; replace the seat if it's worn or damaged.
- **Brake interlock switch** and **blade (PTO) switch:** two-prong switches (white wires on the Craftsman example).

## Tips & life hacks

- If the mower dies every time you hit a bump, you may be bouncing off the seat switch rather than facing a bad part.
- An unplugged seat switch harness doesn't bypass the switch: the engine always shuts off when you release the brake. Check the plug before buying a switch.
