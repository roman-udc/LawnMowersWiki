# Bad alternator

## Summary

On a riding mower the alternator is a set of wire coils attached to the engine under the flywheel. It generates electricity while the engine runs, powers the tractor and recharges the battery. When it fails, the battery isn't recharged: it charges fine on an external charger but drains while you run the tractor, and then the mower won't start. The alternator (stator) is one part of the wider [charging system](faulty-charging-system.md); a bad regulator gives the same symptom. Testing is easy with a multimeter; replacing the alternator means working under the flywheel, and the expert-blog Sources send charging-system repairs to an experienced mechanic.

## Before you start

- Is the battery itself good? If it won't charge fully within 8 hours on a charger, it probably needs replacing; only if the battery is okay does the blame shift to the alternator.
- Are the battery cables and wiring clean, good and tight?
- Is the alternator internal (under the flywheel) or external? Most are internal; some larger mowers have an external one.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Battery keeps dying](../problems/battery-keeps-dying.md): the battery charges on an external charger but drains while the tractor runs, so you have to charge it constantly.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/dead-battery.ltrtr141.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/lawn-tractor-won-t-start.ridtr04.md)

## How to confirm

With the engine running, measure the DC voltage at the battery with a multimeter. If it doesn't read between 13 and 14 volts, the alternator may need replacing. If the voltage doesn't rise above the engine-off reading at all, the regulator or the stator/alternator is bad (see [Faulty charging system](faulty-charging-system.md) and [Test the battery](../procedures/test-the-battery.md) for the full test).

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** the running voltage to expect is 13 to 14 volts ([S1](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/dead-battery.ltrtr141.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/lawn-tractor-won-t-start.ridtr04.md), manufacturer) vs 13.2 to 13.9 volts or higher ([S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), expert-blog; the full test is on [Faulty charging system](faulty-charging-system.md)).

## Fix

Replace the alternator when it doesn't recharge the battery while the engine runs. Because many charging-system parts can fail and electrical parts usually can't be returned, the expert-blog Sources recommend an experienced mechanic diagnose it rather than swapping parts.

## Brand notes

- **Cub Cadet zero-turns:** a bad stator or alternator can make the charging system fail.
- **Craftsman:** the alternator may be external or internal (under the flywheel).

## Parts & fitment

- **Alternator (stator):** coils under the engine flywheel on most mowers; match the engine. The Sources give no part numbers.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/what-are-the-riding-mower-parts-that-require-replacement-most-often.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-clicks-but-wont-start-or-turn-over-solved.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Charge the battery fully before winter storage; it can lose charge in the off-season and may need charging again in spring. That is normal, not an alternator fault.
