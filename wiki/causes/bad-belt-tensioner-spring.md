# Bad belt tensioner spring

## Summary

A tensioner arm (idler bracket) and spring hold the idler pulleys in line and keep the belt tight. On most decks the bracket has the pulley on one side and the spring on the other. Over time the spring weakens, stretches, breaks or falls off, and the hole in the bracket where the spring hooks on wears larger. The belt then loosens and vibrates off the pulleys, the deck shakes, and the blades may stop. If the bracket's bushing isn't greased it wears too, the pulley runs at an angle, and the vibration plus the angle throws the belt. Debris on top of the deck can also stop the tensioner moving. Too little tension lets the belt slip and come off; too much damages the deck components.

The fix is usually easy: replace the worn spring or bracket, grease the bushing, and clear debris from around the tensioner.

## Before you start

- Is the belt that keeps coming off the deck (blade) belt or the drive belt? This page covers the deck tensioner; the spring on the drive belt idler arm is [Missing idler arm spring](missing-idler-arm-spring.md).
- Was the belt itself checked? A stretched belt, a bad pulley and a worn tensioner arm or missing spring all let the belt come off the pulleys.
- Have the owner's manual at hand for your model's tension spec and adjustment.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Belt keeps coming off](../problems/belt-keeps-coming-off.md): a weak, stretched or missing spring, or a worn spring hole in the bracket, lets the belt loosen and vibrate off the pulleys.
- [Blades won't engage](../problems/blades-wont-engage.md): the belt is loose or off the pulleys, so it no longer turns them and the blades stop.
- [Mower is vibrating](../problems/mower-is-vibrating.md): a loose or worn idler bracket and stretched spring shake the deck.
- [Mower won't move](../problems/mower-wont-move.md): without tension on the drive belt the mower won't go.
- [Mower pulls to one side](../problems/mower-pulls-to-one-side.md): a tensioner that no longer holds the drive belt tight makes the hydrostatic drive weak.

## How to confirm

- Look at the tensioner bracket and spring: is the spring stretched, broken or missing, or is the hole it hooks into worn larger?
- Check that the tensioner, its bushing and the spring are in good condition, and that debris on top of the deck isn't stopping the tensioner pulley moving.
- Check whether the idler pulley runs at an angle (a worn, ungreased bushing).
- Check the belt tension with a belt tension gauge against the spec in your owner's manual.

## Fix

- Replace a worn, stretched, broken or missing spring, and a worn bracket (arm) if the hole has worn larger and the bracket is replaceable.
- Grease the idler bracket bushing, and keep the tensioner arm greased so it moves and doesn't seize.
- Remove leaves, mud, wood chips and stones from around and under the tensioner, and make sure it moves.
- Adjust the tension if your deck allows it (see Brand notes), then refit or replace the belt: [Replace the deck belt](../procedures/replace-the-deck-belt.md).
- On a vibrating mower, also check the bearing and bushing in the engagement linkage and replace them if worn.

## Brand notes

- **Bad Boy:** most models need 70-75 lbs of belt tension (check the owner's manual for your model). Some models adjust spring tension by sliding a bolt on the deck; others by loosening the jam nuts and adjusting the eye bolt the spring hooks to. Recheck the tension after about 30 minutes of running.
- **Exmark:** the idler bracket bushing wears if it isn't greased, so the pulley runs at an angle. Most Exmark decks have a self-tensioning spring that needn't be removed to change the belt; older units and the Quest use a tension spring that must be removed.
- **SCAG, Ferris, Spartan:** check the tensioner, bushing and spring, and clear debris that restricts the tensioner.
- **Toro, Craftsman:** a worn bushing lets the pulley run at an angle and cut into the deck (or, for the transmission belt, the underside of the mower).
- The other brand belt, blades and vibration articles (Ariens, Cub Cadet, Gravely, Husqvarna, Hustler, John Deere, Kubota, Poulan Pro, Simplicity, Snapper, Troy-Bilt, Walker, Wright) repeat the same worn-spring and worn-bracket-hole description.

## Parts & fitment

- **Tensioner spring:** match the old spring; replace if stretched, weak, broken or missing.
- **Idler tensioner bracket (arm):** replace if the spring hole has worn larger and the bracket is sold separately.
- **Idler bracket bushing:** replace if worn; keep greased.

## Tips & life hacks

- Dry clippings and leaves collected on top of the deck can catch fire from the belt's heat: keep the deck clean.
- Many of the things that make the belt come off also cause vibration, so check the tensioner on a vibrating mower too.
