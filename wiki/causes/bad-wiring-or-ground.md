# Bad wiring or ground

## Summary

The starting circuit needs unbroken wires and a clean ground. Loose wires lose continuity, corrosion blocks current, a broken wire or damaged harness can short to the frame and blow the fuse, and a bad ground can keep the starter solenoid from getting power or make it fail. On battery-powered push mowers, wires that run along the handle often get pinched where the handle folds for storage, and a short stops the mower from starting or makes it cut out while mowing. Most fixes are easy to moderate: clean or replace a ground cable, or repair a wire with a wire nut. Tracing a break through a harness is more involved.

## Before you start

- Is the mower gas (riding or zero-turn with a key start) or battery-powered? The checks differ.
- On a battery mower, check the warranty first: repairing a damaged wire yourself, or outside an authorized repair center, can void it.
- On a riding mower, note whether the solenoid has 3 posts: a 3-post solenoid is self-grounded, so it has no separate ground wire to check.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): the mower clicks but won't turn over, or nothing happens when you turn the key, because the solenoid or starter isn't getting power.
- Battery mower won't start, or starts then stops while mowing: a loose or pinched wire is shorting.
- Fuse keeps blowing: a broken wire or damaged harness is shorting to the frame.
- Engine dies when you release the brake: if the brake switch passes its checks, a short in the wire harness can be shutting the engine off.

## How to confirm

**Gas riding and zero-turn mowers**

1. Check that wiring and components are securely attached, with no breaks in continuity and no corrosion.
2. Check the black ground cable from the battery to the mower frame: it must make good contact and be free of corrosion. Check the ground from the solenoid too (a 3-post solenoid is self-grounded).
3. Ground wire test (manufacturer method): disconnect the negative, then the positive battery cable and tuck them away. Set a multimeter to resistance and measure from the black wire's female spade (on the solenoid coil) to bare metal on the frame. Near 0 ohms means it is grounded; infinite means a break in the black ground wiring.
4. Red wire: measure from the solenoid post with the red wire to the red wire's spade on the ignition switch harness. Near 0 ohms is good; infinite means a break in the red wire. If every component tests good, look for a break in the white wire between the ignition switch and the solenoid coil.
5. Starter cable voltage drop test: a resistance test only shows that some current flows, not whether a heavy cable can carry enough to spin the starter. Disconnect the spark plug wire so the engine can't start, clip the meter across the cable (solenoid post to the starter motor stud), hold the key in START and read the voltage after the click. Less than 1 volt is ideal; more than 1 volt means broken strands inside the cable.

[Read a wiring schematic](../procedures/read-a-wiring-schematic.md) to follow the circuit, and [test the starter solenoid](../procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid.md) once the grounds check good.

**Battery-powered mowers**

- Wiggle the wires to find a short, or use a multimeter. Start at pinch points, especially where the handle folds into the mower.
- Large battery riding mowers have plug-ins that can come loose: with the mower off and the battery removed, open it up around the battery and unplug and reconnect each one (zero-turns have many).

## Fix

- Tighten loose connections and remove corrosion at the battery and solenoid grounds. Replace a damaged ground cable.
- Repair or replace wires between the starting components that have breaks or damage. Replace a starter cable that shows more than a 1 volt drop.
- If a broken wire or damaged harness (dash, chassis or engine) is shorting to the frame and blowing the fuse, repair the wire or replace the harness, then [test and replace the fuse](../procedures/test-and-replace-a-fuse.md).
- Battery mowers: remove the battery (and the safety key or button) first, then repair a loose or pinched connection with a wire nut.

> **Safety:** disconnect the battery (negative cable first) or remove the battery pack and safety key before working on wiring. Disconnect the spark plug wire for any test where the key is turned to START.

## Parts & fitment

- **Ground cable** (battery to frame) and **starter cable:** replace when damaged or when the starter cable fails the voltage drop test.
- **Wire harness:** replace if the damage can't be repaired.
- **Wire nut:** for repairing a loose or pinched connection on a battery mower.

## Tips & life hacks

- Fold and unfold the handle carefully, keeping the wires on the outside of the handle, so they don't get pinched.
- Corded mowers: constant pulling, flexing or yanking can damage the wires inside the power cord; check it with a multimeter. Cords are easy to replace.
