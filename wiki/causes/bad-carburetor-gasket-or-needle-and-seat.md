# Bad carburetor gasket or needle and seat

## Summary

The carburetor is a common place for a mower to leak gas. Three parts fail:

- **Bowl gasket:** a thin o-ring that looks like a rubber band, between the bottom of the carburetor and the fuel bowl. The carburetor sits next to the engine, so the gasket heats up while running and cools when off. That cycling dries it out and makes it hard and brittle until it no longer seals, and fuel leaks around the bowl. Replacing it is easy.
- **Stuck float:** the float regulates how much fuel enters the bowl, acting as a gatekeeper. When it sticks, it can't shut the fuel off, so the bowl overflows and fuel runs out of the carburetor near the air intake port.
- **Stuck float needle:** the needle works with the float to meter fuel into the bowl. A stuck needle also lets fuel run out.

Old gas left in the carburetor leaves varnish and gummy deposits that make these small parts stick. A float or needle fault means taking the carburetor apart: moderate work, or a job for a small engine shop.

> **Safety:** gasoline is flammable. Shut off the fuel before you open the carburetor, keep a rag ready for the fuel left in the bowl, and wipe up spills before starting the engine.

## Before you start

- Where exactly is the fuel coming from? The bottom of the bowl points to the gasket; the air intake port points to the float or needle.
- Write down the engine make, model and spec number from the engine housing. Carburetor parts are ordered by these, not by the mower's model and serial number.
- Is there a fuel shut-off valve under the tank? If not, have clamps or pinch-off pliers ready for the fuel line.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower is leaking gas](../problems/mower-is-leaking-gas.md): fuel drips or seeps around the bottom of the carburetor bowl (gasket), or wets the area by the air intake port (stuck float or needle). You may see a small puddle under the mower or a wet carburetor body, with the gas smell strongest right after running.
- [Engine only runs with the choke on](../problems/engine-only-runs-with-choke-on.md): a worn gasket behind the carburetor (between it and the engine) lets extra air in, so the engine runs lean and the choke compensates. This Problem has no page yet.

## How to confirm

- Residue or wetness around the bottom of the bowl means the bowl gasket.
- Fuel by the air intake port, near the top of the carburetor, means a stuck float.
- If the float is fine but fuel still runs out, the float needle is stuck.
- If a new bowl gasket doesn't stop the leak, check the float and needle next.
- For the gasket behind the carburetor: check that the carburetor bolts are tight and the gasket is in good shape.

## Fix

**Bowl gasket:**

1. Shut off the fuel at the shut-off valve under the tank, or clamp or crimp the fuel line.
2. Wipe dirt off the outside of the carburetor so none gets in.
3. Remove the screw at the bottom of the bowl and lower the bowl. Have a rag ready for the fuel left in it.
4. Remove the old gasket and fit the new one. Keep carburetor cleaner, lubricants and other material off the new gasket, or it must be replaced.
5. Refit the bowl and tighten the screw.
6. Wipe the carburetor down, turn the fuel back on and check for leaks.

**Stuck float or float needle:** take the carburetor apart to find why it sticks. Cleaning may free it; otherwise rebuild it with a kit (replacing the float or float needle) or replace the carburetor. A small engine mechanic or dealer can do the rebuild.

**Gasket behind the carburetor:** remove the linkages and bolts, take off the carburetor and old gasket, fit a new gasket, then refit the carburetor, bolts and linkages. It's a good time to clean the carburetor.

## Parts & fitment

- Carburetor bowl gasket (o-ring): match by engine make, model and spec number; a local engine dealer or online parts seller can find it.
- Carburetor intake gasket (between engine block and carburetor): it and the bowl gasket tear easily; replace either if torn or contaminated.
- Float needle and float: usually in a carburetor rebuild kit, also ordered by engine model and spec number.

## Tips & life hacks

- To free a stuck needle for now, gently tap the side of the carburetor with a rubber mallet or the handle end of a hammer. It works once or twice only; then take the carburetor apart and replace the needle.
- Gas left sitting in the carburetor leaves the deposits that make the float and needle stick.
