# Bypass valve open

## Summary

A hydrostatic mower drives its wheels with transmission fluid pumped under pressure. That pressure is so high that you can't push the mower by hand, so every hydrostatic mower has a way to bypass the transmission: drive release (bypass) levers, rods, arms, a push/pull button or a knob. With the bypass open, the mower free-rolls so you can push it into a garage or onto a trailer, but the engine can no longer drive the wheels. If the levers are left in the bypass position afterwards, the mower runs but won't move forward or reverse.

On a riding mower with a transaxle the same control is called the freewheel control: in the freewheel position the drive is disengaged.

This is usually the first thing to check, and the fix is easy: put the levers back in the operating position.

## Before you start

- Has the mower been pushed by hand, loaded on a trailer, transported or serviced recently? Owners often forget to return the levers afterwards.
- Zero-turn or lawn tractor? A zero-turn has one bypass lever per transmission (left and right, usually near each rear drive wheel). A lawn tractor or riding mower usually has a single bypass lever or rod at the rear.
- What the control looks like varies by make and model (lever, rod, arm, push/pull button or knob): check the owner's manual.
- **Safety:** use the bypass levers only on a level surface with the parking brake set. With the bypass open on a slope, the mower can roll away.

## How to confirm

1. Find the bypass levers (one near each drive wheel on a zero-turn, one at the rear on a lawn tractor).
2. Check their position. Pulled out (or in the PUSH/OPEN position) means bypassed: the mower free-rolls. Pushed in (or in the DRIVE/CLOSED position) means the transmission is engaged.
3. On a riding mower with a transaxle, check whether the freewheel control is in the freewheel position.

## Fix

Put every bypass lever, rod or arm back in the operating position (on a zero-turn, both of them), so the transmission drives the wheels again. On a transaxle, shift the freewheel control to engage the transaxle. On some models the bypass valve closes by itself once the mower is started and the pedals are used; make sure it isn't stuck open.

There is no separate procedure page: the step above is the whole job.

## Brand notes

- **Bad Boy:** two neutral bypass (drive release) levers at the rear; push them in toward the mower for the operating position.
- **Hustler:** hydrostatic neutral rods at the rear, above the transmissions, on the two wheel motors. Engaged means pointed to the right as seen from the seat.
- **Spartan:** hydrostatic neutral levers on the wheel motors near the rear wheels, one per side; push them to the right as seen from the seat.
- **Ferris:** drive release levers on the transmission cradle between the rear wheels.
- **Simplicity:** two bypass levers near the drive wheels, DRIVE/CLOSED to drive, PUSH/OPEN to free-roll. Simplicity and Cub Cadet riding mowers use a bypass rod that must be disengaged.
- **Kubota:** rods on the Z100, Z200 and Z400 series, levers on the Z700 series, left and right.
- **Husqvarna:** zero-turns have two levers at the rear; lawn tractors have one bypass lever at the rear, near the drawbar or under the seat.
- **Kubota and Walker won't-move articles:** the intro of each says the drive release levers being "in the operating position" stops the mower. That is a slip: the body text of both says the levers must be in the operating position for the mower to move.
- Ariens, Craftsman, Exmark, Gravely, John Deere, SCAG, Snapper, Toro and Troy-Bilt articles give the same advice (levers back to the operating position) with nothing brand-specific.

## Tips & life hacks

- Never tow a hydrostatic mower or pull it with other equipment: it can damage the transmission and lead to a large repair bill. Open the bypass and push it by hand instead.
