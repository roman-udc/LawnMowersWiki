# Hot hydraulic fluid

## Summary

Hydraulic fluid that is low, old, bad or the wrong type can't lubricate the hydrostatic transmission well. The extra friction overheats the fluid, and the mower gets weak or stops moving once it warms up. Hot fluid can also be a sign of more extensive damage inside the transmission, and running hot can cause that damage. Correcting the fluid is easy; if the mower still runs fine cold but quits when hot, it's a pro job for a dealer.

## Before you start

- Does the mower run fine when cold and get weak or stop only after it warms up?
- Is the hydraulic fluid low, old, or not the type the manufacturer specifies?
- Was the oil just changed? Fluid that's extremely hot right after an oil change points to something other than the oil.
- Is the hydraulic pump leaking?

## How to confirm

- Note how the mower behaves cold versus hot: fine cold and weak or stopped hot is the telltale pattern.
- Let the oil cool and try again. If the mower recovers when cool and fails again when hot, the cause is heat in the hydraulic system.
- Check the fluid level and age (see [Low hydraulic oil](low-hydraulic-oil.md)).

## Fix

Let the oil cool. If the fluid is low, old or wrong, correct it: change it outside the normal interval only when it's been getting hot or is contaminated. If the mower still runs fine cold but goes weak or stops when hot, or the hydraulic pump leaks, take it to a dealership: an experienced mechanic checks the transmission for damage and decides whether it needs repair or replacement (see [Bad hydrostatic transmission](bad-hydrostatic-transmission.md)).

## Parts & fitment

Use only the hydraulic oil the mower or transmission manufacturer specifies; the wrong oil can't lubricate properly and overheats.

## Tips & life hacks

- Keep the fluid at the right level and change it on schedule: low or old fluid is the common starting point for overheating.
