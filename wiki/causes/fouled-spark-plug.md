# Fouled spark plug

## Summary

A spark plug that is damaged or very dirty gives a weak, on-and-off spark or none at all. Without a spark the engine can't start and run. A plug is fouled when carbon, dirt or oil builds up on the tip. It is damaged when the porcelain insulator is cracked or the electrode is burnt. A loose plug wire, or a plug that isn't gapped to the engine maker's spec, causes the same misfire and starting trouble, so check those at the same time. Too much oil in the crankcase can also reach the plug and foul it. The fix is easy: clean a plug that is only a little dirty, or replace it. Plugs are one of the cheapest maintenance parts on a mower.

## Before you start

- How many cylinders does the engine have? A two-cylinder engine has two plugs and two plug wires, and it runs rough when one plug goes bad.
- What socket size does the plug take? It is 3/4" or 5/8", depending on the engine model.
- What electrode gap does the owner's or operator's manual specify?
- Has the mower sat over winter or for a long time? An unused plug can corrode.
- Was the crankcase recently filled with oil? Overfilling can foul the plug.
