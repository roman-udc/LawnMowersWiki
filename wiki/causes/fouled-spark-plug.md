# Fouled spark plug

## Summary

A spark plug that is damaged or very dirty gives a weak, on-and-off spark or none at all. Without a spark the engine can't start and run. The usual signs are carbon or oil on the tip, a burnt electrode or a cracked insulator. The fix is easy: clean a plug that is only a little dirty, or replace it.

## Before you start

- How many cylinders does the engine have? A two-cylinder engine has two plugs and two plug wires.
- What socket size does the plug take? It is 3/4" or 5/8", depending on the engine model.
- What electrode gap does the owner's manual specify?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the engine spins normally but doesn't fire, even after a burst of starter fluid into the air intake. A fouled or broken plug can also make the engine run rough or start hard.

Sources: [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-spark-plug.md)

## How to confirm

1. Check that the spark plug wire is firmly attached to the plug. If the wire is damaged, suspect the ignition coil instead, because the wire is part of the coil.
2. Remove the plug and look at the firing end. Look for carbon or oil deposits, a dark sooty tip, a wet tip, a burnt electrode, or broken or cracked porcelain.
3. If the plug looks good, connect a spark tester between the plug and the plug wire and crank the engine. If there's no spark with a good plug, the ignition coil is the likely fault.
4. A wet plug means fuel or oil is reaching it. Find out why before you replace the plug.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/scag-zero-turn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md)

## Fix

Replace the plug if it is fouled, wet, burnt or cracked. Set the gap on the new plug before you install it, then reattach the wire firmly.

## Parts & fitment

- **Spark plug:** get the plug specified for your engine. Many mowers have a sticker under the hood that lists the tune-up part numbers. Set the gap to the owner's manual spec. On the engine in the manufacturer guide it is 0.03". Buy two plugs for a two-cylinder engine.

## Tips & life hacks

- Put in a new spark plug at the start of each mowing season. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- Replace the plug once a year, or as often as the owner's manual says, as part of a yearly tune-up. Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-spark-plug.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-tune-up-a-riding-lawn-mower-video.md)
- Check the plug wire too. A dried-out, cracked wire can ground out. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
