# Loose or corroded battery cables

## Summary

The battery cables, wiring and terminals have to make a tight, clean connection for the starter to get power. Constant vibration and bouncing while mowing shake cables and connections loose, and moisture on the connections starts corrosion (a white or green substance). Either one breaks continuity, so the starter doesn't get the power it needs, the solenoid can't work properly, and the battery can end up with a weak charge. Bad cables can also contribute to a bad battery. Tightening and cleaning them is easy; replacing a cable or terminal is easy to moderate.

- Is it electric start? Many push mowers have no battery at all.

## How to confirm

- **Trace the circuit.** Follow the red (positive) cable from the battery to the solenoid, then the cable from the solenoid on to the starter. Every cable and wire must be secure and in good condition.
- **Look for corrosion** on terminals, cable ends and connections: white or green buildup. Terminals need excellent contact.
- On mowers that won't click at all, also follow the wiring to the ignition switch, safety switches and control module for damage or corrosion.
- **Voltage at the solenoid.** Set a meter to DC volts, put the red probe on the red (battery) post of the starter solenoid and the black probe on battery negative. You should read more than 12 volts. If not, clean the battery terminals and cable ends and recheck; if it is still under 12 volts, replace the red battery cable.

> **Safety:** don't let a meter lead touch both solenoid posts at once: it causes a severe spark and sends current to the starter. The manufacturer strongly discourages shorting across the posts with a screwdriver to start the engine, because it is dangerous and overrides the safety switches.

Loose or corroded wires can mimic a bad solenoid, so check them before replacing it ([Test the starter solenoid](../procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid.md)).

## Fix

Disconnect the battery (negative first), scrub the corrosion off the terminals and cable ends with a wire brush and baking soda, tighten everything, replace cables or terminals that are broken, keep coming loose or are too corroded to clean, and protect the terminals with dielectric grease or petroleum jelly. Full steps: [Clean the battery terminals](../procedures/clean-the-battery-terminals.md).

> **⚠ Conflicting advice (cleaning mix):** 2 cups water to 3 heaping (or rounded) tablespoons baking soda (most Sources, for example [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-wont-turn-over.md), expert-blog) vs a paste of 4 cups water with about six rounded tablespoons ([S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-things-that-are-draining-the-life-of-your-lawn-mower-battery.md), expert-blog) vs one part baking soda to one part water ([S79](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/winter-lawn-mower-battery-care.md), expert-blog) vs sprinkle dry baking soda on the terminals and moisten it, then toothbrush off the rest when it stops bubbling ([S86](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-clicks-but-doesnt-turn-over-video.md), manufacturer).

> **Safety:** if the circuit isn't broken first and the wrench touches the frame or other metal, the battery can arc and throw sparks that could blow it up. Always wear safety glasses and gloves when working on the battery.

- **Battery terminal:** replace terminals that are damaged or too corroded to clean.
- **Dielectric grease** (or petroleum jelly) to protect cleaned terminals.

## Tips & life hacks

- Make sure the mower is dry and store it in a dry place to reduce corrosion.
- In winter storage, check the terminals: a toothbrush handles dirt and minor corrosion, a wire brush handles rust and severe corrosion. Scrub the base and top of each terminal, wipe with a damp then a dry cloth, and tighten the screws and clamps.
- A wire brush speeds up the baking soda cleaning.
- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Battery care and charging](../guides/battery-care-and-charging.md).
