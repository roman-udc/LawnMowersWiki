# Dirty cooling fins

## Summary

A lawn mower engine is air cooled. A fan on top of the engine (on many engines, the flywheel's fins act as the fan) pulls cool air in and blows it down over the cylinder head and engine block, and the hot air leaves at the bottom. The cooling fins push that air around the hot parts. When the fins are packed with dirt, grass clippings, mud or oil, or are broken, air can't get around the engine, the engine overheats, and it loses power or dies after it has run for a while. Debris piled around the engine and under the shroud, or a heat shield that isn't in place, hurts cooling the same way. Cleaning the fins is easy owner maintenance; one Source suggests having a repair shop do it as part of regular maintenance if you don't.

## Before you start

- Is the engine still hot? Several Sources warn to let it cool before you reach in.
- When were the fins and the area under the engine cover last cleaned? Sources say to do it every year (every fall).
- Has the mower been washed with a hose? Spraying water at the engine pushes dirt and water into the engine area.

## How to confirm

- Let the engine cool, then remove the engine cover or shroud and look at the fins. Fins packed with dirt, grass clippings, mud or oil, or broken or damaged fins, are the fault.
- Look for grass, leaves and debris piled around the engine block, under the shroud and around the air intake.
- Check that the heat shield is installed correctly and attached securely.
- The pattern fits this Cause when the mower runs normally at first and then overheats and dies after running a while.

## Fix

Remove the engine cover, clean the dirt, grass and oil out of the fins, replace broken or damaged fins, clear debris around the engine block, under the shroud and around the air intake, and make sure the heat shield is secure. Make it part of yearly maintenance.

## Parts & fitment

The Sources give no part numbers. They say to replace broken or damaged fins; on the engines the Bad Boy Source describes, the fins are part of the flywheel. A missing or loose heat shield should be put back and attached securely.

## Tips & life hacks

- Clean the fins once a year, for example every fall, and look at them between services. A small-engine mechanic checks them during annual maintenance, but owners should look too.
- Don't wash the engine or spray water into it: that pushes dirt and water into the engine area. Clean around the engine with a small bristle brush and no high-pressure water.
- Be gentle: bent fins don't cool well.
