# Sheared flywheel key

## Summary

The flywheel key is a small metal rectangle that keeps the flywheel lined up on the crankshaft. That alignment makes the flywheel magnet pass the ignition coil at the right moment. The key is designed to shear if the blade hits a rock or stump hard enough to make the flywheel slip. This protects more expensive parts. The key can also shear from running the mower with a loose blade. A sheared key throws off the spark timing, so the spark no longer fires when the piston has compressed the fuel and air. Replacing it is a moderate job that needs a flywheel puller.

## Before you start

- Did the blade hit a rock, stump or root just before the engine stopped suddenly? Is the blade damaged or loose?
- Does a spark tester show spark? If not, check the spark plug and ignition coil first.
- Do you have a flywheel puller and a torque wrench?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): the engine stopped suddenly after a blade strike. Now it spins but won't fire, even though the spark tester sparks. The spark is there, but at the wrong time. A badly broken key stops the engine and it won't start.
- Engine runs rough or misfires: a slightly sheared key upsets the timing enough to make the engine run rough or misfire.
- Mower vibrates: the vibration articles list the flywheel key among the parts to inspect.

## How to confirm

1. Disconnect the spark plug wire, then inspect the blade. A damaged blade means an impact may have sheared the key.
2. Pull the starter rope. With a sheared key the rope may spring back instead of pulling smoothly.
3. Check for spark with a spark plug tester. If the plug sparks, the coil is good, but the timing may be off.
4. Remove the blower housing, then the nut on top of the crankshaft and the flywheel, and inspect the small key in the keyway. If the key is damaged or sheared, that confirms the cause.

## Fix

Replace the key: [Replace the flywheel key](../procedures/replace-the-flywheel-key.md). The Sears PartsDirect guide rates it difficulty 4 and about 60 minutes.

1. Disconnect the battery and remove the blower housing.
2. Feed nylon rope into the cylinder through the spark plug hole to jam the piston.
3. Remove the flywheel bolt and fan, then break the flywheel loose with a puller.
4. Remove what's left of the old key.
5. Line up the notch in the flywheel with the slot in the crankshaft, and push the new key in flush.
6. Reassemble and torque the flywheel bolt.

If the blade was loose, tighten it so the new key doesn't shear again.

> **Safety:** disconnect the spark plug wire (and the battery on a riding mower) before touching the blade or flywheel.

## Tips & life hacks

- With an impact wrench, you can remove the flywheel bolt without jamming rope into the cylinder. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- Tighten the two puller bolts a little at a time, alternating, so the flywheel lifts evenly. If it won't break free, tap the top of the flywheel with a mallet. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)
- Leave some rope outside the cylinder so it's easy to pull out, and remember to remove it before starting. Source: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-the-flywheel-key-on-a-riding-lawn-mower.md)
