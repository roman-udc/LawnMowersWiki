# Weak or dead battery

## Summary

Riding mowers, zero-turns and electric-start push mowers use a 12-volt battery to power the starter solenoid and starter motor; battery (cordless) mowers run entirely on a battery pack. A weak battery can't give the solenoid and starter enough power to turn the engine over, so the mower clicks or does nothing when you turn the key. The solenoid coil needs little current but the starter motor needs a lot, which is why a weak battery can still make the solenoid click. On mowers with an electric clutch (PTO), the same weak battery can also leave the blades unable to engage. Batteries go flat from leaving the key on, a faulty charging system or storage in the cold, and they wear out with age, extreme temperatures or mechanical failure. Testing, charging and replacing a battery are all easy.

## Before you start

- Is it a gas mower with a 12-volt starting battery, or a battery-powered mower with a removable pack? The checks differ.
- On an electric-start push mower, try the recoil (pull) starter: if it starts on the rope, the fault is in the electric start, not the engine.
- Did it click, or make no sound at all? Was the key left on, or was the mower stored over winter?
- Check the battery cables and terminals too: loose cables or corroded terminals cause the same starting trouble (see [Loose or corroded battery cables](loose-or-corroded-battery-cables.md)).
- If the battery charges but keeps going flat, suspect the charging system rather than the battery (see [Faulty charging system](faulty-charging-system.md)).

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): nothing happens, or the mower just clicks and won't turn over when you turn the key. The engine may make no noise at all.
- [Blades won't engage](../problems/blades-wont-engage.md) (electric clutch mowers): a weak battery can't power the clutch solenoid.
- [Battery keeps dying](../problems/battery-keeps-dying.md): a battery that loses its charge quickly after charging needs replacing; one that recharges fine but keeps going flat points to the charging system.
- [Battery mower won't start](../problems/battery-mower-wont-start.md), or runs for less time than before: a failing pack no longer holds its charge as long, charges very slowly, or gets very hot.
- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): general "won't start" articles list it for electric-start mowers. A weak battery usually means the engine cranks slowly or not at all (see [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md)).

## How to confirm

- **12-volt battery:** with the ignition off, set a multimeter (voltmeter) to DC volts and put the red probe on the positive (red) terminal and the black probe on the negative (black) terminal. A fully charged 12-volt battery reads about 12.7 volts; anything lower means charge it. A reading near 11.5 volts means it is almost dead. See [Test the battery](../procedures/test-the-battery.md).
- To spin the starter the battery typically needs to be at least 75 percent charged.
- A battery that won't charge above 12 volts after an hour or two on a charger is bad and must be replaced.
- Before diagnosing the starter solenoid, the battery should read at least 12.3 volts.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** a fully charged 12-volt battery reads about 12.7 volts, and below that it should be charged (most Sources, for example [S28](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-mower-wont-turn-over.md), expert-blog) vs a good battery reads "more than 12 volts DC" and under 12 volts is weak or dead ([S130](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-wont-turn-over-or-click-video.md), manufacturer).

- **Battery mower pack:** check the charge on the LED lights (on the battery, on the mower's indicator panel, or by putting the battery on its charger). On mowers with two batteries, both must be charged; the LEDs show the lower one.
- A pack is going bad when a full charge doesn't last as long as it used to, it charges very slowly, or it gets very hot.

## Fix

- **Charge it:** a slow charge with a battery charger is best. See [Charge the battery](../procedures/charge-the-battery.md).
- **Jump-start it** from a car battery with jumper cables if you need to mow right away (12-volt systems only), then keep the mower running so it charges, or charge it fully afterwards. See [Jump-start the battery](../procedures/jump-start-the-battery.md).
- **Replace it** if it won't hold a charge, won't recharge, is leaking or corroded, or (battery mowers) charges slowly or gets very hot. Recycle the old pack.
- On electric-start push mowers that won't charge, check the fuse (see [Blown fuse](blown-fuse.md)); if the fuse is fine, replace the battery; if it still won't charge, have a mechanic check the charging system.
- If it charges but keeps dying, check the [charging system](faulty-charging-system.md) and the [alternator](bad-alternator.md).

## Brand notes

- **Bad Boy, John Deere:** use a battery of at least 300 cold cranking amps (CCA) on a deck with an electric clutch; about 250 CCA is fine without one ("bigger is better"). Check it fits the battery holder.
- **Exmark, Ferris:** buy a 12-volt battery with at least 300 CCA to power an electric clutch.
- **Honda, Lawn Boy (electric-start push mowers):** charge by plugging the charger into the port connector on the handle nearest the battery, then into a 120-volt outlet. The Honda charger is optional on most models.
- **EGO:** a fully charged battery left unused for over 30 days may discharge itself to a lower level for storage. Recycle old packs (Call2Recycle lists drop-off locations).
- **Kobalt:** the battery slides into the compartment grooves; on most models you hear a click when the latches snap in.
- **SKIL:** charger LEDs: blinking green charging, solid green charged, blinking red battery may be defective, solid red battery too cold or too hot (check the charger manual).
- **Sun Joe:** has removable packs and built-in batteries charged with a cord; the charger does not shut off when full, so unplug it once charged. On many chargers red means charging, green charged.
- **WORX:** models with two batteries need both charged; one dead battery can stop the mower.

## Tips & life hacks

- Keep the battery fully charged, especially in storage. A fully charged battery won't freeze; a partly charged one left in the cold can freeze and stop holding a charge. Per the Source charts, a 12-volt battery at 100% (12.78 V) freezes around -80 °F, at 62% (12.27 V) around -16 °F, at 20% (11.67 V) around +19 °F, and a flat one at about +20 °F.
- Don't store the mower with the battery connected in the cold: it drains.
- Leaving the key on drains the battery; it can usually be recharged.
- Clean corroded terminals with a baking soda solution (2 cups water to 3 rounded tablespoons baking soda) and a wire brush.

> **Safety:** wear eye and skin protection when charging, connect the charger's positive (red) clamp first, and touch only rubber-coated parts.
- For general owner know-how, see the Guides: [Battery care and charging](../guides/battery-care-and-charging.md), [Winterizing and storage](../guides/winterizing-and-storage.md).
