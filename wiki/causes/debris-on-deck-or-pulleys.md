# Debris on deck or pulleys

## Summary

Grass clippings, mud and dirt pack the underside of the deck, and sticks, stones, acorns, bark and wood chips lodge on top of the deck, under the belt covers, in pulley grooves and under the belt tensioner. Every deck plugs sooner or later, and wet grass clumps and sticks fastest.

Under the deck, the buildup takes away the open space the blades need to move air: the deck shell, baffles and blade airflow create suction that stands the grass up to be cut. A packed deck gives a bad cut and makes the engine (or electric motor) work harder to turn the blades, so it loses power, overheats and can shut down. On top of the deck, debris under a pulley keeps it from sitting flat, so the deck vibrates; debris under the tensioner leaves the belt slack; and debris in pulley grooves stretches the belt until it rolls off or snaps. Dry debris on top of the deck is also a fire risk from belt heat.

The fix is easy: scrape the deck and clean the belt and pulley area.

## Before you start

- Does the trouble come with the blades engaged, in thick, tall or wet grass?
- When was the deck last scraped, and the top of the deck and belt covers last cleaned?
- Was the mower used in wet or damp grass?
- Is it a battery mower? Remove the battery and safety key before reaching under the deck. On a gas mower, shut it off, remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire.

## How to confirm

- With the mower off and secured, look under the deck for packed clippings, mud and dirt. Lift the deck safely with jack stands or a lift to see the underside.
- Remove the belt covers and spindle covers and look on top of the deck, under the pulleys and tensioner, and in the pulley grooves for stones, wood chips, acorns, bark or packed mud.
- On a battery mower, check that the blade turns freely and nothing is wrapped around the shaft.
- Small items lodge in surprising places: look over every crevice of the mower, not only the deck.

## Fix

There is no separate Procedure page; the steps are short:

1. Shut the mower off, remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire (on a battery mower, remove the battery and safety key).
2. Raise the deck safely (jack stands or a lift) or work from the side the manual allows.
3. Scrape the underside of the deck with a deck scraper; a stiff wire brush or metal putty knife also works.
4. Remove the belt covers and spindle covers and clear dirt, stones, grass, wood chips and mud from the top of the deck, under the pulleys and tensioner, and out of the pulley grooves. On some riding mowers it helps to lower the deck to its lowest position first; if there is no access, remove the deck per the owner's manual. A leaf blower works for loose dirt, leaves and clippings.
5. While the deck is clean, inspect it for damage and check the belt and pulleys for wear. If the blades are dull, sharpen them too: dull blades add to the load.

> **⚠ Safety:**
> - Blades are sharp: protect your hands and disconnect the spark plug wire (or remove the battery) before working under the deck.
> - Support a raised deck or mower securely on jack stands or a lift.
> - Dry debris on top of the deck can catch fire from the heat of belt friction. Keep the belt area clean.

## Tips & life hacks

- Clean the mower after each mowing; this also prevents corrosion from moisture trapped in the debris and makes failing parts easier to spot.
- Avoid cutting wet or damp grass; if you must, scrape the deck afterwards. Wet grass also shows tire tracks.
- Always mow at full throttle, and mow regularly so the grass doesn't get long and clump.
- In thick or tall grass, raise the cutting height and slow down to reduce the load (battery mowers especially).
- Spray a clean, dry deck underside with a silicone or Teflon deck spray and let it dry before mowing. It cuts down scraping but is no miracle product.
- Check and sharpen the blades about every 25 hours of use (one Source).
