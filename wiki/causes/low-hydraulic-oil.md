# Low or old hydraulic oil

## Summary

The hydrostatic transmission runs on hydraulic oil. When the oil is low or old it can't lubricate the system, so the drive feels weak, the mower moves slowly, or it won't move at all. On a zero-turn, low oil on one side makes that side weak. Running on low or aged oil can also lead to transmission problems. The fix is easy: top up to the full mark with the oil cool, find any leak, and change the oil and filters at the manufacturer's interval.

## Before you start

- Is the transmission serviceable? Some transmissions, mostly on entry-level zero-turns and some riding mowers, are sealed: the oil can't be changed. Some sealed units still have tanks you can top up. Check the operator's manual or ask the dealer.
- When was the hydraulic oil last changed, and what interval does the manufacturer give? Most have an earlier first change after a break-in period.
- What oil does the manufacturer specify for this transmission?

## How to confirm

- Check the level before use, with the oil cool, against the FULL COLD mark on the transmission overflow (expansion) tanks, or on the dipsticks, depending on the model. On zero-turns the tanks sit just behind or under the seat.
- Wipe the dirt from around the caps before you open them: dirt is often a factor in failed hydraulic systems.
- If the level is low, look for hydraulic fluid leaks.
- Check the hours since the last oil change against the manufacturer's interval.

## Fix

Add fresh hydraulic oil until it reaches the full level, with the oil cool. If the level was low, find the leak; take hydraulic fluid leaks to the dealer. If the oil is past the service interval, change the oil and filters. Sealed transmissions can't have their oil changed: take those to the servicing dealer or a repair shop.

- [Change the hydraulic oil](../procedures/change-the-hydraulic-oil.md)

Changing or adding fluid lets air into the system, which must be purged afterwards: see [Air in the hydraulic system](air-in-hydraulic-system.md).

## Brand notes

Intervals and oils as the Sources state them; confirm in the operator's manual.

| Brand | First change | Then every | Oil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ariens zero-turn | 75 hours | 400 hours | not stated |
| Bad Boy | 50 hours | 250 hours | Bad Boy hydrostatic oil or 20W-50 |
| Ferris (most zero-turns) | 100 hours | 400 hours | 20W-50 conventional detergent motor oil |
| Hustler | 75 hours or 1 year | 200 hours or 2 years | SAE 20W-50 motor oil |
| John Deere lawn tractors (incl. x500, x700) | 50 hours | 200 hours | John Deere low-viscosity HY-GARD; never mix other oils with it |
| Simplicity | 100 hours | 400 hours | 20W-50 |
| Spartan | 250 hours | 1000 hours | Parker HT-1000 |

- Ariens, Kubota, SCAG, Snapper and Troy-Bilt: change at the brand's recommended interval and check the level periodically.
- Sealed transmissions: some entry-level Ariens, Cub Cadet, Husqvarna, John Deere and Simplicity zero-turns, and John Deere 100, 200 and x300 series riding mowers. Entry-level John Deere sealed units must still be topped off; non-serviceable Simplicity tanks that accept oil are filled left and right to FULL COLD.
- Kubota zero-turns (per Source, verify against the manual): Z121SKH, Z122EBR, Z122RKW, Z125SKH, Z231BR, Z231KH, Z231KW, K251BR, K251KH, Z411KW, Z421KW, Z421KT take 5.1 qt SAE 20W-50; Z723KH, Z724KH, Z725KH take 6.3 qt Parker HT-1000; Z724XKW, Z726XKW, Z751KWi, Z781 take 7 qt Parker HT-1000.
- Walker (per Source, verify against the manual): ZTO, Hydro Gear ZT3400, 5.2 qt SAE 20W-50; ZXL, Hydro Gear ZT4400, 8 qt synthetic 15W-50; ZXT, Hydro Gear 16 cc pumps and 18 cu in wheel motors, 8 qt synthetic 15W-50.
- Bad Boy zero-turns have two reservoirs under the seat.

## Parts & fitment

- Hydraulic oil: use the type the mower or transmission manufacturer names (see Brand notes); the oils differ by transmission (20W-50 motor oil, synthetic 15W-50, Parker HT-1000, John Deere HY-GARD).
- Hydraulic oil filters: serviceable transmissions get new filters with the oil change.

## Tips & life hacks

- Check the hydraulic oil level periodically, not only when the mower acts up.
- On John Deere and Kubota zero-turns, a weak drive can also come from a worn pump drive belt or tensioner pulley; see [Worn drive belt](worn-drive-belt.md).
