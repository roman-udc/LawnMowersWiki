# Wrong fuel

## Summary

A gas mower needs unleaded gasoline with an octane rating of 87 or higher and no more than 10% ethanol (sold as Regular or E10). Gas with more ethanol (E15, E30, E85), low-octane gas, the wrong fuel type (diesel in a gas engine, or gas in a diesel), or the wrong gas/oil setup for the engine (straight gas in a 2-cycle engine) causes starting and running problems and can damage the fuel system and engine. Ethanol attracts moisture; the water and ethanol mix separates, sinks to the bottom of the tank, leaves varnish and gummy deposits, and burns hot in the engine. Fix difficulty: easy (drain and refill), unless the engine is already damaged.

## Before you start

- What fuel went in, and from which pump? Ask whether anyone else filled the gas can.
- Is the engine 4-cycle (straight gas, separate oil and fuel fill ports) or 2-cycle (a gas/oil mix through one fill port)? The gas cap or owner's manual often says.
- Is the mower gas or diesel?
- Was it running fine before the last fill-up?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): wrong gas or high-ethanol gas can keep the engine from starting.
- [Engine starts then dies](../problems/engine-starts-then-dies.md): the engine starts but shuts down after running a while. In one Source's example, a customer's mower died after the E85 pump handle was grabbed by mistake when filling the gas can.
- Sputtering and running problems: wrong gas can make the engine sputter and run poorly.
- Seized engine: straight gas in a 2-cycle engine that needs a gas/oil mix seizes it, and the engine likely has to be replaced.

## How to confirm

1. Read the label on the pump the fuel came from: it shows the octane rating and the ethanol percentage. Don't trust the handle color. Yellow, blue and green handles may mean E85, ethanol-free or diesel at one station, but colors and setups vary between stations.
2. Check that the gas is unleaded, 87 octane or higher, and 10% ethanol or less.
3. Check the engine type against the fuel: straight gas for a 4-cycle engine, the gas/oil mix for a 2-cycle engine.

## Fix

1. Drain and flush the tank, and refill with fresh unleaded gas (87 octane or higher, 10% ethanol or less) treated with a fuel additive that cleans the fuel system, reduces moisture and stabilizes the gas (Sources name Sea Foam): [Drain and refill the fuel tank](../procedures/drain-and-refill-the-fuel-tank.md). A cheap manual siphon pump works well.
2. If the problems continue, take the mower to a small engine mechanic.

For a 2-cycle engine, mix the gas with 2-cycle engine oil at the manufacturer's ratio (see [Choosing the right gas](../guides/choosing-the-right-gas.md)).

## Parts & fitment

- **Gasoline:** unleaded, 87 octane or higher, 10% ethanol or less (Regular or E10). 87 octane is usually sold as Regular.
- **2-cycle engines:** gas mixed with 2-cycle engine oil, which is not the same as regular engine oil. Use the ratio in the owner's manual.

## Tips & life hacks

- The fuel rules are the same across mower types: push, riding and zero-turn mowers and lawn tractors all want the same gas. - E15 and E85 are showing up at more stations, and most stations carry E10 and E15. Read the label every time.
