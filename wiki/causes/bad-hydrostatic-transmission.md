# Bad hydrostatic transmission

## Summary

A hydrostatic transmission uses a pump to push hydraulic fluid to a motor that turns the wheels. On a zero-turn there are two separate systems, a pump and a wheel motor on each side, so each drive wheel works on its own; moving a steering lever away from neutral raises the fluid flow, the motor pressure and the wheel speed. When the pump or wheel motor itself fails or leaks, the mower moves weakly or not at all, and on a zero-turn the failing side moves slower than the other, so the mower pulls to that side.

Most hydraulic failures come from contaminated oil. This is the expensive Cause, and it is usually not the first one to suspect: many owners fear a costly transmission repair when the real fault is old oil, a belt, a pulley or a spring. Repair is a pro job, usually at a dealer.

## Before you start

- Rule out the simple Causes first: bypass levers, drive belt, tensioner pulley and spring, and hydraulic oil level, age, temperature and trapped air.
- Is the transmission sealed? Many entry-level and some brand transmissions are sealed and can't be serviced by the owner (the oil can't be changed). Those go to a dealer or repair shop.
- **Safety:** let the transmission cool before touching it; take care with hot hydraulic fluid.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower won't move](../problems/mower-wont-move.md): the mower won't move or feels weak; it runs fine cold but gets weak or stops when the hydraulic system is hot (hot fluid can signal transmission damage); the hydraulic pump leaks.
- On a zero-turn, a failing transmission makes the wheel on that side move slower, so the mower is weak on one side or won't drive straight (Zero-turn weak on one side, Mower won't drive straight; no pages yet).

## How to confirm

The Sources leave the diagnosis to a dealer: once the simple Causes are ruled out, a mower that runs fine cold but weakens or stops when hot, or a pump that leaks, needs the dealer to diagnose the pump and wheel motor.

## Fix

- A leaking pump or failed wheel motor should be replaced. It is expensive.
- A pump rebuild kit is only a temporary fix: opening the transmission lets in contaminants that lead to failure, and in the authors' experience the pump will most likely need replacing anyway.
- Sealed transmissions: take the mower to the dealer or a service center.

## Brand notes

- **Craftsman:** most Craftsman zero-turns use sealed transmissions the owner can't service; take them to a Craftsman service center.
- **Troy-Bilt:** some entry-level hydraulic systems are sealed.
- **Husqvarna, John Deere, Kubota, Snapper, Toro:** some transmissions are sealed or unserviceable; see the dealer.
- **Hustler:** one Source names the Hustler dealership for pump leaks or a mower that runs fine cold but stops when hot.

## Parts & fitment

Hydraulic pump, wheel motor, or the whole transmission. Get the part for your model from the dealer.

## Tips & life hacks

- Never tow the mower or pull it with other equipment: it can damage the transmission. Use the bypass levers and push it.
- Keep the hydraulic oil clean: contaminated oil causes most hydraulic failures.
