# Bad fuel pump

## Summary

A mower needs a fuel pump when the carburetor sits higher than the fuel tank, because fuel has to be pushed uphill. Most use a plastic or metal vacuum fuel pump that runs off crankcase vacuum. Some engines have electronic fuel injection (EFI) with an injection fuel pump instead. When the pump cracks, leaks or stops pumping, fuel doesn't reach the engine and it turns over but won't start. Replacing the pump is a moderate job.

Most mower fuel pumps are plastic. Today's fuel, and old gas left sitting in the pump for months or years, degrades the plastic. The pump then leaks at its seams or diaphragm, or is damaged inside so it can no longer build the pressure to move fuel to the carburetor. Internal damage is hard to see, so test the pump rather than judging it by looks. Many vacuum pumps have three ports: the fuel inlet, the fuel outlet, and a fitting that uses crankcase pressure ([S65](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S33](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)).

## Before you start

- Does the mower have a fuel pump at all? Not all mowers do. Look for one if the carburetor sits above the tank.
- Is it a vacuum pump on a carbureted engine, or an EFI engine? The test is different.
- For EFI, have the operator's manual for the fuel pressure spec.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-isnt-getting-gas.md)

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): fuel reaches the pump's inlet, but no steady or pulsing flow comes out of the line to the carburetor. The pump may be visibly cracked or leaking.
- A running engine starved of fuel that only keeps going with the choke on.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/riding-lawn-mower-engine-spins-but-wont-start-video.md), [S13](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-runs-with-choke-on.md)

- Engine starts then dies, or dies after running a while: the pump can't keep up with the engine's fuel demand.
- Mower is leaking gas: fuel weeps from the pump's seams or diaphragm, or from a small crack in the housing ([S137](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/leaking-oil-or-gas.ridtr05.md)).

## How to confirm

1. Look for cracks, damage or fuel leaking from the pump. If you find any, replace it.
2. Confirm fuel reaches the pump's inlet port: shut off the fuel, pull the line off the inlet, put it in a container set lower than the tank, and turn the fuel back on. If nothing flows, check the fuel line and fuel filter first.
3. **Vacuum pump:** pull the fuel line off the carburetor and put it in a container. Turn the fuel on and start the engine. A steady or pulsing flow means the pump works.
4. **EFI pump:** read the pressure with a fuel pressure gauge and compare it with the spec in the operator's manual. The flow test doesn't apply.

## Tips & life hacks

- Test the fuel line and filter before the pump. A pump can't push fuel it doesn't receive. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-isnt-getting-gas.md)
- Shut off the fuel before pulling lines, and catch the fuel in a container. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)

- Hose pinch pliers stop the flow on mowers without a shut-off valve. Source: [S79](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/murray-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)
- Old fuel is what usually ruins a plastic pump: keep fresh fuel in the mower. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-isnt-getting-gas.md)
