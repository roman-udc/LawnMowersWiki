# Clogged air filter

## Summary

The air filter keeps dust, dirt and grass clippings out of the engine's air intake. When it isn't cleaned or replaced regularly, dirt builds up until not enough air gets through, and an engine that can't get air won't start. A badly plugged filter can also make the engine overheat or smoke, because the engine starts pulling air from wherever it can, including the crankcase. Fix difficulty: easy.

A dirty filter makes the engine run rich (too much fuel for the air), so it gives off black smoke, loses power and runs hot because it works harder to breathe. Mowing is dusty work, so the filter plugs faster than owners expect.

## Before you start

- What kind of filter does the engine use: a paper element, a paper element with a foam pre-filter, or a foam *primary* filter? The operator's manual says which, and how to clean it.
- Push mower engines usually use a thin, flat paper or foam filter (about 5" x 7"). 12–17 hp engines, often V-twins, use a larger oval paper filter with a foam pre-cleaner wrapped around it. Larger commercial engines may use a canister filter with an inner filter.
- Turn the engine off and remove the spark plug boot before taking the filter out.

- Mower is smoking: black smoke from running rich, or smoke when the engine pulls air and oil out of the crankcase and burns the oil.
- Engine overheats: it works harder to breathe. Running like this for long can cause serious engine damage.

## How to confirm

1. Remove the filter cover. Wipe dirt away from the cover and the filter's rubber seal first, so nothing falls into the intake.
2. Tap a paper filter against a solid surface to knock out loose dirt.
3. Hold it up to a light. If light shines through the element, the filter can be reused. If you can't see light, or the filter is damaged, very dirty or covered in oil, it is the problem.
4. Check any foam filter or pre-filter for tears, dark spots, or foam that has gone dry and brittle.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-lawn-mower-air-filters-differences-how-to-clean-them.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-clean-the-air-filter-in-your-lawn-tractor.md), [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-when-cold.md)

5. A filter that is dark, damp or covered in debris needs replacing.

> **⚠ Source error:** one Source's paper-filter test says to replace the filter if you *can* see light, the reverse of every other Source. It is treated as a typo and not followed here ([S160](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-cub-cadet-quits-when-it-gets-hot-solved.md)).

## Fix

- **Paper filter:** tap out the dirt and reuse it only if light shows through. Otherwise replace it. Wipe the housing out with a clean, dry rag before refitting.
- **Foam pre-filter (used with a paper filter):** wash it with water and mild dish soap, lay it flat to dry (sunlight speeds this up), and refit it with the paper filter. Never oil a pre-filter, or it will damage the paper filter.
- **Foam primary filter:** wash it, let it dry, then oil it lightly so it is coated but not dripping. See the conflicting advice below on which oil.
- Replace a foam filter that is torn or brittle, and a foam pre-filter that won't wash clean.


> ⚠ Conflicting advice (oil for a foam primary filter): [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md) and [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-lawn-mower-air-filters-differences-how-to-clean-them.md) say to use filter oil. [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md) and [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-when-cold.md) say to use clean engine oil.

> ⚠ Conflicting advice (clean or replace a dirty paper filter): [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/riding-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md) and [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-when-cold.md) say to tap it out and reuse it if light shows through. ## Parts & fitment

- **Air filter:** match the engine, not just the mower. Push mower, 12–17 hp oval and large canister filters differ in size and shape. Check the operator's manual or the old filter.
- **Foam pre-filter:** must fit the paper filter it goes with. Replace it when it is torn, brittle or very dirty.

Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-lawn-mower-air-filters-differences-how-to-clean-them.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-clean-the-air-filter-in-your-lawn-tractor.md)

- Have the engine information ready to buy the correct filter.

## Tips & life hacks

- Start each mowing season with a new air filter, then clean it several times through the season. Clean it more often in dusty conditions. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md)
- Rough intervals: change push mower filters after about 25 hours. On 12–17 hp engines, clean every 100 hours and replace at 200. On large commercial engines, clean every 100 hours and replace at 250. Source: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-lawn-mower-air-filters-differences-how-to-clean-them.md)
- On a lawn tractor, tap out or replace the paper filter every 2–3 mowing sessions, and wash the foam pre-filter every 25 hours or once a year. Source: [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-clean-the-air-filter-in-your-lawn-tractor.md)
- Never blow out a paper filter with compressed air, and never clean it with gasoline, oil, kerosene or other petroleum products. Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-turns-over-but-wont-start.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/guide-to-lawn-mower-air-filters-differences-how-to-clean-them.md), [S9](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-clean-the-air-filter-in-your-lawn-tractor.md)
- Never run the mower without an air filter, even briefly while you wait for a new one. Source: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-when-cold.md)

- The most repeated advice across the Sources: replace the filter once a year (a new filter each season), and check and clean it several times a season, ideally before each mow (it takes a minute or two). Clean or replace it more often in dry, dusty conditions or with heavy or commercial use.
- Don't try to stretch filter life. A filter is cheaper than an engine repair.
- Never run the mower without an air filter, not even to finish a job: dirt through the intake scores the cylinder and wears the seals.
- Squeeze a washed foam filter; don't wring it.
- The engine can be hot: let it cool before working near it.
