# Bad valve train

## Summary

A problem inside the engine's valve train, most often a burnt valve: an engine that has overheated burns a valve and its edges fall apart. The mower then smokes. It can only be confirmed by taking the cylinder head off or running a leak-down test, and the repair means cutting the valve and seat, so this is a pro fix for a small engine mechanic.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-reasons-your-lawn-mower-may-be-smoking.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-lawn-mower-is-smoking.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-is-smoking.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/gravely-is-blowing-blue-black-or-white-smoke.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/your-toro-lawn-mower-is-smoking.md)

## Before you start

- Rule out the simple causes first: the air filter and the oil level, then oil on the spark plug. Valve train checks come after these.
- Ask whether the mower overheated before it started smoking, and whether the muffler glows red while the engine runs.

## Fix

Replace the valve and grind the seat. The seat is where the valve contacts the engine block to complete the combustion chamber; the valve and seat are both cut at set angles so the valve seats correctly. A mechanic does this work, and some articles simply list a valve train problem as an internal problem for a repair shop.
