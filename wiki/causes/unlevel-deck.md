# Unlevel deck

## Summary

The mower deck sits lower on one side than the other, or its front-to-rear pitch (also called rake) is off. A deck that is lower on one side cuts that side shorter and leaves the other side taller, which shows as a stair-step cut. A tilted deck also leans the mower and can pull it off course. Most decks are set level side to side with the front sitting slightly lower than the rear, but the exact spec and adjustment steps vary by brand and deck model, so the owner's manual is the reference. Leveling is a moderate job; on some zero-turn decks it is tricky enough that Sources suggest a dealer.

## Before you start

- What brand and deck model is it? Leveling specs and steps differ between models, so have the owner's manual at hand.
- Are all tires inflated to spec and equal left and right? A low tire makes the deck sit unlevel and can look exactly like a deck problem.
- On a push or self-propelled mower, are all the cutting-height adjusters set to the same height?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Uneven cut](../problems/uneven-cut.md): a stair-step cut that looks like slanted steps; one side is cut short and the other is left taller. ## How to confirm

- Check the tires first and set them to spec.
- Park on a flat, level, hard surface.
- Measure from the ground to the bottom of the deck at all four corners, or from the ground to the blade tips on each side and at the front and rear of one blade. Record the readings.
- Compare side to side (should match) and front to rear against the manual's spec. On most decks the front sits slightly lower than the rear; some models want it even.

## Fix

Level the deck side to side first, then set the front-to-rear pitch, following the owner's manual for your deck model. See [Level the deck](../procedures/level-the-deck.md).

Leveling can be tricky on some decks: a trained dealer can take up to two hours, and one Source does not recommend it for the end user on some zero-turn decks. If the adjustment is beyond you, have a dealer do it.

## Brand notes

- Bad Boy, Bobcat, Hustler, Kubota, Scag, Simplicity, Snapper, Ariens, John Deere, Toro and Exmark: leveling steps and specs vary by deck model; follow the operator's manual. Exmark has many deck series with different measuring and adjustment steps.
- Troy-Bilt: riding-mower front 1/4 to 3/8 inch lower than the rear; zero-turn front 1/16 to 1/4 inch lower.
- Cub Cadet and other zero-turn decks: the front may sit slightly lower than the rear; a dealer can take up to two hours to level one.

## Parts & fitment

Leveling is an adjustment, not a part swap: the Sources adjust the existing deck hangers, leveling arms or linkages, lift links and stabilizer brackets.
