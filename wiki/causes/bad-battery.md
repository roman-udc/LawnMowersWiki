# Bad battery

## Summary

A battery mower's pack has failed and has to be replaced, rather than just being flat. Packs fail when they are old, exposed to extreme temperatures, or fail mechanically. A pack that is going bad doesn't hold a charge as long as it used to, charges very slowly, gets very hot, or no longer charges at all. A pack of the wrong size for the mower may not start it and can cause worse electrical problems. For a pack that is simply discharged, and for the 12-volt starting battery on gas mowers, see [Weak or dead battery](weak-or-dead-battery.md). The fix is easy: replace the pack with the one made for the mower.

## Before you start

- Is the pack just flat? Charge it fully first, and check it is seated and the charger works ([Battery not seated](battery-not-seated.md), [Bad charger](bad-charger.md)).
- Is it the battery size and type the manufacturer specifies for the mower?

Sources: [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/electric-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-electric-mower-wont-start.md), [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/dewalt-electric-mower-wont-start.md)

## Symptoms it produces

- [Battery mower won't start](../problems/battery-mower-wont-start.md): there isn't enough power; a fully charged pack runs out quickly, or won't take a charge.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/dewalt-electric-mower-wont-start.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/electric-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/greenworks-lawn-mower-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ryobi-electric-battery-mower-wont-start.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-electric-mower-wont-start.md)

## How to confirm

- Check the charge level: some packs show it on the battery; others must go on the charger.
- Watch the charger's lights against its manual. A charge light that doesn't come on, or an error blink pattern, can mean a bad battery (or a faulty charger).
- Note whether a full charge lasts as long as it used to, whether it charges very slowly, and whether it gets very hot. A cold battery charges more slowly, so check at a normal temperature.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/dewalt-electric-mower-wont-start.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ryobi-electric-battery-mower-wont-start.md), [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-electric-mower-wont-start.md)

## Fix

Replace the pack with a battery compatible with the mower. On electric-start push mowers with a small battery, replace the battery and/or the charger when it won't charge or hold a charge.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-electric-mower-wont-start.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-wont-start.md)

## Brand notes

- **DeWalt:** charger LEDs (check your charger's manual): blinking red = charging, solid red = charged, blinking red plus solid yellow = battery too hot or too cold (it charges slowly until the yellow goes off).
- **RYOBI:** charger lights (and, on zero-turns, the charging port LEDs and charge display) can show charging, full, failed to charge or defective.
- **Toro:** a wrong-size battery may fail to start the mower and cause more severe electrical problems.

## Parts & fitment

- **Battery pack:** use the battery compatible with your mower; the Sources give no part numbers.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/toro-electric-mower-wont-start.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Extreme heat and cold shorten pack life: store and charge the battery at moderate temperatures.
