# Clogged muffler or spark arrestor

## Summary

The muffler quiets the engine's exhaust. The Sources describe a damaged or loose muffler rather than a clogged one: a metal muffler can crack or break from weathering or corrosion, letting the noise break through the steel, and the baffles inside can break off and bounce around, making a metal rattling sound. A loose muffler is also named as a source of engine noise. No Source describes a clogged muffler or spark arrestor, so this page covers damaged and loose mufflers. The fix is to repair or replace the muffler.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-reasons-why-lawn-mowers-are-louder-than-cars.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-noisy-riding-lawn-mower.md)

## Before you start

- Stop using the mower until the noise is found and fixed.

Sources: [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-noisy-riding-lawn-mower.md)

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower is noisy](../problems/mower-is-noisy.md): the muffler is loud, or a metal rattle comes from broken baffles inside it; a loose muffler adds engine noise.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/5-reasons-why-lawn-mowers-are-louder-than-cars.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/troubleshooting-a-noisy-riding-lawn-mower.md)

## How to confirm

Look for cracks or breaks in the muffler and listen for a metal rattle from inside it.
