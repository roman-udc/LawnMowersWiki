# Bad starter motor

## Summary

The starter motor spins the flywheel when you turn the key, which starts the engine. A starter fails in two ways: it doesn't run at all when activated, or it runs but doesn't spin the flywheel because its drive gear is damaged and won't mesh with the flywheel teeth. Starters are pricey, so suspect the starter only after the battery, cables, wiring, ground and solenoid check good. Replacing it is moderate work; many owners have a starter/alternator shop test and rebuild it instead.

## Before you start

Rule out the cheaper causes first: a weak or dead battery, loose or corroded battery cables, bad wiring or ground, and a bad starter solenoid. Also make sure the engine isn't locked up: a seized engine stops the starter from running too.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): you hear a click from the solenoid but the starter motor doesn't run, or the starter whirs but the engine doesn't turn (damaged drive gear).

## How to confirm

1. [Test the battery](../procedures/test-the-battery.md) and check the cables, wiring and ground.
2. [Test the starter solenoid](../procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid.md) and check the starter cable: if the cable is good, the starter is getting power.
3. Make sure the engine turns by hand (turn the flywheel bolt clockwise with a socket wrench). If the engine spins by hand and the cable and solenoid are good, but the starter didn't spin it when activated, the starter motor has failed.
4. If the starter runs but doesn't spin the flywheel, look at the starter drive gear for damage.
5. The starter can also be removed and bench tested.

## Fix

[Replace the starter motor](../procedures/replace-the-starter-motor.md) when it won't run and other causes are eliminated, or when its drive gear is damaged.
