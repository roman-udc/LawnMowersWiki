# Wrong oil or low oil

## Summary

Engine oil lubricates the engine and also helps carry heat away. Too little oil means more friction, and friction means heat: the oil thickens, the engine overheats and shuts down, and the heat can burn the oil and melt internal parts. The wrong oil does damage too. Air-cooled small engines need air-cooled engine oil, which the Sources describe as high in zinc (an additive they call a cooling agent); car motor oil has little zinc because car engines are liquid-cooled. An oil of the wrong viscosity for the outdoor temperature can overheat the engine and shut it down, burn and smoke, or (too thick in the cold) make it hard to start.

Fix difficulty: easy when caught early (top up, or drain and refill with the right oil). Once an engine has shut down from lack of oil, most Sources say adding oil rarely fixes it and the engine is probably damaged: that's a pro job, and it can mean a new engine or a new mower.

## Before you start

- What engine is it (Briggs & Stratton, Kohler, Kawasaki, Honda, …)? The engine make is usually on a sticker on top of the engine, and the engine maker sets the oil type and amount.
- What oil and how much does the owner's or operator's manual specify? The Sources treat the manufacturer's amount as a requirement, not a suggestion.
- What's the outdoor temperature? It changes which viscosity is right.
- Did the engine already shut down or smoke from low oil? Then plan on a mechanic's diagnosis, not just a top-up.
- Do you keep having to add oil? Then something is leaking, burning or using it.

## How to confirm

1. Check the oil before each use, with the mower off and the engine cool (John Deere article). Pull the dipstick, wipe it with a clean rag, push it back in, pull it again and read it.
2. The oil must be at the full mark: not lower, and not higher.
3. Dark oil, or oil below the fill line, means change it.
4. Check which oil is in the engine against the manual: air-cooled engine oil of the right viscosity for the temperature.
5. If the oil is consistently low, find out why:
   - **Leak:** look over the engine for signs of a leak; if it has an oil filter, check that it seals.
   - **Burning oil:** a plugged air filter makes the engine burn oil and work harder.
   - **Using oil:** overheating in use, or a valve or ring problem. That needs a repair center's diagnosis.
6. Low oil can also come from not adding enough at the last oil change, or from burning oil because of the wrong viscosity.

## Fix

- **Low oil, caught early:** add fresh oil to the manufacturer's level (the full mark on the dipstick), and don't overfill. Then find out why it was low. If a low level is behind a hot smell, do a full oil change, since heated oil may have thickened.
- **Wrong oil:** drain it and refill with air-cooled engine oil in the viscosity the engine maker recommends for the outdoor temperature.
- **Leak:** find the source and replace the gaskets that leak; check the oil filter seals. **Burning oil:** clean or replace a plugged air filter.
- **Engine already shut down, smoked or still acting up after the oil is corrected:** stop and have an experienced small engine mechanic, the mower brand's servicing dealer or the engine maker's dealer tear it down and test it. Possible damage includes a blown gasket, a burnt valve and damaged internals. Weigh the estimate against the mower's age and the price of a new mower.
- Change the oil on schedule: skipping changes causes excessive wear and can cause significant engine damage.

> **⚠ Conflicting advice:** adding oil to the right level (and finding why it was low) may fix it if caught early ([S77](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/bad-boy-loses-power.md), [S80](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/honda-lawn-mower-loses-power.md), [S84](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-loses-power.md), expert-blog) vs once the engine has shut down from low oil, adding or changing oil rarely fixes it because the damage is done ([S9](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/zero-turn-mower-starts-then-dies.md), [S12](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/honda-mower-starts-then-dies.md), [S26](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/hustler-mower-starts-then-dies.md), [S45](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/solved-toro-lawn-mower-quits-when-hot.md), expert-blog). The difference is mostly timing: a top-up is worth trying, but have the engine tested if it already quit, smoked or keeps acting up.

## Parts & fitment

- **Air-cooled engine oil:** match the type and viscosity in the engine maker's manual for your outdoor temperature. Don't use car motor oil.
- **Oil filter** (riding mowers and zero-turns that have one): check it seals.
- **Gaskets:** replace the ones an oil leak comes from.
