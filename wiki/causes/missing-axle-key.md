# Missing axle key

## Summary

Many riding mowers and lawn tractors lock each rear wheel to its axle with a small key: a narrow bar about 1" to 2" long that sits in matching slots in the wheel hub and the axle. Without it, the axle turns but the wheel doesn't, so the mower won't move. The key most often falls out when a rear tire has been taken off and put back. Not every mower uses one.

The fix is easy: put a key back in.

## Before you start

- Is it a riding mower or lawn tractor? The Sources describe the axle key on riding mowers only.
- Has a rear tire been changed or removed recently? That is when the key usually falls out.
- Check the owner's manual or parts list to see whether your model uses an axle key.

## How to confirm

Look at where each rear wheel meets the axle and check that the key is in place in the slots.

Sources: [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/john-deere-riding-mower-wont-move.md), [S11](../../raw/Articles/searspartsdirect.com/how-to-replace-a-riding-lawn-mower-rear-tire.md)

## Fix

Install a new key (or the old one if you find it) in the wheel and axle key slots. When you put a rear wheel back on, apply anti-seize to the key.

The tire removal and refit steps are in [Replace a tire](../procedures/replace-a-tire.md).

## Brand notes

- **John Deere and Husqvarna lawn tractors:** the brand won't-move articles name a missing axle key after a tire change; John Deere describes it as a small narrow bar about 1" to 2" long.
- Craftsman, Kubota, Snapper and Troy-Bilt articles repeat the same "small key in the axle can fall out; replace it" advice.

## Parts & fitment

Axle key: a small square key that fits the key slots of the wheel and axle. Match the size of your mower's slots.
