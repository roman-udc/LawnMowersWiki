# Wet electrical components

## Summary

Water from rain, or moisture during storage, gets into the ignition switch, safety switches, spark plug wires and boots, and the wiring. Wet parts make bad connections, and over time moisture corrodes them. On a battery (electric) mower, water that gets in through the motor vents or the battery compartment can short the mower. Drying the parts and displacing the water is usually easy; corroded or chewed wiring needs replacing.

## Before you start

- Was the mower left out in the rain, washed, or used in wet conditions?
- Was it stored over winter with debris or moisture on it, or somewhere rodents could nest?
- Is it a gas mower or a battery (electric) mower?

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): after rain or storage, water in the ignition switch, safety switches or spark plug boot gives bad connections, so the mower won't start.
- Battery mower won't start: after rain exposure an electric mower won't turn on, or runs with less power. It may smoke if water has shorted it.
- Mower won't start after winter: moisture during storage corrodes switches and wiring, and rodents may have chewed the wires.
- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): after the mower sat in the rain, water in the electrical system can keep it from cranking; check this when the starter solenoid tests good.

## How to confirm

- Check the ignition switch, safety switches, spark plug wires and boots for water.
- Check the safety switches and wiring for good connections and no corrosion. Look for chewed wires and nests.
- On an electric mower, after drying, test the insulation resistance before powering it. One Source uses 100,000 ohms or more as the pass mark.

## Fix

- **Gas mowers:** spray WD-40 into the ignition switch, safety switches and spark plug boot to displace the water, then try starting again.
- **Electric mowers:** don't power a soaked mower. Dry the motor and wiring, clean the connections, and check the insulation resistance before use.
- Replace wiring or parts that are corroded or chewed by rodents.

> **Safety:** never switch on an electric mower that is still wet. Water can short it and make it smoke.

- **EGO (electric):** operating in the rain can short the mower so it won't start. Source: [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/reasons-why-your-ego-electric-lawn-mower-wont-start.md)
- **Craftsman and Kubota:** their starter-solenoid guides point to water in the electrical system as another reason a mower won't start after being left in the rain. ## Parts & fitment

- **Wiring, switches:** replace damaged or corroded wiring or parts with ones that match the originals.
- **Water displacer:** WD-40.

Sources: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-after-winter.md), [S8](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/when-your-riding-mower-gets-wet.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Water gets into electric mowers through the motor vents and the gap around a hinged battery compartment. Keep those dry. Source: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/can-you-use-your-electric-lawn-mower-in-the-rain.md)
- Clean debris off before storage, and use a rodent deterrent (packets around the mower and a spray for the wiring) the next time you store it. Source: [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/lawn-mower-wont-start-after-winter.md)
