# Hydrolocked engine

## Summary

A hydrolocked engine has oil in the cylinder. Oil gets past the piston rings (worn rings make this easier) and fills the combustion chamber. The piston is built to compress air, not oil or water, so it can't reach the top of its stroke: pulling the rope or using the starter won't move it. The usual trigger is too much oil in the crankcase; the oil pan on a small mower engine is very small, so there is little room for error. Trying to start a hydrolocked engine can bend the piston or connecting rod, which turns a cheap fix into an engine repair.

## Before you start

Ask when the oil was last changed or topped up and how much went in. An overfilled crankcase is the usual way oil ends up in the cylinder.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): the engine turns over very hard or won't turn at all, with the starter or with the pull rope. It can also lock up completely.

## How to confirm

Check the oil level: too much oil in the crankcase, together with an engine that won't turn or turns very hard, points to hydrolock.

## Fix

Trying to start a hydrolocked engine can bend the piston rod, so stop cranking. Remove the spark plug wire first, then remove the excess oil down to the dipstick level (see the Overfilled oil cause and the Drain excess oil procedure, once they exist). If draining a little doesn't fix it, have a small engine mechanic, dealer or engine-maker repair center inspect it: tearing the engine down yourself may cause more damage.

> **Safety:** disconnect the spark plug wire before working on the engine.
