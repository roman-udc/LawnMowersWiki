# Cracked fuel filter

## Summary

Most mowers have a plastic inline fuel filter in the fuel line after the tank. Fuel sits in it all the time, and the gasoline (its ethanol in particular) degrades the plastic housing. A filter left unchanged past its normal interval goes weak and soft, then leaks at the seams or cracks. Replacing it is easy; the one catch is that the softened ends can break off inside the fuel line.

> **Safety:** a leaking filter drips gasoline, which is flammable. Stop the fuel flow before you pull the filter, and keep a rag ready to catch fuel.

## Before you start

- When was the filter last changed? The Sources say to replace it annually.
- Has the mower been run on old or bad gas? Bad gasoline is corrosive and damages the filter.
- Does the mower have a filter at all? Not every push mower has one.

## How to confirm

- Look for wetness at the filter's seams and check the plastic for cracks.
- Squeeze gently: a filter that has gone soft or looks melted is degraded. The ends may crush when you go to remove it.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/10-reasons-your-lawn-mower-could-be-leaking-gasoline.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-is-leaking-gas.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/ariens-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas-fixed.md)

## Fix

Replace the filter. Fit the new one with the arrow on its housing pointing the way fuel flows: away from the tank, toward the carburetor. Replacement filters are sold online and at hardware stores.

## Parts & fitment

- Inline fuel filter: match the type on the mower; note the flow arrow when fitting. A push mower without a filter can have one installed.

Sources: [S10](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/push-mower-is-leaking-gas.md), [S7](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/honda-lawn-mower-is-leaking-gas.md)

## Tips & life hacks

- Pull the filter ends off the fuel lines carefully. Softened plastic can break off inside the hose.
- Replace the filter at least once a year, so it never sits long enough to soften.
- The author of one Source recommends recreational (non-ethanol) fuel for mowers.
