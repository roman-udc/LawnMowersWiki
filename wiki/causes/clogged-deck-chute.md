# Clogged deck chute

## Summary

Grass clippings (or leaves) and dirt pack the underside of the deck and plug the discharge or bagger chute. The deck shell, baffles and spinning blades create the airflow and suction that stand the grass up to be cut and carry the clippings out of the deck; a packed deck has no room for that air. The result is a bad or uneven cut with clumps, a bagger that won't fill, and an engine (or electric motor) that works harder to turn the blades through the buildup, so it bogs down, runs rough, overheats and can shut down. Dull blades make the load worse. Wet or damp grass clumps and sticks fastest. The fix is easy: scrape the deck and clean the chute.

Scope: this page covers clippings packed under the deck and in the discharge chute (the airflow path). Sticks, stones and debris on top of the deck, under the belt covers and around the pulleys are on [Debris on deck or pulleys](debris-on-deck-or-pulleys.md). The Sources often just say "plugged deck", and the extraction counted those mentions under one page or the other, so the two pages overlap for a packed deck.

## Before you start

- Shut the engine off, remove the ignition key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s) or boots; protect your hands from the blades.
- On a battery mower, remove the battery and safety key before reaching under the deck.
- To look under a riding mower or zero-turn deck, raise it safely with jacks or jack stands that keep it in the air.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Mower doesn't bag grass](../problems/not-bagging-grass.md): a grass-packed deck or a chute plugged with old or wet grass leaves no air moving the clippings up the tubes.
- [Mower cuts uneven](../problems/uneven-cut.md): without suction the grass isn't lifted, so the cut is bad, with clumps; wet grass also leaves tire tracks that look like a bad cut.
- [Engine starts then dies](../problems/engine-starts-then-dies.md): a plugged deck loads the engine and bogs it down until it stalls.
- [Engine overheats](../problems/engine-overheats.md): the strained engine runs hot.
- [Engine runs rough](../problems/engine-runs-rough.md): the loaded engine works harder and seems sluggish or sputtering.
- [Engine loses power](../problems/engine-loses-power.md): the engine has to turn the blades through the buildup.
- [Engine dies when hot](../problems/engine-dies-when-hot.md): the strained engine gets hot and quits.
- [Battery mower won't start](../problems/battery-mower-wont-start.md): grass under the deck keeps the blade from turning and can stop the mower starting.

## How to confirm

Shut the mower off and make it safe, raise the deck, and look underneath: a deck packed with grass (or leaves) needs scraping. Check the discharge or bagger chute for old or wet grass. While you are under there, inspect the deck for damage.

Sources: [S1](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/15-reasons-why-your-lawn-mower-is-not-picking-up-grass.md), [S20](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/pick-up-leaves-with-a-mower.md), [S6](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-dies-while-mowing.md), [S31](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/why-your-craftsman-lawn-mower-dies-how-to-fix.md)

## Fix

1. Scrape the underside of the deck with a deck scraper, metal putty knife or wire brush, and clean out the chute.
2. Sharpen dull blades: they magnify the load.
3. Optional: coat the clean, dry deck underside and the inside of the chute with a silicone or Teflon spray made for mower decks. It reduces sticking and how often you scrape, but it is not a miracle product and won't stop all buildup.
4. Mow at full throttle with the blades engaged, and avoid wet or damp grass.

## Brand notes

- **Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Troy-Bilt:** blades that hit debris on each turn draw on the engine, which can overheat and shut down; inspect the deck for damage, scrape it, sharpen the blades and always run at full throttle.
- **Toro and riding mowers in general:** a clogged deck strains the engine so it works hard and may shut down; scrape the deck periodically.
- **EGO, Greenworks, Worx (battery mowers):** a plugged deck also slows the blades and works the electric motor harder.

## Tips & life hacks

- Don't mow wet or damp grass: it clumps under the deck and in the yard. If you must, scrape the deck afterwards.
- Mow more often so the grass isn't long; double- or triple-cut long grass.
- Scrape more often in wet conditions.
- Silicone spray inside the bagger chute helps grass and leaves flow.
- On a battery mower overloaded by thick, tall or wet grass: raise the cutting height and slow down.
- For general owner know-how, see the Guide: [Cleaning the mower](../guides/cleaning-the-mower.md).
