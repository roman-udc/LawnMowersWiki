# Bad cooling shroud

## Summary

An air-cooled engine relies on its engine guard (also called the shield or shroud) to keep cool air circulating around the engine. If a mounting screw falls out and the guard shifts out of position, or the guard is missing or damaged, the cool air escapes where the shield no longer sits and the engine area stays hot. Grass clippings, dirt and debris packed under the engine shroud block the air passages the same way. The engine overheats and can lose power. The fix is easy: clean under the shroud and put the guard back in place, tightening or replacing its screws or replacing a damaged guard.

## Before you start

- Has the engine guard or shield been off, or has the mower been worked on recently?
- Is there grass or dirt packed around and under the engine shroud?

## How to confirm

Look at the engine guard: check that it is in its correct position, that its mounting screws are all there and tight, and that it isn't damaged. Look under the engine shroud for collected clippings, dirt and debris.

Sources: [S3](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-mower-overheating.md), [S4](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/cub-cadet-mower-engine-overheats.md), [S5](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/husqvarna-mower-engine-overheats.md), [S2](../../raw/Articles/poweredoutdoors.com/craftsman-mower-loses-power.md)

## Fix

- Put the guard back in its correct position and tighten or replace the mounting screws.
- Replace a missing or damaged engine guard.
- Remove grass clippings, dirt and debris collected under the engine shroud, and make sure the heat shield is securely in place.

There is no Procedure for this Cause yet. Packed cooling fins block the same airflow: see [Dirty cooling fins](dirty-cooling-fins.md).

The brand articles for Craftsman, Cub Cadet, Husqvarna, Hustler, John Deere and Kubota give the same check: guard in position, screws tight or replaced, missing or damaged guard replaced. Their brand troubleshooting tables (listed under Sources for Engine overheats below) name a missing or loose engine guard as an overheating cause.
