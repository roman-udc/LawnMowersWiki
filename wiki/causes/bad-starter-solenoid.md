# Bad starter solenoid

## Summary

The starter solenoid is an electromagnetic on/off switch. When the key is turned to start, it sends the larger current the starter motor needs, and you normally hear it click. Every electric-start mower has one. Inside are a spring and a copper plate: it goes bad when the spring weakens or the copper plate corrodes, and it can also fail as a result of a weak starter, a bad battery or a bad ground. When it fails, the starter doesn't get the current to turn the engine. Testing and replacing it is easy to moderate.

## Before you start

- Is the battery fully charged? Sources say to charge it before testing the solenoid, because a weak battery gives the same symptoms. See [Charge the battery](../procedures/charge-the-battery.md).
- Have the battery, cables and connections already been checked? Several Sources only point to the solenoid once those are known good.
- Find the solenoid: follow the positive (+) battery cable from the battery; it attaches to one side of the solenoid. It is usually mounted on the starter motor, but some sit on the frame closer to the battery.
- Note its shape and posts (round or square, 3 or 4 posts). Wiring differs between mowers, and some schematics include lights and 12-volt ports.

## Symptoms it produces

- [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md): a click or hum when the key is turned and the engine doesn't turn over; the mower keeps clicking. Another sign is a wire on the solenoid getting hot and starting to smoke or melt. One Source adds a starter that engages and won't disengage after the key is released. A solenoid that gives no click at all when the key is turned to start is also suspect.
- [Engine cranks but won't start](../problems/engine-cranks-but-wont-start.md): general "won't start" articles list it for electric-start mowers; a click or hum with no cranking points to [Engine won't crank](../problems/engine-wont-crank.md) instead.

## How to confirm

- **Check the feed first.** Before replacing the solenoid, check for loose or corroded wires and a bad ground: they keep the solenoid from getting the power it needs and can mimic a bad solenoid.
- **Bypass test.** If the starter turns the engine over (or the mower starts) with the solenoid bypassed, the solenoid is likely bad.
- **Voltage at the starter post.** Disconnect the starter cable from the solenoid post, put the red meter probe on that post and the black probe on battery negative, and hold the key in START. You should hear the click. More than 12 volts at the click means the solenoid is passing power; no voltage when it clicks means replace the solenoid. Clip-on probes or a helper to turn the key make this easier.
- **Voltage at the coil wires.** Voltage at the solenoid's coil wires with the key at START but no click means the solenoid is bad.
- Replace it if it doesn't click when it gets power from the ignition switch, or if it clicks but doesn't send current to the starter.

Steps are in [Test the starter solenoid](../procedures/test-the-starter-solenoid.md).
