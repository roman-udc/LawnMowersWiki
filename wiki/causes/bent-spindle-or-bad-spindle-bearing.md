# Bent spindle or bad spindle bearing

## Summary

The spindle housing assembly (also called the cutter housing, quill assembly or, in Sears parts lists, the mandrel assembly) is the part bolted to the deck that the blade attaches to and spins on. Inside it are a shaft (the spindle) and bearings. If the bearing wears out or the spindle bends, the blade wobbles. At full blade speed that wobble becomes a strong vibration through the deck and a bad or uneven cut, and on riding and zero-turn mowers it can knock the deck belt off.

Bearings wear with normal use and age; Sears says mandrel bearings often wear out after a season. Unbalanced blades speed up the wear, and hitting a rock or other hard object can bend the spindle or damage the bearing in one go. A worn bearing can also drag, so the engine loses power when the blades are engaged, and a seized one can stop the blade turning at all.

The fix is moderate: replace the bearing in the housing, or replace the whole spindle housing assembly.

## Before you start

- Did the vibration start after the blade hit something hard? Check the blade too (see [Dull or bent blade](dull-or-bent-blade.md)): an impact that bends a blade can also damage the spindle and bearing.
- Write down the deck's model and serial number (on some brands this differs from the mower's), so you get the right housing, bearing or shaft.
- Find out whether your deck's spindle housing can be rebuilt with a new bearing or is sold only as a sealed, complete assembly.
- Remove the ignition key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s) before reaching under the deck, and wear heavy work gloves: blade edges are sharp.

## How to confirm

1. Remove the key and disconnect the spark plug wire(s). Raise the deck as high as it goes and support it safely (jack stands) so you can reach underneath.
2. Wear heavy work gloves. With the blade still installed, grab each end of the blade and rock it up and down.
3. A good spindle feels solid: no movement and no knocking. Up-and-down play or a knocking sound, while the blade itself is tight on the housing, means a bad bearing (most likely) or a bent spindle.
4. Check every spindle on the deck, not just one.
5. To be sure, remove the blade and the spindle housing and inspect the bearing and shaft for wear or damage. A shaft that vibrates or doesn't spin freely inside the housing needs replacing. Some Sources also check the spindle housings for play or noise when tracking down grinding sounds.

Do this check before you remove a blade for sharpening, and after any hard blade impact.

## Fix

Replace the bad bearing in the spindle housing, or replace the whole spindle housing assembly; replace a damaged or bent spindle. Which you can do depends on the deck: some manufacturers sell bearings to rebuild the housing, some sell only the complete assembly (often because the bearing is sealed), and some sell both.

## Brand notes

- **Exmark:** the cutter housing uses a sealed bearing and is sold only as a complete assembly; Exmark doesn't offer the bearing alone, and dealers typically carry only the complete cutter housing and don't recommend rebuilding.
  > **⚠ Conflicting advice:** some Exmark owners find a matching bearing and rebuild the housing; the author of the Exmark articles says that, in his experience, a rebuild doesn't last as long as a new assembly (expert-blog, the author's own experience). - **Scag, Kubota, Ariens:** on most decks the bearing alone can be replaced in the housing (cheaper); some models need the whole assembly. Kubota: use the deck's model and serial number, not the mower's, with Kubota's online parts diagram or a dealer.
- **Cub Cadet:** some spindle housings are sealed (replace the whole assembly), others can be rebuilt.
- **Gravely, Troy-Bilt:** usually either the spindle bearing or the whole spindle assembly can be replaced.
- **Husqvarna, John Deere, Craftsman:** bearing-only or whole-assembly replacement varies by model and deck.
- **Toro:** smaller units call the spindle housing a quill assembly; replace the bearings, or the whole cutter housing if available. Noisy, badly vibrating spindles can throw the belt off the deck.
- **Sears guide (Craftsman, MTD, Troy-Bilt, Husqvarna, Ariens, Cub Cadet, Snapper riding mowers and lawn tractors):** the part is called the mandrel assembly; what it includes varies, so check your model's parts diagram.

## Parts & fitment

- Spindle housing assembly (cutter housing, mandrel): housings and spindles vary by deck, so match the deck model and serial number. What's included varies (Sears's example includes the blade shaft and bearings), so check the parts diagram. A full set can include nut, washer, pulley, key, shaft and housing, but not all need replacing if the fault is caught early.
- Spindle bearing: only where the housing can be rebuilt; sealed housings take a whole assembly.
- Use a manufacturer-approved part. If unsure, bring the old assembly to the dealer; staff can identify it.

## Tips & life hacks

- Keep blades balanced: unbalanced blades wear out spindle bearings.
- Examine the spindles regularly and after any hard hit; rock-test them each time before you take the blades off to sharpen.
- Disassemble first to see which parts you actually need: shaft threads can get damaged on the way out.
